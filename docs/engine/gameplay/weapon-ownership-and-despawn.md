# Weapon ownership, gives, and restores

The inventory's add, the pickup, the equip, and the drop are in [weapon-equip-and-drop.md](weapon-equip-and-drop.md), and a weapon lying in the world in [item-pickup.md](item-pickup.md).

## A weapon is kept by strong references

The spawn system keeps a strong reference to every object it spawned, weapons included. An inventory slot ([`Inventory::m_Weapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_Weapons)) is another strong reference, and so is any reference taken by locking the weapon's weak reference to itself ([`WeaponBase::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeakThis)). The object is destroyed when its last strong reference goes.

The spawn system's per-frame despawn ranking ([`SpawnSystem::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-Update)) reads each spawned object's strong count. An object with a count other than one (the spawn system's own) ranks zero and is never queued for despawn, even when its budget pool is over its instance limit. Any other object is ranked from its distance to the streaming centre and the view, whether it is dead, and its interest; past its pool's threshold it is queued for despawn, and the pool's highest-ranked object is queued when the pool is over its limit. The mission-critical flag protects an object only while a mission or an encounter is active, and is cleared otherwise.

So a held weapon is never despawned by the ranking, and is ranked again from the frame after release. A dropped weapon has no despawn timer, only the rank. Explicit despawns (area clean-ups, despawn volumes, mission clean-ups) and the rank's formula are unverified.

## Into and out of a slot without a drop

[`Inventory::RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RemoveWeapon) empties the slot and releases its reference, nothing else: no drop or despawn, and the weapon's state, user, and holder ([`WeaponBase::m_Holder`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Holder)) stay as they were. A weapon held by another strong reference therefore stays holstered on its character, outside the inventory. The way back is the inventory's add ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md](weapon-equip-and-drop.md#the-inventorys-add)).

A weapon's placement ([`WeaponBase::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) places its guns on its holder's bones for its state, whoever calls it; the inventory calls it for each slot's weapon. [`Inventory::UpdateWeaponVisibility`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponVisibility) shows or hides the slots' weapons with their character. Neither reaches a weapon outside the slots.

## The give

Every weapon the game gives the player goes through the inventory's respawn table: missions and their checkpoint restores, the challenges, the graph script's `SpawnWeapons` node, the `equip weapon` script and console event, a rack's infinite copies, the respawn after a spawn reset, and the game-completion reward.

1. [`Inventory::GivePlayerWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GivePlayerWeapon) writes the slot's entry: the type ([`m_WeaponToSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawn)), the rounds ([`m_WeaponToSpawnAmmunition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnAmmunition)), whether rounds beyond the magazine go to the reserve ([`m_WeaponToSpawnExtraAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnExtraAmmo)), whether the occupant is dropped rather than despawned ([`m_WeaponToSpawnDropOld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnDropOld)), and, when the give starts equipped, the equipped slot ([`m_WeaponToSpawnEquippedWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnEquippedWeapon)).
2. [`Inventory::RequestWeaponSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RequestWeaponSpawn) asks the spawn system for the entry's type unless a spawn is already pending for the slot ([`m_WeaponPendingSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponPendingSpawn)). The disassembler swaps the names of this function and the grappling device's request ([`RequestSpawnGrapplingDevice`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RequestSpawnGrapplingDevice)).
3. The spawn system calls back [`InventoryWeaponSpawnCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/character::inventory##func-InventoryWeaponSpawnCallback), which despawns a weapon that is not a profile item and hands others to the inventory.
4. [`Inventory::WeaponSpawned`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-WeaponSpawned) checks the weapon's spawn tag index against its slot's entry (the slot being the weapon's [`m_EquipSlot`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_EquipSlot)). On a match it clears the slot's removal mark, picks the equip mode (equipped for the entry's equipped slot, after unequipping the wielded weapon; equipped only into the selected slot when no slot is named; not equipped otherwise), and passes the weapon to [`Character::PickUpWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-PickUpWeapon), whose add drops or despawns the occupant by the entry's drop-old flag. It then sets the magazine from the entry, moves the overflow into the reserve when the entry asks, applies the character's visibility to every slot weapon, and clears the entry. On a mismatch it removes the occupant, or despawns the newcomer into an empty slot.

Only respawn-table entries pass through the callback and `WeaponSpawned`: the post-reset handler's requests ([below](#the-spawn-reset-and-the-respawn-table)) and the gives. [`Inventory::RespawnPlayerGear`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RespawnPlayerGear) re-requests every entry not already pending, and the grappling device; the player's post-physics update calls it once the save data has loaded. [`RespawnPlayerWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RespawnPlayerWeapon) requests the table with the two-handed slot as the equipped slot.

A weapon's type is its spawn tag index ([`GameObject::GetUniqueSpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##func-GetUniqueSpawnTagIndex)), and its persistent form is the tag's hash ([`SpawnSystem::GetTagHashFromTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-GetTagHashFromTagIndex), back with `GetTagIndexFromTagHash`, which gives an invalid index for a hash no loaded tag has).

## Removals

A removal is marked for later or done at once:

- [`Inventory::ForceRemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-ForceRemoveWeapon) marks a slot ([`m_WeaponToRemove`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToRemove)), optionally emptying the weapon's magazine and its type's reserve and clearing the slot's respawn entry; [`ForceRemoveAllWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-ForceRemoveAllWeapons) marks every slot, and [`CancelRemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-CancelRemoveWeapon) clears a mark.
- The next pre-physics update ([`Inventory::UpdatePrePhysics`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdatePrePhysics)) carries out each mark through [`Internal_RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-Internal_RemoveWeapon): a mounted gun is stopped and released, and any other occupant is despawned.
- [`Inventory::DespawnWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DespawnWeapon) empties a slot and despawns its weapon at once; the challenges call it directly, past the marks.
- The weapon's despawn ([`WeaponBase::Despawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-Despawn)) unequips it, hides it, puts its bodies into the physics world, sets its spawn ranking so the spawn system's despawn takes it, and stops its fire. The object lives on, hidden, while another strong reference holds it.

The other ways out of a slot are the drop ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md](weapon-equip-and-drop.md#dropping)) and `RemoveWeapon`. The character's damage handling marks every slot and clears the inventory for one instant-removal damage flag (unverified).

## The spawn reset and the respawn table

[`SpawnSystem::ResetSystem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-ResetSystem) sends `spawn.reset_all_spawn_rules` at once, destroys every pending spawn request, releases its references to every spawned object, and posts `spawn.post_reset_all_spawn_rules`, handled later. A held object outlives the reset, unknown to the spawn system.

### What resets

The load game state's entry ([`GameStateLoad::OnEnter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateLoad##func-OnEnter)) runs its reset step ([`GameStateLoad::ResetSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateLoad##func-ResetSystems), which the disassembler mislabels as a console lock getter): it aborts an active challenge unless the no-unload flag is set, then resets the dialogue, the spawn system, and the heat. The frontend's reset ([`GameStateFrontend::ResetSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateFrontend##func-ResetSystems)) resets it on the quit to the menu, before a load or a new game.

The load state is entered for a load, a continue, a new game, and a world reload (unverified), and by every player teleport that asks for it ([`GameWorld::TeleportPlayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game_world::GameWorld##vfunc-TeleportPlayer) with its load-state argument). The reset does not say what it is for; what precedes it does. A save-data load precedes a load's or continue's reset, and the teleports with the load state are of two kinds:

- The player's respawn ([`Player::ReInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-ReInit)), the only one that also revives the character. The restart from a restart point ([`MissionManager::GoToRestartPoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-GoToRestartPoint)) calls it with the point's transform, or with none (the default location) when the point is not found, on the player's death, a mission's reset and failure, the teleport to a mission's start, and the return to a challenge's start; the player's post-physics update calls it on a request flag, with no transform.
- Teleports that keep the character as it is: the fast travels, a challenge's start, retry, restart, and abort, the attack-wave and destruction challenges, and the map's teleport (unverified).

A chapter change resets only if it teleports with the load state (unverified). Teleports without it (a placed teleport object by default, the teleport event handler, the player spawn manager's in-game teleport) do not reset; they force the character's neutral state at the start and respawn the player's gear at the end (unverified).

On a death, `ReInit` revives the character (forcing its neutral state, [`Character::ForceNeutralState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-ForceNeutralState), which unequips the wielded weapon), returns to normal aim, teleports with the load state, fills the slot weapons' reserves ([`Inventory::GiveMaxAmmoToAllWeaponInInventory`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GiveMaxAmmoToAllWeaponInInventory)), and writes the inventory's reset flags ([`Inventory::m_SpawnSystemResetFlags`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemResetFlags)). With a mission active, `GoToRestartPoint` then replays the checkpoint's weapons ([below](#the-mission-managers-checkpoint-cache)), whose gives land in the respawn table before the reset on the next frame, by which time the health is full.

### The inventory's handlers

Each inventory subscribes both events ([`m_SpawnSystemPreReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemPreReset) and `m_SpawnSystemPostReset`) and handles them in [`Inventory::HandleEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HandleEvent), telling them apart with [`EventMatches`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/event##func-EventMatches).

On the pre-reset event it refreshes the HUD's weapons and fills the respawn table. With [`RESPAWN_RESET_LOADOUT`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::SpawnSystemResetFlags##flag-RESPAWN_RESET_LOADOUT) set it copies the reset loadout ([`m_WeaponToSpawnReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnReset)) in; otherwise, for each slot with no pending entry, a weapon, and not the heavy slot, it records the occupant's type with its magazine (with [`FULL_MAGAZINES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::SpawnSystemResetFlags##flag-FULL_MAGAZINES), a full magazine and the extra-ammunition flag). It clears the pending spawns, records the selected slot as the equipped slot, does the same for the grappling device, and clears the flags.

On the post-reset event it marks all eight slots for removal, clears the character's next selected slot, and requests the spawn of every slot's entry at the character and of the grappling device. Each arriving respawn clears its slot's mark before its add replaces the occupant; marks still standing at the next pre-physics update despawn.

So every reset replaces every slot weapon with a new object of the same type, equips the selected slot's, and keeps each magazine (or fills it with `FULL_MAGAZINES`); the selection survives only as the equipped slot. A weapon held outside the slots survives as an object, without spawn-system ownership, and stays where it was before the teleport.

## What a save stores

The player's part of a save ([`PlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/save::PlayerSaveData)) is stored by [`StorePlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-StorePlayerSaveData): each slot's weapon type as a tag hash (zero for an empty slot, and always zero for the heavy slot), every reserve, the grappling device's type, the player's upgrade name, two bavarium upgrade flags, and the downloadable content bits. No magazine, selected slot, or other per-weapon state is stored; upgrades are the profile's. Reserves are per ammunition type, shared by weapons in and outside the slots.

[`LoadPlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-LoadPlayerSaveData) reads them back, resets the respawn table, writes each known type's tag index into it with a full magazine, and sets each reserve. It spawns nothing: the post-reset handler spawns the table, and `RespawnPlayerGear` re-requests its unspawned entries once the save data has loaded. Whether the post-reset event is handled after the load in every load path is unverified.

## The mission manager's checkpoint cache

The mission manager ([`MissionManager`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager)) snapshots the slots at every checkpoint: saving a restart point ([`SaveRestartPointToMap`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-SaveRestartPointToMap)) while a mission is active runs [`CacheWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-CacheWeapons), which records each of the eight slots ([`m_CachedWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##field-m_CachedWeapons), one [`WeaponCacheEntry`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::WeaponCacheEntry) per slot: the type, the reserve of its ammunition type, and the magazine), counting a mounted weapon as empty, and removes nothing.

[`RestoreCachedWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-RestoreCachedWeapons) replays it. Its one caller is `GoToRestartPoint`, right after the respawn, while a mission is active. It follows the shared rules ([below](#what-a-restore-does-to-a-slot)); its gives carry the reserve and the magazine as their rounds, unequipped, with the overflow to the reserve and the occupant despawned.

## The graph script's cache

The graph script node `SpawnWeapons` ([`NGSONodes_SpawnWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/graph_script##func-NGSONodes_SpawnWeapons)) strips, gives, caches, and restores weapons with a [`NodeWeaponCache`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache). Its fields are a removal type (only replace, remove where the field is empty, or remove all and replace), a weapon and ammunition for each of the dual-wield, two-handed, special, hand-grenade, and planted-explosive slots, the equipped weapon, and the cache.

- On its main input it clears the cache, records every slot ([`CacheAllWeaponSlots`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-CacheAllWeaponSlots), called only from this node, which also forces the slots out for the remove-all type), and returns the player to normal aim. Then per field it marks the slot for removal through `ForceRemoveWeapon` for an empty field under remove-where-empty or remove-all, keeps a weapon of the field's type (setting its reserve and cancelling its removal), and otherwise gives the field's weapon, equipped for the equipped field's slot. With no equipped field it unequips the wielded weapon through the deferred unequip ([`WeaponBase::UnequipDeferred`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UnequipDeferred)) and relaxes the character; with an equipped field and nothing to give it selects that slot.
- A second input only caches.
- A third input restores: a return to normal aim, [`NodeWeaponCache::RestoreWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-RestoreWeapons), and a refresh of the HUD's weapons ([`HUDUI::InitWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-InitWeapons)).
- Its update waits until no slot's spawn is pending.

`RestoreWeapons` follows the shared rules but skips the heavy slot and never removes the planted explosive. The disassembler mislabels it as the cache's lookup, and gives its name to an unrelated player-save-data store helper.

## The challenges

The challenge manager and the challenge object keep their own loadout records:

- Starting a challenge ([`ROMManager::StartROM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMManager##func-StartROM)) whose spawn point overrides the loadout records each slot's type and total ammunition, marks every slot for removal, and gives the spawn point's weapons, equipped for the dual-wield and two-handed slots and dropping the occupants. Aborting it ([`AbortROM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMManager##func-AbortROM)) with that record marks every slot again and gives the recorded weapons back the same way.
- The challenge object's initialisation ([`ROMObject::PostInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMObject##func-PostInit)), unless restarting or retrying, records every slot's type and magazine and every reserve; it then despawns at once each slot the challenge's loadout permission does not allow (the grenade and planted-explosive slots, and each of the dual-wield, two-handed, and special slots whose profile item fails the challenge's mask), and gives the weapons the player picked in the challenge's loadout menu.
- [`ROMObject::RestoreWeaponsAndAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMObject##func-RestoreWeaponsAndAmmo) empties every slot and gives each slot recorded with a weapon its recorded type and magazine, then restores every reserve but the beacons' and flares', and requests an autosave. The challenge object's destruction calls it to despawn the slots and give at once; for one kind of challenge the destruction instead marks the challenge manager, and the load state's reset step calls it to take the slots' weapons out without despawning them and write only the respawn table, which the spawn reset then spawns.

Starting, retrying, restarting, and aborting a challenge each teleport with the load state, and so pass through the spawn reset.

## What a restore does to a slot

All three restores compare by spawn tag index, slot by slot, and read nothing but the eight slots and the reserves. Each first zeroes each occupied slot's ammunition reserve. A slot of the cached type keeps its object, with the cached reserve and magazine; a slot holding a different type, or an empty one, gets a give that despawns any occupant rather than dropping it; and a slot holding a weapon where the cache had none is marked for removal. A weapon held outside the slots is never removed, despawned, or duplicated.
