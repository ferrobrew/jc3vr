# The body rig

The rig keeps Rico's body plausibly under the player's head. The player's real head is ground truth, and the camera stays on it throughout: the body follows the head, never the other way round. On the walk and wherever the learned estimate is missing, the legs and the pelvis are the game's own animation, so the feet touch the ground where the game's clips put them; standing on foot with a fresh estimate, the IK body poses them instead, with the feet planted by HumanIK ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md)). Either way the rig moves Rico by driving the game's own character movement. The upper body follows the player's real posture: the spine bends toward the head, the neck carries the rest of the head's turn, and the arms reach for the controllers. When the rig and the player disagree, the result is an extra step, never a broken skeleton.

The rig's pure core (the pelvis solver, the step commit, the yaw follow, and the standing height) is the `body-rig` crate, which the payload runs once per rendered frame and the offline evaluator runs over recordings ([Offline evaluation](#offline-evaluation)). The game-facing parts are in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/body/rig/`. The settings are `controllers.body.rig` (`steps.enabled` and the `pelvis`, `step`, and `yaw` tables); the rest of the rig's numbers are its [tuning](../tuning.md), `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/rig.toml` (the rig tuning below), whose `core` table is the pure core's.

## States

The rig has two kinds of state.

Grounded: Rico is on foot on the ground with the headset driving the view, standing or seated. The rig owns his yaw and his position: the yaw follow turns him, and committed steps move him. While the IK body owns the standing body, the drive follows the floor point under its FK pelvis instead of committing steps, and the feet step for themselves ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#the-root)).

Game-decoupled: the wingsuit, the parachute, a vehicle seat, swimming, a grapple traversal or hang, a stunt traversal, a ragdoll, and getting up from one. The game owns the root and the yaw. The mod poses only the head, the neck, and the arms, plus a spine bend where the clip's pose allows one (a seat, the parachute's harness, the swim). A standing stunt position is its own case, with the on-foot clip's legs and the procedural torso, and the view hangs from the clip's head because the vehicle carries it; the helicopter's hang keeps the animation's hips. The hand-over table that classifies the game's states is in [procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table).

A jump, a short fall, an authored motion (a locomotion one-off, a hard landing, cover, a hit reaction), and climbing with the hands keep the game's legs and pelvis as well; the rig stops stepping while they play and picks up again when Rico is back on foot. The torso stays the player's through a jump and a short flight after it, and through the canopy closed and opened again around a grapple's reel, so it is not handed to the animation and back within a second; a flight that outlasts that hold hands it over once ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)).

## The pelvis solver

Each frame the solver measures three points in the room frame (the cockpit frame with its floor at zero height):

- The neck pivot: a fixed offset back and down from the eyes in the head's frame (`body_rig::body::EYES_TO_NECK`), at the base of the skull. Nodding and turning the head swing the eyes about it and leave it in place.
- The waist pivot: over the floor point under Rico's pelvis, at the calibrated hip height (`HIP_HEIGHT_SHARE` of the player's height).
- The spine: one segment from the waist pivot to the neck pivot, as long as the standing neck height less the hip height. A bend is the segment's pitch and roll from vertical, within limits that are widest forward (`pelvis.forward_bend_deg`), narrower to either side (`pelvis.side_bend_deg`), and narrowest back (`pelvis.back_bend_deg`), with an ellipse between them.

A bend that carries the neck out over the pelvis also lowers it along the segment's arc. So the neck's height bounds how far out a legal bend can explain it, and the solver separates the cases the player's body produces:

- Head-only motion: a look about or a nod moves the eyes about the neck pivot, which barely moves. Nothing below the neck changes.
- A bend at the waist with the head level: the neck moves out and down on the spine's arc. The pelvis stays, and the neck takes the difference between the head's pitch and the spine's.
- A bend at the waist while looking down: the same, with the head pitched further.
- A step: the neck moves out horizontally without the drop the arc demands. The part of the offset past what a legal bend at the neck's height explains is step demand.
- A crouch: the head drops with no bend to explain it.

Near vertical the arc is flat: two centimetres of drop explain fifteen centimetres of reach, and a standing head bobs by about that much. So the lean radius (`pelvis.lean_radius_m`) sets a floor under the reach a bend explains: inside it Rico never steps, and the spine absorbs the offset. Behind the pelvis the floor narrows to `pelvis.back_radius_m`, and the back bend's limit is low, so a player who steps back steps rather than being drawn leaning back with Rico's chest in front of the camera. A cap (`pelvis.max_lean_m`) bounds the reach however low the neck is, so a crouched player who walks still steps.

The hands are a tie-breaker only: both held out along the offset, further than `pelvis.hand_reach_m` ahead of the neck, widen the lean's reach by `pelvis.hand_lean_m`, so a slow drift toward something held in both hands stays a lean longer.

The standing neck height is measured rather than assumed, because the runtime's floor and the calibrated eye height disagree by a few centimetres from session to session, and near vertical a few centimetres are a large lean. It is the height that a share (`core.height.percentile`) of the latest samples lie below, sampled every `core.height.sample_s` while Rico is on foot in the standing posture and the gaze is within `core.grounded.level_gaze_deg` of level, seeded at the calibration and held within `core.height.max_drift_m` of it. Seated, the seated eye reference keeps its role ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/roomscale.md)).

## Steps

Step demand near the line between a lean and a step flickers with every bob of the head, so the step commit has hysteresis: a step commits once the demand passes `step.commit_m`, and a committed step runs until the pelvis is back under the neck, within `step.arrive_m`, rather than until the demand falls away.

A committed step drives the game's own character movement as a closed loop toward the floor point under the neck. The velocity is the remaining gap over `step.response_s`, at most `step.max_speed_mps`, so it tapers on arrival and the walk cycle does not overshoot. Each simulation tick takes the movement completed since the frame measured the gap out of it, so two ticks in one frame never close the same gap twice. The drive is paced in real time, so in slow motion the body keeps up with the player's real steps. The velocity is added to the stick's through the character's wanted velocity, so the game's collision, slopes, and support move Rico.

The game's animation picks its clip from the move stick. So while a step is committed and still more than `grounded.walk_gap_m` from arriving, the bindings tick adds a walk to the move stick's half-axes (`grounded::add_step_walk`): `grounded.walk_stick` of a full deflection, along the gap, in the frame of the head's heading, as the stick locomotion reads the stick. The game then plays its walk, and the stick locomotion takes the added walk back out of the stick before it sets its own velocity, so the walk picks the clip and the drive alone sets the speed. A step about to arrive, and the settle after it, shuffle under the drive with no walk.

What is left once the step arrives is slid out at `step.settle_speed_mps` while Rico idles, for at most `step.settle_s`.

A weight shift inside the lean's reach commits no step, so a player who stands with one hip out would otherwise be drawn bent for as long as they stand there. The idle drift resolves it: while Rico idles and the neck moves slower than `step.drift_steady_mps`, an offset to the side of the body or behind it that stays longer than `step.drift_min_m` for `step.drift_after_s` starts a drift, which shuffles the pelvis under the neck at `step.drift_speed_mps`, with no walk, until the side and back of the offset are within `step.settle_done_m`. The forward part of the offset is left alone: leaning over a table, or looking down at the body, is a reach the player means. A step demand during a drift commits a step as usual. A step that has not arrived after `step.give_up_s` (a wall it cannot pass) stops; the push-back then holds the view at the wall ([Walls](#walls)).

The camera's tracked neck model hangs the eyes from Rico's head in the view's frame, not the body's (`hooks::camera`), since the headset's own translation already carries the player's eyes round with a turn of their torso. On foot in the headset the arm is the rig's own neck offset reversed, hung from the neutral head ([locomotion.md, "The steady view"](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)), so the upright eyes stand exactly `EYES_TO_NECK` ahead of the root, as the pelvis solver assumes. Once the body has turned under the view, Rico's own eyes (the arm turned with him) stand off the camera by the difference between the arm turned by the body and by the view: about 12 cm sideways at a quarter turn from the room's forward, and more past it. The frame half adds that slip to the root point the rig reads (`root::eye_rig_slip`, from the follow's turn under the view and the camera's arm, `root::slip_arm`), so the rig places the pelvis where Rico stands. The idle drift takes out each change of the slip whole, wherever it points: the slip's part of the neck's offset is tracked from its changes and shrinks as the offset along it does, and the rest of the offset keeps the drift's rule. The body steps under the camera; the camera does not move. A recenter's snap puts the pelvis under the neck with the slip taken into account, and a turn of the room frame (a recenter or a follow bank) takes the next frame's slip as the baseline. The recorded rig inputs carry the pelvis with the slip in it but not the slip, so `rig-eval --pelvis recorded` runs the drift without the slip's part.

Thumbstick locomotion is unchanged, and the physical offset adds to it: the stick moves the root and the camera together, so it never changes how far the root is from the head, and a player walking in the room while pushing the stick moves at the sum of the two.

## The yaw

The yaw has two layers, after Dead and Buried's body ([below](#where-the-yaw-comes-from)): the chest follows the head continuously within a clamp, and the legs, which are Rico's facing, turn only for a large or sustained turn of the head, or while he moves. Gameplay readability comes before anatomy: the torso faces where the player looks with no visible turn events, and the stance stays put until the head has clearly gone somewhere else.

The chest is a critically damped spring toward the head's heading, with a smoothing time of `yaw.chest_follow_s`, held within `yaw.chest_clamp_deg` of the legs. The neck takes the rest of the head's turn. The hands pull the chest's target, not the legs': their mean horizontal offset from the neck, once further out than `yaw.hand_min_m`, pulls it toward its own heading by `yaw.hand_bias` of the angle between them, so aiming or reaching to one side turns the torso and leaves the stance.

A turn of the legs starts when the head is further than `yaw.turn_deg` from them, or has stayed further than `yaw.sustain_deg` for `yaw.sustain_s`, or, while the body moves (a step, or the stick), has stayed further than `yaw.moving_deadzone_deg` on one side for `yaw.moving_sustain_s`. The turn runs at `yaw.slew_deg_s` toward the head's heading until the legs face it within `yaw.settle_deg`, so it is a discrete act. A look back inside the sustain angle resets the sustain's clock, and a look back inside the moving deadzone, or a sway past it to the other side, resets the moving one. The moving sustain exists because the head sways from side to side in a walk and looking about while walking passes the deadzone briefly; without it the legs turn to and fro about once a second. The chest keeps its clamp while the legs turn under it, so it never swings past where it stood.

A head held steady a little off the legs, inside all three of those, would otherwise leave Rico standing bladed under the player. The steady settle takes it out: once the head has stayed within `yaw.steady_deg` of where it came to rest for `yaw.steady_s`, with the body standing and the head further than `yaw.steady_settle_deg` from the legs, the legs turn toward it at `yaw.steady_slew_deg_s` until they face it within `yaw.settle_deg`. The settle is slow enough to read as shifting one's weight, and it stops as soon as the head moves out of its band or the body moves, leaving any other turn to take over.

Looking down at the body, the head's heading points at a holster or a hand, not where the player means to face, and bending over swings it about. Past `yaw.look_down_deg` of downward pitch, only the large turn starts, the sustain's, the moving sustain's, and the steady spell's clocks start again, and the chest's follow fades linearly until, past `yaw.look_down_full_deg`, the chest holds where it is. The clamp still holds the chest to a large turn of the legs.

### The learned estimate

Where the learned body estimator runs ([body-estimator.md](body-estimator.md)), its estimate of where the pelvis and the upper chest face takes over the legs and pulls the chest (`body_rig::estimate`). The rig takes it only on the ground with the grounded rig, standing or seated; elsewhere, and in gamepad-only play, where the estimator does not run, the rules above decide alone.

The estimate tracks the player's body with little lag, but it also moves about 60% of the way with a head-only glance and jitters a little, so the legs never follow it raw. A critically damped spring with a smoothing time of `yaw.estimate.smooth_s` follows its pelvis heading, which takes out the jitter and part of a quick glance. The legs hold while that smoothed heading stays within `yaw.estimate.deadband_deg` of them, and once it leaves the band they are carried along at its edge, no faster than `yaw.estimate.slew_deg_s`, so a turn of the body turns them continuously in small adjustments rather than in one discrete turn. Once the smoothed estimate has turned slower than `yaw.estimate.still_deg_s` for a while, the legs settle the rest of the way onto it at up to `yaw.estimate.centre_deg_s`, easing in over `yaw.estimate.still_s`, so the band does not leave Rico bladed. Looking down fades the estimate's turn of the legs as it fades the chest's follow. The chest's target, the head's heading pulled by the hands, moves a further `yaw.estimate.chest_weight` of the way toward the smoothed estimate of the chest.

The estimate's say is a weight between none and all. It eases in over `yaw.estimate.blend_s` while estimates arrive and out over the same time once none does. It scales with the estimate's confidence, which is full until the estimate is `yaw.estimate.fresh_s` old and gone by `yaw.estimate.stale_s`, and it eases toward none over `yaw.estimate.stick_blend_s` while the stick walks the body, where the moving turn above decides where Rico faces. Each frame the legs move by the head's rules' change and the estimate's change blended by that weight, so nothing snaps as the estimate comes and goes, and with no estimate the rig is exactly the rules above. The rules keep running under the estimate, so their clocks are current when it hands back. While the estimate has most of the say, a body turn is under way (the readout's turn, and the turn-in-place animation's gate) on any frame it turns the legs faster than `core.yaw.turning_deg_s`, and the turn's cause reads `estimate`. The band and the gate are wide so that the legs do not follow every glance and the drift does not start the game's turn-in-place acts as a constant shuffle.

The procedural body twists Rico's chest by the chest's heading relative to his facing ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md)), and the hands' estimated shoulders lie across the chest's heading and the hips across the legs'. Off the ground there is no grounded rig, so every estimate of the player's shoulders (the hands' shoulders, the arm remap, the wingsuit's arms, and the ripcord) takes the torso's heading from one follower in the air (`procedural::wings::WingHeading`, [procedural-states.md](procedural-states.md#the-wingsuit)): the line between the wrists while they are spread, and otherwise a slow follower of the head that no glance turns. Elsewhere without a grounded rig (a seat, for example) the head's heading stands in.

### Where the yaw comes from

Dead and Buried (Oculus Studios, 2016) drew a full body under the player's head from the headset and two controllers. Its solver, by RootMotion, became Final IK's VRIK ([VRIK](http://www.root-motion.com/finalikdox/html/page16.html)). VRIK pins the head to the headset and hangs the body from it. The chest turns toward the head and toward the hands within a clamp of the chest's rotation from the head, and its procedural locomotion steps the feet separately from the torso when a foot's target is too far off or the centre of mass leaves the support.

The rig takes the layering: the head turns freely, the chest follows it smoothly within a clamp and is pulled by the hands, and the stance catches up in discrete, generous turns. The game's own animation keeps the legs, so the stance turn is the game's turn-in-place rather than VRIK's procedural footsteps, and the position layer is the rig's pelvis solver and step commit rather than a stiffness.

The body turns under the view without turning it ([The body turning under the view](#the-body-turning-under-the-view)), through the game's orientation executor. The act filter lets the game's turn-in-place acts through while Rico stands still and the rig follows, so a body turn plays the game's own turn-in-place animation ([locomotion.md, "The turning animations"](locomotion.md#the-turning-animations)), except while the IK body plants the feet, which turn by steps of their own ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#feet)). Snap and smooth turning turn the view and the room together, so the rig's headings, which are measured in the room, are unchanged by them.

## The upper body

Outside the IK body's standing and airborne states, the game's animation keeps the hips, the pelvis, and the legs; in them, the clip's hips are first carried onto the FK pelvis ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#seeding-the-pose)). The procedural body poses the torso on those hips ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md)): the spine bends toward the player's neck pivot, the bend shared out over the four spine bones within the lean limit, the chest twists toward the chest's heading, the neck takes a share of the head's turn, and the head override sets the head itself. The arms keep the two-bone IK from the shoulders the spine now places under the player's, and the clavicles reach with the hands.

## Recentering

The two-stick chord, F7, the pause menu's button, the debug UI's `Recenter the body` button in the Body fit feature, and the recenter on resuming gameplay snap the pelvis under the current neck pivot and reset the yaw and the height. Out of a seat, the recenter re-bases the view's yaw and height (`roomscale::manual_recenter_kind`), and on the next grounded frame, within a second, the rig snaps (`grounded::request_snap`; one asked for in a seat or in the air expires, since the recenter there re-based the whole view): the yaw follow faces the head's heading, the standing height starts again from the neck's height, any step under way stops, and the cockpit shift is re-based so the upright eyes over the pelvis under the neck stand over Rico's root. The camera moves to the body rather than the body to the camera, so a recenter never puts the body through a wall. A follow bank turns the rig's room frame with the cockpit frame and snaps nothing.

## Who moves the root

Several systems want the root: the game's own states, the hand climbing, the animation's authored motions, and the rig. Exactly one owns it on each tick (`rig::root::owner`), chosen in the root hook, a post-call detour on the on-foot locomotion task ([`NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask##func-Update)). The task writes the proxy's wanted velocity every on-foot tick, idle included, and velocity written there goes through the proxy's character context, which handles stairs, slopes, and support. The owners, in precedence order:

| Owner | When | The step drive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game | The task did not write the velocity (the task-defined motion state in [`Character::m_CurrentMotionState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_CurrentMotionState) is not set, or Rico is attached to a vehicle), or a seat transition is under way. Every other state that moves the proxy (the grapple, jumps, stunts, ragdoll, swimming, the parachute, the wingsuit) runs a different task. | None |
| Climb | A hand grips the world. The climb replaces the proxy's motion in the pre-simulation callback. | None, and the ledger records nothing |
| Authored | The animation carries the body: an authored one-off, a hard landing, cover, or a hit reaction's hold ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)). | None |
| Seated | Otherwise, in the seated posture. | Added |
| Player | Otherwise: on foot, standing. | Added |

On every tick the task wrote the velocity, the hook composes the wanted velocity in its on-foot order ([physics-substrate.md](physics-substrate.md#the-wanted-velocity)): it first replaces the clip's velocity with the stick's ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md)) and adds the palms' push off what they press into; together these are the task's movement. While the player owns the root, seated or standing, the step drive's velocity is added through the character's world-space accessors ([`Character::GetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetWantedWorldVelocity) and [`Character::SetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetWantedWorldVelocity)), because the proxy's own wanted velocity is character-local.

The same hook requests or releases the crouch capsule ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/roomscale.md)).

## The bookkeeping

The camera is the anchor carried by the root, plus the cockpit position less the cockpit shift, composed with the view's rotation and the world scale. The view's anchor stands a neutral head directly over the root, with the upright eyes ahead of it by the neck model ([locomotion.md, "The steady view"](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)), so in the cockpit frame the root sits at the cockpit shift. When a step moves the root, the anchor moves with it while the cockpit position stays, so without a correction the camera would run ahead by the distance the root covered. The shift (`rig::root::shift`) takes the step's part of the root's displacement back out of the cockpit.

The ledger (`rig::root::ledger`) keeps the split. Each tick the player owns the root, the hook submits the task's and the step's displacements as separate vectors. The local character's pose-finalization entry completes the request against the step's solved root displacement, from [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT0) to [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1). When the achieved displacement matches the requested sum, the whole step is credited (exact); when something stopped or deflected the root, only the step's forward projection on the achieved travel is credited (projected). The ledger keeps the running total of credited movement at the last two completed steps, and the frame half folds in everything completed since it last looked. The shift is a step-spaced pair like the anchor it compensates, so the engine's interpolation moves the anchor and the shift together and the camera holds still between steps.

Attribution errors cannot separate the body from the head. An under-credit leaves the camera moved by the uncredited part, and an over-credit leaves it behind by the excess; either way the next frame measures the gap from the head as it now is, so the pelvis still converges on the neck.

## Walls

The view stays with the real head, so a player who walks or leans into a wall would see through it while the root stands at the wall. The push-back holds the view at the wall, and the fades darken a view that goes past it.

The push-back (`rig::root::pushback`) runs in the frame half after the gap is measured. When less than `ledger.obstructed_share` of what the ledger's last two steps asked for was credited, and the step demand is over `pushback.min_gap_m`, the step is obstructed. The view may then go only a threshold past the root:

| Owner | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Player or Seated, obstructed | `pushback.obstructed_m` |
| Player or Seated, unobstructed | none: the step drive owns the gap |
| Authored | `pushback.authored_m`, which only catches a runaway gap while the rig is suspended |
| Climb | `pushback.reach_m`, about an arm's-length lean |
| Game | none |

The part of the gap beyond the threshold is taken out of the cockpit by sliding the cockpit shift back along the gap, on both sides of the pose pair, so the view holds rigidly at the obstruction. The slide is capped per episode at `pushback.debt_cap_m`; with the cap spent, the push-back fade rises past the threshold, black `pushback.fade_span_m` past it. The episode ends once the gap has stayed under `pushback.calm_share` of the threshold for `pushback.reset_s`. A teleport or a recenter ends the episode.

The head-through-geometry probe (`rig::collision::probe`) catches what the push-back cannot: a head pushed into geometry the root is nowhere near, such as a low beam or a wall the player leans into. On every simulation tick in gameplay under the headset, in every state, from the player aim control's post-simulation update (`rig::collision::tick`), it casts from the last position the eyes held in the open to where they are now, and from the animated head, through the eye centre and `probe.fan_half_width_m` to either side across the view, and the deepest hit drives the head fade. A terrain check backs it up where the animated head is no witness, as when a wingsuit flight ends in the ground and the clip carries the head under the terrain with the eyes: a ray from `probe.terrain_reach_m` above the eyes down onto them against the terrain alone ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRayTerrain`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRayTerrain)) meets the heightfield when the eyes are below its surface, and the depth below counts as a hit's. The vehicle Rico rides is ignored with Rico, since between two ticks of a fast car the head's path runs through its cabin.

## Teleports and seat exits

A teleport (a camera reset, or a single-tick jump of the root further than the body's speed explains; [controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/roomscale.md)) clears the ledger, stops any step, and re-bases the shift so the head sits over the root, with no interpolation from the previous shift.

On the first frame out of a vehicle seat after an exit, a head within `root.exit_rebase_m` of the root is left to the rig: the body steps to the head and the view does not move. A head further off is put back over the root.

## Posture

The posture is `Auto`, `Seated`, or `Standing` (`controllers.roomscale.posture`). Seated and standing follow the head the same way: the solver separates a lean from a step, and the step drive walks Rico after a shift in the chair. A seated player's head moves only by the upper body, so seated steps are rare. The rig's room frame puts the eyes at their height over the STAGE floor standing, and seated at the standing eye height plus the head's travel from the seated eye reference, so a seated player who sits upright reads as standing upright; only the standing posture samples the standing height.

Standing, the crouch capsule follows the crouch the solver measures (the head's drop that no bend at the waist explains, carried onto Rico's proportions) rather than the eyes' drop, so a look at the floor or a lean over a table does not lower the capsule ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/roomscale.md)). Seated, the crouch toggle drives it.

Head-tracked gamepad play keeps working: the rig needs only the head, the hands are a tie-breaker and a pull on the chest that are absent without controllers, and the yaw is the head's.

## The body turning under the view

The view composes the head onto Rico's body frame, so turning Rico would turn the view ([head-and-body.md, "HMD pose to camera"](head-and-body.md#hmd-pose-to-camera)). While the body follows (on foot, in gameplay, under the headset), the view frame is the body frame turned back by the body's heading relative to the view (`headpose::follow`), so the body can turn without the view following it. The view's heading is the turn accumulator's, which only the stick, the mouse, or a snap step moves. The body is steered, through the same face-dir target the turn input uses ([`NStateTask_LocoUtil::EvaluateCharacterOrientation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-EvaluateCharacterOrientation)), to the view's heading plus the rig's body yaw in the cockpit frame, with no deadband of its own: the rig's turn rules and settle already decide when the body turns. Without a fresh grounded frame (off the ground, or before the first frame), the body does not follow (`rig::follow_settings`). The body therefore turns only because the player's head did, and the view turns only because the player pushed the turn input, which is the rule that the player is never reoriented without a movement action.

When the body stops following (a jump, a vehicle, the parachute, swimming, or the setting turned off), the offset freezes and the view rides the body again, turned by a constant. Two cases hold the view's heading instead (`headpose::follow::BodyFollow`):

- Through a vehicle's entry or exit animation (the seat phase `Entering` or `Exiting`), the view's heading is held as while following, so the clip's turn of the body never swings it, and every `follow.step_interval_s` it steps toward the body's heading by up to `follow.step_rad` in one hard step, carrying on after the clip until it faces within `follow.step_settled_rad` of the body (the view crate's tuning). After the clip the vehicle carries the body, so the view rides the body's turn there and steps only the difference the clip left: a vehicle that drives off turning turns the view with it.
- Through a ragdoll, its get-up, and a death, the view's heading is held the same way but never steps, because the get-up's pose matching turns the root by up to half a turn to wherever the ragdoll fell. Once Rico stands, the body follows the torso under the held view. The log has `headpose: the view's heading holds through the thrown body` and `is released from` with the body's and the view's headings.

Once the steps end, at the top of the next frame the frozen offset is folded into the cockpit baseline (the follow bank): the baseline's yaw turns by it, the offset goes to zero, and the cockpit shift and the rig's headings turn with the frame, so the composed view is unchanged and the cockpit frame's forward is the body's again, as the vehicles' physical controls, the driving view, and the flight steering assume. The bank keeps the head's heading in the banked frame, which is its offset from the body when the body stopped following; a seat lock under the default `Ride` re-bases the cockpit's yaw by it, so the player looks along the vehicle's axis rather than off it by that offset. A seat lock while the steps still run waits for them to settle and bank, so the re-base uses the entry's own bank rather than an older one, and the two never turn the view at once ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/roomscale.md)).

## The body's volumes on Rico's skeleton

The sockets and pouches ride Rico's skeleton ([weapon-handling.md, "Pouches"](../input/weapon-handling.md#pouches)), so what the player sees on his body is where they reach. After the pose-finalization pass's solves ([`Character::UpdatePropEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropEffects)), the bones HumanIK drives for [`Effector::HIPS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-HIPS), the hip joints, and the shoulder joints, found through [`HumanIK::m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##field-m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs), are read with the head bone and published with the character's world transform (`rig::skeleton`). The weapon handling carries them into the cockpit frame through the head pose and the view frame on its next input tick (`rig::frames`). The hip and belt volumes ride the pelvis and the shoulder, chest, grenade, and C4 volumes ride the chest, each girdle turned up along the spine (the weapons crate's `volumes::BodyFrame`), except that the grenade and C4 pouches are kept off the forward bend a look down puts in the chest. The skeleton is used while the character's pose is younger than a quarter of a second.

A read must be a plausible body (`rig::frames::implausibility`, whose bounds on the shoulders' distance from the hips, the shoulder and hip spacing, and the head's reach from the shoulders are its constants). The head is held loosely because a clip, a fold over the hips, or a head target the solve cannot reach can still hang the head bone forward of and below the shoulders. Looking down does not: the body IK's head target is the neck pivot under the eyes, not the eye point ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md#body-ik-via-humanik)), so the head bone keeps its height on the neck while the eyes swing down and forward about it. A read that is still not a plausible body, or whose bones cannot be read, falls back to the last plausible skeleton: it is kept in the character's model space and carried by the character's current transform, so the volumes stay on the body as it moves (`rig::skeleton`). Each change between a plausible read and a fallback is logged at info level on the `body` target with the test that failed (`body frame: ...`), and the readout's `Holsters ride` says which skeleton the volumes are on.

## Settings and diagnostics

The pause menu's `VR: COMFORT` page has `POSTURE` and `BODY FOLLOWS YOU` (`steps.enabled`, on). Everything else is in the debug UI's Body fit feature: the `Recenter the body` button, the step drive's switch, and the settings above under `Lean and steps` (`pelvis.*` and `step.*`, including the drift) and `Yaw` (`yaw.*`). The yaw follow, the turn-in-place animation, the clip's hip squaring, and the push-back have no switches. The `Learned body estimate` section holds the estimator's switch and the `yaw.estimate.*` settings ([body-estimator.md](body-estimator.md#in-the-game)).

The live readout shows the owner; the step phase, the steps committed, the demand, the neck's offset, and the lean's reach; the bend's angle, pitch, and roll, the crouch, and whether the hands reach; the body yaw, its target, and whether a turn is under way; the neck's and the standing height, the standing height's samples, and the spine's length; the drive's and the task's velocities and the gap left; the cockpit shift; the push-back; and the ledger's counts with the re-bases at teleports, at seat exits, and at recenters. A change of step phase is logged at debug level on the `body` target (`rig: the step phase changes`).

The in-world diagnostics are on by default (`controllers.overlay.body_rig`, the visualisation drawer's `Body rig`, `rig::visual`): a sprite at the neck pivot and one at the waist pivot, the spine segment between them, the lean radius as a ring on the floor around the pelvis, and a fainter ring at the lean's reach, teal while idle, yellow while settling or drifting, and orange while stepping, drawn through Rico's body. They hang from the head in the player's own metres, so they show the player's body as the rig reads it, not Rico's.

## Offline evaluation

`tracking-synth rig-eval <recording> <out.csv>` runs the same solver over a recorded `tracking.jc3trk` and writes a CSV of the time, the bend, the step demand, the committed steps, the step phase, the yaw error, and the head's pitch ([testing/recorder.md](../testing/recorder.md)); `tools/analysis/plot_rig_eval.py` plots it against the take's video. The flags are documented in `tools/tracking-synth/src/main.rs`.

`--weights <safetensors>` runs the learned body estimator over the whole recording first, from the raw head and grip poses as `estimate-body` does, and hands the rig its estimate as the game would: at the game's submission rate, a little after its window ends, its confidence fading with age, turned from the estimator's fixed room frame into the cockpit frame by the recenters so far. Each row then carries the estimate's columns. `export-rerun --weights` runs its offline rig the same way, and `--ik` replays the IK body over the estimate ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#the-offline-replay)).

`--pelvis` picks where the rig's inputs come from:

- `simulated` takes the eyes, the head, and the hands from the tracking frames and simulates a pelvis that follows the step drive. It is the only source a recording before version 8 has, and the default for one.
- `recorded` feeds the rig exactly the inputs the payload recorded for each frame its rig ran (the `rig` events), so a changed rig or tuning is compared with the game's on the same frames, with the game's closed loop moving the pelvis. It is the default when a recording has those events. Between frames it turns the rig with each recenter, faces the head on a recenter's snap, stops any step across a gap of more than 0.2 s, and forgets the heading across one longer than `grounded.resume_after_ms`, as the payload does when Rico leaves the ground; a teleport's reset leaves no trace and is not replayed.
- `pre-ik` takes the frames' eyes, head, and hands with the pelvis under the hips of the game's animated pose before the IK solves (the pre-IK `pose` events), carried from the character's model space into the world and through the same game frame's view back into the room, so the rig runs against the game's own animation. A pose older than a quarter of a second places nothing.

When the recording has `rig` events, each row also carries the recorded rig's body yaw, phase, and neck offset and the evaluated rig's differences from them, from the latest recorded frame within 50 ms of the row, and the summary adds the mean and largest differences. The calibration and the settings are the ones the recording notes (the `rig_config` events), which rig-eval reports; `--height`, `--eye-height`, and `--tuning` override them. The rig tuning file is not recorded: rig-eval and export-rerun read the repository's `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/rig.toml` each time they run, so a replay runs the file as it is now, and an edit to it applies to the next run without a rebuild.

The defaults were fitted with rig-eval over standing takes in which the player walked about the room, looked down at Rico's body for long stretches, recentred often, and fought. What they trade:

- The lean radius and the commit threshold sit in the middle of a wide flat range: standing a couple of centimetres below the standing height, the arc already explains more reach than the radius, and the steps that commit are real moves of a quarter to two thirds of a metre. Looking down at the body carries the neck pivot ten to twenty centimetres forward and five to nine down, which the solver reads as a forward bend and leaves as a lean. The settle is short because the player's own drift soon outweighs the few centimetres left after a step.
- Two yaw layers rather than one: a single deadzone with the hands pulling the whole body starts about twice as many body turns, most of them small, because the hands pull the target past the deadzone while the head is still inside it. Most turns start while the stick moves; the moving deadzone halves them against a narrower one while a walking body still faces within a chest's clamp of where it looks, and the moving sustain removes the small left-and-right flips while leaving real looks round.
- The steady settle leaves almost no long standing spells with the legs well off the head, at the cost of slightly more turns. The look-down pitch is low enough to stop a sustained turn while the player looks at the holsters (which carried the holster away) and high enough to keep the settle while the player looks at the floor ahead.
- The drift and the back radius take out held side shifts and back bends within a second or two, leaving only forward leans, which the drift leaves by design; a shift drifts out before it grows into a step, so fewer steps commit.
- With the estimate, the legs sit about half as far from the estimate's pelvis as with the rules alone, with the same lag, and turn little more with a glance. A longer smoothing time lags more and fits worse.

## Headset checks

- Walking with the stick and looking about: the legs do not flip left and right; a look held to one side for about a third of a second turns them.
- A jump that outlasts its grace, and closing and reopening the canopy around grapple reels: the torso stays the player's; a long fall hands it to the animation once.
- Standing still with the head held 15 to 30 degrees off the body for a couple of seconds: the legs turn slowly under the head with no visible turn event, and stop as soon as the head moves.
- Standing still after a quarter turn of the body from where the session started, without recentering: within a couple of seconds Rico shuffles a few centimetres under the camera with no walk, and the camera does not move; looking down, his shoulders sit symmetrically under the view.
- Looking down at the holsters and the belt, and turning the head while looking down: the chest and the legs stay put; looking straight down keeps every pouch in place, and `Holsters ride` reads the character's skeleton.
- Holding a gun out to one side: the chest turns toward the hands and the legs stay. Leaning over a table: no step, and the spine marker bends.
- Standing with the weight on one hip, or a little back: after about a second the body shuffles under the head without a walk, and stepping back steps rather than leaning back.
- Walking a metre or two in the room: one committed step that plays the game's walk rather than a slide or a run, coming to rest under the head; if not, tune `grounded.walk_stick` against the game's walk threshold.
- With the estimator on: glancing about barely moves the legs, a held body turn brings them round in one smooth movement, and the floor arrow sits under the hips. A turn played as many small turn-in-place clips, or a constant shuffle, means the estimate's band or gate is too narrow.
- The recenter puts the body under the head at once, the crouch capsule lowers for a real crouch and not for a look at the floor, and seated and head-tracked gamepad play work.
- Looking down, Rico's head stays on his neck above the shoulders in his shadow and the mirror, and the chest and neck do not fold forward under the camera.

## Unverified

- The walk added to the move stick has to land between the game's walk and run thresholds ([`CharacterOnFootSettings`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::CharacterOnFootSettings)); its default is a guess.
- The game may queue its turn-in-place acts only for a large turn, or not at all while the orientation executor is steered; the follow then turns the root alone.
- The proxy's character context may filter or limit a wanted velocity, which would lag the drive and send steps to the ledger's projection.
- The estimator's tuning comes from standing takes evaluated offline through the CPU reference, not the in-game GPU executor and its timing.
- The neck pivot's offset from the eyes is a generic figure; a player whose neck sits further back reads looking down as a larger lean.
