# The render-rate presentation layer

The game simulates at 30 Hz and the headset displays at 75 to 90 Hz. Rico's pose is written once per tick, by the procedural torso and the hand IK at the solve gate and by the head override after the solve ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md)), and the skinning draws each frame between the last two ticks' poses ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#bone-store-hkapose-model-space)), so what the tick wrote is drawn up to a tick behind the player. The view and the hands are placed from the latest tracking at render time ([render-time-anchoring.md](render-time-anchoring.md)). The presentation layer (`crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/presentation/`) does the same for the torso, the shoulders, and the arms, and for the hand bodies' offset, which would otherwise step at the physics rate.

## What is drawn at which rate

Drawn at the render rate: the view; the hands, fingers, and held gun, by the late update's correction (`controllers/hands/late/`); the arms, solved again from the shoulders to the hands; the spine, neck, and head, bent after the headset's motion since the ticks; a hand body's offset; and what rides the drawn hand (below). The grapple device on the left forearm is too: it is a rigid object drawn from the grappling hook's transform, which the hook takes from the forearm's twist bone each tick, so a detour on its render update (`grapple::device`) re-pushes it with the drawn forearm's edit (`presentation::drawn_forearm`).

Left at the simulation rate, interpolated: the hips, pelvis, and legs, which are the game's animation standing on the world, where a render-time edit would slide the feet; the hit shapes, the AI's view of Rico, the shot origins, and the attached effects, which read the skeleton the tick wrote; and objects the physical hands carry, which the engine draws from their own bodies' cached transforms.

## Where the pose is edited

The skinning job blends the two poses into the palette by the frame's interpolation fraction ([`PoseProducer::MakeSkinningPalette`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::PoseProducer##func-MakeSkinningPalette)), and each render block uploads it per skin batch ([`RenderBlockCharacter::SetMatrixPalette`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockCharacter##func-SetMatrixPalette)). The detour on the upload (`hooks::graphics_engine::scene::render_block`) gives the player's body a copy of the palette for the head's collapse and the late update's hands, and the layer edits the same copy: after the engine's interpolation, for the local player's body model only, in every pass so the shadow matches, and never in the pose the simulation and the next tick read. An entry is a bone's model-space transform times its inverse bind pose, so pre-multiplying it by a model-space transform moves the bone's vertices rigidly.

The upper body is re-posed as rigid groups, each rooted at one bone (`presentation::rig::Group`): `Spine1`, `Spine2`, `Sternum`, `Neck`, the head, and per arm the clavicle, upper arm, forearm, and hand. Every bone takes the edit of the nearest group root at or above it, so twist bones ride their segment, fingers their hand, and facial bones the head. A skeleton that lacks a group or hangs one elsewhere than the solve assumes is refused with a warning (`presentation rig: ...`), and the draws fall back to the late update's hands.

Each tick, at the end of the pose pass (after [`Character::UpdatePropEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropEffects) and the mod's post-solve fix-ups), the layer records the group roots' model-space positions ([`AnimationController::GetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-GetJoint)), the head pose the tick posed from, and the procedural torso's weight. Once per rendered frame it snapshots the last two records and the render-time head pose pair. A draw interpolates the records by the frame's fraction, as the skinning does, and solves the upper body again (`presentation::solve`, pure and unit-tested), cached for the frame's other draws with the same instance matrix and fraction.

### The torso and the head

The head's motion since the ticks is measured at the neck pivot, below and behind the eyes ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-torso-on-the-animations-hips)), as the render-time head pose against the ticks' head poses interpolated by the same fraction. Measuring at the eyes would swing the neck whenever the player only turns the head. A motion beyond the late update's plausibility bound (`late::plausible`), such as a teleport or a recenter, is not applied.

The four spine joints turn about their own origins, more of the bend higher, so the head's joint swings toward where the head has gone, scaled by the procedural torso's weight so a torso the animation drives is left as drawn. The bend keeps the bones' lengths and the spine's base, so it covers the head's travel in direction, not distance; the head itself moves by the rest, at most five centimetres, so a fast crouch is left to the next tick. In first person the head's collapse point rides the neck's edit, so it stays inside the re-posed collar.

### The arms

Each arm is solved again with two-bone IK from its shoulder to the hand as the late update draws it. The shoulder rides the sternum's edit, and where the hand lies beyond reach, the clavicle turns toward it by at most `solve.clavicle_max_turn_deg`. An arm the controllers drive bends toward the elbow pole (below), blended by the hand IK's weight for the arm. Any other arm (gamepad play, a hand yielded to the animation, a cutscene, a ragdoll) bends toward the drawn elbow, which is HumanIK's ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-arms)), so with nothing moved since the ticks the layer reproduces the drawn arm exactly.

The forearm meets the hand as the late update's did, stretched along its length when the detach has moved the hand off the end of the arm. A hand further from the re-posed shoulder than the stretched arm reaches (`reach.max_forearm_stretch`) is drawn at that reach: within one tick the body can move against the hands further than the arm covers (a throw during a fall), and a hand falling short stays on the end of its forearm.

The elbow pole exists because HumanIK's elbow swings far about the shoulder-wrist axis between ticks where the hand barely moves: in recorded sessions it flipped about 14 times a minute on foot and 57 under the parachute, and the pole roughly halves that. It is computed in Rico's model space from the shoulders, the drawn wrist, the player's up, and the hand bone's orientation. It points down, out to the arm's side, and a little back, lifting further out for a raised hand or one reaching across the body, plus a pull toward the little-finger side of the wrist (`elbow.palm_weight`), so pronating the forearm drags the elbow as a real one is dragged. It is smoothed about the arm's axis with a frame-rate-independent time constant (`elbow.smoothing_s`), from the last frame's pole in every draw so the shadow agrees, and restarts after a teleport, a control-mode change, a gap of more than 150 ms, or the arm ceasing to be driven.

### The hand bodies' offset

A hand body's hand is drawn where its body is: the offset from the palm the last physics step drove the body toward to where the body is after it, sampled once per rendered frame after the simulation and added to the latest controller pose ([render-time-anchoring.md](render-time-anchoring.md#moving-frame-sampling)). A free hand's offset is near zero, so it keeps the controller's latency; a hand pressed into a wall is drawn on the wall.

## What rides the drawn hand

The late update draws a hand at the solved wrist moved by its correction, which falls short of the controller where the arm cannot reach or while the hand blends to or from the animation. Anything placed in a hand or anchored to it at render time takes that drawn hand (`late::drawn_hands`), with the grip and aim carried on it as the controller holds them: the primed and caught grenade and the C4 charge (`weapons::in_hand`), a magazine in the hand during a reload, the scope's lens, the controls summary on the wrist, the hand-anchored HUD readouts, and the overlay's hand anchors.

## Readouts, cost, and limits

The layer has no settings; its tuning is `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/presentation.toml`. The `Render-rate body` status row (Rendering on the Status page, and atop Body/tracking > Hand presentation) reads `Ok` with the draws re-posed and the smoothed cost, with each arm's elbow pole share in its note (`off` for an arm bent toward HumanIK's elbow); `Waiting` with why; or `Failed, No rig`. The work is timed in the frame breakdown's `presentation` slot; a solve takes under a microsecond, so a frame costs a few microseconds.

Bullets, NPCs, the shot origins, and the attached effects see the tick's pose, a tick behind what is drawn, since writing the render-time pose back would make the next tick start from it. Carried objects trail the hand by a tick; re-pushing them would need a render-update detour per object class. What to check in the headset: the chest and shoulders follow quick head moves without trailing; the elbows move with the hands without flipping or poking into the torso; the forearms never part from the hands; a hand pushed on a wall stays on it; and the grapple device stays on the forearm.
