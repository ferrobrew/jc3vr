# The steering wheel and handlebars

The physical wheel and handlebars ([bindings.md, "Vehicles"](bindings.md#vehicles)) are placed where the driven vehicle's own are, the held wheel turns and steers as a wheel does, and the vehicle's rendered wheel turns with the steering. The code is in `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/driving/`: `wheel.rs` holds the pure geometry and the override table, `fit.rs` reads the vehicle on the input tick, `steering.rs` turns the held wheel, `physical/` grabs and releases the wheel and the handlebars, `view.rs` is the driving view, and `wheel_render.rs` turns the rendered wheel. The engine side is in [vehicle-parts.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-parts.md). Tuning is in the vehicles' [tuning file](../tuning.md) (`physical.wheel`, `physical.handlebars`, `steering`, `wheel_fit`, and `driving_view`).

## Placing the wheel

One offset from the seated character's eyes misses the wheel in most cars: the hub's place relative to the head changes by about a tenth of a metre from car to car, more than the grab radius, the tilt ranges from nearly upright to nearly flat, and the head sways with the driving animation while the wheel does not.

So on each input tick while the player drives, the fit finds the steering wheel part by name among the valid parts of the vehicle's physics body ([`PfxBreakable`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PfxBreakable)) and reads its model-space box: the hub is the box's centre, the radius half its width, and the tilt the lean a thin disc needs to cover the box's depth, clamped to `wheel_fit.max_tilt_deg`. The hub and axes go through the part's root-space transform and the vehicle's world transform at the end of the simulation step ([`m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_WorldMatrixT1)), the side of the step the head pose is composed for, and then into the cockpit frame. Recomputed every tick, the wheel rides the car rather than the head, and a deformed part takes its wheel with it.

Meshes whose box misleads (one that includes the column, or a ship's wheel whose box spans its handles) have an entry in `WHEEL_OVERRIDES`, keyed by the part's name, giving the hub, radius, and tilt in the part's space so the override still rides deformation. The table is keyed by part name rather than by [`m_VehicleTypeNameHash`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_VehicleTypeNameHash) because the part names are one per model, while the type hash is one per variant.

A vehicle with no wheel part (the modern bus, the old truck, the tanks, some boats), or whose wheel part has broken off or has no usable model, falls back to the `physical.wheel` spec in the cockpit frame. So that it too rides the vehicle, the fallback wheel is carried into the vehicle's space once the driver has settled into the seat and held there until the driver leaves. The wheel and the handlebars are centred on the vehicle, so they are never mirrored for a left-handed layout; the sticks and levers are.

The input tick captures the attached vehicle once per tick through [`Character::GetVehicle`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetVehicle) and keeps its owner retained while it fits. Both the fit's latch and the physical controls' state reset from one `VehicleEntryIdentity` (the vehicle's address and its owner) and the seat mode before any target or grip is declared, so a different vehicle, a changed owner at the same address, or a missed observation releases the old controls and clears their placement.

A hand holding a gun, a prop, a grenade, or a charge does not take the wheel or the handlebars: the player drives with the free hand, or docks first ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)). A hand already on the wheel keeps it.

## Turning the wheel

A grip press with the hand within `physical.grab_radius_m` of the rim takes the wheel, and only releasing the grip lets go: a holding hand that drifts off the rim keeps turning the wheel however far it goes. A hand that passes through the rim, or rests on it with the grip open, takes nothing. Each take and let-go logs `vehicle control: took the control` or `vehicle control: let go of the control` with its reason.

Each holding hand turns the wheel by how far it turned since the last tick, by the `turned_by` setting:

- `Position` (the default): the hand's travel around the hub, the angle of its grip projected into the wheel's plane. This is what moves when a player turns a wheel with their arms. The angle is unstable near the hub, so within `steering.hub_share` of the radius the hand's twist stands in, blended back to travel further out. A hand that crosses the hub between two ticks counts its travel by the lesser of the two places' weights, so a swing past the middle turns nothing by travel.
- `Twist`: the hand's rotation about the wheel's axis alone. It does not depend on the hand's place, so a wheel fitted a few centimetres off does not matter, but arms that carry the hands round the rim with still wrists barely turn it.

Only the change since the last tick counts, so grabbing never jumps the wheel. Each tick's change is unwrapped and the turns accumulate, so the wheel goes past half a turn, and a hand that lets go and takes the wheel further round carries on from the wheel's angle (a hand-over). The wheel stops at its lock. The hands' turn past the stop is kept and taken up first on the way back, so the wheel leaves the stop only once the hands are back where they reached it.

Each hand's turn is measured against the wheel as it was when the hand took hold, not the wheel fitted this tick. The fitted wheel rides the vehicle while the cockpit frame the hands are tracked in is pinned to the character's animated head, so the live wheel sways in that frame with the driving animation; measured against it, a still hand would steer.

Two hands turn the wheel as one rigid wheel held by both would. The hand that turned furthest this tick leads, and the other adds its turn only when it turns the other way, holding the wheel back by as much. A relaxed hand riding the rim therefore neither halves the turn nor stops it, and two hands pulling against each other cancel. The jitter of two resting hands has no preferred direction, so the pick does not make the wheel drift.

The steering is the wheel's angle past `physical.wheel.deadzone_deg` as a share of the rest of the way to the lock, raised to `response`. The wheel turns one to one with the hands, so the drawn wheel stays under them. The lock is the `lock_deg` setting, which defaults to a turn that hands at nine and three reach without letting go: a road car turns several times that, but the game's steering is arcade and ramps by itself, and a VR wheel has no rim to push against, so a road car's range would make full lock a hand-over-hand exercise.

Let go, the wheel returns to centre at `return_deg_per_s`, easing over the last `steering.return_ease_deg`, and keeps steering on its way back so the vehicle straightens with it; once centred, the stick steers again. A stick pushed past its deadzone while the wheel returns snaps the wheel to centre.

At the stop the holding hands feel `SteeringStop`, and passing centre `SteeringCentre` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)), each re-armed only once the wheel has left it by `steering.cue_rearm_deg`. The holding hand is drawn where it took the rim, carried round with the wheel's angle, so at the lock it stays on the stopped wheel.

## Handlebars

The ATV, the jetski, and the duck share their classes with cars and boats but steer by handlebars. A vehicle whose parts include a steering fork (behaviour `SteeringFork`, or a part named `<model>_upper_steering`) is offered the handlebars in place of the wheel, motorcycles included. The fork's model-space box at rest (without the hinge's turn) places the grips' middle at the box's rear and mid-height, and the half width at half the box's width less a hand's half width (`wheel_fit.grip_inset_m`). Without a usable fork, the `physical.handlebars` spec places them. The game turns the forks itself by the steering.

A grip press takes the bars anywhere along the hand's own half, from `grab_from_middle` of the way out to a little past the grip (`grab_past_end`), within the bars' own `grab_radius_m`, wider than the other controls' because a hand reaching for the bars lands less exactly than one on a rim, and in the driver's seat a missed press does nothing else. A hand whose grip opens keeps the bars for `controls.bars_grip_grace_s`, so fingers lifted off the handle for an instant, as an Index controller's grip often reads, do not drop them.

The grips measure the bars' turn in the horizontal plane from the line between them, or with one hand from its grip mirrored through the bars' middle. That measure is latched whenever the hands holding the bars change, and the bars move from where they were by the change measured since, so a grab a few centimetres off the end neither jumps the bars nor biases them. One hand alone mirrors through the middle as it was at the latch, since the live middle sways with the riding clip, as the wheel's hands are measured against the wheel as taken.

The bars only turn. Their turn past `deadzone_deg`, as a share of `max_steer_deg`, raised to `response`, is the steering, so the bars steer gently around centre and fully at the lock, and a resting hand's tremor does not weave the bike. The steering is written to `TURN_LEFT`/`TURN_RIGHT` and the same value to `BIKE_LEAN_LEFT`/`BIKE_LEAN_RIGHT`, because the gamepad's one stick writes both and the game's bikes are tuned for them moving together. The grips' heights work nothing, so the grip that drops as the bars turn neither leans nor steers. The bars give the wheel's stop and centre cues. On the handlebars the triggers are the throttle and the brake ([cockpit-controls.md, "Cockpit buttons"](cockpit-controls.md#cockpit-buttons)).

## The driving view

Where the hub sits from the seated character's eyes is a property of the seat, decided by the driver animation and the wheel part, and it differs from car to car by more than the grab radius. The driving view (`view.rs`, the `FIT VIEW TO THE WHEEL` setting on the pause menu's `VR: COMFORT` page) moves the view per vehicle so that the eyes sit at one standard offset from the hub, `driving_view.hub_from_eyes_m`. The view moves rather than the wheel because the rendered wheel is the vehicle's own part, drawn where the vehicle puts it, so a grab volume moved off it would close the hands on air. The standard sits in the middle of a road car's ergonomic range, a little nearer because a VR rim holds no weight and a long reach tires the arms.

On each input tick while the player drives, the fit measures the hub from the character's eyes: the head pose's anchor (the animated head, before the head pose moves it) with the camera's eye arm hung off it (`jc3vr_view::hooks::camera::head_to_eye_point`), in the body frame and divided by the world scale. Neither the player's posture, the height remap, nor a correction already applied enters the measurement. Only a wheel fitted from the part or its override is measured, since the fallback says nothing about the seat. Once the driver has settled (`driving_view.settle_ms`), the measurement is averaged over `sample_window_ms` and kept for the rest of the entry, so the animation's sway does not move the view. The correction is the mean's offset from the standard, limited in length to `max_correction_m`. `SEAT_OVERRIDES`, keyed by wheel part like `WHEEL_OVERRIDES`, skips a seat or gives its correction outright; it skips the transport boat's standing helm. Only cars are corrected, since some boats are driven standing at the helm.

A vehicle's gunner seat is corrected the same way from the manned gun's handles (`vehicles::begin::gunner_view_target`), since the turret's animation puts the head right over them: their midpoint is put at `driving_view.handles_from_eyes_m`, and the measurement logs `vehicles: the gunner seat's handles measured for the view`. A driver's correction wins where both would apply.

The input tick publishes the correction through `jc3vr_body::controllers::facts::driving`. The frame half of the pose composition (`jc3vr_body::controllers::roomscale::compose_cockpit`) eases toward it over the roomscale tuning's `driving_view.blend_s`, as a smoothstep restarted from the current value whenever the goal changes, and back to nothing during a seat transition, when the setting is off, or once the input tick stops publishing. It adds the result after the posture, the height remap, the seated reference, the crouch, and the body rig have been resolved, so the correction neither feeds them nor is undone by them. The view, the hands, the grab volumes, and the head bone's target are all composed from the head pose, so the hands still meet the wheel where it is drawn ([render-time-anchoring.md](../rendering/render-time-anchoring.md)).

Once per entry the fit logs a `driving position` line with the vehicle type hash, the wheel part, the wheel's source, the measured hub from the eyes and from the head, the standard, the wanted and applied corrections, and the correction's source. That line is what a new `SEAT_OVERRIDES` entry is tuned from.

## Turning the rendered wheel

The game never turns a car's steering wheel. [`Vehicle::UpdateRender_ModelMatrices`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-UpdateRender_ModelMatrices) draws each part at its root-space transform times the vehicle's render transform. A detour on it, for the driven vehicle only, turns the wheel part's [`m_RootSpaceTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PartInstance##field-m_RootSpaceTransform) about the hub by the wheel angle, calls the original, and puts the transform back, so collision, deformation, and breaking never see the turn. The turn is skipped once the part, or one it hangs from, has broken off, or when the part at the published index is no longer the one the fit found.

The angle is the held wheel's while a hand holds it or it returns. Otherwise it is the angle that would give the vehicle's own steering ([`m_SteeringTurn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_SteeringTurn)) through the inverse of the lock, deadzone, and response, so stick steering turns it too.

The render update runs on worker threads, so the detour reads only its own slot: an atomic with the driven vehicle's address, which every other vehicle's call compares against and passes, and a small mutex with the part, the hub and axis in the part's space, and the angle, which the input tick's publish and the detour each take only for a copy. The detour touches neither the configuration nor the VR state.

## Settings and diagnostics

The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has `WHEEL TURNED BY` and `WHEEL FULL LOCK` (`controllers.vehicles.physical.wheel.turned_by` and `lock_deg`). Everything else about the wheel and the handlebars is tuning. The debug UI's Vehicles feature shows the wheel's angle and the steering written, the driving view's measurement and correction, the vehicle type hash, the wheel and fork parts found and where the wheel came from, the wheel as placed, whether the fallback is held in the vehicle's space, the bars' turn, and the rendered wheel's angle with a count of the render updates it turned.
