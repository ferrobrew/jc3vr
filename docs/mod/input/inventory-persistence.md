# Inventory persistence

The game takes the player's weapons away and gives them back at three caches, replaces them at every spawn reset, and saves only the slots ([engine: weapon-gives-and-restores.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-gives-and-restores.md), [engine: weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md#the-spawn-reset-and-the-respawn-table)). This page is how the [two-handed inventory](two-handed-inventory.md)'s loadout table survives each: the table's own snapshots and re-keying (`loadout::reset`), the seams that feed it (`weapons::inventory::{reset,missions,challenges}`), and the carried weapons' record and sidecar (`weapons::inventory::{persist,restore,sidecar}`). The seams run while the table does; with the table stopped, the caches and the resets are the game's alone.

The game's word on ownership is final. A mission or challenge that removes the player's weapons removes them from the table, the hands' included; on death and respawn the game's rules decide what the player owns, and the hands come back empty with everything else on its socket.

## The spawn reset

Every spawn reset (death and checkpoint restarts, fast travel, a challenge's start, retry, or abort, a mission's start, loading a save, the frontend) releases the spawn system's references to every object and replaces every slot weapon with a new object of the same type, keeping the magazines and re-equipping the selected slot. The inventory sees it through the local player's [`Inventory::HandleEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HandleEvent); the handler runs for every character's inventory on whatever thread resets the spawn system, so the detour compares the character with the published local player before it takes any lock.

At the pre-reset event ([`Inventory::m_SpawnSystemPreReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemPreReset)) the inventory records the carried weapons (each one's spawn tag hash, magazine, socket, and whether it is a single pistol, with whether the pistol slot's weapon is one), releases every reference it holds, the pins' and the carry set's, and the table begins the reset: every weapon the projection puts in a slot waits for its slot's new object.

The first input tick after the post-reset event completes the reset with the slots it left, once no tracked slot's spawn is pending or `loadout.settle_wait_s` has passed. The weapons lying in the world leave the table, and each new slot object re-binds the item waiting for that slot with the same type, so it keeps its identity and place. A new object nothing waits for goes onto the body, and an item whose slot the game emptied or gave another type leaves.

The reset's kind decides what the hands keep. It is read from the seams before it rather than from the player's health, which the respawn has restored by the time the reset runs:

- a respawn, when the game respawned the player since the last completion ([`Player::ReInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-ReInit), detoured): a death, a mission's failure or restart, the teleport to a mission's start, and the return to a challenge's start. The hands come back empty: their weapons go onto the body and anything else leaves;
- a load, when the game loaded the player's save data or the frontend reset the game's systems ([`GameStateFrontend::ResetSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateFrontend##func-ResetSystems), the quit to the menu, which also forgets the reset's record, so a new game carries nothing in). The table starts again from the slots; a load of the save data completes a load on its own, so one that does not reset first still starts the table from its slots;
- a travel otherwise: a fast travel, a challenge's start, retry, restart, or abort, and the map's teleport. The hands keep what they hold.

The game's own holster at a death, a cutscene, or a vehicle entry puts a hand's weapon on that hand's own hip where it fits there, rather than by the body's preference. A respawn whose pistol slot holds one pistol of the pair, with no carried twin, asks for the twin as a split of the game's pair does and puts it on the empty hip (`dual_wield::single::cover_lone_respawn`); a load or a mission's give-back keeps what was saved.

### Respawning the carried weapons

After the post-reset event, the first input tick with the player respawns the carried weapons: from the load when there was one since the reset, otherwise from the reset's own record when it is younger than `restore.reset_record_s` and for the same character. Each is asked of the spawn system by its type at the player ([`SpawnSystem::SpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-SpawnTagIndex), as the inventory respawns a slot). The callback runs inline on the caller of the spawn system's update and only queues a strong reference; the next input tick adopts the weapon into the carry set through one admission of the table's restore (`inventory::restore::adoption`), so the socket it is carried on and the socket the table has it on are the same. It is taken with [`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup), its magazine set as it was ([`WeaponBase::SetMagazineAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetMagazineAmmo)), a single pistol made single again, and restored onto its recorded socket, or the one the table prefers when that is taken. The pistol slot's respawned weapon is made single when the record says so; a carried single pistol that never comes back makes it the game's pair again. A spawn that has not come within `restore.spawn_wait_s` is given up.

A callback can stay queued in the engine after its request appears finished, and one that fires after the payload unmaps crashes the game, so the restore's, the twin pistols', and the debug UI's spawns are tracked by `inventory::spawn_safety`, which latches the exposure for the process's lifetime and logs it at unload rather than vetoing the unload.

## The caches

At each cache the slots are given the store projection first, so the game records what the player has, and the table takes its own snapshot of the weapons on the body with their places and slots, or restores it.

- The mission manager's checkpoint ([`MissionManager::CacheWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-CacheWeapons), detoured): the table snapshots itself as the checkpoint's cache, and the carried weapons are recorded for a restart. Its replay ([`MissionManager::RestoreCachedWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-RestoreCachedWeapons), detoured, after the player's respawn) marks the respawn for the checkpoint: the reset that follows respawns the checkpoint's carried weapons rather than those carried at the death, and at its completion the table returns to its snapshot. What was gained since leaves, and every item comes back to its socket, all taken out before any goes back so that two that swapped places swap back. The snapshot stands for every restart from the checkpoint, and keeps the hands empty, since the restart is a respawn.
- A mission's graph-script cache ([`NodeWeaponCache::CacheAllWeaponSlots`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-CacheAllWeaponSlots), detoured), which forces the slots' weapons out: the table caches before the game does and takes every weapon on the body, the hands' included, after it. The carried weapons are stashed: hidden, with no grab volume, and still carried. The give-back is the cache's own restore ([`NodeWeaponCache::RestoreWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-RestoreWeapons), detoured), after which the carried weapons are shown again and the table restores its snapshot once the slots' spawns arrive. The detour notes whether the pistol slot held a single pistol, so the give-back restores it as it was. The node's remove-where-empty strips remove single slots through the marked removal rather than the cache, so their weapons leave the table as gone and the carried weapons stay.
- A challenge's start ([`ROMManager::StartROM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMManager##func-StartROM)) and its object's initialisation ([`ROMObject::PostInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMObject##func-PostInit)) record the loadout, the start removing every slot when its spawn point overrides the loadout and the initialisation despawning each slot the challenge does not allow. Before each the table snapshots itself as the challenge's cache, keeping the first through a retry or a restart; when either took a weapon from the slots, the table takes every weapon on the body and stashes the carried ones, since the challenge decides the loadout and its give-back restores everything. The give-back is its abort ([`ROMManager::AbortROM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMManager##func-AbortROM)) or the restore of its record ([`ROMObject::RestoreWeaponsAndAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMObject##func-RestoreWeaponsAndAmmo)).

A give-back is folded once no tracked slot's spawn is pending, and after the spawn reset when one is under way, since the challenges' give-backs come with one. A mission's or a challenge's snapshot is used up by its give-back. The restored slots' objects re-bind the snapshot's items by slot and type, a snapshot weapon whose object is gone waits to be spawned again, and the hands come back empty. A reset during a hold (a death while a mission holds the weapons) does not respawn the carried weapons onto the body: its record is held back until the give-back, and a respawned weapon binds the snapshot's item waiting for its type rather than coming in as a new item.

## Saves, resets, and loads

Each time the game stores the player's save data ([`StorePlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-StorePlayerSaveData), detoured), the slots are given the store projection and the save sidecar is written: `inventory.toml` in the per-user configuration directory beside `settings.toml` ([settings.md](../settings.md)), by one writer thread that keeps only the newest pending snapshot and replaces the file atomically outside the game's and the UI's locks (`inventory::sidecar`). It holds a format version, the carried weapons, and a fingerprint of what the store writes: each slot's weapon type (the heavy slot's always none, as the save has it) and every reserve. It also says whether the pistol slot's weapon is a single pistol (`slot_pistol_single`), and each carried single pistol says so (`single`). The format is documented in `inventory::persist`.

Each time the game loads the player's save data ([`LoadPlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-LoadPlayerSaveData), detoured, after the original), the sidecar is read and its fingerprint compared with what the load put into the inventory: the respawn table's types ([`Inventory::m_WeaponToSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawn)) and the reserves. A slot whose type the game does not know (content not installed) loads empty and still matches. With a match, the sidecar's carried weapons of known types are kept for the respawn; a missing, unreadable, other-version, or differing sidecar carries nothing extra, and the load is otherwise untouched.

The game keeps one save per user, and the mod cannot identify which save a load reads, so the fingerprint is a best-effort compatibility check, not a save identity: an older save with equal slot types and reserves accepts the current sidecar, including carried weapons that save did not contain. A weapon the sidecar recorded in a hand comes back on a socket, since the hands come back empty on every load.

The weapons beyond the slots come to the player after every teleport: the carry set places them on the body's bones whatever the teleport, and a reset, which releases them, respawns them at the player.

## The scheme switch

Switching the control scheme away from the controllers, or the bindings or the controllers off, stops the table (`weapons::inventory::stand_down`); a pause of the controllers (the debug panel up, a frame without controllers, lost tracking) keeps it. The stop puts the table back into the slots as a store would write them, disarms the hands' armed weapons, retires the safe grenades, and keeps the weapons the slots cannot hold in the carry set, stashed and recorded by the sidecar at a save, rather than dropping them or depending on a save to bring them back. When the table runs again it starts from the slots, as a load does, and each kept weapon comes back onto its socket, with any a reset recorded meanwhile respawned. The hands come back empty, since the game's one weapon was wielded in between. Gamepad play meanwhile is the game's one-weapon model.
