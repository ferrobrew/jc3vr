# Human hull discovery and restoration

The human hull controller (`controllers::physical_hands::human_hull` in `jc3vr-physical`) gives each human proxy state a narrower collision hull and restores the authored one when switched off or ejected. It changes the settings tables' shapes and the live bodies' shapes only: fake-contact settings, shape user data, materials, collision filters, character state, and quadruped entries stay as they are.

## The narrowed shape

The game builds each human proxy state's shape in `CSharedPfxCharacterSettings::CreateQuadrupedShape` as a convex hull: a heptagonal prism between two cones, each cone as high as the hull's radius. The character's rigid body treats a contact leaning further from vertical than its maximum slope as a wall, and that slope (set in `CPfxCharacterInstance`'s constructor, not yet bound) lies just above the lean of the bottom cone's faces, so the character slides up any edge as high as the cone.

The replacement keeps the hull's height and halves its width (`human_hull::geometry::narrow_points`), and also scales the bottom cap toward the lowest vertex so the cone keeps its angles; halving the width alone would leave faces far steeper than the maximum slope, and every kerb would be a wall. The narrowed cone is half as high, so it steps up only half as much, and no convex bottom of half the width can do better under that slope.

The game has no step-up of its own to raise: the rigid body climbs only by sliding along shallow contacts, and its only foot probe, `CPfxCharacterInstance::CheckLedges` (not yet bound), keeps it from walking off drops and never lifts. So `human_hull::step_up` lifts the narrowed player up the steps the authored hull would have climbed. In the hull's pre-simulation pass, when the supported proxy is driven across the ground but blocked, it casts a clear ray across above the authored cone's height and one down past the narrowed hull's edge; a walkable top between the two cones' heights lifts the proxy, as the hands' push-up does ([physical-hands.md](../input/physical-hands.md)), until the feet clear it. The heights come from the shapes (`geometry::step_profile`), and the thresholds are in `human_hull.toml`. Each lift logs `human hull: the narrowed player steps up a step the authored hull would`.

## Discovery

Each native pre-simulation pass takes one inventory of the live bodies in [`hknpWorld::m_bodies`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##field-m_bodies), shared by discovery and the shape changes. Construction hooks adopt settings tables as the game makes them; for tables that predate injection, discovery walks the bodies' owners through [`PfxInstance::m_GameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##field-m_GameObject) on every pass until a table is adopted and then every `discovery.interval_passes` (`human_hull/native/scan.rs`). Destruction hooks drop dead tables. Owner pointers never outlive the native phase.

A slot counts as a live body only when it has a static or dynamic flag and a valid motion, the engine's own test. Destroying a body does not clear its shape ([`hknpBody::INVALID_MOTION_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody##const-INVALID_MOTION_ID)), so a dead human's slot keeps naming its replacement until reused; counting it would refuse every restoration. A live body without a shape (every world's fixed body) is skipped too.

## Clearance and restoration

A disable or shutdown restores the narrowed bodies one at a time, each on the first pass that finds its own surroundings clear (`human_hull/native/restore.rs`), so a body against a wall does not hold back one on open ground. Each retained original is checked at the body's actual transform against the candidates [`hknpWorld::queryAabb`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-queryAabb) returns, with bodies approved earlier in the pass counted at their original shape. Convex targets are queried directly; compressed mesh and heightfield targets are walked leaf by leaf, bounded by `restore.max_candidates` and `restore.max_leaf_work`. Anything the controller cannot verify (an unsupported shape or scale, an incomplete enumeration, a heuristic or unknown GSK status, an unwritten output, an exhausted budget) defers rather than counting as clear. A floor contact that the original produces at the same shallow depth and normal as the replacement is allowed, so a body on flat ground restores; any other contact blocks.

Every clearance check finishes before the pass's first write. Each write is read back, and a rejected write rolls the pass back in reverse; a rejected rollback refuses restoration for good. The settings tables keep the replacements until no body is narrowed, so a body changing proxy state never takes an unchecked authored shape. While some body stays obstructed, a disable doubles its wait between attempts up to `restore.max_retry_wait_passes`.

## Eject

The shutdown participant waits `restore.shutdown_attempts` attempts for a restoration. If none completes, the eject leaves every shape as it is and keeps every retained reference, so nothing a body names can be freed. That is safe because the replacements are the engine's own convex shapes on its heap with no pointer into the payload; the cost is a small leak and narrowed humans until their tables are freed.

A replacement left behind must not be read as authored geometry by the next payload, or each reinjection would halve the width again. The eject records each pair it leaves (slot, original, replacement) in the process environment (`human_hull/kept.rs`), and a later payload that finds either shape of a recorded pair adopts the pair instead of narrowing the slot. The leaked references keep both addresses unique for the life of the process.

The debug UI's physical-hands page shows the adapter state, the clearance reason, the adopted tables, and the narrowed body count, and the log records `human hull: the local player's body shape changed` when the player's own hull changes.

## Limits and live checks

Only compressed mesh and heightfield composites are supported; others defer when they overlap. A large terrain chunk can exhaust the leaf budget and defer. The unit tests cover the native-call adaptation, status rejection, contact policy, and rollback, but not the engine's allocation or callback order. The live checks, besides [runtime-testing.md](runtime-testing.md): kerbs and steps climb as with the hull off, and walls and high ledges do not lift; NPCs present at injection and streamed in later are narrowed; a disable beside a wall restores the NPCs at once and the player once clear; two humans too close restore one at a time; an eject in a crowded scene finishes within seconds without a frame spike; and repeated eject and reinject beside a wall keeps a single narrowed width.
