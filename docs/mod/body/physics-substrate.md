# The physics substrate

The hands, the weapons, the grapple, and the vehicle handling reach the physics world through one layer in the body crate, `jc3vr_body::controllers::physics`. It reads and drives bodies, reads the local player's proxy, finds the vehicle behind a body, scans the world's bodies by their boxes, watches the rig for jumps, and composes the character's wanted velocity. Every helper fails with one error, `PhysicsError`, whose message starts `physics:`.

## Bodies, the proxy, vehicles, and scans

- `bodies` reads a dynamic body's pose, velocities, and mass after checking that it still belongs to the object it was taken from, sets its velocities (`drive`), and shoves it with an impulse at a point (`shove`). `push` applies an impulse to a body a ray hit this tick, whoever owns it; the engine leaves a static or keyframed body alone. Both impulses go through [`hknpWorld::applyBodyImpulseAt`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-applyBodyImpulseAt), and both velocity writes through [`hknpWorld::setBodyVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-setBodyVelocity), by way of `World::apply_impulse` and `World::set_velocities`, which take game-space points.
- `proxy` reads the local player's proxy body: its slot, its place beside the character's root (`place`), the velocity its motion holds, and the support its last update found. It gives the proxy a velocity for the coming step as the proxy's own update does, and `hold` also writes the proxy as supported on a body, level, moving with that body's surface, which is how the vehicle rail carries Rico on a helicopter's skid.
- `vehicles` finds the vehicle a hit object belongs to (the vehicle itself or the vehicle a loosened part came from), the vehicle that owns a body, the vehicle's own body checked against its owner, and the vehicle's frame.
- `scan` reads a body's motion kind from its slot flags and its broad-phase box through the world's integer space, and walks the slots for the dynamic bodies whose boxes a test accepts, either all at once (`dynamic_boxes`) or a slice per step (`walk_dynamic`).

## The rig's jumps

A snap turn, a hard step of the view toward the body through a vehicle's entry or exit ([rig.md, "The body turning under the view"](rig.md#the-body-turning-under-the-view)), a recenter, and a follow bank move every palm in the rig at once without the hands moving. The mod makes all four, so each is an event rather than something a reader infers: the view counts the one-tick turns of the body frame (`headpose::xr::rig_turn_count`, a snap step or a hard step) and the re-bases of the cockpit frame (`vr::rebase_count`, a recenter or a follow bank). `rig::RigSample` reads the body's facing and both counts on a reader's tick, and `rig::RigWatch` reports what jumped since the reader's last tick: `Rebase`, `Turn`, `Unannounced` for a turn of the facing past the substrate tuning's `rig.unannounced_turn_rad` with no event (the game turning or placing the body: a respawn, a cutscene, a teleport), and `Settling` for the tick after an event of the mod's. The counts and the facing are separate reads, and a reader on another thread can take the new count with the old facing, so the facing's jump can land a tick after the count changed; the settling tick covers it.

The fallback angle is a quarter turn per tick, wider than any smooth turn: the stick turns at most a few degrees a tick and a fast mouse turn tens. A fast smooth turn is therefore the body turning for every reader, whatever its tick rate.

The hand bodies, the climb, and the grapple's pump start their motion afresh on any jump. The releases' transport ([Release velocities](#release-velocities)) tells them apart: a re-base or an unannounced turn cuts the hand history, while a turn or its settling tick turns only the body frame, so the history stands and the rig is at rest for the tick.

## The wanted velocity

The engine moves the character's proxy by the wanted velocity its movement task writes each simulation tick. The mod's writers adjust it around the task's write, and each is a `wanted::Contributor`. A tick's writes go through one `wanted::Composition`, which applies them in the contributors' order: each contribution reads the velocity as the previous one left it and returns the velocity to write, or nothing. The order within a frame:

1. Before the world update, on the game thread: the vehicle rail's entry, which takes Rico off a helicopter's interaction graph moving with its skid, or its step-off by the jump during the door-side arrival, which takes him off it at his own velocity, and the grapple lock's release and its handback after the reel's cancel. The movement task runs after them and writes the velocity afresh, so these are inputs the task carries into its tick.
2. In the character's update, exactly one movement task runs:
   - On foot, the locomotion task's post-call composes the stick's velocity in place of the clip's horizontal one ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md)), the palms' push added to it ([physical-hands.md](../input/physical-hands.md#pushing-off-surfaces)), and, while the player owns the root, the step drive added to the sum and capped ([rig.md](rig.md#who-moves-the-root)). A step that does not move writes nothing.
   - Swimming, the swim core's post-call joins the arm strokes' speed to the core's velocity along the facing ([swim-strokes.md](swim-strokes.md)).
   - Reeling, the reel's arrival hands the grapple lock's velocity on ([grapple-lock.md](../input/grapple-lock.md)).

The rail and the grapple lock never run in the same frame, and the three movement tasks exclude each other, so the on-foot composition is the only one with more than one contributor. A contributor applied out of order is a programming error, which a debug build stops on. The body's tests compare the on-foot composition with the writers applied one after another, reading the velocity back each time, over every combination of stick, push, and step.

## Release velocities

Everything a hand lets go of leaves it through one path, `physics::release`: a prop or a ragdoll the physical hands throw, a gun let go of, a grenade, a caught grenade, and a C4 charge. For the same swing they leave with the same motion.

- The hand's share is the peak of the controller's timestamped history in the cockpit frame (`jc3vr_input::motion::release_velocity`), read from when the hand took the item, at the released point carried rigidly with the grip: the controller's linear velocity plus its angular velocity crossed with the offset from the grip to the point. The point is a prop's centre of mass, a gun's world point, or a grenade's centre. The spin is the hand's angular velocity.
- The player's own movement is the rig's, sampled once per input tick by `release::sample` from the bindings' hands stage: the character's measured velocity, plus the rig's turn between input ticks crossed with the offset from the cockpit origin to the point. It is added to the hand's share, turned and scaled into the world, once.
- Only the hand's share is capped, at the substrate tuning's `release.max_hand_mps` per real second (`ReleaseMotion::thrown`, through `jc3vr_input::motion::shaped_release`). The carried share passes through, bounded only by `release.max_carried_mps`, past any real vehicle, so a throw from a moving car keeps the car's speed. There is no cap by mass.
- In slow motion the hand's share, measured per real second, becomes the same motion per game second (`ReleaseMotion::in_game_time`); the carried share is per game second already ([slow-motion.md](../input/slow-motion.md#throws)).

Without fresh rig motion (no head pose, or the first tick after a cut) a release has no motion: a gun is dropped at rest, and a prop keeps the velocity it has. With stale or untracked hand history, the release carries the rig's motion alone. An object one hand cannot lift keeps the velocity the hold gave it rather than being thrown. A drop that is not a throw (a gun let go of while the inventory restores it) takes the same path with a hand cap of zero, so it leaves with the player's movement alone.

The climb's fling is not a throw of an object: it launches the player's body with the velocity the climb's pull gave it, measured from the proxy relative to the gripped body, plus the gripped body's own motion. It has no controller history to read, so it keeps the climb's own estimate and shares only the shaping (`shaped_release`, with the climb's fling scale and cap) ([hand-climbing.md](../input/hand-climbing.md)).
