# Weapons in the world and the carry set

The [two-handed inventory](two-handed-inventory.md)'s loadout table keeps the weapons the player let go of near them, and the weapons on the body that no slot of the game's holds. This page is the game's side of both: the weak references and the pins that keep a dropped weapon from the despawn (`weapons::inventory::{world,pins}`), the return of a lost weapon (`weapons::inventory::lost`), the markers over dropped weapons (`hud::lost_weapons`), and the carry set (`weapons::inventory::carry`).

## The weapons in the world

The inventory (`weapons::inventory::world`) keeps a weak reference to each weapon it has seen in a slot, the carry set, or a hand ([`WeaponBase::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeakThis)), which tells whether it lives without keeping it alive. Each input tick it asks what became of each of the table's weapons that none of those keeps: one whose holder ([`WeaponBase::m_Holder`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Holder)) is another character is that character's and leaves the table; one whose object is gone leaves; any other is lying in the world, at a distance from the player. A weapon another character takes is still watched while that character keeps it within `pins.forget_m`, for the readouts and so a single pistol stays one pistol in their hands; it is never pinned, and taken back it is a new item.

The game removes a dropped weapon by its distance from the player and the view, and never one that something else holds a strong reference to. So a weapon the player let go of is pinned (`weapons::inventory::pins`), held by a strong reference, while it is within `pins.radius_m`, and once pinned until it is past `pins.release_m`, so one at the edge does not flicker. At most `pins.max_pinned` are pinned, the nearest first, since a pinned weapon keeps its resources loaded. An unpinned weapon past `pins.forget_m`, or beyond `pins.max_tracked`, is left to the game and leaves the table. Weapons the player never touched are never pinned, so the game's own clean-up goes on as before.

At the spawn system's pre-reset event the inventory releases every reference it holds, so no pinned weapon outlives the reset outside the spawn system, and the reset's end takes the weapons lying in the world out of the table ([inventory-persistence.md](inventory-persistence.md)).

### Markers on the weapons let go of

Each weapon the table has lying in the world (dropped, thrown, knocked from a hand, ejected by a swap, or brought back to the feet) carries a marker: the game's own icon for it, with the grab hint's soft dark halo, standing upright above where it lies (`hud::lost_weapons`, fed from the inventory's input tick). The game's own pickup hints mark only the nearest useful weapon per slot while that slot runs low ([engine: ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md#weapon-icons)), so they cannot find a weapon thrown in a fight. Like the HUD's other quads the marker is drawn without a depth test at a stereo-correct depth, at a constant angular size; it fades in past arm's length, where the grip's own markers take over, and only the nearest few are drawn (the HUD tuning's `lost_weapons` section). It lasts as long as the table keeps the weapon, so a weapon left behind on purpose stops being marked once the player moves on.

## Letting go

A weapon a hand lets go of stays where it falls, kept from the despawn while the player is near ([The weapons in the world](#the-weapons-in-the-world)). Swapping it for another or walking away never brings it back; retrieving a weapon dropped at an ordinary pace is the grapple snatch's job. It returns to the body only when it is lost rather than dropped (`weapons::inventory::lost`):

- out of the world: more than `lost.lost_depth_m` below the sea's level, which only sinking out of reach or falling through the terrain reaches. No terrain query is bound, so the floor is the sea's level less a margin;
- left behind at speed: it was let go of while the player travelled at `lost.fast_speed_mps` or faster (their speed smoothed over `lost.speed_time_s`; the wingsuit, a vehicle, the parachute, a fall) and is now farther than `lost.left_behind_m` away. A distance rather than a time, since the speed that left it makes the distance come quickly.

A lost weapon returns to the first free socket that fits it, or with none free to the player's feet, where the carry set holds it on the body for one tick and lets it fall at the next. A weapon returned to the feet is not lost again for being left behind. The judgement runs before the pins, so a weapon returns before it is far enough to be forgotten.

## The weapons beyond the slots

The game's inventory holds one weapon per slot ([`Inventory::m_Weapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_Weapons)), and the save, the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the missions read only the slots. What keeps a weapon alive is any strong reference, not the slot as such, so a reference of the mod's keeps a weapon beyond the slots in the game and out of the spawn system's despawn ranking, and a weapon moves into and out of a slot without being dropped. Every weapon the table has on the body that no slot holds is in the carry set (`weapons::inventory::carry`): held by the mod's strong reference, holstered, with the player as its holder, so the HUD, the wheel, and the saves do not list it.

Each socket is one of the game's dock bones (each shoulder's the back dock bone of its side, each hip's its holster bone) with an offset in the character's frame (`inventory::sockets`, the tuning's `sockets` section), so a weapon beyond the slots sits beside the slot's weapon on the same bone rather than inside it; a pistol on a hip sits in the holster instead.

A carried weapon is placed after each placement of the inventory's own weapons ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms), the hold's detour): its guns' holstered attach-bone entries name the socket's bone, the weapon's own placement puts them there, and the offset moves them; one in a hand rides the hand's bone. It is shown and hidden whenever the inventory's weapons are ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponVisibility`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponVisibility), detoured), so it hides and shows with them in vehicles and the wingsuit. Each pose-finalization pass is ended for it as the game ends it for the slots' weapons, since a render-interpolation skip otherwise lasts until then and the weapon would be drawn a tick ahead of the body.

A weapon an area clean-up takes out of the spawn system while it is carried lives on through the carry set's reference and is recorded and respawned at the next reset as usual.
