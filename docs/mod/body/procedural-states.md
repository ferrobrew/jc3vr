# The procedural torso outside standing play

The procedural torso ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md)) poses Rico's spine, neck, and clavicles from the player on the hips the game's animation keeps, and the legs are the animation's in every case. Outside standing play the animation carries the body: the seat's pose, the swim, the harness under the parachute, and the wingsuit's flight pose are authored, and they are what the vehicle, the water, and the air move. This doc covers what the procedural torso takes from them and what it leaves. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/procedural/`.

## The torso on the animation's hips

Outside standing play the hips belong to whatever carries Rico: the seat, the water, the harness. The torso is solved as on foot (`solver::anchored`, pure and unit-tested; [procedural-body.md, "The torso on the animation's hips"](procedural-body.md#the-torso-on-the-animations-hips)), keeping the hips and the clip's posture and moving the spine from there by the player's own motion:

1. The chest twists toward the player's torso, by the body rig's heading against Rico's facing ([rig.md](rig.md#the-yaw)), within `chest_turn_limit_deg`, about the clip's own spine and shared out up it as on foot. A spine far from upright takes less of the twist, and none lying flat, since the heading is a turn about the vertical. The rig has a heading only while Rico is grounded, so in a seat, on a roof, under the parachute, and in the water the chest keeps the clip's facing.
2. The spine swings so the neck reaches the point under the player's head, shared out along the four spine bones and repeated to converge, never past `lean_limit_deg` from the clip's own spine. A lean in the seat bends the back; the hips stay where the clip put them.
3. A look down pitches the chest forward by a share of it, as on foot, again by how upright the spine is, and the spine turns back onto the neck's line about its base.
4. The neck takes a share of the head's turn, which the head override composes onto the clip's head, so the neck's share is a share of that turn.

The clip's spine is not settled toward the reference pose first: a bike's lean into a turn and a driver's reach for the wheel stay, and the player's motion is added to them. A player whose head is where the clip has Rico's leaves the clip's pose exactly as it was. The clavicles then turn toward the hands as on foot, the hand IK queues the wrist targets at the gate against the written torso (kept out of it, with no pull, so the solve cannot bend the torso toward a hand), and the game's IK targets on the torso's bones fade by its weight ([procedural-body.md, "The arms"](procedural-body.md#the-arms)). The elbow hints point down and back from the chest, so they are queued only while the written chest is near upright.

The view's anchor in these states is the animated head ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)), captured before the gate writes the pose, so the head the solve reaches for is the clip's head moved by the player's tracked motion, with no feedback from the written spine.

The first gate of each case that poses the torso logs `procedural body: the torso follows the head on the animation's hips` at info level, with the case, the torso's weight, and the neck's distance from its target.

## Seated in a vehicle

Seated and riding, the torso is procedural on the seat's hips and the legs keep the seat's animation. The hand-over case is `seated in a vehicle`; it needs the hand tiers' classification of the seat as riding ([`Character::IsInRidingInVehicleState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInRidingInVehicleState), read by the input tick, so it lags by up to a frame), which an entry, an exit, a seat switch, and a mounted gun are not, and no stunt position. Those stay the vehicle's animation, and the torso blends between the two as the seat's state changes, so an entry blends the seat in after the entry's clip and an exit hands back before it.

The legs stay the seat's: the clip puts the feet on the pedals or the footrests and moves them with the vehicle's own animation (a bike's lean, the steering's turn of the hips), and nothing the player does with their own legs is tracked to replace it. The arms are the controllers' through the hand IK, on the wheel, the bars, or a held control ([steering-wheel.md](../input/steering-wheel.md)), and the clavicles reach with them. The driving view's correction moves the head the solve reaches for as it moves the view, so Rico leans by it as a player leaning the same distance would ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)).

The body IK's torso lean, which posed the torso in a seat before, stands down once the procedural torso has its full weight, as on foot.

The scenario signals `procedural.seated` and `procedural.neck_error_m` read the same, and `testing/scenarios/seated_torso.toml` is the template for a recorded check ([recorder.md](../testing/recorder.md)). The `SEATED TORSO` status row shows the torso's weight and the neck's distance from the point under the player's head; it is `OK` at full weight, and `NOT SEATED` outside a seat.

## Standing on a roof

Standing in a vehicle's stunt area (a car's roof, a boat's deck, a plane's wing; [input/stunt-positions.md](../input/stunt-positions.md)), the torso is procedural on the stunt idle's hips and the legs keep the idle's balance. The hand-over case is `standing on a vehicle`: in a stunt position ([`Character::IsInStuntPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInStuntPosition)), in a stunt area ([`Character::IsInStuntArea`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInStuntArea)), not hanging from a helicopter ([`Character::IsStuntHanging`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsStuntHanging)), and not in a stunt traversal. The torso is solved as in a seat, and the legs keep the idle's balance, which moves with the vehicle. The hang and a stunt traversal stay the vehicle's animation, as before.

The `ROOF TORSO` status row shows the torso's weight and the neck's distance from the point under the player's head, `OK` at full weight and `NOT ON A ROOF` otherwise. The scenario signal `procedural.roof` reads the same, and `testing/scenarios/stunt_positions.toml` covers it.

## The wingsuit

In the wingsuit the body keeps the flight pose (the torso stays the animation's; the hand-over case is `wingsuit`), and Rico's arms follow the player's. The hand tier of the wingsuit stays yielded ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)): the hand IK cannot put Rico's hands where the player's are, since the player stands or sits upright while Rico flies prone, and a hand at the player's height relative to the head is nowhere near a prone flyer's shoulders. Each arm is instead carried over as the arm steering reads it ([`vehicles::arm_steering`](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)), in a frame that keeps the vertical and turns with the heading (`solver::wings`, pure and unit-tested):

- The player's wrist (the grip offset applied, not the body-scale remap) is measured from the player's estimated shoulder, in the frame of the player's heading: the body rig's while it has one, which it does only while Rico is grounded, so in flight the head's. The cockpit frame's vertical is the player's real vertical.
- That reach is scaled from the player's arm (the body dimensions' upper arm and forearm) to Rico's (measured from the reference pose, [procedural-body.md, "The rig"](procedural-body.md#the-rig)), and placed from Rico's animated shoulder joint in the frame of his flight heading, with the world's up. The flight heading is the line from the hips to the neck laid flat, which a prone flyer holds along the flight, or the root's forward laid flat in a dive straight down.
- The wrist frame's orientation turns by the same turn between the two headings.

So the spread is the spread, a hand lowered for a bank is lowered toward the ground, a hand swept forward goes toward where Rico flies, and the wrists' tilt that pitches the wingsuit turns Rico's wrists the same way. The arms follow the player's whether or not the arm steering is switched on or engaged.

The targets are placed at the solve gate from the pose the animation wrote this tick, and queued once the gate has let go of its state (`hands::ik::queue_model_wrists`): a position and a rotation target on each wrist, at the hand IK's reaches scaled by the arms' weight, with no pull, so the flight pose's torso does not bend toward a hand. The weight eases in over `blend_s` in the wingsuit with both controllers read and out over `blend_out_s`, and while it eases out the last targets hold where they were. The body IK does not pin those wrists to the animation while the arms queue them (a tick behind, from the last gate). The first gate of each flight that places a target logs `procedural body: Rico's arms follow the player's in the wingsuit` at info level, with the hands placed.

The wingsuit's membrane is skinned between the arms and the body, so arms held far from the wing pose (by the sides, overhead) fold or stretch it as they would in the game's own arm animation; the arms spread as wings keep it as the flight pose has it.

The `WINGSUIT ARMS` status row shows the arms' weight and the hands queued, `OK` with both at full weight and `NOT IN THE WINGSUIT` otherwise. The scenario signal `procedural.wing_arms` reads the same, and `testing/scenarios/wingsuit_arms.toml` is the template for a recorded check. The Body page's Body fit live status shows the weight and the hands placed and queued.

## The parachute

Under the parachute the torso is procedural on the harness's hips and the legs keep the harness clip's. The hand-over case is `parachute`, from the mode detection, and the torso is solved as in a seat. The arms are the controllers' as before: the parachute's hand tier is free, so a hand follows its controller, or holds the handle it took ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)), and the clavicles reach with them.

The canopy and its lines are a separate model with its own skeleton; whether the lines ride the harness's bones that the torso solve moves (the spine and the clavicles) is not established, so a lean that moves the shoulders far from the clip's may part them from the lines a little.

Coming from a jump the torso stays procedural through the change of case; coming from a fall past the grace the torso's weight blends it in from the animation. Landing hands back to the torso on foot, with the legs the game's landing.

The `PARACHUTE BODY` status row shows the torso's weight and the neck's distance from the point under the player's head, `OK` at full weight, `NOT UNDER THE CANOPY` otherwise. The scenario signal `procedural.parachute` reads the same, and `testing/scenarios/parachute_body.toml` is the template for a recorded check.

## Swimming

Swimming, the torso and the arms are procedural and the legs keep the swim's kick. The hand-over case is `swimming`, and the torso is solved on the swim's hips as in a seat. The swim lies Rico nearly flat, so the chest's twist toward the torso and its pitch for a look down fade out as the spine lies down (the torso's heading is a turn about the vertical, which a spine lying flat has no part in); the spine still swings toward the player's head within the lean limit of the clip's own spine, and the neck takes its share of the head's turn. The elbow hints are not queued while the written chest is far from upright, since they point down and back from an upright chest.

The swimming hand tier is yielded in the plan's table ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)); in the procedural torso's full mode, the hand tiers treat it as free (`HandTierTable::with_swim_arms`), so the arms follow the controllers through the hand IK, queued at the gate on the written torso like any other procedural torso's. The player's hands are placed from the player's head, as on foot, so a stroke the player mimes is the stroke Rico's arms make. The arms do not swim: the stick moves Rico through the water as before ([swim-yaw.md](swim-yaw.md)).

Walking into the water keeps the torso procedural throughout, on the hips of whatever clip plays, and the legs go to the kick as the game's animation blends them. Walking back out does the same the other way.

The `SWIM TORSO` status row shows the torso's weight and the neck's distance from the point under the player's head, `OK` at full weight and `NOT SWIMMING` otherwise. The scenario signal `procedural.swimming` reads the same, and `testing/scenarios/swim_torso.toml` is the template for a recorded check.

## The body mirror

In the headset Rico's body is mostly out of sight: the head is hidden, the view looks down onto the chest, and a seat, the wingsuit, or the water hides the rest. The body mirror is a diagnostic for checking his alignment with the player's (`controllers.overlay.body_mirror`, off by default; the `Body mirror` switch in the debug UI's visualization drawer, with `body_mirror_distance_m`, 1.2 m by default). It is drawn by `controllers::body::mirror`.

It does not render the scene a second time. A second camera would be a third render dispatch per frame, doubling the scene's cost with the render setups' state to save and restore, and a world-space quad showing it would need that render as its texture. Instead it draws the pose itself, which is what is being checked: at the end of the local player's pose-finalization pass, after the head override and the limbs' twist, it reads a stick figure's bones from the finalized pose (the hips, the spine, the neck, the head, the clavicles, the arms to the wrists, and the legs to the feet, found by name once per skeleton), carries them into the world, and reflects them across a plane standing `body_mirror_distance_m` ahead of Rico's facing (the root's forward laid flat, or its up where the forward points at the ground, as a prone body's may). The reflection is drawn through the world-space overlay ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md)), over the scene: lines between the bones (the spine white, the left side blue, the right orange), a ring at the head bone, the mirror's frame, and smaller rings at the reflected targets the body was posed toward, the player's head (green) and each controller's wrist target (in its side's colour). As in a mirror, the left stays on the viewer's left. A wrist ring away from the reflected hand, or the head ring away from the head, is a misalignment the player can see while moving.

The primitives are submitted from the pass's thread each tick into the overlay's `body mirror` slot, which expires a few ticks after the last submission, and are anchored to the head at an offset, so the figure rides with a player moving fast (a vehicle, the wingsuit) rather than trailing at the tick's rate. It costs a handful of joint reads and some forty primitives a tick while on, and nothing while off. The first pass on a skeleton logs `body mirror: found the figure's bones` at info level with any bone it did not find.

The `BODY MIRROR` status row says how to switch it on while it is off, and how many of the figure's bones it found while it is on; `NO BONES FOUND` fails it.

Turn your torso (both hands off the wheel): the chest turns, within its limit. Look down at the lap: the chest leans a little over it. The hands stay on the wheel while you lean.
2. On a motorcycle, ride through turns: Rico's lean with the bike stays, the feet stay on the footrests, and leaning your head adds to it rather than fighting it. Report a jerk at the start or end of a turn.
3. Enter and leave vehicles, switch seats, and take a mounted gun: each logs the case `vehicle` while it plays, and the torso blends without a pop. In a helicopter and a plane, the torso follows the head the same way.
4. Get shot or thrown out of a seat: the ragdoll hands over at once, as on foot.
5. The wingsuit, watching the shadow: open it and spread your arms. The log says `procedural body: Rico's arms follow the player's in the wingsuit`, and `WINGSUIT ARMS` reads `OK, 100%, BOTH HANDS`. Turn your head and lean: the neck and the upper back follow without twisting the lying body. Swim with the stick: the arms stay where you hold them. Report how the arms look against the water's surface and whether the elbows bend sensibly without the hints. Walk back out: the torso stays on Rico's hips without a pop.
