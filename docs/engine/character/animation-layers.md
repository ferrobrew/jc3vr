# Animation layers and the pose job

A character's animation is a stack of layers, each a state machine with its own clips, blended into one local-space pose by a job on a worker thread. This doc covers how the layers are built and named, how the pose job combines them, what keeps running when a layer's pose is left out, and the state tasks the pose-finalization pass runs after its entry, the weapon off-hand grip among them. Related: [idle-stance.md](idle-stance.md) (the animation modifiers that pick the base layer's clips), [humanik.md](humanik.md) (the IK passes), and [skeleton.md](skeleton.md) (the pose store).

## The layers

[`Character::m_AnimatedModel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_AnimatedModel) holds up to seven layers. `CCharacter::InitAnimationModel` creates layer *i* from the *i*-th state-machine file of the character's construction info and names it by `CCharacter::s_LayerNames`, which [`AnimationLayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationLayer) mirrors. It stops at the first missing file. Rico ships four graphs, in `animations/jc_statemachine/main_characters/rico/` (`patch_win64/game1`):

| Layer | Rico's graph | What it plays |
|---|---|---|
| [`MAINBODY`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationLayer##variant-MAINBODY) | `rico_base` | the full body: locomotion, stunts, the grapple, the parachute, vehicles. Nearly every node has weapon-modifier clip variants (the held and aimed stances). |
| [`SYNCED_PARTIAL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationLayer##variant-SYNCED_PARTIAL) | `rico_partial_sync` | upper-body partials kept in step with the base: the turns toward the aim target (`S_PARTIAL_AIM_TARGET_*`), and idle, move, and fall partials. |
| [`GLOBAL_PARTIAL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationLayer##variant-GLOBAL_PARTIAL) | `rico_partial_global` | upper-body one-offs: draw, holster, switch, reload, shotgun pump, sniper bolt, pickup, grenade throws, explosives, and the parachute's arm states (`S_UB_*`, `S_JC_UB_*`). |
| [`SYNCED_ADDITIVE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationLayer##variant-SYNCED_ADDITIVE) | `rico_additive_sync` | additives on the base: leans, starts and stops, landings, the aim-focus additives, and the grapple, grenade, and launcher fire additives. |

There is no separate weapon-fire recoil state in Rico's graphs.

Each layer is one rule system, [`AnimatedModel::m_RuleSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##field-m_RuleSystems) at the layer's index, and one body part, [`AnimationController::m_BodyParts`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##field-m_BodyParts) at the same index. A body part ([`AnimatedBodyPart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::AnimatedBodyPart)) owns the layer's clip driver ([`AnimationDriverTransition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::AnimationDriverTransition)), which follows the rule system's state, starts and blends that state's clips, and owns the layer's blender ([`AnimationDriverTransition::m_Blender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::AnimationDriverTransition##field-m_Blender)). `CAnimationControl::AddAnimatedBodyPart` pushes the body part and adds its blender to the controller's pose producer, so the blenders are in layer order.

## The pose job

The pose producer ([`PoseProducer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::PoseProducer)) builds the local-space pose in `CPoseProducer::UpdateBlendersPoseInRange`, on a worker thread:

1. The first blender samples its clips over the skeleton's reference pose ([`Blender::UpdatePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::Blender##func-UpdatePose)) and also writes its result as the pre-additive pose.
2. Each later blender for which [`Blender::HasSamplers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::Blender##func-HasSamplers) holds runs `UpdatePose` with the previous output as its reference pose and a fresh buffer as its output. A partial layer's clips carry per-bone weights, so it overlays only the bones it animates, and where its total weight falls below the skeleton's reference threshold the reference shows through. An additive layer adds onto the reference.
3. A blender without samplers is skipped, and the pose below it passes on unchanged.

`UpdatePose` writes nothing but its outputs. A layer whose blend is replaced by a copy of its reference pose and floats therefore leaves the pose exactly as a layer without samplers does.

## What a layer does besides its pose

The rule system's state machine, its state tasks, the driver's clip selection and times, the track messages, the segment queries, and the animation-finished checks all run from the driver and the rule system, not from the blended pose. Several game systems depend on them:

- Reload. The reload clips carry a track message that `CWeaponBase::HandleTrackMessage` answers by refilling the magazine (`CWeaponBase::Reload`), and the reload states' exit task (`NWeaponReloadFinishedTask`) ends the reload sequence. A reload whose clip does not play never refills.
- Holster and draw. `NWeaponUnequipTask` and another track message unequip the weapon; `NWeaponEquipTask` equips it.
- Inventory permissions. `NSetInventoryPermissions` tasks, some gated on segments, block firing during the actions ([aim-state.md, "Fire gating"](../gameplay/aim-state.md#fire-gating)).

The engine's level of detail (`CCharacter::UpdateLOD`) turns a distant character's partial and additive layers off through `CAnimationControl::SetInactiveBodyPart`, which resets the layer driver's clips every update. That stops the clips themselves, so their track messages and segments stop too.

## State tasks inside the pose-finalization pass

[`Character::UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel) begins by running the current states' tasks registered in its own state-task range, [`Character::FINALIZE_POSE_STATE_TASK_RANGE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character::FINALIZE_POSE_STATE_TASK_RANGE), through [`Character::UpdateStateTasksInRange`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateStateTasksInRange). Each update pass of a character runs its own range, and every task type is registered in exactly one. The aim IK, look-at IK, locomotion IK, grapple-fire arm IK, and weapon off-hand IK tasks are in the finalize range. So their effector targets and blackboard keys are written after the pass's entry and before the IK solve, and a key zeroed at the pass's entry is written again by the time the solve reads it.

The weapon off-hand IK task ([`NWeaponOffhandIKTask_Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/animation::arm_aim_ik##func-NWeaponOffhandIKTask_Update)) is what puts a hand on a two-handed weapon's grip. Unless [`StateBitFlag::SB_DISABLE_WEAPON_OFFHAND_IK`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_DISABLE_WEAPON_OFFHAND_IK) is set or its segment is not playing on the base layer, it marks the hand with [`StateBitFlag::SB_WEAPON_OFFHAND_IK_LEFT`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_WEAPON_OFFHAND_IK_LEFT) or [`StateBitFlag::SB_WEAPON_OFFHAND_IK_RIGHT`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_WEAPON_OFFHAND_IK_RIGHT) and writes [`ObjectBlackboard::OFFHAND_IK_HAND_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::OFFHAND_IK_HAND_ID) and [`ObjectBlackboard::OFFHAND_IK_WEIGHT_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::OFFHAND_IK_WEIGHT_ID). [`Character::UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass), after the `MAIN` solve, reads them and moves that wrist so the hand's second attach bone lands on the weapon's grip point. The base layer's rifle variants also pose the off-hand on the foregrip in the clips themselves.

The parachute's hands are placed the same way. The parachute handle IK task ([`NParachuteHandleIKTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::NParachuteHandleIKTask##func-Update)) reads each handle link bone of the parachute's rig, offsets it by the character's hand-attach offset, and writes it to the wrist's `SECONDARY` position key ([`ObjectBlackboard::SECONDARY_HAND_POSITION_WS_LEFT_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::SECONDARY_HAND_POSITION_WS_LEFT_ID) and its right twin) with the blend weight in the wrist's weight key ([`ObjectBlackboard::SECONDARY_HAND_WEIGHT_LEFT_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::SECONDARY_HAND_WEIGHT_LEFT_ID) and its right twin), which [`Character::UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass) queues on the `SECONDARY` pass for that arm. Unlike the weapon and grapple keys, these are written with [`PERSISTENCE_SESSION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::PersistenceType##variant-PERSISTENCE_SESSION) rather than the per-frame persistence most blackboard writers use: they stay on the blackboard between ticks while the task runs, and [`NParachuteHandleIKTask::OnExit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::NParachuteHandleIKTask##func-OnExit) removes all four. The parachute object's own bone sync ([`NParachuteObjectControl_SyncBones`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::NParachuteObjectControl_SyncBones)) works the other way round for the rig's non-hand bones, moving the canopy's own bones onto the character, and additionally places the character's hands directly onto the handle-link bones outside segments that suppress it — a second, independent path to the same bones that does not go through the blackboard or either HumanIK pass. No parachute system queues a `MAIN` target on a wrist or gates the HumanIK solve.
