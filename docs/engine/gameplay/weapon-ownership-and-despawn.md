# Weapon ownership, despawn, and saves

What keeps a weapon object alive and in the world, how the spawn system decides to remove one, how a weapon moves into and out of an inventory slot without being dropped, and what a load and a save do to the player's weapons. The pickup, equip, and drop themselves are in [weapon-equip-and-drop.md](weapon-equip-and-drop.md), and a weapon lying in the world in [item-pickup.md](item-pickup.md).

## A weapon is kept by strong references

Every weapon is spawned by the spawn system, which keeps a strong reference to each object it spawned. An inventory slot ([`Inventory::m_Weapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_Weapons)) is another strong reference, and so is any reference something else takes by locking the weapon's weak reference to itself ([`WeaponBase::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeakThis)). The object is destroyed when its last strong reference goes.

The spawn system's per-frame despawn ranking ([`SpawnSystem::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-Update)) reads the strong count of each spawned object's control block. An object whose count is not one, the spawn system's own reference, is held by something else: it ranks zero and is never queued for despawn, nor chosen when its budget pool is over its instance limit. Any other object is ranked from its distance to the streaming centre and the view, whether it is dead, and its interest; past its pool's threshold it is queued for despawn, and the pool's highest-ranked object is queued when the pool is over its limit. The object's mission-critical flag protects it only while a mission or an encounter is active, and is cleared otherwise.

So a weapon in a slot is never despawned by the ranking, and a dropped weapon is despawned once its rank passes its pool's threshold or its pool is over budget: there is no despawn timer on the weapon. A weapon anything else holds a strong reference to is kept the same way as a slot keeps it, and released, it is ranked again from the next frame. Explicit despawns (area clean-ups, despawn volumes, mission clean-ups) and the formula of the rank are in the symbol dump only and unverified here.

## Into and out of a slot without a drop

[`Inventory::RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RemoveWeapon) takes a weapon out of its slot and releases the slot's reference, and nothing else: no drop, no despawn, and the weapon's state, user, and holder ([`WeaponBase::m_Holder`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Holder)) stay as they were. A weapon some other strong reference holds therefore stays holstered on its character, outside the inventory.

[`Inventory::AddWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-AddWeapon) puts a weapon into its slot. An occupant other than the weapon is dropped (or despawned when the slot's drop-old flag is clear) first, so a slot emptied with `RemoveWeapon` beforehand loses nothing. It then runs the weapon's pickup virtual ([`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup)) with the character as the holder and, with the equip modes ([`EquipMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::EquipMode)), unequips the wielded weapon and equips the new one.

`OnPickup` on its own takes a weapon into a character's keeping without any slot: it sets the holder, puts the weapon in the holstered state (its bodies out of the world), disables its pickup interaction and hint, and for the locally controlled character sets the avatar ready for combat and shows the weapon in its slot's arm of the weapon selection ([`HUDUI::OnPickupWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-OnPickupWeapon)). [`HUDUI::InitWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-InitWeapons) rebuilds the selection from the inventory's slots.

A weapon's placement ([`WeaponBase::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) places its guns on its holder's bones for its state, whoever calls it; the inventory's placement calls it for each slot's weapon. [`Inventory::UpdateWeaponVisibility`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponVisibility) shows or hides the slots' weapons with their character. Neither reaches a weapon outside the slots.

## The spawn reset and the respawn table

[`SpawnSystem::ResetSystem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-ResetSystem) sends `spawn.reset_all_spawn_rules` at once, destroys every pending spawn request, releases its references to every spawned object, and posts `spawn.post_reset_all_spawn_rules`, which its receivers handle later. An object something else still holds outlives the reset, but the spawn system no longer knows it.

Each inventory subscribes both ([`m_SpawnSystemPreReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemPreReset) and `m_SpawnSystemPostReset`) and handles them in [`Inventory::HandleEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HandleEvent), telling them apart with [`EventMatches`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/event##func-EventMatches):

- on the pre-reset event it refreshes the HUD's weapons and, for each slot without a respawn entry, records the occupant's type into the respawn table ([`Inventory::m_WeaponToSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawn)) with its magazine, and the selected slot;
- on the post-reset event it marks every occupant for removal and requests a spawn of every respawn entry ([`SpawnSystem::SpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-SpawnTagIndex)) at the character, whose callback hands each new weapon to the character's pickup, replacing the old one.

A weapon's type is its spawn tag index ([`GameObject::GetUniqueSpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##func-GetUniqueSpawnTagIndex)), and its persistent form is the tag's hash ([`SpawnSystem::GetTagHashFromTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-GetTagHashFromTagIndex), back with `GetTagIndexFromTagHash`, which gives `-1` for a hash no loaded tag has). So every reset replaces every inventory weapon with a new object of the same type. Which game states pass through a reset (a save load, the frontend, a death, a fast travel) is unverified.

## What a save stores

The player's part of a save ([`PlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/save::PlayerSaveData)) is stored by [`StorePlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-StorePlayerSaveData): each slot's weapon type as a tag hash (zero for an empty slot, and always zero for the heavy slot), every reserve, the grappling device's type, the player's upgrade name, two bavarium upgrade flags, and the downloadable content bits. No magazine, no selected slot, and nothing else per weapon is stored. Upgrades are the profile's, not the weapon's.

[`LoadPlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-LoadPlayerSaveData) reads them back, resets the respawn table, writes each known type's tag index into it with a full magazine, and sets each reserve. It spawns nothing: the post-reset event's handler spawns the table. Whether the post-reset event is handled after the load in every load path is unverified.

## Mission weapon caches

Missions that take the player's weapons away and give them back cache them in a graph script's weapon cache ([`NodeWeaponCache`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache)): its slot cache ([`CacheAllWeaponSlots`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-CacheAllWeaponSlots)) records each slot's type, reserve, and magazine and, when asked, forces the slots' weapons out. The give-back is inlined into several callers (the cache node's lookup, the mission manager's cached-weapon restore, and the challenges' restore), and the function the disassembler labels as the cache's restore is a schema hash of the player save data instead, so the give-back is not bound and has no single seam.
