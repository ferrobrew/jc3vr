# Head and body movement in VR

The player's tracked head owns where they look, and the turn input, the vehicle, or the flight path owns where the character faces. This page covers how head and body yaw relate, the transform chain from the HMD pose to the camera, the head's hiding from the player's own view, the flatscreen simulated headpose, and the upper-body IK that hangs Rico's torso from the head. The grounded body rig is in [rig.md](rig.md).

## Design

VR sickness comes mostly from vestibular disconnect: the eyes report a rotation the inner ear never felt. So the design question is how much of the character's facing (yaw) comes from the player's real head and body and how much from artificial input. Where the visible torso points is a separate question of presentation and IK, not comfort. Comfortable sustained head yaw is about 30 degrees and the practical maximum about 45; past about 30 the eyes turn rather than strain.

There are four yaw-coupling schemes:

- Decoupled head (A): the body is fixed and the head is free within a cone; the body's yaw comes from movement or input. Nothing rotates the body under the player without input.
- Body follows head (B): the body's yaw chases the head's through a deadzone. It drives the visible torso and the movement frame only, never the camera, since feeding it to the camera injects vection.
- Stick-driven body yaw (C), with the head decoupled on top. A snap turn produces no optic flow and so no vection; a smooth turn is more immersive and more nauseating.
- Physical turning (D): the body's yaw comes from real rotation. It is always honoured on top of the others.

JC3 is an intense open-world action game, and its realistic VR audience already has strong VR legs, so the defaults lean immersive: smooth turning and no vignette. Per mode:

- On foot: the head is decoupled and 1:1 (A), never clamped. The body's yaw is stick-driven (C), smooth by default with snap turning as an option. The body also turns under the view to face the player (B), never turning the view ([rig.md, "The yaw"](rig.md#the-yaw) and ["The body turning under the view"](rig.md#the-body-turning-under-the-view)). The locomotion-forward frame is the view's, decoupled from the torso.
- Vehicles: the body root is fixed, so body-yaw decoupling does not arise. Head-look is 1:1 and unclamped (breaking 1:1 is itself a discomfort source, and the seated pose limits travel). Recentering is a manual, bindable button with no automatic snap-back. Steering stays on the stick, so looking out of a side window never turns the vehicle. The horizon stays earth-stable, and the cockpit interior is a free rest frame.
- Wingsuit and parachute: the body's yaw is locked to the flight direction, since the steering is the body banking, and the head stays decoupled and 1:1. The camera rolls with the wingsuit's bank, the most immersive choice, even though rolling the camera with a bank is a known sickness source.

The closest mechanical analogue is Stress Level Zero's body in Boneworks and Bonelab, and the lesson taken from it is the control topology rather than the physics ragdoll: the head is ground truth, the body hangs from the head, and the body's facing is derived only from the head and the hands, never auto-aligned to the travel direction. The mod implements that topology as kinematic IK on JC3's authored skeleton: the authored body hangs under the head through a neck and spine offset, roomscale translation feeds the character root and the collision capsule so that physically walking, leaning, and ducking move the in-game body, and a vehicle seat locks the body to the vehicle while the head stays free. The character's head bone is driven hard toward the player's real head, so it tracks 1:1. The turn input never bleeds into steering.

Four constraints follow from the topology:

- No synthetic neck model on 6DoF. The HMD's tracked translation already contains the player's real neck pivot, so adding a neck model double-counts the motion.
- No smoothing on the path from the HMD to the camera. Any lag between the rendered viewpoint and real head motion is nauseating. The body's yaw can be smoothed; the head path stays direct.
- The game's head animation (idle sway, look-at, cutscene turns, recoil) fights the HMD, so the HMD owns the head bone's orientation and roomscale position and the animation applies only below the neck.
- Roomscale head translation cannot be hard-collided, because freezing the view while the real head moves is itself nauseating. The mod holds the view at a wall the body cannot pass and fades a head that goes into geometry ([rig.md, "Walls"](rig.md#walls)).

## HMD pose to camera

The transform chain applies the root outermost, so roomscale translation is interpreted in the body's local frame:

    world_camera_eye = body_root · neck/head_attach_offset · hmd_head_pose · (±IPD/2 along head-local X)
    view_eye         = inverse(world_camera_eye)      (paired with that eye's asymmetric projection)

The body's yaw comes from the stick or the vehicle or flight root, while the head's pitch, roll, and residual yaw come only from the HMD. On foot the body also turns to face the rig's yaw, with the view frame turned back by the same amount, so that turn never reaches the camera. The head's pitch and roll never feed back into body rotation: tilting the head must not roll the character. `eye_to_head` is a pure half-IPD translation along head-local X with no rotation; each eye still has its own asymmetric projection.

In the driver's seat of a car, one more offset sits between the head attach point and the HMD pose: the driving view's per-vehicle correction, which moves the view so the eyes sit at a standard place behind the steering wheel ([steering-wheel.md, "The driving position"](../input/steering-wheel.md#the-driving-position)). It is a constant in the body frame for the length of a seat entry, eased in after the driver settles and out through the exit, and added after the posture and height remap, so head motion still passes through one to one. The hands and the head bone's target are composed from the same head pose, so they move with it, and Rico's head leans by the correction as it would for a player leaning the same distance.

### Absolute and relative placement

The chain above is absolute placement: in gameplay ([`GameState::E_GAME_RUN`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameState##variant-E_GAME_RUN)) the mod owns the camera position outright, deriving it from Rico's head anchor plus the offsets. Outside gameplay it must not: pinning the sim-facing camera (the `GameCameraManager` control contexts and `GetCameraMatrix`) and driving Rico's IK fights the engine's own reset and reposition during a fast-travel teleport, and the load never completes.

During a load the engine parks the active camera at the teleport destination and streams the world from it. On entering the load substate (`CGameStateLoad`), it calls `CGameCameraManager::ResetCamera`, which stamps a character or spawn transform into the active camera, then `SetActive(0)`, which stops the camera tree from updating (`UpdateRenderContexts` is gated on `m_Active`), while `PushRenderContext` keeps re-stamping that parked transform every frame. The load completes on streaming, spawn, the landscape, and the teleport flag, not on any camera convergence. There is no 3D scene: the loading screen is Scaleform UI, shown on the floating panel (`current_mode()` returns `Movie` outside `E_GAME_RUN`).

So outside `E_GAME_RUN` the mod leaves the sim camera alone and head-tracks only the render copy:

- `Camera::UpdateRender` writes nothing: no `m_TransformT0` or `m_TransformT1` write, no render-context patch, and no `GetCameraMatrix` override, and the character and IK hooks stop driving Rico. Writing the parked camera, with Rico's head bone or any frozen pose, feeds the streaming system the wrong position and hangs the load.
- The mod holds a frozen upright loading base: the last gameplay viewpoint's position and yaw (`hooks::camera::LOADING_BASE`), captured every gameplay frame and held through the transition, with pitch and roll dropped.
- The `SetupRenderCamera` hook runs on the per-frame render copy ([`GraphicsEngine::m_RenderCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::graphics_engine::GraphicsEngine##field-m_RenderCamera)), not the sim camera the streaming system reads. It overwrites `m_TransformF` and `m_View` with the cockpit-frame HMD pose (`headpose::xr::cockpit_pose`) composed onto the frozen base, before the engine builds the view-projections, and the per-eye IPD and canting apply on top. Only what is displayed changes, so the player looks around a stable viewpoint while the teleport completes. On flatscreen there is no cockpit pose, and the render copy is left as the engine made it.
- The loading panel latches on the first load frame to where the player was looking and holds that world spot. The panel's world-lock and the render camera's head-track share one predicate, `hooks::camera::vr_loading_view_active` (outside gameplay, the VR source, a base captured), so they never disagree on when the loading presentation is active.

On resume, absolute placement snaps back onto Rico's head through the freshly captured anchor (the anchor capture keeps running through the load), the panel reverts to following the head, and the automatic recenter re-neutralizes once the entry pose settles, as on resuming from the menu.

### The camera write

During gameplay, with the camera override enabled, an active head pose, and a valid anchor, `camera_update_render` writes the previous and current head transforms, rotation and translation, to [`Camera::m_TransformT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::camera::Camera##field-m_TransformT0) and [`Camera::m_TransformT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::camera::Camera##field-m_TransformT1) before calling the original. `Camera::UpdateRender` itself lerps T0 to T1 by `dtf` into [`Camera::m_TransformF`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::camera::Camera##field-m_TransformF) and derives `m_View` from it, so a post-call write does nothing for the current frame and is overwritten by the next frame's `PushRenderContext` and `InitTransform`. T0 gets the previous tick's pose and T1 the current one, so the engine's own lerp smooths the tick-rate headpose across rendered frames. Writing at this point also bypasses `PushRenderContext`'s rotation jitter filter (`s_use_jitter_filter`), which snaps sub-epsilon rotation deltas and quantizes head motion into visible steps. `CameraTree::UpdateRenderContexts` patches the control contexts with the same pose pair; the next render context also feeds `GameCameraManager::GetInputMatrix`, so the movement direction follows the headpose. When the tracked head or the anchor is unavailable, the bone fallback updates the translation only, and with the override disabled nothing is placed. The engine's vehicle camera route is in [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md#28-vehicle-camera-path).

## Hiding the head

The camera sits at the player's head, so the head mesh (the face, eyes, hair, and any gear weighted to facial bones) is hidden from the player's own view. It is hidden from the colour passes only: the shadow passes see the real skinning data, so Rico's shadow keeps its head. The implementation is in `crates/jc3vr-render/src/hooks/graphics_engine/scene/render_block.rs`, with the inputs the character hook publishes for it in the view crate's `hooks::graphics_engine::player_draw`; the render-block classes, their `Draw`, `DrawZ`, and `SetMatrixPalette` entry points, and the batch and palette structures are in the pyxis defs (`rendering/render_block.pyxis`).

Three engine facts shape the design:

- The same render blocks are drawn for every pass; each `Draw` branches on the static and dynamic shadow-map bits of [`RenderContext::m_RenderStatus`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::graphics_engine::RenderContext##field-m_RenderStatus) to take the depth-only path. There is no separate shadow render block for characters.
- The skinning palette is built once per frame (`CCharacter::UpdateRender` calling [`PoseProducer::MakeSkinningPalette`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::pose_producer::PoseProducer##func-MakeSkinningPalette)) and shared across all passes, so nothing bone- or palette-side can differ between the shadow and colour passes. Rebuilding the palette between the shadow commit ([`ShadowManager::CommitRenderPassSettings`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::shadow_manager::ShadowManager##func-CommitRenderPassSettings)) and the GBuffer draw is unsafe against the sim thread's pose access; spoofing the palette upload per draw avoids it.
- Rico's skin material packs the face, arms, and hands into one render block, and even one skin batch, so neither block-level nor batch-level draw skipping can remove the head without the hands. The hide must be per bone.

The `Draw` and `DrawZ` hooks of `RenderBlockCharacter` and `RenderBlockCharacterSkin` raise a thread-local flag while one of the player's blocks draws in a non-shadow pass, and the `SetMatrixPalette` hooks then substitute a thread-local copy of the bone matrices with the head and facial bones collapsed: a zero rotation with the translation set to the neck's model-space position, so every weighted vertex contracts to a point inside the collar. The real palette is never modified. The details:

- Ownership is by instance-info pointer. Every block draw receives its owning model instance's embedded `CRBIInfo` as the `info` argument (`CModelInstance::AddToPass` passes the info embedded at [`AnimatedModel::MODEL_INSTANCE_RBI_INFO_OFFSET`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel::MODEL_INSTANCE_RBI_INFO_OFFSET)). The character hook publishes the info pointers of the player's [`AnimatedModel::m_ModelInstances`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##field-m_ModelInstances) slot for slot, and the draw hooks compare `info` against the first slot (the body model) for an exact identity test. A position-radius test false-matches nearby NPCs and loses the player at wingsuit speed, and matching any slot crumples the parachute canopy, which lives in another slot with its own skeleton and clashing bone indices.
- The collapse set is published by the character hook from the live skeleton: the head plus the named facial bones (`offset_facialOrienter`, `fJaw`, the lips, nose, lids, brows, eyes, and ears) through `GetBoneIndex(hashlittle(name))`. The root and implausible indices are filtered, so a failed lookup can never collapse the whole body.
- The spoof is thread-local because character draws run concurrently on several render worker threads, and a shared flag leaks the spoof into other characters' draws. `SetMatrixPalette` reads `matrices[BatchToSkeletonLookup[i]]` per batch slot, so the copy spans the batch's highest referenced skeleton index and rewrites only the collapse set.
- A batch that is mostly facial bones is face gear (eyewear and the like), sometimes also rigged to a limb bone and blend-anchored on the root, and collapsing only its facial bones leaves the rest dangling mid-body. When at least half of a batch's non-root bones are in the collapse set, every bone the batch references collapses, the root included, which is safe because the upload is per batch. Mixed batches (the body skin) keep the selective collapse, so the hands stay.
- The collapse target comes from the skeleton: the character hook publishes the neck joint's model-space position right after `CalculateModelSpacePose`. Where a matrix's translation lives in the palette depends on the block's layout, so a position sampled from the palette can be a pseudo-point. One shared neck target also tucks the vertices blended between collapsed and kept bones into the collar instead of off the chin.
- The palette layout differs per block: some store four 3-float columns with the translation in the last column, others three 4-float rows with the translation in each row's last element. Writing through the wrong layout puts the target into the rotation and crushes geometry toward the ground. The hook scores the 3x3 under both readings for orthonormality across several referenced bones and collapses through whichever layout wins; a zero rotation maps every weighted vertex exactly to the translation under either convention.

Reflections and other non-shadow passes inherit the hide, so Rico is headless in mirrors.

## The headpose

The headpose has two layers:

- `crates/jc3vr-view/src/headpose/mod.rs` is the pose's single source of truth. It holds the current `HeadPose { position, orientation }`, the previous tick's pose (for the engine's T0 and T1 interpolation), and the head-bone anchor. The active source writes it through `set_pose()`, and consumers (the head bone, the camera, the HUD) read it through `query()` and `query_prev()`. It has no latch logic and no mode detection.
- The sources: the VR source (`headpose::xr`) and the flatscreen simulation (`headpose::sim`), which holds the mouse-look accumulation, the latch state machine, the mode detection, the body-yaw target, and the per-frame `update()` that publishes a `HeadPose`.

Beside the world-space pose, `headpose` exposes the head's body-relative rotation (`body_relative_rotation()`): how far the head is turned from the body's own facing, with the body frame divided out. Both sources compose their published orientation as `body × … × this`, so it is the factor each contributes (the sim's accumulated look angles, or the HMD's orientation relative to the recenter baseline), and the accessor picks the right one per source. Anything that poses the head bone reads this rather than the world orientation: the bone lives in model space, where the body frame is already the identity, and neutral is exactly the identity however the head bone's rest orientation sits relative to the model axes. The sim's raw angles are not a substitute, because under the VR source `sim::on_input_tick` returns before writing them.

### The flatscreen latch

The latch is a sim concern: in VR the head comes from the HMD and the body's yaw from the stick and the rig's yaw follow. In flatscreen the latch unifies them: within a cone the head moves freely, and past the threshold the body's yaw tracks the head's direction. The sim's yaw is body-relative (composed with the live body yaw at publish time): on foot the offset is compensated as the body turns, so the head stays world-anchored while the body catches up, and in vehicles the offset rides the body frame, giving cockpit-relative free-look. The sim recenters by zeroing its body-relative angles, and the VR source re-bases its own cockpit ([rig.md, "Recentering"](rig.md#recentering)).

Input capture uses the game's own look effectors: a post-call hook on `InputDeviceManager::Update` reads and consumes the `LOOK_LEFT`, `RIGHT`, `UP`, and `DOWN` effector values and feeds them into the sim, so the game's camera never sees them. The poll runs on the engine's fixed-rate sim tick, not per rendered frame, so look deltas arrive in tick-sized bursts. The whole sim step runs inside the hook, so the published pose pair rotates on the engine's own tick timeline, in phase with its `dtf` reset; a step deferred to the next frame would leave the render lerping a stale pair on every tick frame.

Mode detection uses the on-foot evaluator's liveness (`ModeLiveness` in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/body_frame.rs`: the time the `EvaluateCharacterOrientation` detours last ran for the local player, marked before any config gating). It advances every on-foot sim tick including idle, unlike the move and aim task counters, which stop while idle and would clamp the head to the free-look cone whenever the player stood still. The sim compares the mark once per input tick, since the mark moves only on sim ticks and a per-frame comparison would read "not on foot" on every frame without a tick. On foot with the headpose active, the face-camera idle pin is suppressed, since the camera's forward is the head's and the pin would defeat the decoupled cone; the latch's body-yaw target takes priority over the pin.

The published orientation composes the body-relative offset onto the body's full world rotation, not just its yaw, so the body's pitch and roll carry the head with them and a banking wingsuit rolls the view. Authored animations that invert the body in the bone pose (hanging, ledge grabs) over a gravity-aligned root leave the view upright. Under the flatscreen source the view also stays level through the grapple's upside-down states (`S_GRAPPLE_PRE_UPSIDEDOWN`, `S_GRAPPLE_ATTACH_UPSIDE_DOWN`, and the `S_IDLE_HANG_STUNT` hang).

The free-look yaw limit is the view tuning's `flatscreen_look.free_look_yaw_limit_deg`, made plausible by driving the neck bone: body-relative yaw beyond the character tuning's `neck.twist_start_deg` twists the neck along with the head, up to `neck.twist_max_deg`. The twist pre-multiplies the animated model-space neck orientation with a model-space Y rotation (body-relative yaw is a model-space Y rotation), so no rest-frame knowledge is needed and the neck's translation is untouched.

### The head bone and the eye point

The head-bone override runs in the post-call block of the `Character::UpdatePropEffects` hook (`crates/jc3vr-body/src/hooks/character/`). That call is the last step of pose finalization, so a `SetJoint` there reaches both the camera and the skinning job ([skeleton.md, "Frame order, and where to override"](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#frame-order-and-where-to-override)). The override rotates the head bone by the head's body-relative rotation composed onto the animated model-space orientation, since an absolute orientation write would assume the bone's rest frame matches the model axes, which it does not. It keeps the animated translation plus the roomscale offset.

The headpose anchor is captured earlier, in the pre-IK `UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel` hook ([Body IK via HumanIK](#body-ik-via-humanik)), from the freshly animated head joint. The anchor must come from the animated joint before the override: the bone matrices hold last frame's override, and reading them closes a feedback loop that freezes the position at a fixed world point. On foot, the anchor is Rico's root carrying a measured neutral head rather than the animated head, so the clips' bob and lurch never reach the view ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)); the animated head is the anchor everywhere else.

The camera hangs its eye point from the head anchor through the animated neck bone: the published head position, plus the head-to-neck delta (published by the character hook beside the anchor), plus a neck-to-eyes arm measured from the animated eye bones in the body frame. Under the sim the arm is rotated by the head's orientation, a synthetic neck, so pitching the head swings the eyes forward over the chest. Under VR it is rotated by the body's rotation only, as at a neutral head, so the eye point moves exactly as the tracked pose does; swinging the arm as well would apply the neck twice and drop the view into Rico's chest when looking down. With the headpose active, the whole camera placement comes from the tick-spaced pose pair rather than from per-frame bone reads, because `GetSafeBoneMatrix` and `GetJoint` carry only the finalized sim-rate pose and would step at the tick rate while the mesh interpolates.

### The aim direction

The game splits its camera getters by phase ([aim-pipeline.md, "The camera getters"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md)). `GetCameraMatrix` reads a sim-phase context that, once the render-phase patch has consumed the look input, is frozen at its injection-time direction. The mod hooks `GetCameraMatrix` post-call and overwrites the output with the render camera's headpose transform, so every sim-side aim consumer follows the HMD gaze.

That override would let the game turn the body toward the head natively, through `NStateTask_LocoUtil::GetAimMoveAngle` and the aim-relative `track_face_dir` blackboard ([aim-pipeline.md, "Body-turn reactions to the aim direction"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md)). The mod hooks `GetAimMoveAngle` and returns zero for the local player while the head is decoupled on foot, idle, and not aiming; in every other case the original runs. In the same state the `force_face_camera` shim forces `track_face_dir` off, since the executor's continuous yaw would otherwise turn the body with the head (`crates/jc3vr-body/src/hooks/input/locomotion.rs`).

## Grapple comfort

The grapple rotates and teleports the character violently: the fire act's alignment, the reel's rotation toward the target, and the landing snap. The filter for it is in [grapple-comfort.md](grapple-comfort.md): a body-frame hold from fire to landing, a yaw hand-off that turns the character to the view at landings, and a landing-snap absorber for the head anchor. Only body-driven components are filtered; the HMD's tracking path stays direct.

## Body IK via HumanIK

The body hangs from the head through the engine's own HumanIK solver, so the spine and neck bend toward where the player looks the same way the game's aim IK bends them. The solver's layout, lifecycle, effector ids, and coordinate spaces are in [humanik.md](../../engine/character/humanik.md). The code is `crates/jc3vr-body/src/hooks/character/body_ik/`.

### The head target

A pre-call hook on `Character::UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel` queues a head effector position target, and a rotation target, on the `MAIN` pass at the `SPINE_HEAD_ONLY` solve step, before the engine's `HasTargets` gate, so one head target pulls the upper body toward the headpose that frame. The solve step decides which joints the whole pass may articulate: a head target that admits the arms lets the solver meet it by swinging them, which is cheaper than bending the spine, so `SPINE_HEAD_ONLY` keeps the head target to the spine and neck. The torso targets below go at `UPPER_BODY` (the pass's step is the maximum over queued targets), so the arms re-enter the solve exactly on the frames where the hand pins hold them.

The target is the head bone's place under the headpose, in character-model space: the eye point less the HMD's rotation of the arm from the neck pivot to the eyes (`body::procedural::head::head_bone_world`), the same point the procedural torso hangs the neck from. A look down turns the eyes about the neck, so aiming the head bone at the eye point would drag it down and forward along the eyes' arc, below the shoulders' line at steep angles. At the pivot the head bone holds its height, the override turns it to the HMD's orientation, and the camera stays at the eye point. The torso lean below measures the same point, so a look down is not read as a lean. Each target writes only its own effector's slots, so the subsystem coexists with the game's own targets on the same pass. The engine's global HIK enable and the per-character reduced-LOD gates are respected as the engine checks them, so nothing is queued in a frame the solver would not run.

The anchor capture is in the same pre-IK hook, so the anchor reads the pre-solve pose and never chases the target the solver pulls it toward. The head-bone override still runs at the end of the pass, on top of the solved spine, and composes its body-relative offset onto a pre-IK orientation snapshot, so it does not double-count the yaw the solver already applied. HumanIK owns the spine and neck bend; the override owns the final head orientation.

HumanIK also places the head, and the override does not move it again. The head position target and the override's roomscale add aim at the same place, so running both displaces the head twice, which reads in Rico's shadow as the head detaching from the shoulders. The positional add stands down whenever a head target is queued, and `head_reach_t` is full so the solve lands the head where the player's head is.

### The torso

A head target alone cannot move the shoulders, for two reasons. The engine splits arriving at the target from carrying that into the rest of the body across two control values ([humanik.md, "The four control values"](../../engine/character/humanik.md)), so the hook writes `m_TargetPull` beside the position target and `m_TargetResist` beside the rotation target. But pull acts only on the positional target, and the head's positional target differs from the animated head bone only by the roomscale displacement: turning the head, the dominant motion, gives pull nothing to propagate.

So the torso is driven directly, as one rotation about a pivot at the base of the spine (`SafeBoneIndex::SPINE`). A lean is an articulation of the spine, not a displacement of the shoulder girdle: offsetting each shoulder by a share of the head's movement telescopes the spine on a duck and can never rotate the girdle, which is most of what leaning does. The rotation has two components:

- The lean: the swing about the pivot carrying the animated head to where the player's head is, scaled by `lean_share`. The pivot is a blend between the base of the spine and the hips (`pivot_drop`), because a sideways lean bends about the spine's base while a forward lean is mostly a hip hinge; dropping the pivot resolves the same head displacement to a smaller rotation about a longer arm, so the shoulders move further. The hips joint is found through the solver's node table ([`Effector::HIPS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-HIPS)), and the pivot falls back to the spine if the rig has no hips node. A rotation cannot express the head changing its distance from the pivot; the neck and the head effector's own reach take that residual.
- The yaw: `yaw_share` of the head's body-relative yaw about the pivot's vertical axis, taken as the heading (`body_rig::math::angle::heading`) of the body-relative rotation rather than from the animated head bone, whose rest orientation would bias the twist at neutral. While the rig is grounded the chest's yaw is the rig's chest heading against Rico's facing ([rig.md, "The yaw"](rig.md#the-yaw)) instead, so the torso twists through what the legs have not turned. In a vehicle the body cannot turn, so the torso carries the full head-to-body angle.

That rotation is applied through two channels, which by construction cannot disagree:

- Shoulder positions. Each shoulder effector ([`Effector::LEFT_SHOULDER`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-LEFT_SHOULDER), [`Effector::RIGHT_SHOULDER`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-RIGHT_SHOULDER)) is targeted at its animated position carried through the rotation, with its own reach and pull. The bone each effector drives is found by scanning the solver's `m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs`, not through a `SafeBoneIndex`: the table is keyed by the bone whose name matches the HumanIK node's name, a different naming scheme from the game's safe bones. They coincide for the head but not for the shoulders, where the safe-bone lookup returns no effector and the targets would silently never queue; the debug readout reports mapped and driven counts separately for that reason. Constraining the girdle keeps the camera coupled to the torso, since pull alone lets the solver reach a moved head by bending the neck or swinging the arms, which in a vehicle takes the hands off the wheel.
- Chest orientation. The same rotation is queued on the chest-end effector (`GetChestEndEffectorId`) as an axis-vector target with `m_TargetReachR`, the mechanism the reel-in aim IK uses to swing the chest toward the grapple hook. The lean and the yaw go as one rotation, because a second target for the same effector updates the first.

A small chest turn with no physical lean can round the rotation to the identity, and then nothing is queued; the readout shows the torso lean and yaw near zero in that case.

The construction is expressed in character-model space, which is body-attached, so the pivot-to-head vector is the body's own up axis whether Rico is standing, seated, or prone. The yaw term is likewise body-frame: the VR source's cockpit orientation is defined as `body⁻¹ × world` (what [`compose`] undoes), so its Y-Euler is rotation about the body's up. A banking wingsuit does not confuse it. The mode detection is binary (on foot and everything else), so the wingsuit and the parachute share the vehicle's shares.

[`compose`]: ../../../crates/jc3vr-view/src/headpose/xr.rs

### The game's own targets

Nothing is written over a target the game already placed. The game's IK systems queue during animation-graph evaluation, before this hook runs, so their `MAIN`-pass targets are already in the pass's lists. A second target for the same effector updates the first, so a wrist target on a frame when `NRightArmAimIK` has aimed that wrist would replace the aim and the gun would stop pointing where the player aims. The shoulders, hands, and chest therefore stand down whenever the game has spoken for that effector (aiming a weapon, gripping, swinging toward a grapple hook). The head is the exception: the HMD owns it.

While aiming, the whole torso drive stands down. The weapon-aim IK queues a single rotation target on one arm effector (`NRightArmAimIK::UpdateAimEffector`, at `UPPER_BODY` on `MAIN`) and relies on the rest of the chain being free: in VR the body does not turn toward the aim, so everything past the authored aim sweep comes from the solver swinging the arms, and the pins on the other wrist and the torso targets would stop the aim about 30 degrees off the body's facing. So while [`Character::m_AimFlags`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_AimFlags) reports weapon or grapple aiming, only the head target queues.

Spoken for means a valid entry. `ClearTargets` keeps a consumed entry with its `is_valid` flag cleared until its reach decays to zero, and that leftover keeps steering its effector with its stale pose ([humanik.md, "Target entry lifetime"](../../engine/character/humanik.md)). A deferral that matched any entry would read its own last-frame pin as the game's claim and skip itself, leaving the hands to a decaying ghost. Only a set `is_valid` marks the game's live claim, since the game re-queues its active targets every frame. For the same reason the subsystem queues its targets with `blend_out` off: the game's targets are world-anchored and stay meaningful while fading, but these are animation-anchored and wrong a frame later.

### The hand pins

Moving the shoulders drags the arms, because nothing else holds the hands: a grip on a steering wheel is animation, not a constraint the solver knows. Each wrist effector ([`Effector::LEFT_WRIST`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-LEFT_WRIST), [`Effector::RIGHT_WRIST`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-RIGHT_WRIST), resolved through the solver's node table like the shoulders) is targeted at its own animated position, so the arms absorb the shoulders' movement and the hands stay. Their pull is zero, so a pinned hand cannot drag the torso back. The pins apply only on frames where the girdle is driven; on every other frame the head target's `SPINE_HEAD_ONLY` step keeps the arms out of the solve.

A position alone leaves the solver free to reach it from any orientation, so the hands would spin in place, and the weapon with them, since it rides a hand attach bone. Each pinned wrist also gets a rotation target of zero angle: a queued rotation is an offset applied to the effector's current state, sampled from the pre-solve pose, so a zero offset targets the animated orientation. Queuing it also puts the wrist on the pass's rotation list, the only list that drives resist, so `hand_resist` is available if the hands still drift under a strong lean.

### Limits

The construction assumes the head stays above the pivot and within the spine's cone, as it does for a seated or standing player leaning and turning. It is torso-only: the pivot does not travel, and the lower body is untouched. As the head nears the pivot's height, the swing approaches a half turn about an axis that is barely determined, and `lean_max_deg` bounds it so the pose saturates instead of flipping.

A deep crouch is hip and knee flexion, not a spine bend, and it moves the pivot itself. The mod leaves it to the game: standing, the crouch capsule follows the head's drop that no bend explains ([rig.md, "Posture"](rig.md#posture)). On foot and in the cases the procedural torso takes, the procedural torso poses the spine from the player on the animation's hips ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md), [procedural-states.md](procedural-states.md)), and the torso targets here stand down once it has its full weight. This construction poses the torso wherever the procedural torso does not.

### Diagnostics and tuning

The debug UI's Body/tracking > Animation and IK status, and a once-per-second debug log line (enabled by its log checkbox or a `body_ik=debug` filter directive), show the mode and animation state id, the resolved head and chest effector ids, the positional target delta available to pull, the roomscale offset, the torso lean and yaw, the shoulders mapped against driven, whether the chest was driven, the hands pinned, the aim deferral, and the engine-side `m_ReachT`, `m_ReachR`, and `m_Pull` for the head as the solver holds them after the previous frame. It also names the gate when nothing is queued.

The body IK has no settings. Its tuning is the `body_ik` table of `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/character.toml` ([tuning.md](../tuning.md)): the head's reach, pull, and resist; the torso lean and yaw shares, the pivot drop, and the lean cap; the shoulder reach and pull; the pinned hands' reach and resist; the chest rotation reach; a master weight; and a model-space target offset. The pull, resist, and chest values are starting points rather than validated values; the open risks are listed in [humanik.md](../../engine/character/humanik.md).

## Suppressing idle motion

The authored animation plays Rico's idle motion while he stands still, which moves the torso independently of the player's tracked pose. The periodic fidget is held off; the breathing in the base idle clip is left alone.

The fidget is the periodic weight shift and look-around. On foot the character cycles between the rule states `S_IDLE` and `S_IDLE_ONE_OFF`. `NStateTask_InputLocoIdleTask::Update` counts down [`Character::m_IdleFidgetTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_IdleFidgetTimer), which resets to a random interval when the aim reference moves, and queues `ACT_TO_IDLE_ONE_OFF` when it elapses. The idle-task hook holds the countdown positive for the local player (`crates/jc3vr-body/src/hooks/input/locomotion.rs`). Holding the timer preserves the task's later walk and start-act checks, which swallowing the queued act with the timer expired would skip. The hold is always on.

The breathing is baked into the base `S_IDLE` clip and plays as authored, because holding the clip's time would freeze the pose, a bigger visual change than the breathing. The additive layer cannot suppress it: `CMiscAdditiveState` is inactive during on-foot `S_IDLE`, `COverrideAdditiveAnimLayerCondition::Evaluate` always returns false, and `S_IDLE` does not use `CIdleState`.
