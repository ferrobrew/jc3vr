# Stick locomotion and the steady view

On foot the game moves Rico by his clips: the movement task's speed is the animation's root speed ([engine: the locomotion task](../../engine/character/root-motion-and-proxy.md#the-locomotion-task-runs-at-idle-and-writes-the-proxy-input-directly)), so a start ramps up through a wind-up clip, a stop decays through a stop clip, a turning start swings the body, and a run surges with every step. A view hung from Rico's animated head carries all of that, plus the walk's bob and the idle's sway, into the headset. Neither matches what the player's own body does, and both are discomforting.

The stick locomotion takes both away from the animation. The code is in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/locomotion/`, and its tuning is `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/locomotion.toml` ([tuning.md](../tuning.md)). Its one switch, `controllers.locomotion.stick.enabled` (on), is the pause menu's `VR: COMFORT` page's `SMOOTH STICK MOVEMENT`; the act filter and the steady view have no switches. The debug UI's On foot feature, under Movement/vehicles, shows their status in its `Stick velocity`, `Act filter`, `Act watchdog`, `Steady view`, and `Neutral head` rows ([status.md](../status.md)).

## The velocity

`locomotion::after_task` runs from the post-call detour on [`NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask##func-Update) (`rig::root`), on the ticks where the task wrote the proxy's velocity. It is the first of the on-foot contributions to the wanted velocity, before the palms' push and the body rig's step drive ([rig.md](rig.md#who-moves-the-root)) add theirs ([physics-substrate.md](physics-substrate.md#the-wanted-velocity)). It replaces the task's horizontal velocity with its own through [`Character::SetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetWantedWorldVelocity), and keeps the task's vertical component.

- The direction is the move stick, read from the player action map's move half-axes, turned into the world by the view's heading on the ground plane. The bindings already rotate the stick into the off hand's frame when `controllers.locomotion.direction` asks for it, so the view is the frame either way. Rico's facing never steers the movement: the body follows the body rig's yaw ([rig.md](rig.md#the-yaw)).
- The target speed is the game's own: the float the input tasks write at [`ObjectBlackboard::SPEED_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::SPEED_ID), the walk, run, or backpedal speed for the stick's deflection, and zero where the game would stop Rico at a drop or a wall ([`SetupTargetSpeeds`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_InputLocoSetTargetSpeedsTask##func-SetupTargetSpeeds)). Distances and chases therefore stay balanced, and the ledge stop keeps working.
- The velocity moves straight toward the target at a fixed rate (`accel::step`): by at most the acceleration times the step while speeding up, and by the deceleration while slowing or turning away (`stick.rates`). A start, a stop, and a change of direction all take a time proportional to the change and never overshoot, so the view feels only a steady push.
- After a gap in the task (a jump, a fall, a vehicle exit), the integrator starts from the proxy's real velocity, so a landing carries its speed. A velocity faster than the stick's target coasts down at the deceleration, but never faster than the proxy's own horizontal speed (`accel::coast_limited`): what slows the body (the ground, a slope, a wall) slows the coast too, so the end of a fast reel on the ground does not leave the integrator holding the reel's speed for the first step off the ground.
- While the animation carries the body, the task's velocity passes through untouched: an authored motion (the state bits the procedural body's hand-over table reads for it: a locomotion one-off, a hard landing, a looping context action, a full-body pickup, and cover) and a hit reaction's hold ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)). Their root motion is the motion, and a stumble flattened to the stick's velocity would go nowhere. The integrator then starts from the proxy's velocity again.
- Nothing infers a ground sit or suppresses the stick for one: low head poses keep ordinary stick locomotion.

The legs play the game's clips at the clips' own speed and cadence, so they slide a little against the ground when the two speeds differ.

The task's velocity, the target, the written velocity, and the proxy's velocity are logged once a second while moving (`locomotion: stick velocity`, debug level on the `controllers` target). The game's speeds are measured once each: for every target speed the stick holds long enough, the clips' root speed is averaged after `speed_log.settle_ms` over `speed_log.measure_ms` and logged at info level (`locomotion: the game's on-foot speed`, with `target_mps` and `root_mps`).

## The turning animations

With the stick driving the velocity, the animations that turn the body are left with nothing to do but swing it. Most of them write the angle correction that turns the root, and so the view. The act filter (`locomotion::acts`, one of the act arbiter's owners: [act-arbiter.md](act-arbiter.md)) handles the local player's acts while the headset drives the view and the player is on foot on the ground:

- the rotate-on-spot acts and their warp flavors, the standing rotation starts, the quarter and half turns, and the heavy weapon's aim-focus rotations ([`QueueAimRelativeOnSpotActions`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueAimRelativeOnSpotActions)) are dropped. The body still turns: the orientation executor yaws the root toward the body rig's yaw. These acts are let through while the rig is grounded and Rico stands still, so a body turn plays the game's own turn-in-place ([rig.md, "The yaw"](rig.md#the-yaw)); the watchdog does not count them.
- the turning run and walk starts are replaced by the straight start, the turning stops by the straight stop (warp and non-warp alike), and the turning flicks by the straight flick: the running flicks a start plays when a stop is called for inside its flick segment ([`QueueFlickStick`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueFlickStick)), and the aiming flicks ([`QueueAimFlickStick`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueAimFlickStick)). The state machine still passes through its start, stop, and flick states. Each replacement goes through the game's act pre-flight ([`AnimatedModel::TryAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-TryAct)) first, and the original act is queued when the state machine would not take the straight one.

Standing in a vehicle's stunt area, the stick turns the body toward it when the state machine would not take a stunt transition that way ([`QueueStuntTurns`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueStuntTurns)), and the view rides the body there. The filter replaces all three stunt turns, the straight one too, since its angle correction still turns the root, with `ACT_TO_IDLE`: the act the same function queues in their place when the stick points nearly straight ahead, so the state the turn was queued from moves on as it would with no turn to make. The replacement goes through [`AnimatedModel::TryAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-TryAct) like the others, but a refusal drops the stunt turn rather than queuing it. The game queues them only while it applies its stunt modification to the character ([`Character::ShouldApplyStuntModification`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-ShouldApplyStuntModification)), which is being attached to a vehicle, where the mode detection may read a vehicle rather than on foot. So the stunt turns have their own scope: the headset drives the view, gameplay owns the character, the controllers are on, and the game's own test holds when the act is queued, called on the same thread just after the input task called it. They need neither the on-foot mode nor `stick.enabled`, since the stick locomotion does not run on a roof. With nothing turning it, the body faces where it stood, and the stick still steps it to the next stunt area where a transition goes. The input tasks queue a stunt turn every tick the stick is held toward a side with no stunt area, so the stunt scope is kept out of the watchdog below: since its replacement is the game's own act for no turn, nothing is left waiting for a release to unstick, and a release would let a turn through.

The mortar's rotate-on-spot states are left alone: the game's code registers no mortar turning act, only the mortar's aim and fire acts, and what drives those states in the shipped state machine is not established.

The act names are bound in the pyxis defs ([`EventIdSymbolTable`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable)); they are the `NCharacter` ids the game registers at startup, so resolving them never adds a name to the table.

A filter that removes acts can leave the animation state machine waiting for one it never gets. The watchdog guards against that on foot: the filter normally acts in short bursts, at a start or a stop, so when it has acted with no gap longer than `acts.burst_gap_ms` for `acts.watchdog_s`, it lets every act through for `acts.release_s` (the locomotion tuning's) and logs `locomotion: the act filter acted without a break; letting every act through` at warning level. The readout counts the dropped, straightened, and refused acts and the releases; a refused stunt turn counts as refused and is dropped.

## The steady view

`locomotion::anchor::stabilize` runs at the anchor capture at the entry of the pose-finalization pass (`hooks::character::body_ik`), once per sim tick, and decides what the view hangs from. There are two anchors:

- The root-fixed anchor: Rico's root ([`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1), and `T0` for the previous tick of the pose pair) carrying a neutral standing head, neck, and eye midpoint in model space. The head and the neck ride the root only at their neutral heights, straight above it: their small horizontal offset would swing around the root whenever the body turns under the view (the yaw follow, [rig.md](rig.md#the-body-turning-under-the-view)) and carry the view sideways, and the recenter takes up the constant offset instead. No clip moves this anchor, so the view moves only with the root, at the stick's velocity, and with the player's own head.
- The animated head.

Outside the headset the mode decides: on foot, and in a jump through the grace period (`anchor.jump_grace_s`), the view holds the root; everywhere else (a vehicle, the parachute, the wingsuit, a grapple traversal, swimming, a ragdoll, and a fall past the grace) it keeps the animated head.

In the headset the hand-over table's case decides instead (`Case::view_holds_root`). The anchor reads the character the way the procedural body's hand-over table does ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table), with a tracker of its own), and the view holds the root through everything that happens to Rico on foot: walking with the stick, a jump, climbing with the hands, a fall past the jump's grace and its landing, a stumble or a knock-back, an authored one-off (a hard landing, a context action, a full-body pickup, cover), the movement task stopping on the ground, a ragdoll and the get-up after it, and a death. The player stands on their own feet through all of these, so the view follows their real head and never a clip's, which would otherwise swing the view on a drop, dip it in a landing's crouch, and tumble it through a ragdoll. Where the clip carries the body away from under the view (a get-up from the ground, a hard landing's crouch), the player sees Rico's body below or in front of them rather than riding with it.

The view keeps the animated head in the cases where the player rides something the game moves: vehicles and their stunt positions, the grapple's traversals and hangs and its ledge climb, swimming, the wingsuit (going into it from the ground included, [`SB_IN_TRANSITION_TO_WINGSUIT`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IN_TRANSITION_TO_WINGSUIT)), the parachute, and a scripted sequence.

### Following the head through a ragdoll

A ragdoll, its get-up, and a death (`Case::view_follows_head`) carry Rico's body far from the root for seconds and leave it somewhere else, so through them the held view follows his animated head instead of standing over the root (`locomotion::view_follow`). The view stays upright and keeps the neck model's eyes; only its position follows. The follower eases the head's offset from the root, critically damped over `follow.smoothing_s` and never moving the offset faster than `follow.max_speed_mps`, and adds the root back each tick: a ragdoll's first fall about the body eases in and a get-up's rise is followed, while the body's own motion (a ragdoll thrown off a car, a fall from height) carries the view with it uncapped.

A landing, a fall, a stumble, an authored one-off, or the movement task stopping (`Case::view_may_escape`) escapes to the follower too when it carries the head more than `follow.escape_m` from the root horizontally, or more than `follow.escape_drop_m` below it (`view_follow::escape_distance`, which scales the drop onto the horizontal distance), for longer than `follow.escape_s`, as a fast fall's landing that pitches Rico forward does. It returns once the head is within `follow.escape_return_m` again. A landing's dip is shorter and shallower, so a jump off a roof never moves the view. A handover into the root-held view from a case that held it on the animated head (the wingsuit, a vehicle) with the head already past the escape's distance starts escaped (`Escape::seed`): the view stays on Rico's head while he rises, as after a crash out of the wingsuit, and returns once the head is over the root, rather than the handover's blend carrying the view through his body. A ragdoll, a get-up, or a death that ends with the head still past that distance is held on the head the same way. Switching between the two never jumps the view: the follower starts where the view is, and on the way back the difference winds down over `follow.return_smoothing_s`.

### The floor and the upright frame

Wherever the view hangs, its head never stands lower than the character tuning's `body_ik.view_floor_clearance_m` over the ground under Rico (`headpose::anchors::Geometry::floor`, from the height over the ground, water excluded, that his body rule system writes), so a wingsuit crash's recovery, which slides the animated head prone along the ground, and a death's clip, which lowers it under the root, cannot put the camera under the road. The exception is the cases that hold the body where its root does not stand on the ground below it (`Case::head_may_lie`: a seat, a hang from the hands or a line, a climb, and a swim): the rule system zeroes the reading while the proxy is supported, which puts the floor at the root, and a head that rightly hangs below the root there would be pushed up.

The root-held head stands straight up in the world from the root's position, not along the root's own up, and the view's frame turns toward the root's upright heading by the anchor's weight (`headpose::anchors::Geometry::view_frame`, with the eye arm's frame likewise in `arm_frame`), so a ragdoll's root, which tilts with the physics body, never tilts the view. On foot the root is upright and both are unchanged.

### The neutral pose and the handover

The neutral pose is measured, not assumed: while Rico stands still on the ground, not aiming, the animated head, neck, and eyes are averaged over `anchor.capture_s` of standing, once per session (`locomotion: measured Rico's neutral standing head (model space)`, info level). Until then the view keeps the animated head. The On foot feature's `Measure the neutral head again` button measures it again.

The anchor hands over between the two over `anchor.blend_s`, eased, so leaving the ground or entering a vehicle never jumps the view. The anchor counts the movement task as running while it ran within the freshness tuning's `locomotion.movement_task_grace_s`, so a few ticks' jitter in its stamp does not hand the view over and back. The task's stamp is written only while the headset drives the view, so outside the headset the mode alone decides. Its read also publishes whether Rico is off the ground on foot, in a jump or past its grace before the mode detection reports a fall (`AnchorStatus::airborne`), which the wingsuit gestures watch from ([wingsuit-gestures.md](../input/wingsuit-gestures.md#opening-and-closing)).

### The camera's anchor in the headset

In the headset the anchor moves only the camera. `stabilize` returns the camera's head, neck delta, and eye arm separately from the anatomy's, and the geometry carries both (`headpose::anchors::Geometry::view_head` picks the camera's while it holds). The headset's pose is composed from the camera's head, both in the display frame's pose pair (`vr::frame`) and when a new tick's anchors carry the retained pose over, and `headpose::anchor` reports the same head, so a consumer that subtracts it from the pose has the player's tracked offset. The skeleton's animated head and neck stay as the clip posed them. Outside the headset both use the blended anchors.

The root-held camera hangs its eyes from the neutral head by the tracked neck model rather than by the neutral pose's own neck and eyes (`locomotion::view::neck_model_anchors`): the neck pivot is the head bone itself, and the eyes sit `body_rig::body::EYES_TO_NECK` from it, reversed, in the frame the camera turns the eye arm with. The rig and the body read the player's neck pivot as the eyes plus that same offset turned by the head, so with the player's head at the recenter's orientation the pivot is the neutral head, and Rico's head bone, which the body aims at the camera's pivot ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-torso-on-the-animations-hips)), stands where his neutral head does with no bend. The neutral pose's own eye arm is the idle clip's, which holds the head pitched down and turned a little and stands the neck behind the head bone, so a camera hung from it would sit lower and further forward than a level head carries its eyes, and the head bone would stand off the camera's pivot. The offset is constant against the root, so the view still follows the headset one to one.

### The face clearance

On the animated head the camera's eyes are the clip's eye bones, so they pitch and recline with the clip's head, while the view itself stays the headset's, composed upright onto the root: a clip that folds Rico forward or lays him back never pitches the view. The eye bones sit just inside the skull, though, and a clip that folds the chest toward the face (a parachute's opening, a hard landing's crouch) or lays the head back over it (the grapple's wall attach) leaves the chest and the harness past the near plane, filling the lower view.

So while a clip, a script, or physics poses the body out of a seat (`Case::game_poses_body`: a traversal or a hang, a ledge climb, the wingsuit, the parachute, a fall, a ragdoll and its get-up, a hit reaction, an authored motion, a takeoff not classified yet, or standing on a vehicle's roof, where the stunt clips crouch, step down, and bring the arms across the face), the eyes are held `anchor.face_clearance_m` out in front of the animated face, along the face's forward: the direction from the neck to the eyes with the skull's own axis taken out, so it turns with the clip's head. The clearance eases in and out over `anchor.blend_s`. On foot, in a jump, climbing with the hands, in a vehicle, and swimming the eyes are unchanged. In the headset the eye arm is rebuilt after the anchor capture in the filtered head frame (the root turned by the head's filtered animation rotation, `headpose::anchors::Geometry::animation_rotation`), so `stabilize` returns the clearance as a model-space offset (`Stabilized::face_offset`), and the capture turns it into that frame and adds it to the rebuilt arm; outside the headset it rides the anatomy's anchors. Since the headset holds the view over the root in a fall, a hit reaction, an authored motion, and a takeoff, and follows the head's position with the view upright through a ragdoll, its get-up, and a death, the clearance shows in the headset in the rest: a traversal or a hang, a ledge climb, the wingsuit, the parachute, and standing on a vehicle's roof.

### The height

While the proxy stands on the ground that tick (not [`Character::IsInAir`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInAir)), the root's height is smoothed by a critically damped follower (`height.smoothing_s`), so the steps of a staircase, which the proxy climbs in small pops, read as a ramp. Leaving the ground winds whatever the smoothing still hides down to nothing over `height.release_s`, so a jump's rise reaches the view at once and without a jump of its own. The smoothed height trails by at most `height.max_lag_m`, and a change beyond `height.snap_m` (a drop, a teleport) passes at once. The readout shows the current offset.

Because the camera's anchor does not contain the animation, everything that composes onto it does not either: the roomscale cockpit, the head-height remap, and the body IK's head target all measure the player's head against the root-fixed head.

## What needs confirming in the headset

- Standing still after the measurement, the view does not move when the anchor switches on (the handover log line, and `Steady view` reading `Engaged`).
- Walking, jogging, and sprinting with the stick: no bob in the view, no surge, constant-rate starts and stops. In the debug log, `commanded` follows `target` at the configured rate and `proxy` follows `commanded`; a proxy velocity that lags well behind means the proxy's own smoothing is adding to the ramp.
- With the act filter on, the state machine still enters and leaves movement, starts, stops, and flicks look straight, and the watchdog's warning does not appear in normal play. Reversing the stick during a start, and aiming well to the side with a heavy weapon while standing, do not swing the view.
- On a car's roof, holding the stick toward a side with no stunt area does not turn the body or the view for as long as it is held, and the stick still steps the player onto a neighbouring area. Rico does not get stuck mid-stop or mid-step after the stick is released; the readout's refused count shows how often the state machine would not take the return to the idle.
- A hit stumble carries Rico as the game authored it, and a ledge stop stops him. A melee lunge that is not an authored motion comes through the movement task as root motion and is flattened to the stick's velocity; if it stops working, turn `SMOOTH STICK MOVEMENT` off and report it.
- A jump from a run carries its speed, and landing seeds the velocity from the proxy again.
- A yank-boosted grapple reel onto the ground ends at running speed or below within a second or so, and the first jump or drop after it does not throw Rico forward at reel speed.
- Stairs and slopes feel smooth, and a long drop does not lag the view.
- The parachute's opening and the grapple's wall attach: the view stays in front of Rico's face, upright, and his chest and harness show only when looking down.
- Dropping from a grapple hang, a fall's landing, a hard landing, a crash into a ragdoll, and the get-up: the view stays upright at standing height over Rico's root, or follows his head through the ragdoll and the get-up, and moves only with the root and the player's own head. Check how the get-up and a hard landing's crouch look from above, and whether any of them leaves the view inside a wall or the ground (the roomscale fade should catch it).
