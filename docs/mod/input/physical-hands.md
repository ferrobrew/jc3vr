# Physical hands

Each empty hand has a palm that collides with the world. With the hand bodies on (the default), each empty hand is a body in the game's physics world, driven toward its controller within the hand's strength: it stops at walls and is drawn there, it shoves and topples what it meets, it strikes characters, who stagger or fall and, from a hard enough blow, take damage, and pressing it into what does not give pushes the player away. With them off, the palm is probed with ray casts instead, as the rest of this doc describes from [the probes](#probes) on. With the probes, pressing a palm into a wall pushes the body away from it and holds the rendered hand at the surface; a palm passes through characters; a palm pressed into a loose object shoves it, slowly or hard, and a heavy one resists; and, with the probes or the bodies, a grip press near a loose object takes hold of it where the hand touched it, or near a weapon lying in the world, collects the weapon. The code is `payload/src/controllers/physical_hands/` (the hand bodies in its `physics/`); the engine side is in [havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md), [melee-and-damage.md](../../engine/gameplay/melee-and-damage.md), and [item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md).

## Hand bodies

A hand body is a capsule along the hand's fingers, centred on the palm box's centre (`bodies.radius_m` and `bodies.half_length_m`, scaled by the VR world scale), of `bodies.mass_kg`. It is made the way the grappling hook's hang proxy makes its free capsule ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#making-a-free-dynamic-body)): a capsule shape, a dynamic body left out of the simulation, and the engine's wrapper ([`PfxRigidBody`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody)) around it, whose owner stays null. The wrapper turns the body's gravity off, so the drive does not fight it, and the body's motion never sleeps.

### What a hand touches

The body is on the weapons layer ([`WEAPONS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-WEAPONS)) in the player's own collision system group, read from the player's character proxy each step. In the player's group with a zero subsystem id, it never touches the player's capsule, nor the other hand. On the weapons layer, it meets the static world, terrain, props, destruction debris, character capsules, ragdolls, and vehicles, and the aim's ray query passes through it. A player whose proxy is in system group zero gets no hand bodies, since they would collide with the player.

### The drive

The pre-simulation callback ([`PfxPreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback)) drives the bodies before each physics step, on the game thread and outside the step. Each step, for each acting hand:

1. The goal is where the palm will be at the end of the step: the palm pose the player aim's post-simulation update last published, carried forward by the palm's smoothed velocity over the time since it was published plus the step ([`PhysicsSystem::STEP_SECONDS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem)).
2. The body is asked to reach the goal in one step: the linear velocity that closes `bodies.linear_gain` of the position error, capped at `bodies.max_speed_mps`, which at a gain of one reaches the goal without overshooting or ringing (the discrete critically damped drive). A hand the last step held back is driven more softly: its gain falls with the share of its strength it pressed with, to `bodies.contact_gain` of it at full press, so it rests on a wall rather than pounding it every step. The body is also asked to turn at the angular velocity that closes `bodies.angular_gain` of the rotation error, capped at `bodies.max_angular_speed_rps`.
3. The change of velocity that asks for it is capped by the hand's strength over the step: the pull at `bodies.max_force_n` through the body's mass, and the twist at `bodies.max_torque_nm` through its world inertia, each times `bodies.strength`. The result is set as the body's velocities ([`hknpWorld::setBodyVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-setBodyVelocity)).

A snap turn or a recenter (a turn of the body's facing of more than a fifth of a radian between two palms' ticks, or a change of the recenter count) moves every palm at once without the hands moving. The palms' velocities start afresh, and the next step puts each acting body where it keeps its place and turn against its palm, at rest, rather than driving it there through whatever is around; the body ignores its contacts for a fifth of a second after, so the turn strikes nothing. A gripping hand's body stays on the wall.

The step then integrates the velocities and resolves the body's contacts. A free hand reaches its goal; a hand against a wall stops at it, and the next step asks again, no harder than its strength; a hand against a crate pushes it, and a crate too heavy for the hand's strength holds it back. The math is in `physics/drive.rs` with its tests.

The default strength is "Rico strength": a three-kilogram hand pulling at up to five kilonewtons and twisting at up to four hundred newton metres, exaggerated but bounded so that a tracking glitch cannot fling what the hand touches. The strength is the caps, not the mass: the solver shares a contact's impulse by mass, so a light hand meets a light prop without throwing it or making it jitter against the world, and still shoves a heavy one within its strength over a few steps. A body that falls more than `bodies.break_distance_m` behind its controller's palm (measured against the palm now, without the goal's lead, or against a grip's pin) is put back on its goal at rest, but only when a ray from the body to the goal, through the aim's filter, meets nothing: a hand pulled through a wall or a character keeps pressing at the surface rather than passing through it, and is put back once the controller comes back to a clear path. A body put somewhere rather than moved there (made, added, or put back) ignores its contacts for a few steps, so that a body that starts inside something does not strike it, and a body held in place ignores them while it is held.

### Drawing the hand at its body

Each step also measures where the last step left the body against where the controller's palm was predicted for the end of that step: the controller's, not the goal the body was sent to, so a hand whose body a grip pins to a wall is drawn on the wall. The offset is a shift of the palm's centre and a turn about it, so a wrist twisted into a wall is drawn turned as its body is rather than through the wall; a turn within a few hundredths of a radian is not drawn, so a free hand keeps the controller's rotation. With `bodies.render_at_body`, the hand's cockpit poses are moved by that offset ([`apply`](../../../payload/src/controllers/physical_hands/mod.rs)), so the IK targets and the late update put the rendered hand where its body is: on the wall, not through it. The offset is updated at the simulation rate and added to the latest controller pose at the render rate, so a free hand, whose offset is near zero, keeps the controller's latency.

### Pushing the player

A hand that something holds back falls behind its goal. With `bodies.push`, the lag past `bodies.push_deadband_m` pulls on the hand like a spring of `bodies.push_stiffness_n_per_m`, independent of the hand body's mass, up to the hand's strength; the reaction, over the step, moves `bodies.body_mass_kg` of player the other way. The push is added to the on-foot locomotion task's wanted velocity, as the probes' push is ([pushing off surfaces](#pushing-off-surfaces)), smoothed over `bodies.push_smoothing_s` and capped at `bodies.max_push_speed_mps` for both hands together. A lag that is mostly vertical (`bodies.min_push_horizontal`), pressing down on a table or the floor, does not push. A hand that moves what it meets, or tracks freely, has no lag and pushes nothing, and as the player moves away the goal moves with them, so the push settles with the hand resting on the surface.

A held-back hand vibrates (`Sustain::BodyPressure`, [haptics.md](haptics.md)) in proportion to its lag past the deadband, full at `bodies.pressure_full_lag_m`.

### Pushing up

The press down that the push leaves alone pushes the body up instead, a jump assist for vaulting onto a ledge or a low wall (`physical_hands/vault/`). With `vault.enabled` (on by default, `PUSH UP WITH THE HANDS` on the pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page), a hand that neither grips nor holds anything and whose lag is at least `vault.min_vertical` downward lifts the player at `vault.stiffness_per_s` per metre of press past `vault.deadband_m`, up to `vault.max_lift_mps`; with both hands pressing, the stronger lift wins. The lift is set in the pre-simulation step, after the characters' proxy updates, as the climb sets its velocity ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md#climbing)): the proxy keeps its own horizontal velocity and rises at least at the lift. Lifting the player lifts the rig, and with it the controller, so the press shallows as the player rises and settles with the hands resting on the surface: the player is held up as long as the hands press, and rises no more than the press is deep, and never more than `vault.max_rise_m` above where the push started. While a pressing hand reaches the static world, the proxy is written as supported on it, as a climbing hand's surface is ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md#keeping-the-game-on-foot)), so the game keeps the player on foot; otherwise the game may judge the player in the air, as after a jump. The stick then carries the player forward over the top. While a hand climbs, nothing pushes up.

The log has, at info level, `physical hands: the hands push the body up` with the feet's height and the lift when a push starts, and `the push up ended` with the rise. The readout and the `VR: STATUS` page's `PUSH UP` row, under `HANDS IN THE WORLD`, count the pushes and show the lift and the last rise; the recorder's signals are `push_up.pushes` and `push_up.lifting`, and the template is `testing/scenarios/push_up.toml`.

### Hits

With `bodies.hits.enabled`, each hand body reports the contacts it starts. The engine's wrapper of each body is given a copy of the wrapper's virtual table whose contact-impulse slot is the mod's, and its contact events are switched on ([`PfxRigidBody::SetContactImpulseEventActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-SetContactImpulseEventActive)); every other slot, and the run-time type information in the slot before the table, is the engine's own ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#contact-impulse-events)). A contact's started event carries the impulse the solver applied over the step it began, the momentum the hand gave up to what it met, and names the other body and its owner. The event arrives inside the physics step, so it is only queued there (`physics/contact.rs`); the next frame tick, on the game thread ahead of the game's update, acts on it (`physics/blows.rs`):

- The hand plays `Event::HandStrike` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)) in proportion to how fast it met what it touched, as the momentum of an arm of `bodies.hits.blow_mass_kg` at the hand's speed, full at `bodies.hits.full_pulse_impulse_ns`: a tap on a table is faint, a punch into a wall hard.
- With `bodies.hits.reactions`, a contact with a character other than the player is a blow when the hand swung into it: the hand's speed toward the character's chest before the contact, from the velocity it was driven at for the step, must reach `bodies.hits.min_approach_speed_mps`, so a hand resting on a character, pushing it slowly, or met by a character walking into it strikes nothing. The blow's impulse is the momentum of an arm of `bodies.hits.blow_mass_kg` at that speed, the hand's own share of the closing speed (the character's own speed toward the hand, from [`Character::m_MeasuredWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_MeasuredWorldVelocity), adds nothing), not the solver's contact impulse, which grows with the hand body's own mass; a blow under `bodies.hits.min_impulse_ns` is none. The character is found again from the contact's body, so one freed since the step is never touched. It reacts through its own hit reaction ([`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction), [melee-and-damage.md](../../engine/gameplay/melee-and-damage.md#the-hit-reaction)) as to a melee blow along the hand's travel. The blow is calibrated against the character's own knockdown threshold: a blow of `bodies.hits.knockdown_impulse_ns` reaches it and knocks the character down, carrying the blow's momentum into its ragdoll, and a weaker one gives the same share of it, so the character's own stagger threshold decides whether it staggers or only flinches. The defaults put a swing of a few metres per second into a soldier around a stagger and a hard one, past seven, at the knockdown.
- Only a character the player's faction is hostile to ([`GetRelation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/faction##func-GetRelation) from the player's faction to the character's, [`E_RELATION_HOSTILE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::target::FactionRelation##variant-E_RELATION_HOSTILE)) is knocked down or damaged: an ally, an escort, or a bystander is staggered at most and never hurt, so a careless swing cannot kill a mission's own characters. Which relation the faction table gives civilians and escorted characters is unverified in the headset; the readout and the log say whether each blow was hostile.
- A hand whose blow reached half a knockdown follows through: its drive keeps `bodies.hits.follow_through_gain` of its gain for `bodies.hits.follow_through_s`, so it carries on rather than stopping dead on the character.
- With `bodies.hits.damage`, a blow of at least `bodies.hits.damage_impulse_ns` deals damage too: the player's melee damage ([`Player::m_MeleeDamage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##field-m_MeleeDamage), so the hammer upgrades count) times `bodies.hits.damage_scale` times the blow's share of the knockdown impulse, at least one point. Such a blow is a melee damage message to the character, which runs the same hit reaction from the message's impulse, so it reacts once.

A hand that struck a character ignores its further contacts with that character for `bodies.hits.cooldown_s`, so one swing through a body, whose contact may start and end several times, is one blow, and a character a blow damaged takes no further damage from either hand for `bodies.hits.damage_cooldown_s`. Only the started event of a contact is used, and a hand resting on a character does not swing into it, so it strikes nothing. Hits on props, vehicles, and the world move them physically and pulse the hand, and deal nothing more.

There is no gesture: the melee gesture and the palms' strikes are gone, and a blow is only ever a hand body's contact.

### Lifecycle

A hand acts on foot, on the ground or falling (so that a hand flung or jumped at a ledge can catch it), for a tracked hand the controllers drive that holds nothing and is not carrying a loose object with the grip (a hand that holds one through a joint acts). Its body is made on the first step it acts and added to the simulation at its goal. A tracked hand that takes a gun (or the foregrip, a grenade, or a charge) or starts carrying what it grabbed has its body taken out of the simulation (parked) at once, so that it does not stand frozen in the world as an obstacle. A hand that stops acting for another reason (a lost controller, a vehicle, a swim, the parachute, or a flicker of the mode) has its body held in place for a quarter of a second, ignoring its contacts so it strikes nothing, then parked until it acts again. Both bodies are destroyed when the game leaves gameplay (a load, the main menu), when the hand bodies are switched off, when the player's collision system group changes (a new character after a death), when the hand's size or mass setting changes, and when the payload unloads. A body whose slot no longer holds it, when it is read or when it is destroyed, is forgotten without touching the engine; since the forgotten wrapper may still point at the mod's copy of the virtual table, forgetting one that listened for its contacts pins the payload's image for the rest of the game's run. A body that cannot be made returns the wrapper's memory to the engine allocator ([`MemoryFree`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/memory##func-MemoryFree)).

A body made with `bodies.hits.enabled` listens for its contacts from its creation; switching the hits on or off remakes the bodies. The callback is registered once, from the game thread's frame tick ahead of the game's update, so never while the engine walks its callbacks, and removed by the shutdown cleanup, which also destroys the bodies, before the payload unloads. The bodies keep no Havok-space positions: every step converts game space with the world's current shift, so a world recentring needs nothing of them.

A hand its body drives does not probe, push off surfaces through the probes, shove, or knock; it still reaches for what its grip would take, casting its reach rays from where its body was before the last step rather than from the controller, so that a hand held at a wall reaches what its body touches and not what lies beyond the wall, and grabbing a loose object or a weapon works as described below. A hand that carries what it holds has no body, so it never fights the object; one that holds it through a joint ([holding through a joint](#holding-through-a-joint)) keeps its body.

## Probes

With the hand bodies off, the palms are probed with ray casts. The casts reuse the player aim's filtered ray query ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay) through the aim's filter, as in [the aim raycast](../../../payload/src/controllers/aim/raycast.rs)), which never hits the player and already names the hit object and body. The game's own melee uses a box overlap query ([`PhysicsSystem::CollideBox`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CollideBox)) for objects; it is bound but not used, because its all-hits collector owns Havok heap memory the mod would have to manage.

## The palm

The palm is a box in the wrist frame, sized from the player's hand (`palm_half_extents_m`, about 9 by 4 by 10 cm) and centred toward the fingers (`palm_offset_m`), scaled by the VR world scale. It is probed from six points: its centre, the middle of the palm's face, and the four corners of the face toward the fingers, which lead a reach and a push.

A palm acts on foot, on the ground or falling, for a tracked hand the controllers drive (its tier weight is above zero) that holds nothing: no gun, not the foregrip, no grenade or charge being thrown. The hands are taken before the two-handed pose, recoil, and the anchored grapple hand are applied, so the probes see where the player's hand is.

## Contacts

Each tick, from the player aim control's post-simulation update, each probe point is cast from where it last was in the open to where it is now, as the head probe does. A hit on world geometry or an object means the point crossed the surface: it is pressed in by its depth behind the surface along the normal, and its open position stays put until the hand comes back out. A cast from the shoulder forgives a stale open position, so that a hand carried around an obstacle counts as clear again, and a teleport forgets the open positions. A palm passes through a character: characters are never pressed against, and a probe point that crosses into one counts as in the open. Hitting characters is left to a later, physically simulated hand; the game's own melee is not started either ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Melee"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#melee)).

The hit object's kind comes from the engine's run-time type test ([`GameObject::IsType`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##vfunc-IsType) with [`Character::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-TYPE_ID)) on the hit's physics game object.

## Pushing off surfaces

A palm pressed past a small deadband (`push_deadband_m`) into a surface whose normal is mostly horizontal (`min_push_horizontal`) asks for a push along the normal, at `push_stiffness_per_s` per metre of press, up to `max_push_speed_mps` for both hands together. Pressing down on a table or the floor does not push. As the body moves away the press shallows, so the push settles with the palm resting on the surface.

The push is added to the on-foot locomotion task's wanted velocity from the roomscale simulation half, before the chase reads the task's velocity, so it counts as the task's own movement: the view moves with the body as it does for the stick, and the cockpit shift does not take it back out. Only the on-foot task's ticks carry it, so a palm in a vehicle, a swim, or a fall never pushes.

With `clamp_hand`, the hand's cockpit poses are moved out of the surface by the deepest press, which the IK targets and the rendered hand follow. The probes use the unclamped hands, so the press, and with it the push, still measures where the real hand is.

## Shoving objects

A palm pressed into a dynamic body shoves it every tick it presses, however slowly the hand moves, so a crate slides, a barrel rolls, and a tall thing on a narrow base tips. The contact is soft, at the velocity level: the body's point under the palm is asked to move into the body at least as fast as the hand moves into it, plus `shove.stiffness_per_s` per metre of press past `shove.deadband_m`, up to `shove.max_speed_mps`. The impulse that asks for it is the point's mass along the surface normal times the speed it lacks, computed from the body's mass, inertia, and velocities ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world)), so a push off the centre turns the body as well as moving it. It is applied at the contact point ([`hknpWorld::applyBodyImpulseAt`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-applyBodyImpulseAt)) and capped at `shove.max_force_n` over the tick, the hand's strength, so a car takes a push slowly. The palm drags the surface it slides along by friction, up to `shove.friction` times the push.

How much a body gives is measured rather than assumed: each tick compares the speed the point reached with the speed the last tick asked of it, and the share it did not give, or that the strength cap held back, is the resistance, smoothed over `shove.give_smoothing_s`. The push off a surface ([pushing off surfaces](#pushing-off-surfaces)) is scaled by the resistance, so a light crate in the open slides away without pushing the player, while a crate against a wall, a parked car, or a body joined to the world pushes the player away as a wall does. World geometry and bodies that are not dynamic resist fully. The rendered hand is held at the body's surface either way, so it stops at what does not move and follows what does.

A palm meeting an object at `min_object_speed_mps` or faster also knocks it on the first tick of contact, with a share (`object_impulse`) of the player's melee impulse ([`Player::m_MeleeImpulse`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##field-m_MeleeImpulse)) scaled by the hit's strength, which reaches one at `full_hit_speed_mps`, on top of the shove: a hit throws a prop harder than a push of the same speed. The engine leaves static and keyframed bodies alone.

## Picking things up

An acting palm that holds nothing looks, every tick, for what its grip would take: five short rays from the back of the hand through the palm, out of the palm, along the fingers, between the two, and out of either edge, reaching `pickup.reach_m` past the palm, and the surface the palm presses into. The nearest hit that a hand takes is the hand's target, shown with `pickup.show_targets` as a ring in the world-space overlay: blue for an object one hand lifts, red for one it does not, gold for a weapon, teal for an articulated object ([articulated objects](#articulated-objects)), green for a vehicle's door ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)), violet for a character ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md)). A hand takes two kinds of thing, told apart by the engine's run-time type test:

- a weapon lying loose in the world ([`WeaponBase::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-TYPE_ID), in the [`E_WS_DROPPED`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponState##variant-E_WS_DROPPED) state);
- a rigid object ([`RigidObject::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObject##func-TYPE_ID)) whose body is dynamic and of finite mass: a crate, a barrel, a gas canister, debris.

Ragdolls and vehicles are not targets of this kind, nor the body the player's proxy stands on ([`PfxCharacterInstance::m_GroundBodyId`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxCharacterInstance##field-m_GroundBodyId)), which a hand would lift from under the player. Collectibles have no body, so the rays never find them, and they are left to the game's use input. The same rays find characters ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md)) and a vehicle's doors ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)), each through its own reach.

### The grip

The target reaches the grip as a grab volume, the pickup volume ([`GrabVolume::Pickup`](../../../payload/src/controllers/bindings/grab.rs)), reported for a hand with a target that holds nothing. Every placed volume wins over it: a holster, a pouch, the foregrip, a vehicle control, and a parachute handle are where the hand goes on purpose, and a crate beside the hip must not block a draw. It wins in turn over the climb's volume, which a hand its body drives reports for the static world in its reach ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md)). As with every grab volume, the grip press is latched at its edge, so it neither grapples nor reaches the game for the whole press.

A grab of a weapon takes that weapon straight into the grabbing hand, with its magazine and no pickup animation, with `weapons.hold.direct_pickup` on ([physical-weapons.md, "Picking a weapon up into the hand"](physical-weapons.md#picking-a-weapon-up-into-the-hand)). With it off, or for a weapon the direct pickup refuses, the grab clicks the game's use-item action ([`USE_ITEM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-USE_ITEM)), the action the Y button already carries, so the game picks a weapon up its own way: its pickup animation, its inventory rules, its HUD, and its choice among the weapons in range (see [item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#picking-up-a-weapon)). Either way the hand's body does not hold the weapon; the game equips it. Ammunition and grenades need no grab, since the game collects them as the player walks over them. `pickup.weapons` turns the weapon grab off.

A grab of an object asks the next simulation tick to take hold of it, and releasing the grip asks it to let go.

### Holding

Unless a joint holds it ([holding through a joint](#holding-through-a-joint)), a held object stays dynamic and is carried: driven, not attached. A hand holds it at the point it touched: at the grab, the point is recorded in the body's frame and in the palm's, and the body's orientation in the palm's frame. Each tick the palm gives where that point should be and how the body should be turned, and the hold is solved as a pair of soft constraints between the palm and the body, the way a physics engine solves its joints ([`hold`](../../../payload/src/controllers/physical_hands/hold.rs)):

- the grab point is asked to move with the palm (the palm's velocity at that point, from its linear and angular velocity) plus `pickup.stiffness_per_s` per metre it is off, up to `pickup.max_speed_mps`;
- the body is asked to turn with the palm plus `pickup.angular_stiffness_per_s` per radian it is off, up to `pickup.max_angular_speed_rps`.

The impulses that give those velocities are found together over a few passes: the pull at the grab point through the point's mass matrix, and the twist through the body's inertia, each accumulated and capped by the hand's strength over the tick, `pickup.hand_force_n` and `pickup.hand_torque_nm`. The solve starts from where gravity (`pickup.gravity_mps2`) will have the body after the next step, so the pull includes holding it up. The result is set as the body's velocities ([`hknpWorld::setBodyVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-setBodyVelocity)) before the step, which integrates them, resolves the body's contacts, and resolves any joint it hangs from.

What falls out of the strength caps:

- one hand lifts what weighs less than its pull (about 35 kg by default), two hands twice that; a heavier object sinks from the hand and can be dragged along the ground slowly;
- a long object held at one end droops and swings from the hand when its weight's lever outweighs the wrist's twist, and turning the wrist swings its far end like a lever;
- a heavy object comes up to speed slowly, since the pull's cap limits its acceleration.

A hand takes hold of any loose object up to `pickup.max_mass_kg`; the reach ring is red when the hands could not lift it. Holding the rendered hand at the grab point (with `clamp_hand`), the hand stays on the object wherever the object is: it stops at a crate the hand cannot move rather than passing through it. A hand that pulls at near its strength without moving the object (`pickup.resist`) pushes the player toward the grab point, as a wall does, so pushing a held object that will not move pushes the player back, and walking away from one holds the player back. An object that lags more than `pickup.break_distance_m` behind the hand, caught on something, is let go.

### Holding through a joint

With `bodies.joint_grabs` (on by default, though unverified in the headset; see the list below), a hand its body drives holds what its grip takes through a joint instead: a fixed constraint between the hand's body and the object's ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#joining-bodies-with-a-fixed-constraint)), made by the next pre-simulation step in the pose the two bodies are in, so the object stays where the hand took it. The hand's body keeps acting: the drive pulls the hand, and with it the object, toward the controller within a holding hand's strength, the same budget as a carried hold (`pickup.hand_force_n` and `pickup.hand_torque_nm`, no stronger than the hand's own), so one hand lifts about what it carries and two hands twice that, and with both hands on the object each wrist's twist is weakened to `pickup.two_hand_angular_share`. The hand's goal is moved by the keep-out ([the player's own body](#the-players-own-body)) as a carried object's target is, so a joined object is not driven into the player either. A held object has mass, a heavy one lags and sags and the hand is drawn where its body is, and pressing a held object into a wall pushes the player as a hand pressed into the wall does. Two hands holding one object hold it through two joints, and the hands' pulls add. Letting go ends the joint, and the object leaves with the velocity the hand gave it; there is no separate throw.

The step owns the joint (`physics/joint.rs`): the aim update publishes what each grip holds through a joint, and the step makes the joint, keeps it while the object still owns its body and the world's constraint slot still holds the joint's constraint, and ends it when the grip lets go, the hand's body stops acting or is put back on its hand, or the hands leave gameplay. Its data comes from the game thread's Havok heap; the world's constraint holds the only reference once it is made, so ending the joint frees it. A joint is destroyed only after its slot is checked to still hold it. A joint that ends against the grip's wish, or cannot be made, drops the hold, as a lost hold.

### Two hands

Both hands may hold one object, each at its own point. Each hand pulls its own grab point, so a long object hangs between the hands and tilts as one hand rises; the two pulls add, so together they lift twice what one does. The wrists' twists are weakened to `pickup.two_hand_angular_share` of their strength while both hold, so that the two grab points, not two wrists that rarely agree, set the object's line, and the wrists still roll it about that line. Letting go with one hand leaves the object to the other, which holds it if it can.

### Articulated objects

A body joined to the world or to another body by a Havok constraint, such as a hinged gate, a hatch, or a lever, is held the same way: the hold asks for the velocities that carry the grab point to the hand, and the physics step removes what the joint forbids, so a gate swings about its hinge and a drawer slides along its runner. With `pickup.articulated` (on by default, `MOVE HINGED THINGS BY HAND` on the pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page), the reach tells such a body apart (`physical_hands/articulated.rs`): a dynamic body owned by the object the ray reported, other than a weapon or a vehicle, for which the world's constraint manager ([`hknpWorld::m_constraintManager`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##field-m_constraintManager)) holds an enabled constraint ([`hknpConstraint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpConstraint)) to another body that is not a hand body, so a hand's own grip joint does not count. Such an object is an articulated target, of any type, marked with a teal ring, and taken whatever its weight up to `pickup.max_articulated_mass_kg` rather than `pickup.max_mass_kg`, since its constraint carries it: a heavy gate swings slowly within the hand's strength. A placed constraint joins objects found by id, not by type ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#joined-bodies)). A vehicle's doors, hood, and trunk are found by their own reach and held on their hinges the same way, a closed one first gripped at its handle area until its latch gives ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)). Which world props carry a joint, and whether any is keyframed (moved by a script, which the hand cannot move), is unverified.

Taking hold of one logs `physical hands: a hand took hold of an articulated object` with the body, its mass, and whether the hold is joined. The readout counts the articulated holds among the holds, and the `VR: STATUS` page's `HINGED THINGS` row, under `HANDS IN THE WORLD`, counts them and says when one is in reach; the recorder's signal is `articulated.grips`, and the template is `testing/scenarios/articulated.toml`.

### The player's own body

The held object's centre is kept out of the player's own body (`PlayerKeepOut` in `physical_hands/hold.rs`), so a held crate is not driven into the player's capsule, where it would shove the player. While the procedural body poses the torso this tick ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-arms)), the keep-out is the same chest and belly capsules the wrist targets are kept out of, carried from Rico's model space into world space by the character's own transform (`body::procedural::torso_keep_out`), so a held object rests against the torso's own shape rather than a looser bound. Otherwise it falls back to a cylinder around the player's body (`pickup.keep_out_radius_m`, the player's height tall): a target inside it is moved out horizontally. The object and the capsule still collide; making the held body ignore the player would need its collision filter word changed on the live world and restored on every release, which the mod does not do.

A holding hand does not probe or push off surfaces, and the other palm's probes pass through what the hands hold, so a free hand never pushes the body off the carried object. The reach rays do find a held object, so that the second hand can take hold of it.

### Letting go and throwing

Releasing the grip lets go. The last hand to let go of an object one hand lifts throws it: the grab point leaves with the palm's velocity at that point times `pickup.throw_scale`, capped at `pickup.max_throw_speed_mps`, and the body keeps its spin, so a flick of the wrist spins it and swings its far end. The palm's velocity is its world-space motion smoothed over a few hundredths of a second, so it includes the player's own movement. A heavier object keeps the velocity the hold gave it.

Every hold is checked each tick before it is driven: the body id must still have a dynamic body, owned by the object it was taken from ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world)), so a body freed or reused for another object is let go. Holds are dropped without a throw when the palms stand down (a vehicle, a swim, a teleport), when the hand stops being tracked or empty, and when the bindings stop reading the grips; a game menu keeps them.

## Haptics

A hand that meets something hard enough also makes the game's melee impact sound for the struck material ([impact-sounds.md](impact-sounds.md)).

The cues are the contact category's ([haptics.md](haptics.md)). A palm meeting a surface plays `Event::PalmTouch` on its hand, weaker for a slower meeting. Taking hold of an object or grabbing a weapon plays `Event::Take`, letting go `Event::LetGo`, and a door's latch giving `Event::Unlatch`. While a hand presses or pulls, it sends a level every tick in proportion to its load: a shoving palm (`Sustain::PalmPressure`) by the share of its push used (or half its resistance, whichever is more), a holding hand (`Sustain::HeldLoad`) by the share of its pull or twist used, and nothing below a tenth.

## Threading

The hand bodies are made, added, parked, driven, and destroyed only in the pre-simulation callback, which the physics system calls on the game thread outside the step, and in the shutdown cleanup on the game thread; the aim update only publishes the palms' poses, so it may run on any thread. The palms' poses go to the callback, and the callback's offsets, loads, and push come back, through the hand bodies' own leaf lock, never the VR runtime's; each goes stale after a fifth of a second without a tick. The payload is built to abort on a panic, so a panic in the callback would end the game; the step's paths do not panic, and handle a missing body, joint, or grip as a lost one.

The contact handler runs inside the physics step, on the game thread, where the world must not change: it reads the event and the other body's instance, and queues the contact under a leaf lock of its own. It does not panic either. The blows are dealt from the game thread's frame tick, outside the game's update, where the engine's own damage and reactions are safe to run; dealing them from the pre-simulation callback instead could knock a character into a ragdoll, whose own callback would then be registered while the engine walks the callbacks.

The shoves and knocks and the held objects' velocities change the main thread's engine state. The input tick notes its thread; the palms act only when the aim update runs on that thread, and otherwise stand down and count the tick in the readout. The push is read by the locomotion task's post-call and the clamp by every caller of the active hands, through the physical hands' own lock, never the VR runtime's. Both go stale after a fifth of a second without a tick. The pickup grabs and releases are read on the input tick and acted on at the next aim update, through the same lock. The shoves and holds read and write the Havok motion directly, between physics steps, as the engine's own melee impulse does; they would be unsafe from inside the step or from a Havok callback, and are never made there.

## Settings and readouts

The hand bodies' settings are `physical_hands.bodies`, under "Hand bodies" in the debug UI's Controllers tab, with their readout beside the physical hands': whether the callback is registered, the steps it ran, the player's system group, the push, and for each hand its phase, body id, collision-filter word, lag, speed, and the share of its pull and twist used. The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has `PHYSICAL HANDS` (`bodies.enabled`) `HAND STRENGTH` (`bodies.strength`), `HAND BLOWS DEAL DAMAGE` (`bodies.hits.damage`), `CLIMB WITH THE HANDS` (`climb.enabled`) and `CLIMB MOVING THINGS` (`climb.moving`, both [hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md)), `PUSH UP WITH THE HANDS` (`vault.enabled`, [pushing up](#pushing-up)), `MOVE HINGED THINGS BY HAND` (`pickup.articulated`, [articulated objects](#articulated-objects)), `OPEN DOORS BY HAND` (`doors.enabled`, [vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)), `GRAB CHARACTERS` (`characters.grab`), and `PULL OCCUPANTS OUT BY HAND` (`characters.hijack`, both [character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md)) under "HANDS AND AIM". The readout also counts the contacts reported and dropped and the blows dealt, and shows the last blow's hand, impulse, share of a knockdown, and damage. The hits' settings are `physical_hands.bodies.hits`, under the hand bodies' own.

The log has the lifecycle at info level: `hand bodies: registered the pre-simulation callback`, `the pre-simulation callback first fired` (a warning instead when it fires on another thread than the registration's), `created a hand body` with its body id, collision-filter word, and system group, `added a hand body to the world`, `removed a hand body from the world` and `destroyed a hand body` with the reason. With joints, the log has `hand bodies: joined a hand to what it holds` with the constraint and both bodies, `could not join a hand to what it holds` (a warning) with the error, and `hand bodies: ended a joint` with whether the constraint was still the joint's (`destroyed`) and the reason. `created a hand body` also says whether the body listens for its contacts (`listens`) and whether the world holds its contact-event flag (`contact_events`, `Some(true)` when it does). The first contact reported logs `hand bodies: the first contact event arrived on the game thread`, or a warning, `arrived on another thread than the game's`, with both threads. Each blow logs `hand bodies: a hand struck a character` with the blow's impulse, the hand's and the character's speeds toward each other, the solver's contact impulse, whether the character was hostile, its share of the knockdown impulse (`strength`), the reaction impulse given, the damage carried and dealt, and the character's own stagger and knockdown thresholds and type factor; a character without the tuning to scale a blow by logs `a struck character has no hit-reaction tuning to scale a blow by` at debug level. Once a second while a hand acts, `hand bodies: drive` at debug level gives each hand's lag, speed, pull and twist shares, and the push.

The palms' settings are `physical_hands` in the controller settings, under "Physical hands" in the debug UI's Controllers tab, with the readout (each palm's press, push, and speed, its shove's impulse, load, and resistance, what it reaches or holds with the mass, the lag, and the pull and twist used, and the counters) beside the other subsystems. The shove settings are `physical_hands.shove` and the pickup settings `physical_hands.pickup`. `show_boxes` draws the palm boxes in the world-space overlay, coloured by whether they press into the world or an object, with a line along each shove, longer for a harder push and redder for a body that resists. With `pickup.show_targets`, a disc marks each hand's grab point and a line joins it to where the hand would have it, redder the harder the hand pulls.

## Unverified in the headset

- That the callback fires once per simulation tick, on the game thread, and that the bodies are created with the weapons layer in the player's system group (the readout's filter word against the player's group).
- That the player's capsule never moves when a hand is inside it, which confirms the group exclusion.
- How the drive feels at the default strength: whether a free hand tracks without visible lag, whether a hand against a wall is steady, and whether props jitter between the hand and the world.
- How the engine's listeners treat a hand body, an instance with no owner, meeting a ragdoll, a vehicle's wheel rays, and an explosion; and whether a fast hand damages props through the game's own damage listeners.
- Whether a parked body's removal and re-addition each time a gun is drawn and holstered is cheap enough.
- The contact impulses of real swings and the soldiers' own stagger and knockdown thresholds, against which the hits' defaults are guesses; and whether the characters' own capsule listener also reacts to a hand's contact on top of the blow.
- Whether a character struck while in a vehicle, in cover, or in a scripted state reacts sensibly to a bare hit reaction.
- Everything about joint grabs, which are on by default and are the first thing to switch off if a held object misbehaves or the game crashes while one is shot apart: whether a three-kilogram hand holds a heavy object steadily or the two jitter, whether the joint frames are the body frames the engine expects, and what a step does with the joint when the game destroys or replaces an object's body (a crate shot apart) after the pre-simulation step checked it. The world does not end a constraint when one of its bodies is destroyed ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#joining-bodies-with-a-fixed-constraint)), so the joint names a freed body until the next pre-simulation step finds it gone and ends it. A body destroyed during the game's update is caught by that check before the step; one destroyed inside a step (the destruction system breaking it apart) leaves the joint on a dead body for the rest of that step. Subscribing to the world's body-destroyed signal would close that window, but the signal's slot layout is not bound.

- That the aim update runs on the input tick's thread. The readout's off-thread count says.
- Whether holding the hand at the surface reads well with the render-time late update.
- How the push feels against the roomscale chase when the player leans into a wall.
- How the push up feels: whether the lift settles without bouncing at the default stiffness, whether the game keeps the player on foot while the pressing hand finds no static world under it, and whether a vault over a low wall carries on smoothly with the stick.
- That the aim's ray filter hits loose props and dropped weapons at all, and their bodies' masses and inertias: the strength defaults are guesses.
- That the simulation's gravity is the default `pickup.gravity_mps2`; a mismatch shows as a held object that sags or rises at rest.
- That one physics step runs between two aim updates; several steps per update would integrate the set velocities several times, and a held object would overshoot.
- That the Havok solver respects the set velocities against contacts and joints as expected: a held crate stops at a wall, a hinged gate swings about its hinge.
- Which world props are joined by a placed constraint.
- That a held object kept out of the player's own body, by the torso's capsules or the fallback cylinder, never shoves the player's capsule, and whether the cylinder's default radius fits the capsule where the procedural body is off.
- Whether the shove's resistance settles without the body chattering between pushed and not, and whether its strength default moves a car at all against its brakes.
- Which weapon the game picks up when several are in range, and that the use-item click alone starts the pickup.
- Whether the hold's defaults hold a crate steadily at the game's simulation rate, and how a throw feels at `throw_scale` one.

## Headset checklist

With the hand bodies on:

1. In the log, after loading in: `registered the pre-simulation callback`, then `the pre-simulation callback first fired` without the other-thread warning, then `created a hand body` and `added a hand body to the world` for each hand. The readout's filter word has the weapons layer in its low field and the player's group in its high half.
2. Wave the hands in the open: the readout's lag stays within a couple of centimetres and the pull share near zero; the rendered hands keep up with the controllers.
3. Press a hand into a wall: the rendered hand stops at the wall, the lag grows with the press, the body is pushed back, and the hand buzzes. Press down on a table lightly: nothing moves.
4. Stand at a waist-high wall, put both hands on its top, and press down hard: `the hands push the body up`, the body rises by about the press, and `PUSH UP` says `LIFTING`. Walk forward with the stick onto the top; ease off the press and the body comes down, `the push up ended` with the rise.
5. Push a crate: it slides; push a barrel at its side: it rolls; push a parked car: it barely moves and the body is pushed back.
6. Put a hand inside the player's chest: the player does not move.
7. Draw a gun: that hand's body is removed (`removed a hand body from the world`); holster it: added again. Enter a vehicle: both removed. Load a save: both destroyed.
8. Toggle `PHYSICAL HANDS` off in the pause menu and resume: both destroyed, and the palm probes take over.
9. `created a hand body` shows `listens=true contact_events=Some(true)`. Tap a wall: `the first contact event arrived on the game thread`, and the hand buzzes, harder for a punch than a tap.
10. Swing a hand slowly into a soldier: `a hand struck a character` with a small `strength`, and the soldier flinches or staggers. Swing hard: a `strength` near or above one, the soldier falls into a ragdoll pushed along the swing, and the log shows `damage` and `dealt`. Rest the hand on the soldier: no further blows. With `HAND BLOWS DEAL DAMAGE` off, the hard swing still knocks the soldier down and `dealt` is zero.
11. With `joint_grabs` on in the debug UI, grip a crate: `joined a hand to what it holds`, the readout says `joined to`, and the crate moves with the hand, sagging if heavy. Let go while swinging: `ended a joint` with `destroyed=true reason="the grip let go"`, and the crate flies on. Grip it with both hands, then shoot it apart: `ended a joint` with `the constraint or the object's body went away` and no crash.

With the hand bodies off:

1. Lay a palm flat on a wall at arm's length: the hand rests on the surface, does not pass through, and the body does not drift.
2. Push the wall: the body (and the view) moves back, settling with the palm on the wall. Push the floor or a table top: nothing moves.
3. Swing a hand through a civilian: nothing happens to either, and the body is not pushed.
4. Push a crate slowly across the floor: it slides with the hand, the hand stays on its face, the body does not move, and the hand buzzes lightly. Push a barrel at its side: it rolls. Push a tall object high up: it tips.
5. Push the crate into a wall and keep pushing: the readout's resistance climbs, the hand stops at the crate, and the body is pushed back as off a wall. Push a parked car: it barely moves, and the body is pushed back.
6. Hit a crate fast: it is knocked harder than a push.
7. Draw a gun: that hand's palm stops (the readout says not acting); the other, empty hand still collides.
8. In a vehicle, swimming, and under the parachute: no palm acts.
9. With `show_boxes`, the boxes sit over the palms, turn orange against walls and green against objects, and a shove line grows with the push.
10. With `pickup.show_targets` on, reach toward a small crate's corner: a blue ring marks it. Grip: the crate stays where it was in the hand, held by that corner; turn the wrist and it turns about the corner. Carry it into a wall: it stops, and the hand stays on it. Release it while swinging: it flies along the swing, spinning with the flick.
11. Grip a long plank by one end: it droops and swings from the hand. Grip the other end with the other hand: it comes level between the hands; raise one hand and it tilts.
12. Grip a heavy object with one hand: a red ring before the grab; it does not lift but drags along the ground. Grip it with both: it lifts if both hands are strong enough. Push it toward a wall: the body is pushed back.
13. With `MOVE HINGED THINGS BY HAND` on, reach toward a hinged gate, hatch, or door in a base if one exists: a teal ring, whatever its weight. Grip: `a hand took hold of an articulated object`, and it swings about its hinge as the hand moves.
14. Hold a crate against the chest: it stays in front of the body rather than shoving the player.
15. Kill a soldier, reach for the dropped weapon: a gold ring. Grip: the weapon is in the gripping hand at once (with `weapons.hold.direct_pickup` off, the pickup animation plays and the weapon is equipped). Walk over another of the same type: the ammunition is collected without a grab.
16. Hold a crate and touch it with the other palm: no push off it, and the other hand can grip it too.
17. Reach toward a holster with a crate beside it: the holster draws. Open the pause menu while holding a crate: it is still held after resuming; enter a vehicle while holding: it drops.
