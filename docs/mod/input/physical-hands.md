# Physical hands

Each empty hand is a body in the game's physics world, driven toward its controller within the hand's strength: it stops at walls and is drawn there, shoves and topples what it meets, strikes characters, and pushes the player away from what does not give. A grip near a loose object takes hold of it where the hand touched it, and a grip near a weapon lying in the world collects the weapon. An object the grapple snatches from range ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md#phase-3-holstered-weapons-on-characters-and-props)) is handed to the same hold when it arrives (`physical_hands::remote`). A hand whose body does not act is probed with ray casts instead ([probes](#probes)).

The code is `crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/` (the hand bodies in its `physics/`); the engine side is in [havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md), [melee-and-damage.md](../../engine/gameplay/melee-and-damage.md), and [item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md). How a grip decides what it takes, and the grip markers, are in [grip.md](grip.md); the haptic cues and the mixer are in [haptics.md](haptics.md); what a held object's contacts do is in [impacts.md](impacts.md).

## Hand bodies

A hand body is a capsule along the fingers, centred on the palm box's centre (`hand_bodies.radius_m` and `hand_bodies.half_length_m`, scaled by the VR world scale), of `hand_bodies.mass_kg`. These keys are in `crates/jc3vr-physical/tuning/physics.toml` ([tuning.md](../tuning.md)). It is made the way the grappling hook's hang proxy makes its free capsule ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#making-a-free-dynamic-body)): a capsule shape, a dynamic body left out of the simulation, and the engine's wrapper ([`PfxRigidBody`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody)) around it, whose owner stays null. The wrapper turns the body's gravity off, so the drive does not fight it, and the body never sleeps.

### What a hand touches

The body is on the weapons layer ([`WEAPONS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-WEAPONS)) in the player's own collision system group, read from the player's character proxy each step. In the player's group with a zero subsystem id, it never touches the player's capsule or the other hand. On the weapons layer it meets the static world, terrain, props, debris, character capsules, ragdolls, and vehicles, and the aim's ray query passes through it. A player whose proxy is in system group zero gets no hand bodies, since they would collide with the player.

### The drive

The pre-simulation callback ([`PfxPreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback)) drives the bodies before each physics step, on the game thread and outside the step. For each acting hand:

1. The goal is where the palm will be at the end of the step: the palm pose the palms' tick last published, led by the palm's smoothed velocity over the time since it was published plus the step ([`PhysicsSystem::STEP_SECONDS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem)).
2. The body is asked to reach the goal in one step: the linear velocity that closes `hand_bodies.linear_gain` of the position error (at one, the discrete critically damped drive), and the angular velocity that closes `hand_bodies.angular_gain` of the rotation error, capped at `hand_bodies.max_speed_mps` and `hand_bodies.max_angular_speed_rps`.
3. The change of velocity is capped by the hand's strength over the step: the pull at `hand_bodies.max_force_n` through the body's mass and the twist at `hand_bodies.max_torque_nm` through its world inertia, each times the `bodies.strength` setting. The result is set as the body's velocities ([`hknpWorld::setBodyVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-setBodyVelocity)).

The speed cap applies relative to the frame that carries the hand. The palms' tick tracks the world velocity of the cockpit frame's origin, which moves only with what carries the player (a moving platform, a fall), and the drive feeds it forward (`drive::drive`'s carrier, held to `rig.max_carrier_speed_mps`), so a hand keeps up with a fast carrier while a tracking glitch is still capped. The carrier's part of the goal's lead counts simulation steps rather than time, since the carrier moves only with the steps; a frame that runs no step would otherwise put the goal a frame's travel ahead and the hand would snap back and forth. The carrier estimate restarts with the palms' on a teleport, a snap turn, or a recenter, and a hand gripping a pin or a climbed wall is driven without it.

A hand the last step held back is driven more softly: its gain falls with the share of its strength it pressed with, to `hand_bodies.contact_gain` of it at full press, so it rests on a wall rather than pounding it.

A snap turn, a hard view step, a recenter, or a follow bank ([physics-substrate.md, "The rig's jumps"](../body/physics-substrate.md#the-rigs-jumps)) moves every palm at once without the hands moving. The palms' velocities start afresh, and the next step puts each acting body at its place against its palm, at rest, rather than driving it there through whatever is around; it ignores its contacts for `lifecycle.rig_mute_steps` steps after, so the turn strikes nothing. A gripping hand's body stays on the wall.

The step integrates the velocities and resolves the contacts: a free hand reaches its goal, a hand against a wall stops at it, and a hand against a crate pushes it unless the crate is too heavy for its strength. The strength is the caps, not the mass: the solver shares a contact's impulse by mass, so a light hand meets a light prop without throwing it or jittering it against the world, and still shoves a heavy one within its strength over a few steps. The math is in `physics/drive.rs` with its tests.

A body that falls more than `hand_bodies.break_distance_m` behind its controller's palm (the palm now, without the lead, or a grip's pin) is put back on its goal at rest, but only when a ray from the body to the goal, through the aim's filter, meets nothing: a hand pulled through a wall or a character keeps pressing at the surface and is put back once the controller has a clear path. A hand that holds an object through a joint is never put back this way; the hold's strain decides when the object is torn away ([holding](#holding)). A body put somewhere rather than moved there (made, added, or put back) ignores its contacts for `lifecycle.placed_mute_steps` steps, so a body that starts inside something does not strike it.

### Drawing the hand at its body

Each step measures where the last step left the body against where the controller's palm was predicted for the end of that step: the controller's, not the goal, so a hand whose body a grip pins to a wall is drawn on the wall. The offset is a shift of the palm's centre and a turn about it, so a wrist twisted into a wall is drawn turned as its body is; a turn within `render.turn_deadband_rad` is not drawn, so a free hand keeps the controller's rotation. The hand's cockpit poses are moved by that offset ([`apply`](../../../crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/mod.rs)), so the IK targets and the late update put the rendered hand where its body is. The offset is updated at the simulation rate and added to the latest controller pose at the render rate, so a free hand keeps the controller's latency. The late update's render-time hands take an offset sampled after the simulation instead ([render-time-anchoring.md](../rendering/render-time-anchoring.md#moving-frame-sampling)).

### Pushing the player

A hand that something holds back falls behind its goal. The lag past `hand_bodies.push_deadband_m` pulls on the hand like a spring of `hand_bodies.push_stiffness_n_per_m`, up to the hand's strength, and the reaction over the step moves `hand_bodies.body_mass_kg` of player the other way. The push is added to the on-foot locomotion task's wanted velocity, as the probes' push is ([pushing off surfaces](#pushing-off-surfaces)), smoothed over `hand_bodies.push_smoothing_s` and capped at `hand_bodies.max_push_speed_mps` for both hands together. A lag that is mostly vertical (below `hand_bodies.min_push_horizontal` level), pressing down on a table or the floor, does not push. A hand that moves what it meets, or tracks freely, has no lag; as the player moves away the goal moves with them, so the push settles with the hand resting on the surface.

### Pushing up

The press down that the push leaves alone lifts the body instead, a jump assist for vaulting onto a ledge or a low wall (`physical_hands/vault.rs`, tuning in the `vault` section of `climb.toml`). A hand that neither grips nor holds anything, whose reach finds a static surface under it facing up at least `vault.min_vertical`, and whose lag points down at least that share, lifts the player at `vault.stiffness_per_s` per metre of press past `vault.deadband_m`, up to `vault.max_lift_mps`; with both hands pressing, the stronger lift wins. No grip is needed: a grip on a surface climbs it instead ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md)), and while a hand climbs nothing pushes up.

A push is a deliberate act on something above the ground:

- A surface less than `vault.min_height_m` above the feet takes no press, so a hand reaching down for a magazine or a weapon on the ground rests there rather than popping the body up.
- A press starts to lift only once it has stayed past the deadband for `vault.hold_s`, so a brush on a table or a moment's overshoot onto a car's hood lifts nothing. Once lifting, it carries on while the hands press.
- The lag alone is not a press: while the player falls the rig carries both goals down faster than the bodies follow, so a falling player's hands read as pressing on nothing. The lift therefore starts only while the character proxy found the player standing on that step, and never lifts a player moving down faster than `vault.fall_tolerance_mps`; a downward velocity is left alone, never overwritten.

The lift is set in the pre-simulation step, after the characters' proxy updates, as the climb sets its velocity ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md#climbing)): the proxy keeps its own horizontal velocity and rises at least at the lift. Lifting the player lifts the controller with the rig, so the press shallows as the player rises, and the rise never exceeds `vault.max_rise_m` above where the push started. While a pressing hand reaches the static world, the proxy is written as supported on it, as a climbing hand's surface is ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md#keeping-the-game-on-foot)), so the game keeps the player on foot, and the stick carries the player over the top.

The log has, at info level, `physical hands: the hands push the body up` with the feet's height and the lift, `the push up ended` with the rise, and, once per refused press, `the hands pressed down, but the player is off the ground or moving down, so nothing lifts` or `the hands pressed down on a surface at the feet, which is never pushed on`. The `Push up` status row counts the pushes and the refusals and shows the lift and the last rise.

### Hits

Each hand body reports the contacts it starts. The engine's wrapper of each body is given a copy of the wrapper's virtual table whose contact-impulse slot is the mod's, and its contact events are switched on ([`PfxRigidBody::SetContactImpulseEventActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-SetContactImpulseEventActive)); every other slot, and the run-time type information before the table, is the engine's own ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#contact-impulse-events)). A contact's started event carries the impulse the solver applied over the step it began and names the other body and its owner. It arrives inside the physics step, so it is only queued there (`physics/contact.rs`); the next frame tick, on the game thread ahead of the game's update, acts on it (`physics/blows.rs`):

- The hand plays `Event::HandStrike` in proportion to how fast it met what it touched, as the momentum of an arm of `hand_bodies.hits.blow_mass_kg` at the hand's speed, full at `hand_bodies.hits.full_pulse_impulse_ns`.
- A contact with a character other than the player is a blow only when the hand swung into it: the hand's speed toward the character's chest, from the velocity it was driven at less the player's own ([`Character::m_MeasuredWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_MeasuredWorldVelocity)), must reach `hand_bodies.hits.min_approach_speed_mps`. A hand resting on a character, met by a character walking into it, or carried into it by the running player strikes nothing. The speed is judged per real second, so in slow motion a reach is not taken for a swing ([slow-motion.md](slow-motion.md)).
- A hand going for the gun in the character's hands (`hit::Intent`, from the grip latch and the weapons' offer of that gun, [physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-gun-from-a-characters-hands)) strikes only at the speed of a solid punch while open, and not at all while its grip closes on or holds the gun, since the grab takes it. A hand that already holds a gun always swings, whatever its latch says.
- The blow's impulse is the momentum of an arm of `hand_bodies.hits.blow_mass_kg` at the hand's own share of the closing speed, not the solver's contact impulse, which grows with the hand body's mass; a blow under `hand_bodies.hits.min_impulse_ns` is none. The character is found again from the contact's body, so one freed since the step is never touched, and reacts through its own hit reaction ([`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction), [melee-and-damage.md](../../engine/gameplay/melee-and-damage.md#the-hit-reaction)) as to a melee blow along the swing.
- The blow is calibrated against the character's own thresholds ([`Character::m_HitReaction_ImpulseStumbleThreshold`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_HitReaction_ImpulseStumbleThreshold) and [`Character::m_HitReaction_ImpulseRagdollThreshold`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_HitReaction_ImpulseRagdollThreshold)): a blow of `hand_bodies.hits.stagger_impulse_ns` lands just past the stagger threshold (by `hand_bodies.hits.over_stagger`), a harder one rises toward the knockdown without reaching it, and a blow of `hand_bodies.hits.knockdown_impulse_ns` reaches the knockdown and ragdolls the character with the blow's momentum. A weaker blow gives the same share of the stagger threshold, short of it, and the character flinches. A character whose stagger threshold is not below its knockdown threshold takes the knockdown's share alone.
- A blow of at least `hand_bodies.hits.damage_impulse_ns` also deals the player's melee damage ([`Player::m_MeleeDamage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##field-m_MeleeDamage), so the hammer upgrades count) times `hand_bodies.hits.damage_scale` times the blow's share of the knockdown impulse, at least one point. It is sent as a melee damage message, which runs the same hit reaction from its impulse, so the character reacts once.
- A blow that reached `hand_bodies.hits.follow_through_strength` of a knockdown follows through: the drive keeps `hand_bodies.hits.follow_through_gain` of its gain for `hand_bodies.hits.follow_through_s`, so the hand carries on rather than stopping dead.

Every character is struck alike, whatever its relation to the player's faction, and the game's own reactions to being hit (the alarm, the heat) follow. The relation ([`GetRelation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/faction##func-GetRelation)) is read only for the readout and the log.

A hand that struck a character ignores its further contacts with that character for `hand_bodies.hits.cooldown_s`, so one swing through a body is one blow, and a damaged character takes no further damage from either hand for `hand_bodies.hits.damage_cooldown_s`. Hits on props, vehicles, and the world move them physically and pulse the hand, and deal nothing more. There is no melee gesture: a blow is only ever a hand body's contact. A punch on a seated occupant reached through a broken window is in [character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md#through-a-broken-window).

### Lifecycle

A hand acts on foot, on the ground or falling (so a hand flung at a ledge can catch it), not while the grapple carries the player (`tick::mode_acts`), for a tracked hand the controllers drive that holds nothing but what its body holds through a joint. Its body is made on the first step it acts and added to the simulation at its goal. A hand that takes a grenade or a charge, or starts carrying what it grabbed, has its body taken out of the simulation (parked) at once, so it does not stand frozen in the world as an obstacle. A hand holding a gun, or bracing one on its foregrip, keeps acting and holds the gun's own body through a joint ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md)). A hand that stops acting for another reason (a lost controller, a vehicle, a swim, the parachute, or a flicker of the mode) has its body held in place for `step.park_after_ms`, ignoring its contacts, then parked until it acts again.

Both bodies are destroyed when the game leaves gameplay, when the player's collision system group changes (a new character after a death), and when the payload unloads. A body whose slot no longer holds it is forgotten without touching the engine; since the forgotten wrapper may still point at the mod's copy of the virtual table, forgetting one that listened for its contacts pins the payload's image for the rest of the game's run. A body that cannot be made returns the wrapper's memory to the engine allocator ([`MemoryFree`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/memory##func-MemoryFree)).

The callback is registered once, from the game thread's frame tick ahead of the game's update, so never while the engine walks its callbacks, and removed by the shutdown cleanup, which also destroys the bodies, before the payload unloads. The bodies keep no Havok-space positions: every step converts game space with the world's current shift, so a world recentring needs nothing of them.

A hand its body drives does not probe, push off surfaces through the probes, shove, or knock. It still reaches for what its grip would take, casting its reach rays from where its body was before the last step rather than from the controller, so a hand held at a wall reaches what its body touches and not what lies beyond the wall.

## Probes

A palm whose body does not act (none made yet, the player in system group zero, or the step's publications stale) is probed with ray casts. The casts reuse the player aim's filtered ray query ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay) through the aim's filter, as in [the aim raycast](../../../crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/aim/raycast.rs)), which never hits the player and already names the hit object and body. The game's own melee box overlap query ([`PhysicsSystem::CollideBox`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CollideBox)) is bound but not used, because its all-hits collector owns Havok heap memory the mod would have to manage.

The palm is a box in the wrist frame (`palms.palm_half_extents_m` in `crates/jc3vr-physical/tuning/hands.toml`), centred on the hand's palm centre (the body crate's hands tuning's `pose.palm_centre_m`) and scaled by the VR world scale. It is probed from six points: its centre, the middle of the palm's face, and the four corners of the face toward the fingers. A palm acts in the same modes as a hand body, for a tracked hand that holds nothing. The hands are taken before the two-handed pose, recoil, and the anchored grapple hand are applied, so the probes see where the player's hand is. Seated in a vehicle, the palms reach only that vehicle's doors (`seated`, [vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md#seated-doors-and-palm-pressure)).

Each tick each probe point is cast from where it last was in the open to where it is now, as the head probe does. A hit on world geometry or an object means the point crossed the surface: it is pressed in by its depth behind the surface along the normal, and its open position stays put until the hand comes back out. A cast from the shoulder forgives a stale open position, so a hand carried around an obstacle counts as clear again, and a teleport forgets the open positions. A palm passes through characters. The hit's kind comes from the engine's run-time type test ([`GameObject::IsType`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##vfunc-IsType) with [`Character::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-TYPE_ID)). The hand's cockpit poses are moved out of the surface by the deepest press, while the probes use the unclamped hands, so the press still measures where the real hand is.

## Pushing off surfaces

A palm pressed past `palms.push_deadband_m` into a surface whose normal is mostly horizontal (`palms.min_push_horizontal`) asks for a push along the normal, at `palms.push_stiffness_per_s` per metre of press, up to `palms.max_push_speed_mps` for both hands together. As the body moves away the press shallows, so the push settles with the palm resting on the surface.

The push, from the probes or from the hand bodies, is added to the on-foot locomotion task's wanted velocity in the rig's root hook, after the stick's and before the step drive's ([rig.md, "Who moves the root"](../body/rig.md#who-moves-the-root), [physics-substrate.md](../body/physics-substrate.md#the-wanted-velocity)), so the ledger counts it as the task's movement and the view moves with the body as it does for the stick. Only the on-foot task's ticks carry it, so a hand in a vehicle, a swim, or a fall never pushes off a surface; in water the hands swim by their strokes ([swim-strokes.md](../body/swim-strokes.md)).

## Shoving objects

A palm pressed into a dynamic body shoves it every tick it presses, however slowly the hand moves. The contact is soft, at the velocity level: the body's point under the palm is asked to move into the body at least as fast as the hand moves into it, plus `shove.stiffness_per_s` per metre of press past `shove.deadband_m`, up to `shove.max_speed_mps`. The impulse is the point's mass along the normal times the speed it lacks, from the body's mass, inertia, and velocities ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world)), so a push off the centre turns the body. It is applied at the contact point ([`hknpWorld::applyBodyImpulseAt`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-applyBodyImpulseAt)), capped at `shove.max_force_n` over the tick, and drags the surface by friction up to `shove.friction` times the push.

How much a body gives is measured: each tick compares the speed the point reached with the speed the last tick asked of it, and the share it did not give, or that the cap held back, is the resistance, smoothed over `shove.give_smoothing_s`. The push off a surface is scaled by the resistance, so a light crate slides away without pushing the player while a crate against a wall pushes the player as a wall does. World geometry and bodies that are not dynamic resist fully.

A palm meeting an object at `palms.min_object_speed_mps` or faster also knocks it on the first tick of contact with `palms.object_impulse` of the player's melee impulse ([`Player::m_MeleeImpulse`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##field-m_MeleeImpulse)), scaled by the hit's strength, which reaches one at `palms.full_hit_speed_mps`. The engine leaves static and keyframed bodies alone.

## Picking things up

An acting hand that holds nothing looks, every tick, for what its grip would take: five short rays from the back of the hand through the palm (out of the palm, along the fingers, between the two, and out of either edge), reaching `reach_m` past the palm (`crates/jc3vr-physical/tuning/pickup.toml`, read through `pickup::PICKUP`), and the surface the palm presses into. The nearest hit a hand takes is the hand's target, offered to the grip and marked by its kind ([grip.md](grip.md#markers)). The engine's run-time type test tells apart what a hand takes:

- a weapon lying loose in the world ([`WeaponBase::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-TYPE_ID), in the [`E_WS_DROPPED`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponState##variant-E_WS_DROPPED) state);
- any other object whose reached body is dynamic and of finite mass: a rigid object ([`RigidObject::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObject##func-TYPE_ID)) such as a crate, an object of another class with a loose body, such as a barrel, and a vehicle part that has come off its vehicle, one no part of the vehicle still hangs from by its own constraint, or whose vehicle is gone (`doors::engine::broken_off`). A part still on its vehicle is not a loose object; a door is the doors' reach's ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md));
- a placed rigid object at rest whose body is not yet dynamic: a fixed or keyframed rigid object whose wrapper keeps the mass it was placed with ([havok-bodies.md, "Placed bodies becoming dynamic"](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#placed-bodies-becoming-dynamic)). It is offered as a loose object of that mass (`bodies::placed_mass`), and the grip turns it loose first (`bodies::loosen`, through [`RigidObject::MakeDynamic`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObject##func-MakeDynamic)), as the game does when the object is knocked loose. A static rigid object is part of the world and is climbed, not taken.

The same rays find characters ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md#reaching-a-character)), a ragdoll among them, and a vehicle's doors ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)), each through its own reach. Vehicles are not targets of this kind, nor the body the player's proxy stands on ([`PfxCharacterInstance::m_GroundBodyId`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxCharacterInstance##field-m_GroundBodyId)). Collectibles have no body and are left to the game's use input. Each hit the reach makes is noted with its kind, its body, and why it was not taken, and the notes are logged with the hand's next grip press ([grip.md, "Readouts and logs"](grip.md#readouts-and-logs)).

### The grip

The target is offered to the grip at the reach's distance (`GrabVolume::Pickup`) and competes with every other subsystem's targets by the distance of its grab point; it wins over a surface to climb, which is a fallback. A grab of an object asks the next pickup tick to take hold of it, and releasing the grip asks it to let go.

A grab of a weapon takes it straight into the grabbing hand ([physical-weapons.md, "Picking a weapon up into the hand"](physical-weapons.md#picking-a-weapon-up-into-the-hand)); for a weapon the direct pickup refuses, the grab clicks the game's use-item action ([`USE_ITEM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-USE_ITEM)) so the game picks it up its own way ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#picking-up-a-weapon)). The physical hands reach the weapon handling's take, its record of the pickup, and its grant of that click through the pickup sink the weapon handling registers in the body crate (`controllers::held::pickup`), so neither names the other. Ammunition and grenades need no grab, since the game collects them as the player walks over them.

Under the controller scheme the hands are the only way to pick a weapon up (`weapons::hold::button_pickup`). The game's weapon pickup panel is kept hidden: in place of the player's pickup readout ([`Player::UpdateWeaponHUD`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-UpdateWeaponHUD)) the mod clears the panel and its remembered offer. For the length of the weapon handling ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponHandling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponHandling)) the use-item weapon input ([`E_INPUT_USE_ITEM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::WeaponInput##variant-E_INPUT_USE_ITEM)) reads zero, except in the short window the fallback click is granted. The use action itself is untouched, because the same button enters vehicles. Under the gamepad scheme the panel and the button pickup are the game's own.

### Holding

Unless a joint holds it ([holding through a joint](#holding-through-a-joint)), a held object stays dynamic and is carried: driven, not attached. At the grab, the touched point is recorded in the body's frame and in the palm's, with the body's orientation in the palm's frame. Each tick the hold is solved as a pair of soft constraints between the palm and the body, the way a physics engine solves its joints ([`hold`](../../../crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/hold.rs)):

- the grab point is asked to move with the palm plus `stiffness_per_s` per metre it is off, up to `max_speed_mps`;
- the body is asked to turn with the palm plus `angular_stiffness_per_s` per radian it is off, up to `max_angular_speed_rps`.

The impulses are found together over `hold.iterations` passes: the pull at the grab point through the point's mass matrix and the twist through the body's inertia, each capped by the hand's strength over the tick (`HandStrength::force_n` and `HandStrength::torque_nm`, the hand bodies' caps at the strength setting). The solve starts from where gravity (the body crate's `physics::GRAVITY_MPS2`) will have the body after the step, so the pull includes holding it up. The result is set as the body's velocities before the step, which resolves its contacts and any joint it hangs from.

The palm moves in real time while the world runs on the game's clock, so the hold is solved in real time (`HoldTuning::in_real_time`): under slow motion, at the clock's update speed ([`Clock::m_UpdateSpeed`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/clock::Clock##field-m_UpdateSpeed)), the stiffnesses and top speeds are raised by the slowdown factor and the strength caps by its square. Gravity stays the world's, so a heavy object is easier to hold up in slow motion.

What falls out of the strength caps: one hand lifts what weighs less than its pull, two hands twice that, and a heavier object sinks from the hand and drags along the ground; a long object held at one end droops when its weight's lever outweighs the wrist's twist; a heavy object comes up to speed slowly. A hand takes hold of any loose object up to `max_mass_kg`, and the marker shows when the hands could not lift it.

The hands ride on the character, so an object the holding hands can lift takes on the character's own change of velocity each tick (from `Character::m_MeasuredWorldVelocity`, scaled by `carrier_motion_share`), and the strength works only on its motion relative to the body: a jump or a landing carries a held tyre along. An object the hands cannot lift is not carried along, and stays a snag.

A hand that pulls near its strength without moving the object pushes the player toward the grab point, as a wall does; a carried object's lag pushes only past `palms.carry_resist_deadband_m`, so normal carrying near the strength does not drift the player. With the rendered hand held at the grab point, out to `tear_distance_m` from the controller, the hand stays on the object wherever it is.

A hold is let go when the object is caught rather than slow (`grasp::Strain`, the same for a carried hold and a joined one): when the grab point lags more than `break_distance_m` behind the hand and has not closed on it at `stuck_speed_mps` or faster for `stuck_s` (a crate snagged on a wall, a door at its hinge's limit), or at once past `tear_distance_m`. Closing is judged against the hand's own motion, so a door held while riding a fast car stays held. For an object the hands cannot lift, only the lag across the ground counts, since its sag is its weight resting on the ground. An object resting against Rico's torso capsules is never caught ([the player's own body](#the-players-own-body)). A hand torn away leaves the other judged at once with its own strength alone. The strain runs in real time and ends with the hold, so a new grip starts with no stuck time.

A hold that ends other than by the grip opening logs `physical hands: pickup: a hold was lost` at info level with the hand, the body, and the reason; the recording's grip release carries the same reason as its cause. Every hold is checked each tick before it is driven: the body id must still name a dynamic body owned by the object it was taken from ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world)), so a body freed or reused is let go. Holds are dropped without a throw when the palms stand down (a vehicle, a swim, a teleport), when the hand stops being tracked or empty, and when the bindings stop reading the grips; a game menu keeps them.

### Holding through a joint

A hand its body drives holds what its grip takes through a joint: a fixed constraint between the hand's body and the object's ([havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#joining-bodies-with-a-fixed-constraint)), made by the next pre-simulation step in the pose the two bodies are in. The drive pulls the hand, and with it the object, within the same budget a carried hold of the object has (`HandStrength`, or the more a two-handed carry of a heavy object gets from `HoldTuning::carrying`, published with the hold as `HoldTarget::hand_force_n`). The hand's goal is moved by the keep-out as a carried object's target is. Two hands holding one object hold it through two joints, and their pulls add. Letting go ends the joint, and the object leaves with the velocity the hand gave it.

A ragdoll or an articulated object hangs most of its mass on constraints of its own, which the drive does not see, so a hand joined to one is pulled softly, at `hand_bodies.constrained_gain_share` of the drive's gains and half that with both hands on it (`state::holding_tuning`, `HoldTarget::constrained`). Closing the held bone's lag in one step overshoots, and the body orbits the palm even held still. Two hands on one such assembly hold it by points (`JointKind::Point` in `physics/joint.rs`, a ball-and-socket constraint at each palm, [`hkpBallAndSocketConstraintData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkpBallAndSocketConstraintData)) rather than a fixed pose each, since two fixed poses on two bones of one chain rarely match what the assembly's joints allow, and the hands would fight each other through the solver. A hand's joint is made again when the other hand joins or leaves. The held assembly is also damped within itself (`pickup::damping`; a ragdoll through `characters::damp_held_ragdoll`, an articulated object through `articulated::damp_assembly`, [articulated objects](#articulated-objects)).

The step owns the joint (`physics/joint.rs`): the palms' tick publishes what each grip holds through a joint, and the step makes the joint, keeps it while the object still owns its body and the world's constraint slot still holds the joint's constraint, and ends it when the grip lets go, the hold is torn away, the hand's body stops acting, or the hands leave gameplay. Its data comes from the game thread's Havok heap; the world's constraint holds the only reference once made, so ending the joint frees it, and a joint is destroyed only after its slot is checked to still hold it. A joint that ends against the grip's wish, or cannot be made, drops the hold as a lost hold. The world does not end a constraint when one of its bodies is destroyed, so a body destroyed inside a step leaves the joint on a dead body until the next pre-simulation step finds it gone.

### Two hands

Both hands may hold one object, each at its own point. Each hand pulls its own grab point, so a long object hangs between the hands and tilts as one rises, and the pulls add. The wrists' twists are weakened to `two_hand_angular_share` of their strength while both hold, so the two grab points, not two wrists that rarely agree, set the object's line. Letting go with one hand leaves the object to the other, which holds it if it can.

### Articulated objects

A body joined to the world or to another body by a Havok constraint, such as a hinged gate or a hatch, is held the same way: the hold asks for the velocities that carry the grab point to the hand, and the step removes what the joint forbids. The reach tells such a body apart (`physical_hands/articulated.rs`): a dynamic body owned by the object the ray reported, other than a weapon or a vehicle, for which the world's constraint manager ([`hknpWorld::m_constraintManager`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##field-m_constraintManager)) holds an enabled constraint ([`hknpConstraint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpConstraint)) to a body that is not a hand body. It is taken whatever its weight up to `max_articulated_mass_kg`, since its constraint carries it. A placed constraint joins objects found by id, not by type ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#joined-bodies)). A vehicle's doors, hood, and trunk are found by their own reach and held on their hinges the same way ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)).

While a hand holds one, its assembly is damped within itself on every pickup tick: the bodies its enabled constraints join it to, transitively, leaving out the hand bodies and the drawn torso's capsules and passing through no body that is not dynamic (a fixed or keyframed body would join everything hung from it), up to `articulated.max_assembly_bodies`, each eased toward the assembly's mass-weighted velocity at `assembly_damping_per_s` by impulses that add up to nothing. The hand drives only the body it holds, so without this the neighbours kept their momentum and rang against the hand. Each hold logs once `physical hands: damping a held articulated object's assembly within itself` with the body count and mass, and taking hold logs `physical hands: a hand took hold of an articulated object`. The `Hinged things` status row counts the articulated holds and says when one is in reach.

### The player's own body

The held object's centre is kept out of the player's body (`PlayerKeepOut` in `hold.rs`), so a held crate is not driven into the player's capsule. While the procedural torso poses the torso ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-arms)), the keep-out is the chest and belly capsules the wrist targets are kept out of (`body::procedural::torso_keep_out`); otherwise it is a cylinder of `keep_out_radius_m` around the player, the player's height tall.

While it is held, the held body (with the rest of its component, or every body of a ragdoll) and the player's bodies are excluded from colliding with each other through the physics system's pair filter (`physical_hands::carry_collision`), and the pairs collide again once the object has come clear of the player after release (`carry_collision.release_margin_m`, `carry_collision.release_grace_s`). The player's bodies are those the player owns and those in the player proxy's system group, less any a vehicle or vehicle part owns, since standing in a vehicle's stunt area puts the proxy in the vehicle's group. Each change in the paired bodies logs `physical hands: carry collision: the held bodies kept off the player changed`. Whether the character's movement casts consult the pair filter is not established: a carried fence has been seen stopping the player walking into it.

The keep-out moves only the centre, so a long prop's end could swing through the torso. Two keyframed capsules therefore follow the chest and belly keep-out as bodies of their own (`physics::drawn_body`), at `drawn_body.radius_share` of its radius so a reach for a holster meets nothing first, and with no head capsule. They are on the hand bodies' layer in the player's system group, so they never touch the player's capsule or the hand bodies, and keyframed, so a collision never moves them and they never push the player. Each pre-simulation step sets their velocities to land on the drawn capsules at the step's end (one more than `drawn_body.snap_m` off is put there). The carry collision leaves them out of the bodies it parts a held object from, so a held prop or ragdoll meets the torso. They exist while the procedural torso poses the body under the controllers.

A held object's contacts with the capsules give no strike haptics, sounds, or lag push ([impacts.md](impacts.md)). Instead each is noted (`drawn_body::note_contact`), and the object rests against the torso for as long as its broad-phase box comes within `drawn_body.reach_margin_m` of a capsule (`drawn_body::rests_against`): a resting contact is reported only as it starts, and the box alone reaches the torso well before a long prop does. While it rests there, the strain does not count its lag as caught (`grasp::StrainSample::own_body`), though the tear distance still applies. The log has `drawn body: a held object rests against Rico's torso capsules` and `came clear of` as each spell starts and ends. A held ragdoll's contacts are not observed this way, so a ragdoll stopped against the torso still counts toward its tear.

A holding hand does not probe or push off surfaces, and the other palm's probes pass through what the hands hold, so a free hand never pushes the body off the carried object. The reach rays do find a held object, so the second hand can take hold of it.

### Letting go and throwing

The last hand to let go of an object one hand lifts throws it through the one release a gun or a grenade takes ([physics-substrate.md, "Release velocities"](../body/physics-substrate.md#release-velocities)). A heavier object, and a release with no fresh rig motion, keep the velocity the hold gave it. A ragdoll is thrown as a carried one is, even while joined.

## Haptics

The cues are the contact category's ([haptics.md](haptics.md)). A palm meeting a surface plays `Event::PalmTouch`, weaker for a slower meeting (down to `haptics.min_touch_scale`). Taking hold of an object or grabbing a weapon plays `Event::Take`, letting go `Event::LetGo`, and a door's latch giving `Event::Unlatch`. While a hand presses or pulls it sends a level every tick in proportion to its load: a shoving palm (`Sustain::PalmPressure`) by the share of its push used or `haptics.pressure_resistance_share` of its resistance, whichever is more; a holding hand (`Sustain::HeldLoad`) by the share of its pull or twist used; and a held-back hand body (`Sustain::BodyPressure`) by its lag past the push deadband, full at `hand_bodies.pressure_full_lag_m`. Nothing below `haptics.min_pressure_load` is sent. A hand body's strikes are under [hits](#hits), and the impact sounds that go with touches and strikes are in [impacts.md](impacts.md#impact-sounds).

## Threading

The hand bodies are made, added, parked, driven, and destroyed only in the pre-simulation callback, on the game thread outside the step, and in the shutdown cleanup on the game thread. The callback is the one place that begins a step on the mod's physics-step clock (`jc3vr_core::published::Step`), which every step-stamped publication (the palms, the guns in the hands, the climb's palms' tick, the contact mutes, and the collider view's reads) ages by. For the callback, and for what decides whether a hand acts, a published pose goes stale once the body crate's freshness tuning's `physics.stale_steps` steps have run without a new one; counted in steps, a hitch that holds up the whole simulation ages nothing, so it never parks a hand or tears down a held gun's joints. For readers between the steps, the callback's results go stale after `step.stale_after_ms`. They pass through the hand bodies' own leaf lock, never the VR runtime's. The payload aborts on a panic, so the step's paths do not panic and handle a missing body, joint, or grip as a lost one.

The contact handler runs inside the physics step, where the world must not change: it reads the event and the other body's instance and queues the contact under a leaf lock of its own. The blows are dealt from the game thread's frame tick, outside the game's update, where the engine's damage and reactions are safe; dealt from the pre-simulation callback, a blow could knock a character into a ragdoll whose own callback would be registered while the engine walks the callbacks.

The shoves, knocks, and held objects' velocities change the main thread's engine state. The player aim control's post-simulation update, which the world object manager's queue may run on a worker thread, only notes that a palms' tick is due and the simulation time it covers (`tick::after_aim`). The tick runs on the game thread at the start of the frame's render update (`tick::main_thread_tick`, from the payload's [`Game::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::Game##func-UpdateRender) hook): after the simulation and before the VR frame waits on the compositor, and the next simulation's input tick reads its targets. Ticks noted before it runs are run as one, over their summed time. It acts only when the calling thread's role ([threading.md](../threading.md#the-guard)) is the game thread's outside its frame scopes; anywhere else it stands down and the `Palms` row counts a failure. The device poll's thread is not the reference, since the game's input pre-update runs on a CPU fragment worker ([the engine's input doc](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#where-the-device-poll-runs)). The push and the clamp are read through the physical hands' own lock and go stale after `state.stale_after_ms` without a tick. The grabs and releases are read on the input tick ([controllers.md](controllers.md#bindings-and-injection)) and acted on at the next palms' tick. The shoves and holds read and write the Havok motion directly between physics steps, as the engine's own melee impulse does, and are never made from inside the step or a Havok callback.

## Settings and readouts

The one setting is `controllers.physical_hands.bodies.strength`, the pause menu's `HAND STRENGTH` on `VR: CONTROLS`, which scales the hand bodies' caps and the holds' strength. Everything else is always on under the controller scheme, with its tuning in `physics.toml` (the hand bodies), `hands.toml` (the palms, shoves, holds, and sounds), and `pickup.toml` (the pickups). The grab view and the collider view ([grab-view.md](grab-view.md), [collider-view.md](collider-view.md)) draw what the hands reach and meet.

The debug UI's Physical hands feature has the `Palms`, `Push up`, and `Hinged things` status rows, and under Diagnostics the `Hand bodies` rows (the callback, each hand's body, the bodies made and the last error, the contacts, and each held gun's body), the player's system group, the push, and for each hand its collision-filter word, lag, speed, pull and twist shares, resets, joint, and the last blow.

The log has the lifecycle at info level: `hand bodies: registered the pre-simulation callback`, `the pre-simulation callback first fired` (a warning instead when it fires on another thread than the registration's), `created a hand body` with its body id, filter word, system group, and whether it listens for contacts, `added a hand body to the world`, `removed a hand body from the world`, and `destroyed a hand body` with the reason. Joints log `hand bodies: joined a hand to what it holds`, `could not join a hand to what it holds` (a warning), and `hand bodies: ended a joint` with the reason. The first contact logs `hand bodies: the first contact event arrived on the game thread` (or a warning naming both threads). Each blow logs `hand bodies: a hand struck a character` with the blow's impulse, the speeds, the solver's impulse, the hostility, the share of a knockdown (`strength`), the reaction impulse, the damage, and the character's thresholds. Once every `step.readout_period_ms` while a hand acts, `hand bodies: drive` at debug level gives each hand's lag, speed, shares, and the push.

### Interaction evidence

`pickup::diagnostics::status()` returns copied, bounded evidence of each hand's grip acquisition: its latest input observation (the captured `ControllerFrame::sequence`, the resolver's candidates, the reach's scan with each branch's offered or refused result, the latches, and the routed grabs), its last grip press, and its last few queued requests, each keyed by the input sequence, a per-hand grip episode, and a request ordinal rather than a timestamp. The simulation consumer records its actual result against that key, and an accepted hold can retain the body snapshot, the solver's requested velocities, and the drive call's result. The records add no engine reads. A missing scan means unavailable evidence, not an observed absence, and a commanded velocity or a successful drive call is not proof that the body lifted.

The slot is a leaf whose writers neither format nor log under the physical-hand or bindings guards. The Physical hands feature shows it under "Grip acquisition evidence", and while a tracking recording or the lookback collects, each acquisition stage is recorded as a `grip_acquisition` event ([recorder.md](../testing/recorder.md#recording)), built after the slot's lock is released. Nested values are Rust `Debug` text identified by the recording's build stamp, not a stable schema.
