# Single-pass collapse defects

Under single-pass stereo ([single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)) one walk renders both eyes side by side into a double-wide target, from a render camera centred between the eyes that carries eye 0's projection and no cant. Anything that takes a per-eye quantity from that camera, or pairs a per-eye quantity with a full-width one, is wrong in at least one eye. This page covers the passes that run once over the whole target; [single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md](single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md) covers the inputs of draws that render both eyes in one submission and the eye-camera buffer most pixel-shader rewrites share.

The engine's screen-size constant (staged in [`RenderEngine::m_FPGlobalConstData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##field-m_FPGlobalConstData)) is not a source of these defects: the mod's `ResizeBuffers` substitute sets it to the double-wide width, and the buffers it addresses are double-wide too (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.3). A 2x horizontal scale error is a 2x motion gain, so most of these defects show as content sliding over the world.

## Four ways content goes wrong

### 1. Draws that never get an eye viewport bound

A draw on an entry point nothing detours inherits whatever the slots hold, mostly the full viewport.

- GPU-indirect draws (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.2), which the near terrain patches and the main foliage path use. `single_pass.indirect_per_eye` re-issues them once per eye with both slots pinned. The instance counts live in a GPU buffer, so the re-issue gives the right size but no parallax unless the block or shader supplies a per-eye transform.
- Non-indexed world geometry. `Draw` is mostly the fullscreen passes', which reset to the full viewport, and that would stretch the decals, roads, and skidmarks across both halves. `single_pass.slot13_per_eye` routes an allowlist of passes (`GEOMETRY_SLOT13_PASSES`) through the per-eye re-issue, and a patched non-indexed draw is promoted to one two-instance draw. It is an allowlist because a fullscreen triangle and a decal box are identical at the draw call, and misclassifying a fullscreen pass is a visibly wrong frame, while a missing pass only stays stretched.

### 2. One eye-0 reconstruction basis over the whole double-wide target

A fullscreen pass that rebuilds world position from depth gets one basis while its quad spans both halves, so the error turns with the camera, and the passes that sample the sun cascade over the reconstructed positions make the shadows slide. The `PerspectiveFovInverse` consumers are a closed set (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.1). `single_pass.reconstruct_per_eye` splits the deferred resolve, with the atmospheric scattering always split beside it (splitting only the resolve leaves the scattering painting the error back), and the subsurface skin and the depth of field are always split.

`reconstruction::split_fullscreen_pass` (`jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::screen`) masks each run with a scissor rather than a half viewport, so the quad keeps its NDC-to-pixel mapping, and folds the full-target-to-eye NDC remap and the eye's turn into the substituted inverse (`reconstruction::eye_half_inverse`). A run that never masked has drawn the whole target, so the split stops after it rather than double-exposing an accumulating pass; the split reports `Split`, `Demoted`, or `NotTaken`, because only `NotTaken` means the pass still needs issuing. With `single_pass.fullscreen_camera_per_eye` each masked run also gets its eye's camera position and the matching cascade translation in the fragment globals (`eye_globals`). Compute work cannot be masked (a dispatch ignores scissor and viewport, and a texture UAV cannot select a sub-rectangle), so the depth of field's compute prepass runs whole-target on the first run (`reconstruction::DispatchPhase`), and the seam bleed of its blur is inherent.

The screen-space decals have the same defect outside that set: [`RenderBlockTypeSSDecal::Setup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeSSDecal##func-Setup) builds its basis inline, and the pixel shader derives its depth UV from its own clip position. `single_pass.ssdecal_per_eye` re-issues the block per eye with that eye's basis (`ss_decal::eye_view`) and splices a bias of the depth UV into the eye's half (`dxbc_stereo::bias_ssdecal_depth_uv`); the same UV feeds both the reconstruction, which wants the per-eye value, and the depth fetch, which wants the double-wide one. `single_pass.ssdecal_geometry_per_eye` reprojects the decal box.

The volumetric fog generation ([`RenderBlockFogVolume::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockFogVolume##func-Draw)) inverts the render context's [`m_ViewProjectionF`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::graphics_engine::RenderContext##field-m_ViewProjectionF) itself and ray-marches the sun cascade towards each reconstructed pixel. The collapse runs the block once per eye with the at-entry mask, giving the render context that eye's view-projection pre-multiplied by the inverse NDC remap, and puts it back after. The upsample and composite map whole textures and need no split.

#### SSAO, per eye in one run

[`RenderBlockSSAO::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockSSAO##func-Draw) builds the generation basis from the render context's projection and view, and the generation is also wrong in ways no basis fixes: its taps are isotropic in screen UV (twice the view wide), taps near the seam read the other eye, and its rotation comes from `SV_Position`. `single_pass.ssao_per_eye` fixes it without a split: `dxbc_stereo::localize_ambient_occlusion` takes the eye from the half the UV falls in, reads that eye's basis and rotation from a mod-owned buffer (`single_pass::ambient_occlusion`), places taps in the eye's UV, clamps samples to the half, and makes the pixel position eye-local; `localize_ambient_occlusion_filter` clamps the blur and composite. The temporal resolve is seeded fresh every stereo frame (the `m_FirstPass` force in `screen::ssao`), so the block keeps one run.

`single_pass.ssao_exact_sampling` makes every mode, multipass and the isolated eyes included, compute its SSAO coordinates the same way. In three places the occlusion samples on or next to a texel boundary (the generation's centre samples, the blur's odd taps, and the composite's bilinear weights), and the interpolated UV is not the same function of the pixel in a target twice as wide, so single-pass and an isolated eye disagree in stripes. With the switch, the UV is computed from the pixel position over sizes read from the texture (`snap_ambient_occlusion_uv` outside the collapse), the centre samples move off the boundary, the blur loads whole texels, and the composite blends in the shader, so the left eye agrees with an isolated eye to the bit wherever the inputs do.

#### Screen-space reflections, per eye in one run

[`RenderBlockScreenSpaceReflection::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockScreenSpaceReflection##func-Draw) marches each reflected ray through the render context's view and projection in the target's pixels and reads the hit from the previous frame's scene. None of those matrices is the basis a split substitutes, so a split leaves both runs projecting one eye-sized projection onto the double-wide depth. `single_pass.ssr_per_eye` traces both eyes in one run: `dxbc_stereo::localize_screen_space_reflection` takes the eye from the UV, rebuilds the view ray and reads the eye's matrices from a mod-owned buffer (`single_pass::screen_space_reflection`), marches against a horizontally halved target size so it stops at the eye's edge, and moves every depth sample into the eye's half. The previous view-projection is the engine's moved onto the eye's half, which is the lookup an isolated eye makes, since an isolated render's previous view-projection is the centred camera's too. `localize_reflection_blur` clamps the blur. The compute blur is a known gap ([single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)).

### 3. A projective screen UV against a double-wide buffer

The legacy `Water*` and `WaterBox*` family takes its reflection, refraction, and depth UV projectively from a CPU-staged matrix normalized over the viewport (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.3). The block re-issue (`jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::scene::water`) restages those rows per eye with one more bias, `u' = (u + eye) · 0.5`, and puts the type's rows back. It is not reprojected, because the geometry still rasterizes from the centre and the UV must describe where it landed.

#### The WaveWorks water

`NvWater*` builds its screen UV from `SV_Position` and the screen size, which is consistent under double-wide, but writes clip from a baked model-view-projection, so both eyes would see the centre view at the wrong depth, which a screenshot does not show. `single_pass.nvwater_per_eye` substitutes the eye's view and projection into the render context, re-runs [`NvWaterHighEndRenderBlock::Setup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::NvWaterHighEndRenderBlock##func-Setup) so the block rebakes, and re-issues `Draw` into that eye's half without the foam and wake passes. The second eye's [`WaveWorksSimulationStep`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::WaveWorksSimulationStep), the one non-idempotent call, is suppressed.

The planar reflection read divides the screen UV by `g_UVScale.x`, so the re-issue binds pixel-shader variants (`single_pass::water_pixel_variants`) that use the eye-local numerator `2u - eye`; the refraction, depth, and other lookups keep the full-target UV. The re-issue also moves the fragment globals' camera position to each eye, and `water_fragment_state` captures the pixel state the first eye's draw had bound (shadow maps, WaveWorks constants, sky lighting) and fills any slot the second eye's draw finds empty; without the sky lighting the second eye's water comes out about 2% darker.

#### The water box surface

The water-box surface grid (drawn by `NWater::DrawWaterBoxSurface` from inside `CNvWaterHighEndRenderBlock::Draw`) has no per-eye treatment. Like the whole legacy family it computes `clip = cb0[0..3] · (box_transform · position + cb0[4])`, through the full view-projection the remap does not cover, while its `cb0[4]` reference lets the remap claim it, so the eye offset is added and viewed from the centre: the water sits displaced by half the IPD, in the wrong direction. Reprojection is the right transform, but the family is off the allowlist, and reprojecting the geometry would also require rebuilding the projective UV rows of mechanism 3 from the eye's view-projection; the two changes are one, and doing either alone trades a known defect for a subtler one. The grid's draw also bypasses the water hook's re-issue.

### 4. A reduced-resolution target handed the scene's viewport

A target-blind split re-derives the halves from the scene viewport, so a draw into a shared quarter-resolution target (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §9) is magnified 2x and cropped, and the right eye's is clipped away. `single_pass.collapse_viewport_follows_target` splits from the record of the live bind instead; the scene record cannot follow the engine, or a half-resolution post target would mis-split the scene. The composites must not be split: they map the whole low-resolution texture over the whole target and blend, so re-issuing them would double-composite.

## The glare chain at the eye seam

The glare chain (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §3.3) runs about two dozen fullscreen draws over targets down to a sixteenth of the screen, each reading by screen UV. At the seam the blurs, downsamples, and upsamples read the other eye where an eye-sized target would clamp, and the levels accumulate, so a sun near one eye's inner edge brightens the other eye's inner half. `dxbc_stereo::localize_post_filter` clamps every filtered sample to the texel centres of the pixel's half of the sampled texture (measured with `resinfo`), clamps gathers a texel in, and zeroes loads outside the half. The chain draws with shared programs, so `single_pass::post_filters` builds each qualifying program's rewrite as a variant at creation, and the `Draw` detour binds it only inside [`GlareEffect::Apply`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::GlareEffect##func-Apply). The composite's bloom lookups are rewritten at creation (`localize_composite_bloom`, on top of `localize_screen_overlays`). The eye boundary falls on a texel boundary at every level only while the eye width is a multiple of sixteen.

## The clustered froxel grid, per eye

Uncorrected, the grid is built with one eye's projection over the double-wide tile count, so every local light lands in the wrong tiles, for the deferred resolve and for the forward-lit block types that sample it (`foliage` reads it a frame early). Engine [lighting-shadow-pipeline.md](../../engine/rendering/lighting-shadow-pipeline.md) §4.1 and §4.2 describe the grid. `single_pass.clustered_per_eye` rides the resolve split: each run narrows the assignment viewport to its half of the tile grid, rebuilds the geometry transform from its eye's projection, makes the tile bounds affine in the absolute tile index, and suppresses the second run's clear. The halves compose because the clear is the only whole-target step, the assignment blend is commutative over disjoint tiles, and the fill is per tile. Each eye's lights are assigned from that eye by writing its offset into the translation row of the uploaded `cb2` view (`clustered_lighting::split::eye_assignment_view`), where the engine's own zeroed translation goes.

The split declines, and logs once, when the seam would not fall on a whole tile column, when the bound target is not the grid it sized for, or when there are no lights. When an off-scene dispatch passes the assignment's check but its resolve proves unmaskable, the split stands down for that graphics context, since otherwise half the shared grid would stay unfilled on the main scene.

## The shipped settings under single-pass

The capture profile pins or turns off several things a player runs with ("Running the shipped settings" in [single-pass-validation.md](single-pass-validation.md) puts them back).

- FSR, run once over the double-wide target, would scale the motion vectors to the double-wide width, reproject one history across the seam, and add no parallax correction. `single_pass.fsr_per_eye` resolves each half through its own eye's context ([fsr.md](../rendering/fsr.md)).
- Foveation, laid out for one eye, would put a single fovea on the seam. `single_pass.foveation_per_eye` lays it out per half ([foveation.md](foveation.md)).
- Exposure is metered once per frame over both eyes, so both get one exposure, as multipass's eyes get eye 0's.

Neither the motion blur nor the depth of field runs while a VR session runs, whatever the `post_fx` switches say, because both smear the view with the camera's motion and focus, which in the headset are the player's own head and gaze. `clear_blurs` (`jc3vr_view::hooks::camera`) zeroes the camera contexts' blur factors, and the post hooks (`jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::post::post_effects`) skip [`MotionBlurEffect::Apply`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::MotionBlurEffect##func-Apply) (which also carries the heat haze and the SMAA T2x composite) and answer [`PostEffectsManager::IsBokehActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::PostEffectsManager##func-IsBokehActive) with false, dropping the depth of field's own passes. [`DepthOfFieldEffect::Apply`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::DepthOfFieldEffect##func-Apply) itself still runs, since its composite is the tonemap, so `post_fx.skip_dof` is ignored in VR.
