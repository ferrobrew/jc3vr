# The character ragdoll

A character's ragdoll is a set of Havok bodies, one per ragdoll bone, joined by ragdoll constraints. It exists only while the character wants a ragdoll preset ([`m_CurrentRagdollParamsID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_CurrentRagdollParamsID) not zero): the animation graph's `SetRagdoll` state task sets the preset on entering a state and clears it on leaving, and the pre-physics pass's ragdoll sync, on [`m_SyncRagdollState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_SyncRagdollState), makes the ragdoll and adds its bodies in the character's proxy system group for a non-zero preset and destroys it for zero. For the player that is most of the time no ragdoll at all. While it exists, what changes is who moves it. This doc covers how to tell a ragdolling character, how to reach its bodies, what puts a character into its ragdoll and takes it out again, and how the game marks the characters it protects. The hit reaction that knocks a character down is in [melee-and-damage.md](../gameplay/melee-and-damage.md#the-hit-reaction).

## The ragdoll instance

The character holds its ragdoll in [`m_Ragdoll`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_Ragdoll), a shared pointer to a [`RagdollInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ragdoll::RagdollInstance) (`NAnimationSystem::CRagdollInstanceImpl`). The instance keeps the physics world its bodies live in and the Havok ragdoll itself ([`hknpRagdoll`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpRagdoll)), whose [`m_bodyIds`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpRagdoll##field-m_bodyIds) lists one body id per ragdoll bone, in the order of the ragdoll's skeleton, with an invalid id for a bone without a body.

Each body gets an engine instance of its own whose owner is the ragdoll's owner, the character; that a physics query hitting a ragdoll body therefore reports the character as the object hit is unverified. The instance's other members (the bone-to-body maps, the closest-body and impulse helpers, the activation of sleeping bodies) are known by name and not bound.

## Ragdolling

While the animation drives a character, its ragdoll's displacement is controlled by its owner: the bodies follow the animated pose and are not free. [`Character::IsRagdolling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsRagdolling) is true when the character has a ragdoll whose displacement its owner does not control: the bodies simulate as dynamic bodies, collide, and fall, and the character follows them. A knocked-down character, a thrown or tethered one, and a dead one ragdoll this way. A preset that keeps any bone keyframed leaves the ragdoll under the owner's control, so the character does not ragdoll and the graph never sees it, while the preset's other bodies are dynamic and driven toward the animated pose by constraint motors: the grapple hang's preset (`GRAPPLE_HANG`, the head, neck, spine, shoulders, and arms keyframed, the hips and legs dynamic) and the canopy's (`PARACHUTING`, the lower spine dynamic too). The ragdoll's blend weight ([`RagdollInstance::m_BlendWeight`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ragdoll::RagdollInstance##field-m_BlendWeight)) sets how much of the simulated pose the post-physics pass ([`Character::PostPhysicsUpdateRagdoll`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-PostPhysicsUpdateRagdoll)) writes over the animated pose, before the finalize pass's HumanIK solve; a new ragdoll starts at zero, and the preset's sync never raises it. The canopy's own ragdoll update raises it under the canopy, at half a unit a second, and lowers it at three.

A dead character is not a ragdoll at once: its death animation may play first, and until it ragdolls its bodies are not free. A settled ragdoll's bodies go to sleep with the rest of the world; they stay dynamic. The instance wakes them body by body when the game needs them moving; that Havok's own velocity and impulse setters wake a sleeping ragdoll body too is unverified.

The hit reaction's ragdoll path checks ragdolling the same way: a character already ragdolling takes a hit's impulse onto its ragdoll for some damage types and otherwise ignores it.

## Into the ragdoll

A non-player character goes into its ragdoll when a hit's scaled impulse reaches its knockdown threshold: [`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction) queues the hit reaction's ragdoll act (`ACT_HITREACT_RAGDOLL`, unbound) and the impulse onto the ragdoll. The faction relation plays no part in it. Two things keep a character standing whatever the impulse:

- the state bit [`SB_SKIP_HITREACTION_RAGDOLL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_SKIP_HITREACTION_RAGDOLL), which sends the hit to the stumble instead;
- a damage-state flag on the character ([`m_DamageFlags`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_DamageFlags)) that has the reaction return before doing anything.

The reaction moves the character without taking health; called without a damage message it knocks a character down without harming it. The ragdoll and the get-up are animation-graph states; what the character's AI does while it lies in its ragdoll is not established.

## Out of the ragdoll

The get-up is the animation graph's own: the `NGetUpFromRagdoll` state task runs it, entered from the ragdoll states through the graph's conditions, among them `CRagdollIsSettledCondition` (the ragdoll has come to rest), `CRagdollingTimerCondition` (the time spent ragdolling against a threshold from the graph's data), and `CBlockedGetUpCondition`. None of these is bound, and the thresholds are data the graph loads.

The two timers live in the character's control parameters, in its ragdolling controller parameters ([`ControlParameters::m_RagdollingControllerParameters`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::control_parameters::ControlParameters##field-m_RagdollingControllerParameters)): the total ragdolling time, the settled ragdolling time, and the ragdoll's average limb speed. The ragdolling state task (`NRagdolling`) adds each update's time to the total while the ragdoll's displacement is not its owner's, sets it below zero while it is, and refreshes the average limb speed from the ragdoll instance's average speed of its bodies relative to each other; on entering a ragdolling state whose data asks for it, it resets the total below zero, the settled time to zero, and the limb speed to the largest float. `CRagdollingTimerCondition` compares the total with its threshold, above or below as its data says. `CRagdollIsSettledCondition` adds each update's time to the settled time while the average limb speed is under its speed threshold, the character's height over the ground under its height threshold, and the pose-matching error under its limit, resets it to zero otherwise, and holds once the settled time passes its time threshold; for the player it also holds early when the body lies aligned with the surface beneath it. So a ragdoll whose limbs move together, as a body carried whole does, counts as settled however fast it moves as a whole, and the total timer runs whatever the ragdoll does. The layout and both conditions' reads are bound and verified; the state task's writes, and its entry flag, are not. While the get-up runs the character has [`SB_IS_GETTING_UP_FROM_RAGDOLL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_GETTING_UP_FROM_RAGDOLL), and its ragdoll goes back under its owner's control. A dead character never gets up.

The ragdoll's collision listener (`CRagdollCollisionListener`) gathers its bodies' hardest contacts and reports them to the character as impacts. That these impacts harm a ragdolling character through its impact thresholds, so that a body thrown hard against a wall can die of it, is unverified.

## Protected characters

The game marks the characters a mission depends on: [`m_IsMissionCriticalCharacter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_IsMissionCriticalCharacter), which the character rule that spawns the character copies from its data. The interaction graph and the AI's vehicle entry read it; the hit reaction does not, so a mission-critical character ragdolls like any other. Beside it sit the character's melee invulnerability ([`m_InvulnerableToMelee`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_InvulnerableToMelee)) and its invulnerability to other characters ([`m_InvulnerableToOtherAI`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_InvulnerableToOtherAI)).

A character a scripted sequence drives has [`m_InSequence`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_InSequence) set until the sequence lets it go.
