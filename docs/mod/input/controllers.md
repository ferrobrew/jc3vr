# Motion controllers as built

The motion-controller implementation as it stands: the input foundation, scheme selection, mode detection, the bindings and their injection, the aim-state pin, controller-owned hands, per-hand aiming, the sight lines and their tuning tool, the world-space overlay layer, posture and roomscale, the weapon handling, vehicles, scopes, and the finger curls. The design and its reasons are in the plan of record, [controllers-and-roomscale.md](controllers-and-roomscale.md), whose "Implementation status" section maps each part to its settings and to its section here, lists the unverified assumptions of milestone 1, and holds the headset test checklist. The unverified assumptions of the later parts are under "Unverified" at the end of this page. Engine facts are in the engine docs linked from each section.

The code is in `payload/src/controllers/` (everything built on the controller snapshot), `payload/src/vr/input/` (the OpenXR side), and `payload/src/ui/controllers/` (the Controllers tab of the debug UI). Every setting is in the `controllers` section of the mod's config (`ControllersConfig`), and the Controllers tab shows each setting and a live readout of each subsystem. The master switch `controllers.enabled` gates the per-frame input sync and the publication of the controller snapshot, so with it off every subsystem is inert. Each subsystem that changes how the character behaves also acts only under the controller scheme, so the mod behaves as it did before controllers existed whenever no controller is in use.

The defaults are the full `Vehicles and advanced` preset (see "Test controls and saved tuning"), so every subsystem, including the wingsuit's arm steering and the parachute's handles, works out of the box.

## Test controls and saved tuning

The Controllers tab offers `Input only`, `Hands and aim`, `Weapon handling`, `Roomscale`, `Vehicles and advanced`, and `Gamepad baseline` presets. Presets change feature switches while preserving numeric tuning, handedness, grip offsets, and sight entries. Hand remapping remains consistent from `Hands and aim` through the later presets. The selected preset is marked modified when its owned switches differ. Optional stick-click weapon cycling, hand-aimed grenade fallback, finger source, and finger drive-all settings remain unchanged. `Gamepad baseline` disables the controller and roomscale features.

The controller settings, the shared VR turn settings, and the body dimensions are saved in the settings file with the rest of the configuration, whenever they change and without a save control ([settings.md](../settings.md)). The load validates them: a group whose values are out of range or inconsistent resets to its defaults, and a failure elsewhere in the controller settings keeps the per-weapon sight calibration.

The diagnostic capture section records an opt-in history of up to 120 samples, at 30 samples per second on average: samples are scheduled at a fixed interval and each is taken on the first rendered frame at or after its time. A sample is taken after the engine's render update, so its late-correction readout is evaluated at the same sub-frame fraction that frame rendered with. Its capture action writes a uniquely named file to the session's `controller_diagnostics` folder (`sessions/<timestamp>/controller_diagnostics/`, beside the log of the run it diagnoses) without replacing earlier captures, through a temporary file, so that a failed write leaves no truncated capture. Each sample includes the scheme, mode, action set, frame age, tracking validity, hand ownership and IK readouts, late-correction acceptance or rejection reason, wrist positions, aim and sight information, and roomscale offset and requested velocities. The fields are independently read subsystem snapshots, not an atomic simulation snapshot. File writes run outside the UI thread. Both file-writing workers participate in payload shutdown before unload.

## Input foundation

The OpenXR actions are in two layers. OpenXR gives every action exactly one owning set, so the layout is:

- `hands`, always active: the grip and aim poses of both hands, the haptic output, and the raw inputs, named for the physical inputs of the Valve Index controller (triggers, grips, sticks, trackpads, face buttons, the system button, and their touches). The published snapshot is read from these, whatever the mode, so everything built on it (the bindings, the gestures, the finger curls, the chords, input recovery, scheme detection, and the pointers) does not depend on the active set. Their localized names ask the player not to rebind them.
- `global`, always active, and one mode set at a time: the semantic actions, named for what they do in the game. The mode sets are `on_foot` (on foot, swimming, falling, and the parachute), `wingsuit`, `land_vehicle` (cars, motorcycles, boats, and vehicles of no resolved class), `helicopter`, `plane`, and `menu` (the game's menus and the native map). Falling and the parachute share `on_foot` because their tables read the same inputs, so a grapple jump does not switch sets. A vehicle seat selects its class's set, driver or passenger; the actions only one seat uses (`accelerate` and the passenger's `fire`, for example) share an input and are told apart by the seat.

Every set has priority 0: a runtime ignores a lower-priority set's binding on an input that a higher-priority active set also binds, and the raw and semantic actions bind the same inputs. Every action has both hands as subaction paths and is read per hand, so the hand a press came from stays known after the player remaps it. The runtime's binding interface (SteamVR's or xrizer's) remaps per set, and there the player sees game meanings such as "Reload (hold: holster)"; the localized names mention the overloads that the game or the mod resolves within one action.

Suggested bindings are shipped for the Index controller profile and the Oculus Touch profile. The raw inputs bind by position (the left Touch controller's X, Y, and menu button stand for A, B, and the system button). The semantic actions' defaults (`vr/input/semantic/defaults.rs`) are written per action as a side (the dominant hand, the off-hand, both hands, or a fixed hand) and a component named by its position, and lay out the tables of the plan ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Per-mode bindings"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#per-mode-bindings)). The face buttons mirror the gamepad by side: on foot the dominant A jumps and the dominant B uses and enters vehicles (the gamepad's Y), the off-hand A reloads (X) and the off-hand B releases the tethers (B); in a vehicle the dominant B exits, the off-hand B is nitrous, and the off-hand A the handbrake; in the wingsuit the off-hand A closes the suit and the dominant B boosts. The dominant stick click detonates. The off-hand trackpad of an Index controller selects weapon slots: a press latches the quadrant under the thumb and holds that slot's select action until the release, up for the pistols, right for the two-handed slot, left for the special slot, and down for the explosives, as on the gamepad's d-pad. The same press opens the weapon wheel, which a held press steers by pointing the hand, and the dominant stick held up (`weapon_wheel`) opens it too ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)). A few actions ship unbound: `open_commlink`, `recenter_view`, and `toggle_debug_panel` (the stick-click chords stay the built-in way) and `throw_grenade` (grenades come from their pouch). Changing the defaults changes the fingerprint below, so a payload with new defaults needs a game restart. The left-handed table is generated from the right-handed one by moving every dominant-hand and off-hand row to the other hand; rows on both hands or on a fixed hand (the menu's previous and next pages, its focus and map recenter) keep their sides. The table is chosen from `controllers.dominant_hand` when the actions are created, and suggestions are fixed once the sets are attached, so changing hands takes effect for the runtime's defaults only after a game restart. OpenXR has no per-user presets, so the mirrored table is the left-handed preset: Monado follows it, and SteamVR follows it while the player has no custom binding for the game; a player with one goes back to the default binding in SteamVR's binding interface after switching hands, so no separate SteamVR binding file ships. Changing the hand in the pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page or the Controllers tab says so beside the setting (the page's "BUTTON LAYOUT" readout reads, for example, "RIGHT-HANDED UNTIL RESTART"), and the restart notice below shows until the game restarts or the hand is changed back. The mod's hand roles follow the hand the attached bindings were suggested for until that restart (`ControllersConfig::acting_hand`, read from `vr::input::bound_hand`): the tables, the gun hand and the grapple's ray, the wrist readouts, the mirrored sticks and levers of the vehicles, and the rest, so that the roles never disagree with the buttons. With no bindings attached they follow the configured hand. The masks and the summary read the runtime's bound sources, so they place the actions where the runtime actually put them.

Each frame the per-hand state of `global`'s and the active mode set's actions is published as a `SemanticFrame` beside the snapshot, read in the same sync, and the binding tables read it (see "Bindings and injection"). Each action also carries a digital state: a boolean action's value, or a float action past the press thresholds of the input it reads by default (the trigger's, the grip's, or the trackpad's), with hysteresis, so a grip action keeps the mod's force thresholds wherever it is bound. Its press and release edges accumulate between input ticks as the raw buttons' do. The Controllers tab's "Semantic actions" section shows it with each set's active flag and the runtime's bound sources for every action. The bound sources need the session, so the game thread queries them inside the frame's input update (see [threading.md](../threading.md)) and publishes the results, reduced for each action to the physical inputs it reads on each hand, which the bindings' masks act on, and matched to the known components, which the controls summary draws ([controls-summary.md](controls-summary.md#glyphs-from-the-bound-sources)). They are queried in full after the interaction profiles change, on the frame after the controls summary is rebuilt, and once a second while the section is open; per set the first time it is active; and the active sets once a second while the summary's panel shows, because a rebinding within one profile raises no event.

`XR_EXT_hand_tracking` is enabled when the runtime offers it, and `XR_EXT_hand_joints_motion_range` beside it when offered too; with `controllers.input.hand_tracking` set, the hand trackers are created and each hand's joints are published in the snapshot, located with the controller-conforming motion range when the instance has that extension. Whether the persisted instance was created with each of the two extensions is stashed with its handle, so a reinject re-wraps it with the same set.

Once per rendered frame, inside the VR frame loop, `vr::input` syncs the actions, locates both hands' grip and aim spaces at the frame's predicted display time, re-bases them against the recenter baseline exactly as the eye views are re-based, and publishes a `ControllerFrame`. Poses are in the cockpit frame: the OpenXR LOCAL space re-based by the recenter baseline, with OpenXR axes. A controller pose therefore composes into the world through the same transform as the head. The trigger, the grip, and the trackpad are also thresholded into digital presses with hysteresis. The trigger press reads the trigger value (`controllers.input.trigger_press`). The grip press reads whichever grip input the hand's interaction profile provides: the squeeze force on the Index (`controllers.input.squeeze_force_press`), whose capacitive squeeze value is full whenever the hand rests round the handle, so only a deliberate squeeze presses; and the squeeze value elsewhere (`controllers.input.squeeze_press`), with a press threshold high enough that a resting hand does not press. The choice is made per hand from the bound profile's suggested bindings, not from the values: a profile the runtime emulates has no force of its own and reads the value. The squeeze value still drives the finger curls. The trackpad press reads the trackpad force (`controllers.input.trackpad_press`) and is split into a left-half and a right-half press, the half decided by where the thumb is when the press starts (a press on the centre line names neither) and kept until the release. The Controllers tab's "Input" section shows each hand's squeeze value, force, which of them presses, and the trackpad force beside the sliders. A hand whose joints cannot be located (hand tracking is on but `xrLocateHandJointsEXT` fails) keeps its poses and inputs and goes without joints for that frame.

The snapshot stands for the controllers' state only while the frame loop keeps sampling. It is cleared when the session stops running, when a frame cannot begin, and when the controllers are disabled, and a snapshot older than half a second is treated as absent even if nothing cleared it. That bound sits above a main-thread hitch, which delays the publish and the input tick alike. With no snapshot the bindings inject nothing and the scheme falls back to the gamepad.

Render frames outpace the engine's roughly 30 Hz sim tick, so a button pressed and released between two ticks would be invisible to a consumer that compared states tick to tick. The snapshot's edge latch accumulates press and release edges across publishes until they are taken. A change of mode set does not interrupt it, since the raw inputs come from the always-active `hands` set. The input tick is the single taker. The first snapshot after a clear has nothing to compare against and latches no edges, so a button held across a clear is not pressed a second time.

The OpenXR instance and session persist across an uninject and reinject, and action sets can be attached to a session only once. On uninject the action handles are stashed in the process environment with a fingerprint of the action layout (every set and action name with its type) and of every binding it suggested (for each shipped profile, the raw inputs' bindings and the semantic suggestions for the configured dominant hand), and on reinject they are re-wrapped. The suggestions are part of the fingerprint because a runtime takes them only before the sets are attached: a payload that re-wrapped the handles under different suggestions would keep the old ones without a sign. A payload whose layout, suggestions, or dominant hand differ from the stashed one cannot re-wrap the handles and cannot attach new actions to the persisted session, so its controllers stay unavailable until the game restarts; bring-up reports this and puts the stash back for a payload with the matching layout.

Bring-up records why a restart is needed (`vr/input/restart.rs`): the stash was made for another layout, the stash differs only in the dominant hand (its fingerprint matches the other hand's), or the session refused the sets because it already has some attached. A changed `controllers.dominant_hand` while the input is attached is the fourth reason, which leaves the controllers working with the other hand's layout. While any of them holds, a head-locked notice panel (`hud/restart_notice.rs`) shows ahead of and below the view in the headset, and so on the desktop mirror, saying "Restart the game to use the motion controllers" or "Restart the game to apply the dominant hand" with the reason under it; the debug UI shows the same as a banner at the bottom of its surface. The notice reads the published input status and the config, so the draw task can ask for it without the runtime lock. If the action spaces cannot be created after the actions are attached, the handles are stashed rather than destroyed, since the session would not accept new ones, and the next reinject retries. The actions are created and attached whether or not the controllers are enabled, because attachment is possible only once per session.

Haptic pulses go through a queue that any thread can write. The frame loop flushes it once per rendered frame. OpenXR plays one vibration per controller at a time, so requests between two flushes merge into one pulse per hand at the strongest amplitude. The requesters are the weapon haptics (see "Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand"), the grenade fuse, and the true reload.

### Action schema

The sets, their actions, and the actions' types are the action schema. The runtime keys a player's saved bindings on the names, so a name never changes meaning: a rename is a removal and an addition. A unit test holds a snapshot of every set, action, and type. Changing it bumps the schema version, which is also the OpenXR application version, and adds an entry here. Adding actions is a minor change; removing, retyping, or moving one breaks saved bindings and needs a release note telling players to reset their bindings.

- Version 1: the raw inputs move into `hands`, the identical `onfoot`, `vehicle`, `airborne`, and `ui` sets of raw inputs are replaced by `global` and the six semantic mode sets, and `hands` gains `squeeze_click` (the grip as a boolean the runtime thresholds, read only by the self-check). This breaks every saved binding once, and a payload with this layout cannot re-wrap an older payload's stashed actions, so the game must be restarted.
- Version 2: `on_foot` gains `weapon_wheel`, a float suggested on the dominant stick's vertical axis, which opens the weapon wheel when pushed up ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)). It is an addition, the minor kind of change.

### Input self-check

Once per session (and per reinject), at the first focused frame at which a hand has an interaction profile, `vr/input/self_check.rs` logs one block at info level under the `vr` target, starting `input: self-check`, and the "Semantic actions" section shows it. It is on by default (`controllers.input.self_check`) and the section can run it again, for example after entering a mode whose set was inactive the first time. What to look for:

- The runtime line and each hand's profile path say what was tested.
- Under "one input in two active sets at priority 0", every line pairs a semantic action with the raw action bound to the same input on that hand. Both should read `active`. A semantic side that reads `INACTIVE` while the raw side is active means the runtime gave the input to one set only, and the semantic layer cannot share inputs with `hands` on that runtime.
- Under "grip as a boolean and as a float", `squeeze_click` (a boolean on the Index grip force) and `squeeze_force` should both be active on an Index controller. An inactive boolean means the runtime does not threshold the force for a boolean action, so grip actions must stay floats.
- Under "bound sources", each sampled action should list its default input with a localized name, in the inactive sets as in the active ones. `no sources` for an inactive set only means the runtime answers for active sets alone; an error means the call fails through the runtime or wineopenxr.
- After the block, pressing and releasing each paired input once logs an `input: self-check finding` line: "both report" is the expected result, with similar frame counts. For the grip, the finding gives the force at which the runtime's boolean pressed and released, beside the mod's own thresholds.

## Scheme selection

The controller scheme and the gamepad scheme switch on the most recent deliberate input while `controllers.scheme` is `Auto`. A controller button press (a capacitive touch does not count), a trigger or grip press, or a stick or trackpad past `controllers.input.stick_deadzone` selects the controller scheme. A gamepad button, trigger, or stick, or any keyboard or mouse input, selects the gamepad scheme. Input from both in the same frame, or from neither, keeps the current scheme. Without a controller snapshot, `Auto` is always the gamepad scheme. `Controllers` and `Gamepad` pin the scheme regardless of whether a controller is present or active.

`controllers.scheme` defaults to `Controllers`, as part of the full gameplay preset. `Controllers` pins the controller scheme while a controller is present, so a keyboard hotkey or a touch of the gamepad does not take it away; with no controller frame it falls back to the gamepad scheme, so a player who never connects motion controllers keeps the game's own behaviour. `Gamepad` pins the gamepad scheme regardless.

The observation runs once per rendered frame, right after the snapshot is published. Gamepad input is read from XInput directly, and keyboard and mouse input from `GetLastInputInfo`, which the OS updates on every hardware input event. The engine's own effectors are not used for this, because once the bindings inject controller input into them they no longer tell the devices apart. While the mod's panel is up, keyboard and mouse input drives the panel and does not count.

## Body dimensions

The player's dimensions are the `[body]` section of the settings file ([settings.md](../settings.md)), which the calibration on the pause menu's `VR: BODY` page writes. Every field is optional and all values are in metres. A missing field takes the generic adult dimension, an unknown one is skipped and logged, and dimensions that cannot describe a body (`BodyDimensions::validate`) reset to the generic adult ones. The character's own proportions are an estimate from his stated height (`BodyDimensions::CHARACTER_ESTIMATE`), except for his arms, which the hands measure from the skeleton (see "Hands", "Arm reach").

The section has one field without a default, `seated_eye_height`: the head's height above the STAGE floor while the player sits upright in their chair, below the standing eye height. While it is set, it is where the seated eye reference starts and what the automatic posture's thresholds sit against ([Posture and roomscale](#posture-and-roomscale), "Posture" and "Head height"). Without it, the key is left out of the file.

The file can be written by hand or by the calibration on the pause menu's `VR: BODY` page ([pause-menu.md](../pause-menu.md#body-calibration)), which measures two things in the headset: the standing eye height (the head above the STAGE floor), or the seated eye height when calibrating seated, and the distance between the controllers' grip poses in a T-pose. Seated, the height is estimated from the arm span, which is about the height for most adults (the grip span over one less the fingertip share of both hands), and the player can correct it before saving; everything else is derived from the height as below. `controllers/body/calibration/derive.rs` derives the rest with generic adult proportions, the Drillis and Contini segment ratios: the height is the eye height divided by the eye-height ratio; the arm span is the grip span plus, on each side, the part of the hand beyond the grip (the grip pose is the centre of the closed hand, about three tenths of the hand's length from the wrist); the shoulder height, shoulder width, and hand scale with the height; and the upper arm and forearm split what the arm span leaves once the shoulders and hands are taken out, in the ratio of their standard lengths. `BodyDimensions::GENERIC_ADULT` follows the same height, shoulder, and hand ratios, but takes its arm segments straight from the tables' per-height lengths, which with the shoulders and hands add up to more than its arm span. The calibration's segments add up to the measured span instead, so for the generic adult's own measurements they come out about a sixth shorter than the defaults. Only the debug UI and the review step show the segment lengths; nothing else reads them yet. An eye height outside a standing adult's range is refused, since it most likely means a seated player or a wrong floor, and so is an arm span too short for the height (arms not spread) or longer than any adult's; a span well over the height is accepted, since the controllers cannot be further apart than the arms reach, and the review flags it (see [the pause menu doc](../pause-menu.md#body-calibration)). The values are rounded to the millimetre and validated before the file is written, through a temporary file so that a failed write leaves the old file whole, and the new dimensions apply at once.

The remap is a set of pure functions: the hands' offsets from the shoulders scale by the ratio of the arm spans about estimated shoulders (`controllers.body.remap_hands`, on by default), unless the measured remap replaces it (`controllers.hands.reach.measured_remap`, see "Hands", "Arm reach"), and the eye height and vertical head travel scale by the ratio of the heights (`controllers.body.remap_head`, on by default; it moves the view for every player). The head remap reaches the view through the posture (see "Posture and roomscale").

## Mode detection

The detector runs once per engine input tick, from the post-call detour on [`InputDeviceManager::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_device_manager::InputDeviceManager##func-Update), on the main thread outside the world update. It reads a snapshot of the engine state and resolves the plan's rules over it ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Mode detection"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#mode-detection)), first match wins:

1. A menu is up: `ui`. The signals are the game clock's stop flag ([`Clock::m_Stop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/clock::Clock##field-m_Stop)), which covers the pause menu, the commlink, and the other pause reasons; the UI blocker bit ([`InputSystemBlocker::Ui`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::InputSystemBlocker##variant-Ui)) of the player action map, which covers screens such as the map that do not pause; and the mod's own panel.
2. A deferred teleport is in flight ([`GameWorld::IsTeleporting`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game_world::GameWorld##vfunc-IsTeleporting)), or the character is not readable (no character, no control parameters, or no animation instance in the first frames after a spawn): hold the current mode.
3. Seated in a vehicle, including the entry and exit animations ([`Character::IsInVehicleAttachState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInVehicleAttachState)): `vehicle`. The class is resolved in the order helicopter, plane, motorcycle, car, and sea, and the seat from the vehicle's local-player seat, which selects the driver bindings. The class predicates walk the interaction graph, so they run only while the attach state holds.
4. Grapple traversal (the pre-reel, reeling in, the wind-up to a hang, and reeled in on an attached wire): the gameplay mode holds, and the traversal is flagged for the hand tiers.
5. to 7. The wingsuit, the parachute, and falling: `airborne`, with a variant for each.
8. and 9. Swimming and everything else: `onfoot`, with a swim variant.

The mode selects the semantic mode set: `onfoot` and the falling and parachute variants of `airborne` select `on_foot`, the wingsuit `wingsuit`, a vehicle its class's set (`helicopter`, `plane`, or `land_vehicle` for the rest), and `ui` the `menu` set.

A change between gameplay modes must persist for `controllers.mode.debounce_ticks` ticks (two by default) before it takes effect. Entering and leaving `ui` is immediate, because the UI signals do not flicker and a late switch would send a menu press to the gameplay bindings. The detector selects the OpenXR action set whenever the controllers are enabled, and changes nothing the game sees. The headpose sim keeps its own on-foot test.

## Bindings and injection

On every input tick, after the device poll and before the game reads its actions, the bindings read the tick's semantic actions (the latest `SemanticFrame` and the edges since the last tick), evaluate the current mode's table over them, and inject the result. The gestures, grabs, and chords read the raw snapshot instead, reduced to logical controls (deadzoned sticks and triggers, per-hand buttons with their edges, and the grab volume each hand is in). The bindings inject only while `controllers.bindings.enabled` is set, the controller scheme is in effect, a controller is active, the game's device poll runs, and the mod's panel is down, so a real gamepad under the gamepad scheme is never fought. The `turn` action (the dominant stick's horizontal axis by default) drives the body turn, which is handed to the headpose's VR turn scheme (`headpose.vr_turn`) rather than injected, except in the helicopter and the plane, whose tables read their own yaw and rudder actions there.

Each table names the actions of its mode's set, never a hand or a button, so it acts on whatever the player bound them to. A press counts on either hand. The actions get their deadzone from the input they read by default: the trigger deadzone for a throttle, the move deadzone for a stick or a stick axis. The default bindings follow the plan's per-mode tables ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Per-mode bindings"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#per-mode-bindings)), whose right and left are the dominant hand and the off-hand (`controllers.dominant_hand`), so a left-handed layout mirrors every gameplay binding. The layout is mirrored, and a gun rides the hand that holds it: the dual-wield pistols one per hand (see "Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand"), and any other weapon the hand that drew it, took it off the ground, or was handed it, which for a draw the game starts on its own is the dominant hand ([physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md)). Only the holding hand's trigger fires a gun. The move action's forward is the head's facing, or the off-hand controller's pointing direction when `controllers.locomotion.direction` is `OffHand`. Out of the driver's seat the throttle actions are not read, and the fire actions fire the hand weapon or the mounted gun.

Where one input means different things by context, each meaning is its own action with the same default binding, and the table reads only the one that fits: `retract_tethers` on the off-hand trigger, or `retract_tethers_alt` on the off-hand stick click while that trigger fires a pistol (`fire_off_hand_pistol`); `next_weapon` on the same stick click only when weapon cycling is on and not dual-wielding. The off-hand trigger fires a gun in the off-hand whatever the weapon, so a single gun the hold put there moves the retract to the stick click too ([physical-weapons.md, "Firing"](physical-weapons.md#firing)).

What the mod claims of a hand is masked from the semantic actions bound there, by the physical inputs the runtime reports each action bound to (the bound sources, matched against the inputs of the shipped profiles; until a set's sources have been read, its default bindings stand in). A masked action reads as released on that hand. A grip press latched to a grab masks that hand's grip for the whole press, so a holster draw never also grapples. With a pistol in the off-hand, its trigger is masked from everything but `fire_off_hand_pistol`. In a menu, the native UI pointer's trigger is masked from everything but `pointer_select`, which the page actions do not read. The debug panel chord masks the left stick click and the right B once it fires, and input recovery masks a whole hand. An action bound to a stick click reads that hand's click as the stick-click chord arbitrates it, rather than its own state. A source the mod does not recognise is masked only with its whole hand. The yank gesture presses the grapple hand's `fire_grapple` for a tick, so it reaches whatever the grapple drives.

In the menus both hands navigate alike, whichever is dominant, with the `menu` set's default bindings on both hands. Either A confirms ([`GUI_OK`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-GUI_OK)) and either B goes back ([`GUI_CANCEL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-GUI_CANCEL)), the action the game's menus take as cancel. Either stick steps through the list and, pushed left or right, adjusts the selected row. Each hand's grip is that hand's modifier: with it held, the stick's left and right step the tabs ([`GUI_TAB_PREV`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-GUI_TAB_PREV) and `GUI_TAB_NEXT`), and A and B are the gamepad's X and Y (the special and filter actions, and the use action, which resets a setting to its default). The grip press is the deliberate one, the force on the Index, so a hand resting on the handle does not modify. Whether a press is modified is latched at its press edge, so squeezing or letting go of the grip mid-press never turns a confirm into an X or a value step into a tab step. A grip alone does nothing. Where a controller has a trackpad, a press on the left or right half of either hand's trackpad also steps the tabs; a touch alone does not, so the off-hand's touch stays free to show the controls summary. The triggers turn pages, the left back and the right forward, except the pointer hand's trigger while the native UI pointer claims it, and the stick clicks keep the gamepad's menu stick clicks (`GUI_FOCUS` on the left, `GUI_RECENTER` on the right, which recenters the map, not the view). The previous and next tabs and pages keep their physical sides.

The menu stick is read as one direction at a time. The game's base menu input ([`UIBase::BaseOnManageInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_base::UIBase##func-BaseOnManageInput)) reads only the left stick's menu actions, past a fixed threshold, ranking up over down over left over right, and it repeats a held direction itself: one step at once, then repeated steps that come faster the longer the direction is held. The d-pad actions take the same repeat through separate handlers, and injecting both would step twice. So both sticks drive the left stick's menu actions, and the bindings add no repeat of their own. A stick read as raw half-axes would step up when pushed mostly sideways with a little lift, so each hand's push latches the direction of its larger axis once that axis passes `controllers.bindings.menu_stick_push`, holds it at full value until the push along it falls below half of that, and reports nothing else meanwhile. The tab steps are press edges in the game (one tab per push), so a held tab push does not repeat.

Holding both stick clicks together for `controllers.bindings.recenter_hold_s` recenters the view, in every mode, with the deferred recenter of the manual kind (see "Posture and roomscale") and a short pulse on both hands. It fires once per hold, and both clicks must be released before it can fire again. Each stick click also has its own binding, which the game acts on as soon as it sees the press (a weapon step, a tether retract, a horn), so a lone click is withheld for `controllers.bindings.stick_chord_window_s`: if the other click joins within the window, the two are the chord and neither reaches the game until both are released; otherwise the click passes through from the window's end, and one released within the window passes through as a tap. A lone stick click therefore acts that much later than it did. The left click waits for the longer of that window and `controllers.bindings.panel_chord_window_s`, since it also starts the debug panel chord.

Pressing the right B while the left stick click is held shows or hides the mod's debug panel, exactly as F8 does: the panel's visibility and egui's capture of the game's input change together. The panel's capture disables the game's device poll, which stands the bindings down (see below), so the chord is read from the raw snapshot and its edges on every input tick before the bindings decide whether to inject, and it injects nothing; the panel closes the way it opened. The B must be pressed within `controllers.bindings.panel_chord_window_s` of the stick click (0.35 s by default), the time a lone left stick click is withheld from the tables, so the click never reaches the game before the chord claims it; a B pressed later is the B's own binding. Once the chord fires, the tables forget the withheld click, and the left stick click and the right B are masked from the bindings, the native UI pointer, and the debug panel's own controller pointer until each is released, so neither cycles a weapon, uses or enters a vehicle, honks, exits a vehicle, goes back in a menu, nor right-clicks on the panel it opens or closes. The chord stands aside for the recenter chord: once the right stick click is held as well, the left click belongs to the recenter chord until it is released. The toggle fires once per B press, with a short pulse on the right hand and an info log line.

The tables are declared once and read twice: by the injection, and by the controls summary on the off-hand wrist, which lists what each input does in the current mode ([controls-summary.md](controls-summary.md)).

The hand weapons are still in use while falling and under the parachute. There the dominant trigger fires (`FIRE_RIGHT`), the off-hand A reloads, and the dominant stick click detonates the placed charges, as on foot. In the wingsuit the dominant trigger and grip fire the weaponized suit's weapons, the dominant B boosts, and the off-hand A closes the suit. The wingsuit's `open_parachute` (the dominant A) evades only on a flick: the move stick must be past `controllers.bindings.evade_flick` and must have left its rest (below half of that) within `controllers.bindings.evade_window_s` before the press. A stick held over to steer or dive is not a flick, so A still opens the parachute. The weaponized wingsuit's guns aim by the gaze, because the gun aim covers only the wielded slot; aiming them by the dominant hand is not built.

While a game menu or the map is up, the weapon and vehicle handling pause instead of standing down. A held foregrip, a primed grenade or C4 charge, a round in hand for the reload, and a held physical control are still held when the menu closes. A snapshot sampled from the previous action set is not interpreted through the new mode's bindings; injection waits for a matching sample without cancelling paused interactions.

Loss of a usable grip pose, controller activity, focus, the fresh input frame, or the controller scheme clears shared interaction state. Held grenades and C4 charges are cancelled through their state machines rather than released as throws, and grabs, vehicle controls, pending strikes, and dual-trigger state are cleared. Both hands then require neutral controls before input resumes, because resetting shared hold timing while retaining a held trigger would create a new click. Each hand rearms independently, so one-controller use does not require the other controller to return. The neutral sample and its queued edges are discarded. Valid inferred poses remain usable for hands, but physical gesture sampling requires tracked poses.

Neutral means that nothing the bindings read from the hand would act. No button other than a capacitive touch is down, the trigger is under both its digital release and its analog deadzone, the grip's press reading (the force on the Index, the value elsewhere) is under its release, and the stick is under the smallest deadzone of its consumers. For the off hand that is the move deadzone. For the dominant hand it is also the turn threshold: the smooth-turn deadzone, or half the snap threshold, where a snap rearms. A stick under a fixed floor always counts as neutral, even when a deadzone is set smaller, so that a worn stick's resting drift cannot keep a hand masked. A stick that close to centre only feeds a slow walk or turn, never an edge. The "Settings" section of the Controllers tab shows each hand as ready, not injecting, or masked, along with the control that holds it. A hand that stays masked for more than a second is logged, again at a fixed interval while it stays masked, and its rearm is logged after that.

Where a semantic action stands for a gamepad button, the table holds every gameplay action that the game's default gamepad keymap puts on that button, and the game's own context selects the one that applies. This keeps the game's tap and hold overloads intact: the reload button is also the holster (a short hold), `USE_VEHICLE_MOD` is nitrous when held and the turbo jump when tapped, a held `ENTER_VEHICLE` enters the gunner seat, and a held `FIRE_GRAPPLE` tethers. The `pause` action (the system button of either hand by default) is the one overload the mod resolves itself: a press shorter than `controllers.bindings.long_press_s` clicks pause on release, and holding it that long clicks the commlink. Melee (`PUSH_GRAPPLE`) is left unbound, because on the button that releases tethers it would melee on every release. The sight tuning tool's capture chord is armed only while the tool is on and its marker is placed: on foot, holding both grips and pressing the dominant A captures. While it is armed, the dominant grip withholds the off-hand's grapple, so the dominant grip is squeezed first, and both grips together withhold the dominant A's jump. With the dominant grip held, the off-hand's grip press is part of the chord and not a foregrip grab, so the chord neither takes nor lets go of a foregrip. A grip press is latched at its press edge to the grip's default action or to a grab of the volume the hand is in, so a hand leaving a volume mid-press does not turn a grab into a grapple. Outside every grab volume, an off-hand grip press fires the grapple (see "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier").

The game's readers tell a tap from a hold by the effector's state time, and [`LocalPlayerActionMap::ForceSetPressed`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::LocalPlayerActionMap##func-ForceSetPressed) zeroes that time on every call ([input.md, "The write API"](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#the-write-api)). The hold tracker therefore keeps each held action's time itself and writes the effector's whole state: the first tick of a press writes a click with no time, every held tick writes a press with the time held so far, and the tick after the press ends writes a release with the final time, which the next device poll turns idle. An analog action above a fixed value also reads as pressed, standing in for the device button threshold, which is not read.

Each write resolves its action through the same lookup the game's readers use, so it lands in the effector the next reader picks. Values are only ever raised, so a physical device's reading in the same effector is never reduced. An action no device binds resolves to the null effector, and the write is skipped and reported. The writes go to one of three targets: the player action map through its blockers; the player map ignoring the blockers, for the pause and commlink toggles, which the game reads that way while a menu is up; and the UI action map ([`ui_action_map`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::ui_action_map)), which the game's menus read. The pause and commlink clicks go to both the player map and the UI map.

A write must not outlive the bindings' intent, and the device poll cannot be relied on to end it. While [`InputDeviceManager::m_Enabled`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_device_manager::InputDeviceManager##field-m_Enabled) is clear, which is how the mod's debug panel takes the game's input, [`InputDeviceManager::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_device_manager::InputDeviceManager##func-Update) reads no devices, so every effector keeps the value and state last written to it. A click left there reads as a fresh click on every frame: the pause click alone opens and closes the pause menu in a loop. The device-manager detour reads the flag before the poll and passes it to the input tick, and while it is clear the bindings stand down like any other idle reason, logging the change in either direction.

The hold tracker therefore remembers every effector it wrote on the last tick, the bare clicks and raised values as well as the holds, and every stand-down (the toggles going off, the gamepad scheme, a lost controller or pose, the panel, the disabled poll) undoes them explicitly. While the poll runs, each hold is released with its final time, keeping a physical press in the same effector as before, and the poll has already overwritten the raised values. While the poll is disabled, every written effector is returned to idle with no value. Idle rather than released, because nothing moves a released effector on and it would read as a release on every frame. The physical-press guard is bypassed there, because no device is being read and every pressed state is a stale write. When the poll runs again, the tracker is already empty and the poll starts from idle effectors.

A bare click (pause, commlink, and the other one-tick taps) is also undone on the next tick when it is not clicked again. The poll moves a clicked effector to pressed or released on every run, so an effector still clicked a tick later is one the poll skipped, which it also does when the clock's real time has not advanced. The tracker records the effector each click landed on and releases it only when that same effector is still clicked, so a fresh physical click on another device is left alone. On a tick that waits for a sample from the new action set, the holds are kept for when injection resumes and the poll releases their effectors, but the clicks are undone the same way. The writes that undo earlier ones resolve the player map ignoring its blockers, so a menu that blocked the map after the write cannot hide the effector it landed on. The Controllers tab lists these writes with the rest of the tick's writes.

## Gamepad semantics

The game applies the rules of the input device it considers active, from the setting [`SettingType::EPCUseGamepad`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/settings_manager::SettingType##variant-EPCUseGamepad): aim assist only on the gamepad, a different melee trigger on the keyboard, and the matching button prompts. The game sets the value from the frame's device events. The controllers inject at the action level and produce no device events, so the value would stay at whatever device the player last touched. The bindings are written against the gamepad rules, so while the controller scheme is in effect and `controllers.gamepad_semantics` is set, the entry is held at the gamepad's value. It is written directly rather than through the setter, which would also re-select the controller port and may persist the change. The previous value is restored when the pin ends, unless the game has written its own value from a real device event in the meantime.

## The aim-state pin and the mental-state cuts

The engine side is [aim-state.md](../../engine/gameplay/aim-state.md). Each part has its own toggle under `controllers.aim_state`, all on by default, and acts only under the controller scheme unless `controllers.aim_state.without_controllers` is set, which applies them under the gamepad scheme for testing.

- `pin`: [`StateBitFlag::SB_NO_WEAPON_AIM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_NO_WEAPON_AIM) is set for the duration of the player's aim-input function, the only code that reads it, and restored afterwards. The function then leaves [`AimState::m_AimingWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AimState##flag-m_AimingWeapon) clear and mirrors the result into [`StateBitFlag::SB_IS_AIMING_WPN_OR_GRPL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_AIMING_WPN_OR_GRPL). The trigger still fires, because fire gating reads neither.
- `pin_grapple`: [`AimState::m_AimingGrapple`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AimState##flag-m_AimingGrapple), which the weapon pin does not reach, is cleared after the grapple's pre-simulation update. On a held tether the body then no longer turns to face the hook.
- `skip_arm_ik`: the player's `NAimIKTask`, `NRightArmAimIK`, `NGrapplingFireArmIK`, and `NReelInAimIK` updates return early only while controller wrist IK is enabled and owns the affected hands. Ownership requires gameplay, a fresh focused controller frame, usable grip poses, and positive controller tier weights. The right-arm task requires the right hand, the grapple-fire task requires the left hand, and shared aim and reel-in tasks require both. This gate does not prove that the later HumanIK solve succeeds at the character's current level of detail.
- `neutral_aim_point`: the body's aim point on the control-parameters blackboard ([`ControlParameters::m_AimTargetWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::control_parameters::ControlParameters##field-m_AimTargetWeapons)), which turns the body toward it, is held `controllers.aim_state.neutral_aim_distance_m` ahead of the head along the body's forward. The character-level weapon point is left to the gun aim (see "Aiming").
- `clear_wall_block`: after the wall-penetration component's update ([`WallPenetrationWeaponComponent::UpdateWeapon_Serial`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WallPenetrationWeaponComponent##func-UpdateWeapon_Serial)), the block it computes from where the animation would put the barrel is cleared on the player's held weapon.
- `mental_state_cuts`: [`StateBitFlag::SB_DISABLE_MENTAL_STATE_DRAW_WEAPON`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_DISABLE_MENTAL_STATE_DRAW_WEAPON) is set before each of the player's [`Inventory::UpdateWeaponHandling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponHandling) calls, because the inventory clears it every tick. The existing [`Character::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-QueueAct) detour swallows `ACT_ON_UNEQUIP_WEAPON_SLOW` for the local player, and swallows `UB_ACT_HOLSTER` while the local player's weapon handling is running, unless a deliberate holster is pending. A detour on [`Character::HolsterWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HolsterWeapon) marks the holster as deliberate, because the player's holster takes effect through the same relaxed branch that the automatic holster uses.

The FPS-movement shim ([input.md, "Tapping the move input"](input.md#tapping-the-move-input)) forces the aim flags for the duration of each locomotion task's `Update`, so the lower body plays the aim-family strafe acts while the rest of the body stays in the no-aim family. While controller gun aiming is enabled, it also holds the selected wielded weapon's aim point ahead of the head during the task call and restores its exact original value afterwards. Pointing the gun sideways therefore does not select a sideways strafe for forward movement. The point is level with the head, along the head's heading with its pitch removed, as the neutral aim point is, and it is at least a fixed minimum distance away. The engine takes the strafe direction from the point minus the character's position. A point that kept the pitch would close in on the head when the player looks down, and the head's small offset from the body would then pick the strafe act. When the head looks almost straight up or down, the body's forward is used instead.

## Hands

The engine side is [humanik.md](../../engine/character/humanik.md) and [hands-locomotion-and-state.md](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md). Everything here is inert unless the controller scheme is in effect and the hand's controller is tracked, and each part has its toggle under `controllers.hands`.

Each hand's wrist target is composed from its controller, all in pure functions:

1. the grip pose in the cockpit frame;
2. times the grip offset (`controllers.hands.grip`, a position and a rotation in the right hand's grip frame, mirrored for the left), giving the wrist frame: the origin at the wrist joint, -Z toward the fingers, and +Y out of the back of the hand. OpenXR gives both hands' grip frames the same axes (+X points into the right palm and out of the left one, so to the player's right on both hands), so the mirror flips the grip's X as it flips the wrist's;
3. the body remap: the wrist's offset from the player's shoulder, scaled by the ratio of the arm lengths and placed at the character's shoulder (see "Arm reach" below);
4. into the world through the head's transform, the same composition the head pose uses, so that the hands and the view agree.

With `ik` set, each controller-driven wrist is queued on HumanIK's `MAIN` pass at the entry of [`Character::UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel), the seam the head and body IK already use. The position target overwrites the effector's translation. The rotation target is the delta from the animated wrist frame, which is built from the animated knuckles, to the target frame, so it never depends on how the game's hand bone is oriented at rest. Both are queued at the `UPPER_BODY` step, without interpolation or blend-out because they are re-queued every tick, with the reaches (`reach_t`, `reach_r`) scaled by the hand's tier weight. The wielded weapon rides the hand's attach bone, and the engine refreshes the weapon's cached transform from that bone after the `MAIN` solve, so the gun follows the IK-driven wrist within the same tick. While the procedural body poses the torso, the targets are queued at the HumanIK solve gate instead, on the torso it writes, kept out of the torso and with elbow hints ([procedural-body.md, "The arms"](../body/procedural-body.md#the-arms)).

With `rigid_grip` set, a gun in a controller-driven hand is held fixed to the wrist. The attach bone is animated, so the clips playing decide how the gun sits in the hand ([hands-locomotion-and-state.md, "The gun's pose in the hand is animated"](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md#the-guns-pose-in-the-hand-is-animated)). With the animated wrist this is the game's own handling, but with the wrist on a controller it reads as the gun turning over in a still hand: in play a right-hand gun pivoted about its grip by up to about 170° over a few ticks, muzzle up and over until it lay upside down along the forearm, while the hand and fingers held still, and snapped between a few fixed in-hand poses. So a pre-call step in the inventory's placement detour ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms), shared with the dual-wield layout) writes the attach bone of each gun of the local player's wielded weapon, for each hand with a controller weight above zero (the hand tiers' blend, whether or not a wrist target was queued this tick), to the hand's current wrist joint composed with the hand's canonical grip, blended from the animated pose by that weight so the gun settles into the hand as it blends onto its controller and back out as it yields: its primary attach bone relative to its wrist in the skeleton's bind pose ([`AnimationController::GetBindPoseBoneMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-GetBindPoseBoneMatrix)), read once per animation controller. The placement runs after the state tasks, after the `MAIN` solve, and after the `SECONDARY` solve, and each run is pinned, so the last one places the gun on the solved wrist. Everything that reads the gun's cached transform follows it: the muzzle effects, the gun's model parts, its physics proxy, the shot origin, the sight line, and the late update, which carries the gun and the hand by the same correction. The shot's direction is the aim point and does not depend on it. Both of a hand's attach points take the same canonical grip, so a state task moving the gun between them does not turn it, and with dual-wield each pistol is pinned to the hand whose attach bone it rides. The foregrip and the one-handed weight move the wrist target, and the gun stays rigid on the wrist they produce. A gun on a holster or back bone, a weapon in any other state, and a hand the controllers do not drive are left to the game, so drawing and holstering play as animated until the gun is in a driven hand, and a reload's flourish that keys the attach bone is not shown (the magazine, carried on the off-hand's bone during a reload, is unaffected). The game's own visual recoil on the attach bone, if any, is replaced by the mod's recoil on the hand. The code is `controllers::hands::grip`. A gun the hold puts in the left hand can take the right hand's grip mirrored onto the left wrist instead ([physical-weapons.md, "The left hand's grip"](physical-weapons.md#the-left-hands-grip)).

The gun-in-hand watch (`controllers::weapons::grip_watch`) checks the result. Each placement records, per hand, how the gun sits on the solved wrist bone, how far the animation had put the attach bone from the canonical grip before the pin, and the solved wrist's distance and rotation from its target. On every input tick the watch compares the gun's rotation on the solved wrist, and relative to the wrist's target (the controller's pose for it, so a gun turned by a wrist the solve left behind counts too), taking the larger change, with the previous tick's (a change past 90° is a snap) and with the rotation it had when the hand took the gun (a departure past 60° held for 100 ms is a sweep, which a turn blended over several ticks makes). That baseline starts afresh when the gun moves to another attach bone or the pin takes hold or lets go, across which the rotation is expected to change, and a move to another attach bone is logged. A warning comes with the bone, the weapon state, whether the bone was pinned, the animated deviation, the solved wrist's miss and drift since the grab, the IK weight, the hand tier, and the character's animation state id, and warnings are limited to one a second per hand. With the grip pinned the watch should stay quiet; the Weapons section of the Controllers tab shows each hand's readings and counts.

With `suppress_secondary_ik` set, the game's secondary hand IK ([`Character::UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass)), which runs after the `MAIN` solve and can move the wrists again, is switched off for each controller-driven hand. The pass is driven by blackboard keys that animation state tasks write: each wrist's weight key, which the grapple fire arm IK and the parachute handle IK write (the parachute's persist between ticks), and the weapon off-hand grip's weight key. Some of those tasks run inside the pose-finalization pass after its entry ([animation-layers.md](../../engine/character/animation-layers.md#state-tasks-inside-the-pose-finalization-pass)), so the switch acts at the `SECONDARY` pass's own entry: zeroing a present, positive key there switches that part off for the frame, and a key the game did not write is never created. At the same entry the switch zeroes the driven wrist's current translation reach. A `SECONDARY` target queued before the hand was driven, such as the parachute handle's on the tick the hand tier blends in from the wingsuit's yielded tier, would otherwise outlive its key, because the pass removes an old entry only once its reach is zero and the `MAIN` target keeps the wrist's reach up every frame ([humanik.md](../../engine/character/humanik.md#target-entry-lifetime-and-the-is_valid-flag)); it would pull the wrist back to the handle whenever the pass solves for another target. The `MAIN` solve has used the reach by then, and the next `MAIN` pass drives it back up. A wrist with no reach is not held by the `SECONDARY` solve either, so while the weapon off-hand grip still runs for the other hand (its weight key positive after the switch), the driven wrist gets a weak `SECONDARY` target of its own at its `MAIN` target's position, through the wrist's world-space position key ([`SECONDARY_HAND_POSITION_WS_LEFT_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard##field-SECONDARY_HAND_POSITION_WS_LEFT_ID) and its right twin) and its weight key at half reach, written for the frame; otherwise the grip's solve pulls the body and the released wrist rides along off the controller. The reach is unverified in the headset. The weapon-agnostic body also switches the off-hand grip off for a hand the controllers do not drive ([unarmed-body.md](../body/unarmed-body.md)).

The hand tiers classify each hand once per input tick from the mode detector's snapshot and a few state-machine predicates. The table (`controllers.hands.tiers`) holds one tier per state and defaults to the plan's table ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Hand tiers"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#hand-tiers)). The free, action-runs, and yielded tiers are built, and the anchored tier is built for the grapple reel and hang states and the mounted guns. A yielded hand blends from its controller to the animation over `yield_blend_s` and back over `return_blend_s`. A state assigned the anchored tier where that tier is not built yields. The action-runs tier is described under "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier", and the anchored tier under "Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand" and "Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight".

With `late_update` set, the rendered hands and weapon are corrected at render time. Once per rendered frame, after the VR pose is published, the late update snapshots the latest controller poses composed with the render-time head pose pair and the targets the last two ticks' IK was asked to reach. For the engine's interpolation fraction, each hand's correction is the latest controller pose interpolated between the head pose pair's two sides, times the inverse of the two ticks' targets interpolated the same way; the grip offset is on both sides and cancels. The correction limit checks physical wrist displacement and the shortest relative rotation, rather than the translation component of a world-space correction matrix. Partial weights interpolate the reference wrist toward the latest wrist before deriving the correction, so rotating a stationary wrist does not move its pivot with distance from the world origin. The post-call detour on [`WeaponBase::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdateRender) re-pushes the model matrix of a gun the local player holds in a hand attach bone as that correction times the engine's interpolated matrix. The character render-block palette hooks pre-multiply the hand and finger bones' palette entries by the same correction, and the forearm bones' entries by a swing about the elbow that takes the rendered wrist to the corrected wrist, so the forearm stays attached at both ends. The gun and the hand therefore never separate. A gun's separate model parts (a magazine, a pistol's slide, a minigun's spinning barrel) are weapon components with their own cached matrices and their own render update ([`ModelAttachementWeaponComponent::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ModelAttachementWeaponComponent##func-UpdateRender)), so the gun's re-push left them a tick or two behind it. A post-call detour on that update re-pushes each part of the local player's wielded weapon as the correction of the hand it rides times its own interpolated matrix. A part rides its gun's hand unless an animation task put it on one of the holder's bones for the placement (a magazine carried in the off-hand during a reload); that bone is reset before the render update runs, so a post-call detour on the part's placement ([`ModelAttachementWeaponComponent::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ModelAttachementWeaponComponent##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) records it for the local player's weapons. The code is `controllers::hands::parts`, and the Hands section of the Controllers tab counts the re-pushed parts. The muzzle flash, the attached effects, the shot origin, and the grapple wire's near end stay on the sim-rate transform.

## Finger curls

The code is in `payload/src/controllers/fingers/`, and the settings are `controllers.fingers`. Each finger of a hand the controllers drive gets a curl from 0 (straight) to 1 (a fist), and each finger but the thumb a splay, its sideways turn at the knuckle.

The curls come from one of two sources, chosen by `source`: `Auto` takes the tracked joints for a hand the runtime reports them for and the estimate otherwise, and `Joints` and `Estimate` take one source only (with `Joints`, a hand without joint data keeps the animated fingers).

- From the joints. Every `XR_EXT_hand_tracking` joint has -Z along its bone and +Y out of the back of the hand, on both hands, so a joint's flexion is its rotation relative to its parent joint about the parent's -X, and a knuckle's splay is the rotation about +Y. A finger's curl is the sum of its knuckle, middle, and last flexions over their sum at a full fist; the thumb's is its two outer flexions over theirs. A hand with any invalid finger joint falls back to the estimate.
- The estimate, from the inputs the fingers rest on. The thumb bends onto the face when it touches the thumbstick, the trackpad, or a face button, a little further for the buttons, which sit nearer the palm, and further again while it presses; off the face it is straight. The index finger points off the trigger, rests bent on it when it touches it, and closes with the pull. The middle, ring, and little fingers share the grip: on the Index controller they follow the grip value and its force carries them to a fist, and on a Touch controller they rest on the handle and the grip's pull closes them. The estimate has no splay.

The curls follow their source with a time constant (`smoothing_s`), which turns the estimate's jumps when a touch sensor changes into a quick motion.

The pose is absolute: each joint's bend is measured from a straight finger and replaces the animated bend rather than adding to it. The game's free-hand animation already holds the fingers curled, so a bend added on top of it left an open hand curled and pushed a full curl into the palm. The straight finger is built from the animated palm, which stays as animated: a finger's open direction is the one from the wrist to its knuckle, and the open thumb lies in the palm's plane, turned `thumb_spread_deg` from the fingers' direction toward the thumb side. The skeleton's bind pose is not used: the defs bind no reference pose for it, and whether its hands are open is unknown. A joint's bend at a curl is its finger's range (`curl_ranges`, the bend at the open and closed ends as a share of the full joint bends `finger_bend_deg` and `thumb_bend_deg`) plus that joint's `rest_offset_deg`, clamped to between 30 degrees back past straight and 110 degrees toward the palm. A finger bends toward the palm about the axis square to its open direction and to the palm's normal, and turns sideways by its splay (`splay_scale`, clamped to `max_splay_deg`) about the axis square to its open direction and to the thumb side; the thumb bends across the palm toward the little finger's side. Because the bones' own frames are unknown, each bone's axis along its length is read from the animated pose (the distal bone, which has no joint beyond it, is taken to share the middle bone's), and each bone turns by the shortest arc from its animated direction to its target, so its animated roll is kept. The hand's tier weight is the share of that arc taken, so a yielded hand blends back to the animated fingers. The palm's frame is built from the animated wrist and the index, middle, and little knuckles, as the wrist IK's is. Every axis comes from the hand's geometry, so the left hand is the right one's mirror image with no per-side sign. The rest offsets and the thumb spread, on the Controllers tab, correct the straight reference in the headset if the wrist-to-knuckle direction or the distal bone's axis is off for Rico's rig.

The fingers are written once per sim tick at the end of the pose-finalization pass, in the post-call detour on [`Character::UpdatePropEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropEffects), with [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) over the finalized model-space pose, as the head override is ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md)). The bones are Rico's `{Side}HandThumb1-3`, `{Side}HandIndex1-3`, `{Side}HandMiddle1-3`, `{Side}HandRing1-3`, and `{Side}HandPinky1-3`, found by name; the in-hand ring and little metacarpals stay with the palm. Each finger's joints are read before any is written and written from the root outward, so the result does not depend on how a write carries the bones beyond it. Writing at the sim tick rather than in the skinning palette is enough because the late update already moves the finger bones rigidly with the corrected hand, so the curled fingers follow the controller at frame rate.

A hand is curled only while the controllers drive it, that is while its wrist target was queued this tick, so the curls need `hands.ik`. A hand that holds a weapon (a gun rides its attach bone while the weapon handling runs, or it holds the foregrip) drives only its trigger finger, and its other fingers keep the game's grip, which wraps the weapon; `drive_all_when_holding` drives them all anyway. A hand that grips through a sticky grab, which is an anchored hand on the grapple line or a mounted gun's handles, or a hand on a physical vehicle control, is curled into a fist whatever the controller reports, because the grab outlasts the grip press.

The Controllers tab's finger section shows whether the joints are located with the controller-conforming motion range and, per hand, the source in use, the tier weight, whether the hand holds a weapon, and each finger's curl, splay, and whether it is driven.

## Aiming

The engine side is [aim-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md) and [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md). Both parts act only under the controller scheme, in gameplay, and for a tracked hand, each behind its toggle under `controllers.aim`. The hands are composed and their rays cast in every such update, even with both toggles off, because the grenade throw and the melee target follow the hands as well.

Everything runs from the player aim control's post-simulation update ([`PlayerAimControl::UpdatePostSim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-UpdatePostSim)), the simulation-phase update in which the game casts its own aim ray. Before the update, the hands are composed into the world from the latest controller snapshot and the grapple ray is handed to the grapple's hooks. After it, the hand rays are cast, the guns' aim points are published for the per-weapon update, and the tick's results are published for the overlay. For the player, the aim update and the weapon updates run one after the other on the same thread, aim first, so the weapon update always sees the current tick's points.

The raycasts use the engine's closest-hit query ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay)) with the player aim's own filter ([`PlayerAimRaycastFilter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimRaycastFilter)), built the way the game's aim ray builds it. The filter rejects every physics instance of the ignored objects (the character's capsule, ragdoll bodies, hit proxies, and held weapons), which an instance-only filter would not.

With `gun` set, each gun of the wielded weapon aims along its own sight line: the gun's transform carried to the controller's current pose by the hand's late-update correction, composed with the gun's current fire point, whose -Z axis is the barrel, and with the weapon's sight correction (see "Sight lines"). The line is cast once per aim update up to `max_distance_m`. Its hit, or a far point along it, becomes that hand's aim point, never nearer than `min_target_distance_m`, because the game fires along the barrel for an aim point near the muzzle. The per-weapon base update ([`WeaponBase::UpdateFromUserPostSim_ParallelBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdateFromUserPostSim_ParallelBase)) is handed that point in place of the character's shared one, for the gun that can fire in that call ([`WeaponBase::m_NextWeaponToFire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_NextWeaponToFire)), and the weapon stores it as its own aim target before it builds the shot. The character-level aim point follows the dominant hand's point, so the game's own setter still marks the point as set for the frame.

With `grapple` set, the grapple slot follows the off-hand. The ray starts at `grapple_origin_m` in the off-hand's wrist frame. Every part of the aim control that fills the grapple slot takes its ray from one of two getters: [`PlayerAimControl::GetAdjustedCameraMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-GetAdjustedCameraMatrix) and [`GameCameraManager::GetCameraMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::game_camera_manager::GameCameraManager##func-GetCameraMatrix). While a thread-local override is set, both return a camera matrix built from the grapple ray, so the engine fills the slot with its own logic:

- The direct-aim update ([`PlayerAimControl::UpdateDirectAim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-UpdateDirectAim)) runs twice. The first pass, under the override, fills every slot from the grapple ray with the engine's own filter, range, holey-geometry handling, and target resolution, and the grapple slot is taken out of it. The second pass, without the override, refills every other slot from the camera as the game does, and the grapple slot is put back.
- The grapple target scoring ([`PlayerAimControl::SetTargetGrappleFitness`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-SetTargetGrappleFitness)) runs under the override, so entity attachment and tethering select their target the way the game does, around the hand's ray. The grapple's visibility check ([`PlayerAimControl::IsTargetVisibleCast`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-IsTargetVisibleCast)) does not go through the engine: the engine keeps one scheduled visibility raycast per target and answers every target type from its latest result, so a grapple request from the hand would leak into the camera-based types and theirs into the grapple. The grapple's check is instead a synchronous cast from the hand ray's origin to the target's body position, clear if it hits nothing or hits within 0.75 m of the end, ignoring the player and the player's vehicle.
- The last-valid grapple cache is re-validated under the override ([`PlayerAimControl::IsLastValidGrappleAimPosStillValid`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-IsLastValidGrappleAimPosStillValid)).

The per-slot game objects are weak pointers. Moving one out and back is a raw copy with the source zeroed, which leaves its weak count unchanged; clearing one goes through the engine's own assignment, which releases the reference.

While the controller scheme is in effect, the gamepad-semantics pin holds the active-input-device setting at the gamepad, so melee, aim assist, and the prompts behave as on a gamepad. The game's own aim assist stays on the gaze; per-hand aim assist is described under "Grenades, melee, aim assist, and reloads".

## Sight lines

A weapon's sight correction moves the line's origin from the fire point and turns it, in the fire point's frame, and is looked up by the weapon's name hash in order: the per-weapon entry in the config (`aim.sights`, which the Controllers tab and the tuning tool write), then the embedded sight table, then none, which leaves the line on the fire point. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/aim/sights/`.

The embedded table is `tools/sights/sights.json`, derived offline from the game's weapon models by the sight tool ([`tools/sights/README.md`](../../../tools/sights/README.md)) and compiled into the payload. Only its `scoped`, `pistol`, and `tuned` entries are used; the `iron_uncertain` and `failed` entries are not trusted and leave the weapon on its fire point. The config's table holds up to 32 entries, room for every weapon the embedded table does not trust. The Controllers tab's aiming readout shows where the held weapon's correction comes from.

The sight tuning tool (`aim.sight_tuning.enabled`, off; `aim.sight_tuning.marker_distance_m`) is in the Controllers tab's aiming settings. While a weapon is held, it shows the weapon's offset and angles and their source, including the grade of an embedded entry and whether a config entry overrides one. "Line up" places a marker in the world `marker_distance_m` straight ahead of the head, which the overlay draws over the scene with a dot where the dominant hand's sight line passes closest to it. The player lines the gun's sights up on the marker and captures, with the tab's "Capture" button or the capture chord (see "Bindings and injection"). A capture is serviced on the next aim update: it solves the offset that makes the dominant hand's sight line, at the weapon's current angles, pass through the marker, taking the point on that line directly above or below the muzzle, and stores it as the weapon's config entry. A capture is refused when no weapon is held, the dominant hand holds no gun, the marker is not ahead of the muzzle, or the solved origin is more than half a metre from the muzzle. Drag values adjust the offset and angles finely, writing the config entry; "Reset to the table" removes the config entry and "Reset to the fire point" stores an empty one. Tuned entries are saved in the settings file (`controllers.aim.sights`, a list of the entries held) like any other setting ([settings.md](../settings.md)). "Copy as JSON" puts the weapon's entry on the clipboard in the table's line format, graded `tuned`, for pasting into `tools/sights/sights.json`, whose extractor keeps tuned entries when it regenerates the table.

## The world-space overlay

The overlay draws coloured, alpha-blended sprites and thick lines into the scene per eye, for readouts that need their own depths. It draws at the end of [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw), onto each eye's final back buffer after the post chain and the upscaler and before the HUD panel, so the readouts keep their colours through exposure changes and do not smear under temporal reconstruction. It works with and without single-pass stereo, and it skips the far-field pass and cinematic mode. Everything is behind `controllers.overlay.enabled` and acts only under the controller scheme.

Producers run at different rates on different threads, so each source owns a retained slot that its submission replaces wholesale and stamps, and the renderer draws a slot until its time to live runs out. A producer that stops submitting (its toggle turned off, a mode change, a menu) disappears on its own. The store's lock is a leaf, so any thread may submit. A position is in world space, anchored to a hand, or an offset in world axes from the head; an anchored position is placed from the render-time hand pose or head position, so a hand-attached end follows the controller at frame rate even when its producer submits at the sim rate, and a point placed in the cockpit frame rides with a falling or driving player instead of trailing them by a tick's travel.

The overlay's own producers run once per rendered frame on the render thread:

- `grapple_highlight`: a ring on a valid, in-range hook point, in its own colour with a centre dot when the target can be tethered;
- `grapple_beam`: a beam from the wrist to the grapple's target point;
- `gun_readout`: nothing by default (`None`), a dot at the hit point (`Dot`), or a line from the sight to the hit point (`Laser`);
- `ghost_hands`: a translucent ghost at the controller of each yielded hand, drawn at `xray_alpha` where the scene is in front of it;
- `debug_gizmos` (on by default): each hand's grip and aim axes;
- the sight tuning marker, while the tuning tool is on and its marker is placed: a ring and a dot at the marker, and a dot where the dominant hand's sight line passes closest to it, all over the scene.

The grapple and gun producers submit only when the aim has run since their last submission; the ghost hands and gizmos follow the render-time hand poses and submit every frame. The grab-volume highlights are submitted by the weapon handling and the physical vehicle controls, at the input tick's rate (see "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier"). The debug panel's controller pointer submits its laser at the input tick's rate too, whatever the control scheme, with both ends anchored to the pointing hand's aim pose ([hud.md, "Pointing at the debug panel with the controllers"](../hud.md#pointing-at-the-debug-panel-with-the-controllers)).

The game's own HUD reticles (`overlay.hide_game_reticle`, on, independent of `overlay.enabled`). The HUD movie's weapon reticle sits at the centre of the screen and its grapple reticle at the gaze's grapple aim, pinned to the centre when the grapple aims at a direct position ([aim-pipeline.md, "The reticles"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#the-reticles)), so under hand aim both mark a point nothing aims at. `controllers/overlay/game_reticle.rs` decides each group by the detected mode. The grapple reticle group is hidden everywhere under the controller scheme: on foot, swimming, falling, in the wingsuit, under the parachute, in every seat, and with `aim.grapple` off too, since the overlay's grapple highlight and gauntlet beam mark the grapple's target in the world and a cursor on the panel adds nothing to them. The weapon reticle group is hidden wherever the guns' aim belongs to the hands. On foot, swimming, falling, in the wingsuit, and under the parachute, it is hidden whenever `aim.gun` is on, armed or not, so it does not show in the gaps between hand-aim results (drawing, holstering, switching weapons, an empty hand). In the driver's seat it is hidden while `vehicles.driver_weapons_by_hand` is on, since the vehicle's built-in weapons then aim at the dominant hand's point. At a mounted gun it is hidden while the gun's point comes from the hands (`vehicles.mounted.aim_by_hands`), which has its own freshness. In a passenger seat, and with a menu up, it is hidden while the aim's latest results aimed the wielded weapon by hand, a result counting only while the aim's update count has moved in the last quarter second. A gun the gaze still aims keeps its reticle: the driver's weapons with `driver_weapons_by_hand` off, a mounted gun with its hand aim off, and every gun with `aim.gun` off. The groups are held invisible at the HUD's capture seam through the same clip handles and intent tracking as the full-screen overlay suppression (`hud/scaleform.rs`), which absorb the movie rewriting the weapon group's visibility on every HUD update, and are restored when the rule stops holding. Hiding them loses the reticle's hostile and allied tint, its out-of-range dimming, the weapon's spread, the grapple's in-range cue, and the attached-wire marker. The overlay's `grapple_highlight` covers the in-range cue, drawn only on a valid hook point and tinted for a tetherable one, and `gun_readout` (`Dot` or `Laser`) can mark the gun's hit point; nothing replaces the target tint, the spread, or the attached-wire marker. Hit feedback (`MCI_inflict_damage`) and the remote-explosive indicators are other clips and stay.

The frame's primitives are resolved against the head (the cyclopean eye) rather than per eye, so both eyes draw identical world geometry and the stereo pair fuses. They are made relative to the frame's origin and sorted far to near, because the overlay writes no depth. Each eye projects them through a translation-free view-projection, as the engine's own geometry does, because at the game's world coordinates an absolute `f32` world-to-clip transform jitters by about a millimetre. The renderer uploads the primitives once, on the first eye's draw, and issues one instanced draw per eye over a structured buffer (`payload/src/shaders/overlay_vs.hlsl` and `overlay_ps.hlsl`). No depth target is bound: the pixel shader reads the main depth texture and tests against it with a tolerance (`depth_tolerance_m`, or `depth_tolerance_rel` of the view distance where that is larger), so the engine's depth-stencil view is never touched. Lines thinner than `min_line_px` fade instead of thinning. The draw saves and restores the pipeline state it changes, except the viewport, which every mod draw at this seam sets itself.

## Posture and roomscale

This is milestone 2, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/roomscale/` and `payload/src/vr/stage.rs`, and the settings are under `controllers.roomscale`, except the head remap (`controllers.body.remap_head`) and the seated crouch flick (`controllers.bindings.crouch_flick`). The design is in the plan's ["Posture"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#posture) and ["Roomscale design"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#roomscale-design). These parts do not depend on the controller scheme, because a gamepad player standing in a room moves the head in the same way. They act only while the VR source owns the head pose, and each part that changes behaviour is on by default, as part of the full gameplay preset.

When the runtime offers a STAGE reference space, the session creates one beside the LOCAL space. Once per frame the mid-eye pose in LOCAL is located against STAGE, which gives the head's height above the floor, and the play area's bounds are read again whenever the runtime reports a reference-space change. The eye views, the controllers, and the compositor layer stay in LOCAL, so a runtime without a STAGE space loses only the floor height.

The work is split in two halves. The frame half runs once per rendered VR frame, from the pose composition on the game thread, after the simulation tick and before the render update. It resolves the posture, remaps the head height, folds the chase into the cockpit offset, tracks the seat phase, and advances the fade, and it publishes the posture, the head's horizontal offset from the body, whether the character counts as crouched, and the seat phase for the simulation half. The simulation half runs in a post-call detour on the on-foot locomotion task ([`NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask##func-Update)), which writes the character proxy's wanted velocity on every on-foot tick, idle included. It acts only on a tick where the task wrote that velocity: the task set the task-defined motion state ([`Character::m_CurrentMotionState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_CurrentMotionState)) and the character is not attached to a vehicle. Every other state that moves the proxy (the grapple, jumps, stunts, ragdoll, swimming, the parachute, and the wingsuit) runs a different task, so none of them receives the chase.

Posture (`posture`). `Seated`, `Standing`, and `Roomscale` select the posture directly. OpenXR has no signal for a seated user, so `Auto` tells seated from standing by the head's height above the STAGE floor (`roomscale/posture.rs`, `PostureClassifier`), and a standing player becomes roomscale when both sides of the play area reach `roomscale_min_extent_m`. The thresholds sit between the seated eye height (the saved `seated_eye_height`, or 0.72 of the standing eye height without one) and the standing eye height: until a head height is located the player counts as seated, because a seated treatment never forces a crouch while a standing one turns a seated player into a crouching stander; the first located height and the height at a manual recenter classify at once against the midpoint; after that a seated player stands once the head stays above 60% of the way to the standing eye height for `auto_posture.stand_after_s` (1 s), which a seated player cannot reach without standing up, and a standing player sits once the head stays below 40% of the way for `auto_posture.sit_after_s` (20 s), which is long so that a crouch behind cover stays a physical crouch. The head must stay at a chair's height for that (`auto_posture.chair_height_only`, on by default; a debug UI switch): steady, within eight centimetres of where the wait started (a head that moves further starts it again from where it is, so a crouch that rises to peek and ducks again never sits), and above the floor level, the seated eye height less 0.12 of the standing eye height. A standing player whose head goes lower than that has got down onto the floor of the play area, sitting or kneeling on it, and stays standing however long it lasts, so the head keeps its true height and the procedural body sits on the ground under it ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-lower-bodys-poses)); getting up from the floor onto a chair is seated after the usual wait. The first located height and a recenter still classify against the midpoint, so a player who starts or recenters sitting on the floor is seated. A crouch or an upright kneel held steady longer than the wait is the classifier's known failure; the explicit postures avoid it. Each change of the detection, or of whether the head is at floor level, logs `roomscale: the automatic posture's detection changes` at info level with the head's height and the thresholds. The controllers' heights are not used, because a crouching player's hands sit as low as a seated one's. With a standing eye height of 1.70 m and a seated one of 1.22 m, the midpoint is 1.46 m, so a head at 1.05 m is seated from the first frame.

Head height (`controllers.body.remap_head`, on). In the standing and roomscale postures with a floor, the true eye height is remapped onto the character by the ratio of the heights, and the view's vertical cockpit offset is the remapped height less the character's standing eye height, which is where the animated head anchor already puts the eyes. In the seated posture with a floor, the head's travel from the seated eye reference (`roomscale/seated.rs`) scales by the height ratio, so the eyes sit at the character's standing eye height while the player sits at the reference. A single calibrated height is fragile, because it belongs to one chair, one way of sitting, and one moment, so the reference follows the player. It starts at the saved `seated_eye_height`, or at the head's height on the first seated frame without one, and a new calibration starts it again. A manual recenter while seated captures the head's height as the reference. The adaptive baseline (`seated_reference.adaptive`, on) samples the head height over `seated_reference.window_s` (120 s) and, once a quarter of the window is filled, moves the reference toward the median at most `seated_reference.rate_mps` (1 cm/s) whenever the two are more than `seated_reference.deadband_m` (2 cm) apart; a lean, a duck, or a look around held for less than half the window does not move the median, so deliberate head movement stays one to one. Otherwise in the seated posture, and in a standing posture on a runtime without a STAGE space, the baseline is the eye height at the last recenter, and vertical travel from it scales by the height ratio. The standing postures stay measured from the floor with no captured reference: the standing eye height is fixed and calibrated, and a recenter taken while ducking would otherwise move it. With the remap off, the vertical offset is the raw cockpit height, which already puts the seated eye height at the last recenter at the character's standing eye height. The seated crouch toggle lowers the seated eyes by `crouch.seated_drop_m`, with or without the remap, but only out of a vehicle seat, where it would drop the view into the footwell; the toggle keeps its state for when Rico is out. On foot, flicking the dominant stick down past `controllers.bindings.crouch_flick` toggles it, and the toggle acts only in the seated posture (including a seated `Auto`), because standing and roomscale players crouch physically.

The chase loop (`chase.enabled`, on). The chase runs in the roomscale posture, out of a vehicle seat. On each on-foot tick the chase velocity walks the body toward the physical head. It starts once the head's horizontal offset from the body leaves `chase.deadzone_m` and runs until the offset is back within half of it. While it runs, it closes the offset over `chase.time_constant_s`, up to `chase.max_speed_mps`, and it scales down as the move stick is pushed, so that stick locomotion takes precedence. The task's own velocity is read and the sum written back through the character's world-space accessors ([`Character::GetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetWantedWorldVelocity) and [`Character::SetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetWantedWorldVelocity)), because the proxy's wanted velocity is character-local. The sum may not exceed the faster of the task's own speed and the chase's limit, so the chase never pushes a running body past its animation's speed.

The camera is the animated head anchor plus the cockpit offset composed with the body rotation. When the chase moves the body, the anchor moves with it while the cockpit offset stays, so without a correction the camera would run ahead by the distance the body covered and the offset would never close. The cockpit shift takes the chase's part of the body's horizontal displacement back out of the cockpit offset. The locomotion task's post-hook submits each step's request, with the task and chase kept as separate vectors through the combined speed cap. The local character's pose-finalization entry completes it against the step's solved root displacement, from [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT0) to [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1). When the achieved displacement matches the requested sum, the correction removes exactly the chase vector, including sideways or opposing stick movement. The match tolerates the `f32` rounding of the two absolute positions, which grows with the distance from the world origin, so free movement far from the origin still matches. Collision response does not expose each input's contribution. In that case, the correction projects the chase onto achieved travel and limits it to both the achieved distance and the requested chase distance; a blocked chase perpendicular to wall sliding contributes nothing. This fallback can still attribute overlapping stick and chase movement imperfectly. The post-physics pose-finalization entry accumulates the correction and publishes its previous and current totals. Rendering reads that pair without consuming simulation requests, so multiple simulation steps between rendered frames retain their compensation. A completion without a chase request settles the pair. Each side of the head pose pair composes with its corresponding shift.

Teleports. A post-call detour on [`GameCameraManager::ResetCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::game_camera_manager::GameCameraManager##func-ResetCamera) counts a teleport, because the game resets the camera right after every discontinuity it makes to the player: an instant or deferred teleport, a respawn, a load's start position, and a playlist commit. A move that bypasses the game world, such as a vehicle teleport carrying its driver, is caught by a per-tick distance test: a body displacement further than `chase.warp_min_m`, and further than three times what the proxy's speed covers in the tick, counts as a teleport too. The test forgets the body's last position when the locomotion task stops running, because the body may move any distance in between. On a teleport the chase stops and the head probe forgets its last clear position. While the chase is on, the cockpit shift also re-bases, with no interpolation from the previous shift, so that the head sits over the body.

Recenters. A full recenter makes the head the cockpit origin and clears the shift. Turning the chase off, or leaving the roomscale posture, keeps the shift where the chase left it, because clearing it would move the view by the distance the chase walked the body; the next full recenter spends it. A yaw-and-height recenter keeps the horizontal position and turns the shift with the cockpit frame. The manual recenters (F7, the debug UI's button, the stick click chord, and the recenter on resuming gameplay) re-base yaw and height only while the chase is active (the roomscale posture, the chase on, and out of a seat), because the chase keeps the position converged; otherwise they re-base fully. A manual recenter also classifies the automatic posture at once and, while seated, captures the seated eye reference. The seat transitions request their own re-bases (`vr::request_seat_recenter`), which do neither, because they happen on the game's schedule rather than the player's.

The head fade (`head_fade.enabled`, on). On each on-foot tick a ray is cast from the last position the head held in the open to where it is now, and a hit means geometry lies between them. A second ray from the animated head, which the body's capsule keeps in the open, forgives a stale last position, so a head the body has carried around the obstruction counts as clear again. The casts use the player aim's filter (see "Aiming"), because a plain ray query can hit the player's own ragdoll and hit-proxy bodies, which a head moving down or forward past the torso crosses. The head's depth past the hit sets the fade: clear up to `head_fade.start_depth_m`, black from `head_fade.full_depth_m`, and smooth between. When the probe stops running with the locomotion task, its result expires and its fade clears.

The comfort fade (`fade.out_s`, `fade.in_s`). The head fade and the seat transitions each set a target darkness. The level eases toward the darkest target, darkening at the rate `fade.out_s` sets and clearing at the rate `fade.in_s` sets, and the VR blit scales each eye's image by one minus the level as it copies the image into the swapchain. The engine's own screen fade is not used, because the game drives it for its own transitions and a second writer would fight them. Darkening in the blit also leaves the flat mirror unchanged. A gap in the rendered frames resets the fade.

The visual crouch (`crouch.visual`, on). The eyes' drop below the character's standing eye height, after the head remap, marks the character crouched once it passes `crouch.enter_drop_m`, and standing once it is back above `crouch.exit_drop_m`. At the entry of the pose-finalization pass, after the body IK and the hands, a head drop queues HumanIK targets for the local player on foot and out of a seat: the hips lowered by `crouch.hips_share` of the drop, and both ankles pinned where the animation put them ([`HumanIK::AddEffectorTargetPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##func-AddEffectorTargetPosition) on the `MAIN` pass at [`SolveStep::FULL_BODY`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::SolveStep##variant-FULL_BODY)). The body then folds through the legs instead of bending the spine over. The pass takes the highest step queued, and only the full-body step includes the legs. A character at reduced level of detail, or with HumanIK disabled, is left alone.

The crouch capsule (`crouch.capsule`, on; requires the visual crouch). While the character counts as crouched, the simulation half requests the game's crouching proxy state ([`ProxyState::CROUCHING`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::ProxyState##variant-CROUCHING)) through the character's pending proxy state ([`Character::m_PendingProxyState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_PendingProxyState)), which the engine applies on the simulation thread after the state tasks run. The request is made only while the pending state is the character's default ([`Character::m_DefaultProxyState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_DefaultProxyState)) or already the crouch, so the game's own proxy states (swimming, the parachute, vehicles, and ragdoll) always win. When the head rises, the request is released only once a ray up from above the feet to the top of a standing character finds the headroom to stand. The frame half also releases it when the capsule is switched off or the locomotion task stops running, and the simulation half releases it on any tick the headset is not driving the view (the headpose source is not VR, the roomscale frame is stale because the headset went idle, or the game is out of gameplay).

The standing stance (`stance`). Under the controller scheme, with the headset driving the view, a standing character on foot plays the wielded weapon's held stance, because the game reaches its unarmed relaxed idle only by holstering automatically, which the mental-state cuts prevent ([idle-stance.md](../../engine/character/idle-stance.md)). The forced aim-family locomotion is not what picks it: the FPS-movement shim forces the aim flags only inside the movement tasks, the aim-state pin keeps them clear everywhere else, and the idle's stance follows the weapon's animation modifiers, which the engine sets each tick from the wielded weapon and [`Character::IsTryingToAim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsTryingToAim). The aimed stance plays only while the trigger is pulled, a grapple tether is held, or an explosive is wielded. Each tick at the pose-finalization entry the stance logic decides whether the character stands still: the controller scheme, the VR source, gameplay, the on-foot mode, the body's rule system in `S_IDLE`, the aim flags clear, no tether, not crouched, and a horizontal speed below `still_speed_mps` (the chase moves the body inside the idle). Leaving the ground, the headset, or the scheme ends the stance at once; anything else blends it out over `blend_out_s`. Two stages act on that decision, both off by default because neither has been seen in the headset. The weapon-agnostic body ([unarmed-body.md](../body/unarmed-body.md)), on by default, keeps the weapon's stances off the body on every on-foot tick, which covers the first stage; the first stage matters only with the weapon animations set to native:

- `relaxed_idle`: while the latest decision is standing still, a detour on [`Character::SetCharacterModifiers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetCharacterModifiers) keeps the weapon's modifiers off the body's rule system: the [`Character::SetModifier`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetModifier) calls made inside it reach every rule system but the first. The idle then plays the unarmed relaxed clip, and the upper-body graphs keep the weapon's modifiers. The relaxed clip's arms and hand pose take no account of the weapon, so it suits tracked controller hands and looks wrong on a hand that has yielded to the animation.
- `square`: once the character has stood still for `settle_s`, the squaring blends in over `blend_in_s`. At the full-body step it holds the hips at their animated position (unless the visual crouch has lowered them), turns the pelvis by `pelvis_share` of its animated turn away from the body's forward, places the ankles `feet_width_m` apart across the body at their midpoint's depth and their own animated heights, and turns each foot by `feet_yaw_share` of its turn, with `ankle_resist`. The chest turn, `chest_share` of the chest's animated turn, joins the body IK's torso rotation rather than queuing a second chest target, so it needs the body IK. Each turn is measured from the animated joints HumanIK drives (the upper-leg joints for the pelvis, the upper-arm joints for the chest, ankle to ball for each foot), each part is blended geometrically from the animation by the weight, and every turn is capped at 45°. The ankles replace the visual crouch's pins on the frames both run, which only happens for a small dip, since a crouch stands the stance down.

The Controllers tab's "Standing stance" section shows the body's rule state by name, the decision and its reason, the speed, the blend, the animated pelvis, chest, and feet turns, the feet's spacing and stagger, what the squaring queued, and which idle clip the modifiers select and whether the relaxed idle applied. The settings are at the end of the "Posture and roomscale" settings.

Seat transitions (`seat_transition`). The game's vehicle predicates do not separate an entry, a locked seat, and an exit, so the seat phase is classified from the body rule system's current state by name: entering, locked (including a manned mounted gun and the motorcycle), shuffling between seats, and exiting. The names are hashed at startup with the engine's state-name hash, so no hash is embedded. A state in none of the tables counts as out of the vehicle, which applies no fade and no re-base, and a state in none of them seen while the character still has a vehicle is logged. A locked state while the character has no vehicle keeps the previous phase. At each edge the mode acts:

- `Ride` (the default) does nothing, and the view rides the animations.
- `Instant` darkens the view during the entry, clears it and re-bases fully when the seat locks, shows the exit, and re-bases yaw and height when the character is out.
- `Fade` does the same, darkens the view during the exit as well, and holds the view dark for `seat_fade_hold_s` after the seat locks and after the exit ends, so that the re-based view settles before it clears.

A shuffle between seats stays locked and does not re-base. The re-base at seat lock is needed under a head-anchored camera: the seated head bone sits lower than the standing one and elsewhere, so the standing cockpit offset is wrong once seated.

The Controllers tab's roomscale section shows the posture, the STAGE space and its bounds, the head's height above the floor, the head height remap and its source, the eye drop and the crouch states, the chase's offset and velocities, the cockpit shift, the teleports and re-bases, the head probe, the fade's level and targets, and the seat phase with its last edge. It also has a button that toggles the seated crouch.

The section opens with the camera height readout (`payload/src/hooks/camera/height.rs`), which diagnoses a view that sits too low or too high. Each gameplay frame the headpose places the camera, the camera hook records the camera's eye point and Rico's animated eye midpoint, rebuilt from the published anchors the way the camera hangs its eye point from them (the head anchor, the neck delta, and the eye arm), both measured up from the character's root, the translation of its world matrix. Beside the two heights and their difference the readout shows the vertical offset the cockpit composes with (the head remap's offset times the world scale), whether the remap is on, the head's height above the STAGE floor, the player's standing eye height from the body dimensions and the character's eye height the remap assumes, the posture setting and the posture, the automatic posture's decision with its reason, thresholds, and dwell, the seated eye reference with its source and the adaptive baseline's median and sample count, where Rico is (on foot or in a vehicle seat), and the crouch, seated crouch, visual crouch, and capsule states, with the capsule's lack of headroom. With a zero vertical offset and no configured camera offsets the two heights agree, so a low view with a small offset points at Rico's animated eyes sitting below the assumed eye height, and a large negative offset points at the remap or at a seated eye reference above where the player sits. While Rico is on foot or in a vehicle seat the same readout is logged at info level (target `vr`) at most every five seconds. It only reads: nothing it records feeds back into the camera.

## Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier

This is the first half of milestone 3, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/` (`mod.rs`, `volumes.rs`, `holster.rs`, `foregrip.rs`, and `engine.rs`) and `payload/src/controllers/bindings/grab.rs`, and the settings are `weapons.holsters`, `weapons.foregrip`, `weapons.block_fire_during_actions`, and `overlay.grab_volumes`. The design is in the plan's ["Grip semantics"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#grip-semantics), ["Holsters"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#holsters), ["Two-handed weapons"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#two-handed-weapons), and ["Hand tiers"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#hand-tiers). Everything here acts only while the bindings inject, which is only under the controller scheme with a controller active.

The weapon handling runs on the input tick, in two halves around the bindings' tables. The first half reads the wielded weapon, places the grab volumes, and finds the volume each hand's grab point (the controller's grip, in the cockpit frame) is in, which the tables latch at the grip's press edge (see "Bindings and injection"). The second half acts on the grabs the tables report and holds the fire inputs back during a weapon action. Nothing draws or holsters a weapon except a grab, so a drawn gun stays in the hand until the player puts it away.

A grab volume is a sphere where a grip press grabs instead of performing the grip's default action, which for the off-hand is the grapple. The volumes are the body holsters, a held two-handed weapon's foregrip, the grenade, C4, and reload pouches, and a gun's slide, charging handle, or bolt while its chamber waits (see [reload.md](reload.md)), and the physical vehicle controls (see "Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight"). Each owning subsystem places its volumes on every input tick, with the hands that may grab each one. A hand is in a volume that contains its grab point and that the hand may grab; where such volumes overlap, any other volume wins over a holster, so a press meant for a pouch that also lands in a holster's edge takes from the pouch rather than drawing a weapon, and among the rest the hand is in the one whose centre it is nearest as a fraction of the radius. A hand in no volume but within `pouches.reach_margin_m` (0.05 m) of a pouch's surface is in the nearest such pouch: a hand feeling for a pouch on the body lands a little off it more often than not, and a grip press there must take from the pouch rather than fire the grapple.

The body volumes are placed in the cockpit frame. The cockpit frame composes onto the character with the character's animated head at its origin, so in it the body faces along the frame's axes with the eyes at the origin, turned to the player's estimated torso when the body alignment's estimate is on ([alignment.md](../body/alignment.md#the-holsters-on-ricos-skeleton), which also covers placing them on Rico's skeleton with `holsters.anchor`). The volumes therefore stay on the body when the player turns or leans the head to look at them, and move with the body when a stick turn or the chase moves it. Vertically, the body's origin follows the head down once the head drops further than `holsters.crouch_deadband_m` below its upright height, so a crouch carries the holsters down and a glance at one does not. A head that drops while it looks well down is bending over to look at the body rather than crouching: from 20° below level to 40° the body follows such a drop less and less, holding its last height at 40° and past, so the pouches stay where the hand is reaching for them; a head that rises is followed at once. Each volume sits at its configured offset (`offset_m`, mirrored for the left side) and radius (`radius_m`) from a landmark estimated from the player's body dimensions (`[body]`, [Body dimensions](#body-dimensions)): the hips, at a generic fraction of the standing height, as far out as the shoulder joints and as deep; the shoulder joints; the chest, midway between the shoulders; the chest's front on each side, 0.065 of the height below the shoulder joints, about half the shoulder width out from the midline, and 0.02 m in front of the eyes' vertical line, for the grenade and C4 pouches; and the belt's front on the side of the hand without a gun, at 0.58 of the standing height, an eighth of the shoulder width out from the midline (toward the middle of the belt, well inside the hip holster), and 0.03 m in front of the eyes' vertical line, where the belly is, for the magazine pouch. A pouch's offset carries it out from the body's surface, where a hand laid on the body closes. On the player's body the body volumes are tested against the player's own grip, before the body-scale remap, so a hand on the player's own belt finds the pouch on it whatever the character's proportions; the volumes on a held weapon are tested against the hand on the weapon.

The defaults are a hip radius of 0.12 m at (0.04, 0.04, 0) m from the hip landmark, a shoulder radius of 0.17 m at (0, 0.10, 0.10) m from the shoulder joint (above it and behind it), a chest radius of 0.09 m at (0, -0.16, -0.14) m from the middle of the shoulders, and a radius of 0.075 m 0.04 m in front of its landmark for each pouch. The pouches are laid out around the gun hand rather than the dominant hand: the hand holding a gun, or with none drawn, the hand that last held one, or before any, the dominant hand. A single gun stays on the hand attach bone the game gives it, so a left-handed player can hold it in the right hand, and a layout keyed off the dominant hand would put the magazine pouch on the gun's side; remembering the last gun hand keeps the pouches from changing sides at each holster. The grenade pouch is on the gun hand's side of the chest and the C4 pouch on the other (`pouches.grenade_side` puts the grenades on the free hand's side, and the C4 then takes the gun hand's side), and the magazine pouch is at the front of the free hand's hip, or with both hands holding pistols, at the hip away from the gun hand; a layout for a left gun hand mirrors one for a right. With these, every volume keeps at least 0.01 m from every other, and every pouch at least 0.04 m from every holster, for the generic adult, the character's proportions, and a 1.8 m player, standing or seated, with either hand dominant and either grenade side (a unit test holds this).

The body is placed for one of two stances. Standing, the upright eyes are at the cockpit origin, as above. Seated, the torso above the hips is the standing torso, but the seat is where a standing body's hip joints are, so the hip and belt-front landmarks move to where a seated player's hands reach for them: a fifth of the way up from the hip joints to the shoulder joints (the belt line at the side of the waist), and 0.15 m forward over the thighs. The shoulder volumes come 0.05 m forward, over the tops of the shoulders rather than behind them, where a backrest is. The chest stays. The stance comes from the resolved roomscale posture (`controllers.roomscale.posture`, [Posture and roomscale](#posture-and-roomscale)), the same one the head height and the chase use, rather than a detector of its own: the seated posture is seated, with the upright eyes at the cockpit origin, which the roomscale head remap already measures from the seated eye reference; the standing and roomscale postures are standing; and the automatic posture is whatever the automatic posture classifier currently detects, seated or standing. Without a roomscale frame recent enough to trust (the roomscale subsystems off, or the headset just started), the body is placed standing. Sharing the posture this way keeps the holsters from disagreeing with the view about whether the player is seated.

`pouches.show` (on) draws each placed pouch through the world-space overlay at all times while the weapon handling acts (in gameplay, under the controller scheme): a faint ring the size of the pouch, brighter while a hand that may take from it is within its reach margin, and highlighted while a hand is in it, so that the player can learn where the pouches are. On the player's body the volumes are reached by the player's own hand before the body-scale remap, but the hand is drawn remapped, so each body volume's ring is drawn shifted by the remap of the hand that reaches for it (the hand in it, the one hand that may take from it, or the nearer hand), where the drawn hand meets it. A pouch is placed only while its feature is: the grenade pouch with the grenade throw, the C4 pouch with the C4 throw, and the magazine pouch while the true reload reloads the held weapon. `holsters.show_volumes` (off) draws every body volume, the holsters too, at all times, fainter than a highlight, with the volume a hand is in highlighted as usual, so that the volumes can be found and their offsets and radii tuned live from the Controllers tab (the offsets are sliders per axis). Both need the overlay on, and work with or without `overlay.grab_volumes`. The tab also shows the stance the volumes were placed for and the last near miss. A grip press that grabs nothing within 0.3 m of a holster's or a pouch's surface is logged at debug level, at most once a second, with the nearest volume, how far outside it the hand was, the hand's offset from its centre, and the stance.

Holsters (`holsters.enabled`; the volumes `holsters.hip`, `holsters.shoulder`, and `holsters.chest`; with `holsters.follow_docks` a holster with a gun docked in it moves onto the gun, [physical-weapons.md, "Holsters on the docked guns"](physical-weapons.md#holsters-on-the-docked-guns)). Each holster carries one inventory slot ([`WeaponSlot`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::WeaponSlot)): the hips the dual-wield slot, the dominant shoulder the two-handed slot, the other shoulder the special slot, and the chest the planted explosive. Either hand may grab any holster. The wielded weapon is read from the selected slot ([`Character::m_SelectedWeaponSlot`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_SelectedWeaponSlot)) and from the hand attach bones its guns ride in the weapon's current state. A grab does one of three things:

- At the wielded slot's holster, by a hand holding that weapon, it holsters the weapon through [`Character::HolsterWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HolsterWeapon), as the game's own holster input does. The reload button's hold is not used, because with a heavy weapon that hold also drops the weapon and blocks melee until the melee input is released. The heavy slot has no holster, so a grab never holsters a heavy weapon; the reload button's hold still drops it, as in the game.
- At any other holster, it clicks that slot's select action ([`Action::SELECT_DUEL_WIELD`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-SELECT_DUEL_WIELD), `SELECT_TWO_HANDED`, `SELECT_TWO_HANDED_SPECIAL`, or `SELECT_EXPLOSIVES`), which the game acts on when the input is released: it switches weapons when one is wielded, and draws the slot when none is.
- At the wielded slot's holster, by the hand that does not hold the weapon, it does nothing.
- At any other holster, by the hand without a gun while the other hand holds one, it does nothing either: that hand is the one that reaches for the pouches beside the holsters, and a press that lands in a holster must not switch away from the gun. With independent pistol draws it still draws its own pistol while the pistols are wielded.

The hips work per hand with `dual_wield.independent_draw` (on, with the split): either hip acts on the grabbing hand's own pistol. A hand without a pistol draws its own, and a hand with one holsters it, unless it is the last pistol out, which holsters the slot through `HolsterWeapon` as above. A hip grab while the pistols are not wielded clicks `SELECT_DUEL_WIELD` as before, and the draw it starts brings out only that hand's pistol; a draw the game starts on its own (a slot select from the stick click, a switch, or the game's own draw) brings out both. See "Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand" for how the pistols are placed.

`holsters.stick_click_cycles` (on) keeps the off-hand stick click cycling to the next weapon while the holsters are on, as a fallback for a player who cannot reach them. With the holsters off, the click always cycles. While the dual-wield split acts the click retracts tethers instead and does not cycle, so the two never share a press. Nothing else is bound to the off-hand stick click on foot.

The foregrip (`foregrip.enabled`). A two-handed weapon is a weapon in the two-handed, special, or heavy slot that is wielded rather than mounted. It rides the hand the game attaches it to (the holding hand) and aims by that hand alone. While one is held, the off-hand has a grab volume of `foregrip.radius_m` around the weapon's own grip point for the off-hand ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition) for that hand's [`WeaponGripHand`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponGripHand)), or, for a weapon without one, around a point `foregrip.fallback_distance_m` along the barrel from the holding wrist. The weapon's geometry in the holding wrist's frame (the foregrip point, and the barrel's direction along the gun's sight line) is measured on the input tick from the gun's last placement relative to the wrist target it was placed from. It is measured only when that target was reached at full weight, and it is frozen for the length of a hold.

While the off-hand holds the foregrip, the holding hand's poses turn about its grip so that the foregrip lies on the line from the holding hand to the off-hand, and the off-hand's poses move so that its grip lands on the foregrip, keeping the controller's rotation. The turn is the shortest rotation that carries the direction from the holding grip to the foregrip onto that line, so a foregrip below or beside the barrel leaves the barrel where the weapon's geometry puts it rather than on the line itself, and the roll stays the holding hand's. The turn stops at `foregrip.max_turn_deg` from where the holding hand points the weapon; past it the off-hand falls away from the foregrip and the hold lets go at the release distance, so an off-hand that crosses behind the holding hand cannot spin the weapon round toward the player. With the hands closer than `foregrip.min_span_m`, the line between them is too short to aim along and the hands stay as they are, and between that and twice it the pose fades out smoothly rather than snapping back. The pose is applied to the cockpit-frame hands that every hand subsystem starts from, so the IK targets, the late update, the gun's sight line, and the overlay all see the same two-handed pose. The engine's own off-hand grip IK stays suppressed (see "Hands"): the mod places the off-hand on the `MAIN` pass with the rest of the arm, so the late update corrects it like any other controller-driven hand. Engaging and releasing blend over `foregrip.blend_s`. With `foregrip.hold` (on; the pause menu's `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD FOREGRIP`), the foregrip is held as a real one is, while the off-hand's grip is held, and the hold ends when the grip opens; off, the grip is a toggle, and the next grip press lets go. A hand holding the foregrip is always in its volume, so a grip press there never fires the grapple. The hold also ends when the off-hand moves further than `foregrip.release_distance_m` from the foregrip (measured with the holding hand in its full two-handed pose), when the weapon leaves the hands or another weapon is drawn, and when a hand loses tracking, the controllers go inert, or the foregrip is switched off. The recoil spring is stiffer under the grip (see "Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand").

The action-runs tier (`block_fire_during_actions`). The hand tiers classify a reload, a weapon draw, or a holster ([`StateBitFlag::SB_IS_RELOADING`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_RELOADING), [`SB_IS_DRAWING_WEAPON`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_DRAWING_WEAPON), and [`SB_IS_HOLSTERING_WEAPON`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_HOLSTERING_WEAPON)) as the weapon-action state, which the plan's table assigns the action-runs tier. The grapple states are classified before it. In the action-runs tier the game plays the action (the reload's timer and the weapon model's own magazine and bolt animation, the draw, or the holster) while the hand stays on its controller at full weight. With `block_fire_during_actions` set, the fire inputs are dropped from the tables' output while the state holds and the effective tier of the hand holding the weapon (or, during a draw, the hand the pouches are laid out around) is action-runs, so a trigger pull does not fire during the action.

Fire with empty hands (`fire_draws_weapon`, off). The game draws the selected weapon when the fire input arrives with nothing wielded or during a holster, and when the precision-aim input arrives with nothing wielded ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponHandling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponHandling)). Under the controller scheme a weapon comes out of a holster, the weapon wheel, or a slot select instead: for the length of that call the local player's fire and precision-aim entries of [`Character::m_WeaponInputs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WeaponInputs) read zero whenever they would draw (nothing wielded by [`Character::GetWieldingWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetWieldingWeapon), or a holster playing for the fire input), and they are put back afterwards for everything that reads them later in the tick. The hold-back lives in the weapon handling's existing detour (`controllers::aim_state`), with the logic in `controllers::weapons::unarmed_fire`. Setting `fire_draws_weapon` restores the game's draw.

The highlights (`overlay.grab_volumes`). The weapon handling submits a ring on the volume each hand is in, drawn at reduced alpha where the scene is in front of it, a ring on the gun's well while a round for the reload is in hand with a dot on the hand holding it, and a dot on a held foregrip.

The Controllers tab's weapon section shows the wielded weapon, its hands, and whether it is two-handed; each hand's volume and tier; the foregrip's measured geometry and whether it is the weapon's own grip or the fallback; the hold and its weight, the turn the last two-handed pose wanted and whether the limit held it, and the grabs and releases; the draws the empty-hands fire input was kept from; a watch on each held gun's rotation relative to its wrist target from one tick to the next, which counts and logs a change of more than a right angle as the gun turning over in the hand (a diagnostic for a gun seen inverted: a flip there is the gun's attachment changing, while a flip in the headset without one is the hand's pose); whether fire is held back; and the recent grabs and what each did.

## Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand

These are the second half of milestone 3, and have not been run in the headset.

Recoil (`controllers.weapons.recoil`). A detour on [`WeaponBase::Fire_ProjectileBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-Fire_ProjectileBase) reads the change in the weapon's per-tick recoil ([`m_RecoilPitch`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_RecoilPitch) and [`m_RecoilYaw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_RecoilYaw)) across each shot of the local player's gun, and kicks a per-hand damped spring by it: about the controller's grip, a pitch of the barrel (the wrist frame's fingers axis, along which the hand holds the gun) about the axis across it, a yaw about the axis up the handle square to the barrel, and a shift back along the barrel. The spring's offset is applied to the active hands after the foregrip pose, so the IK, the late update, the sight line, and the overlay all carry it. Under the foregrip the kick is smaller, the spring stiffer, and the off-hand moves with the gun. Held in one hand, a long gun's kick is multiplied by its class's one-handed kick scale, which gives way to the two-hand scale as the foregrip engages (see the one-handed weight below). While the guns aim by hand, a replacing detour on [`WeaponCameraModifier::ProcessCameraContext`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::weapon_camera_modifier::WeaponCameraModifier##func-ProcessCameraContext) writes the camera transform without turning the boom by the recoil (or running the snap-aim assist), because the boom's turn persists into the gameplay camera's yaw and pitch.

The one-handed weight (`controllers.weapons.heft`, on). Held in one hand, a long gun is awkward, so that the player braces it on the foregrip. The class comes from the weapon's [`m_Class`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Class): pistols, revolvers, and SMGs are sidearms, which carry no one-handed weight by default (the SMGs are told apart for the two-handed weight below); assault rifles, shotguns, and sniper rifles are rifles, which get a little; launchers, miniguns, mortars, the mounted-calibre guns, and the special weapons are heavy, which get a lot. An unset class counts as a rifle in a two-handed slot and a sidearm otherwise. Each class has its own tuning (`sidearm`, `rifle`, and `heavy`). The input tick publishes the held weapon, its hand, and its class, and the weight is applied to the active hands after the foregrip pose and before the recoil, so the IK, the late update, the sight line, and the overlay all carry it, and a shot goes where the drooping gun points. The holding hand's poses turn about the controller's grip. The barrel is the wrist frame's fingers axis, along which the hand holds the gun, not the grip's -Z, which runs up the handle. The barrel pitches down about the horizontal axis across it by `sag_deg` plus `reach_sag_deg` as the hand moves from `reach_near_m` to `reach_far_m` from the head, scaled by how level the barrel is, so a gun aimed up or down still droops toward the ground; it sways by `sway_deg` on two incommensurate waves at `sway_hz` (half of it close in, all of it at full reach), in pitch about that horizontal axis and in yaw about the vertical, so the sway never rolls the gun; and the result is followed on a damped rotational spring (`lag_stiffness`, `lag_damping_ratio`), so the gun trails a fast turn of the wrist and settles after it. The whole turn is limited to `max_angle_deg`. A one-handed shot's kick is multiplied by `kick_scale`, and with `spread` set a detour on [`WeaponBase::GetCurrentScatterValue`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetCurrentScatterValue) multiplies the scatter the game gives the local player's shot by `spread_scale`. The scatter starts from the weapon's accumulated scatter, so the first shot of a burst from a settled weapon may have little to widen. The foregrip's weight scales all of it down, to nothing once the grip has engaged. The code is `controllers::weapons::heft`, and the Controllers tab shows the weapon's class and hand, the unbraced share, the reach, the sag, the sway, the spring's lag, the whole turn, and the count of widened shots. The volumes on a held gun (the foregrip, the well, and the action's handle) are placed on the gun as it is shown, in its two-handed pose and with its weight (not its recoil), so they are where the player sees them. While a round for the reload is in hand, and while the off-hand is in the foregrip's volume before it grabs, the weight freezes: the gun keeps the turn it had on the hand, without lagging or swaying, so the well and the foregrip move rigidly with the hand, and the spring follows on from there once the freeze ends.

The two-handed weight (`controllers.weapons.heft.braced`, on). Braced on the foregrip, a long gun keeps a weight of its own, so that it handles as a heavy object held in two hands rather than a pointer: it follows where the hands turn it on a critically damped rotational spring (`stiffness` per class, the damping ratio fixed at one), so a quick swing leaves it a little behind and it settles on the aim without overshooting, and its barrel sags a little (`sag_deg` per class). The classes are `smg` (the lightest), `rifle`, and `heavy` (the launchers and the other heavy weapons, the heaviest); pistols and revolvers carry none in either hold. A swing at a steady speed trails by about twice the speed over the square root of the stiffness: at a sixth of a turn a second, by under two degrees for an SMG, about three for a rifle, and about five for a heavy weapon by default. `strength` (1) scales it all, the sag by the strength and the lag time by the strength (the stiffness by its inverse square), and zero carries no weight. The whole turn is limited to `max_angle_deg` (8 degrees). While the foregrip is held, the off-hand is turned with the gun about the holding hand's grip, so the gun drives both hands and they stay on it; the drawn off-hand strays from its controller by at most the capped angle across the span between the hands. As the grip engages, the one-handed and the two-handed weights blend by the grip's weight: the sags and the limits linearly, and the springs by their natural frequencies, a rigid side counting as a very stiff spring. The kick and the spread keep their one-handed scaling. The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has the one-handed weight's switch, the two-handed weight's switch, and its strength.

Haptics (`controllers.weapons.haptics`). Two detours cover every source of the game's rumble, one-off and continuous alike, so a vehicle's engine and road rumble reaches the controllers as well as weapon fire, damage, impacts, and the rest. A pre-call detour on [`RumbleManager::SpawnRumble`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/rumble_manager::RumbleManager##func-SpawnRumble), which every one-off rumble goes through, picks out the weapon fire rumble inside a shot and pulses the hand whose gun fired it, raised by the shot's kick, and always lets the game's own call through so the rumble it starts still feeds the game's per-frame sum. A second pre-call detour on [`Gamepad::SetMotorValue`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::gamepad::Gamepad##func-SetMotorValue) sees that sum once a frame: the manager's left and right motor strength, already decayed and distance-attenuated, covering every active rumble at once, including a vehicle's own continuous engine and road rumble, which never calls `SpawnRumble` at all ([`RumbleManager::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/rumble_manager::RumbleManager##func-Update)). It forwards the low (left) motor to the left hand and the high (right) motor, which also carries the trigger motors, to the right hand as a continuous pulse refreshed every frame, skipping a hand a shot just pulsed precisely so the two paths do not double up on the firing hand, and, unless `gamepad_rumble` says otherwise, replaces the value it passes through with zero so the physical gamepad stays quiet while the controllers take over. The grapple hand pulses separately when a wire appears (the hook leaves the gauntlet), when the active wire attaches, and on a yank. With `volume_cues` (on; the pause menu's `HOLSTER AND POUCH CUES`), a hand that enters a holster or a pouch, or moves from one into another, gets a faint tick (`volume_enter`), and a draw, a holster, and a foregrip grab or release (whatever ended the hold) a firmer pulse (`grab`), on the hand that did it; the pouches pulse their own outcomes. Both scale with `intensity` like every other pulse.

Dual-wield (`controllers.weapons.dual_wield`). The pistols ride their own hand attach bones, so the controller-driven wrists carry them. With the split on and either `place_by_handedness` or `independent_draw`, the mod owns the pistols' attach bones while the pair is wielded: gun 0 belongs to the dominant hand with `place_by_handedness` (the right hand otherwise) and gun 1 to the other, and each rides its hand's attach bone while that hand has it drawn and the hip holster bone on that hand's side while it does not. The game rewrites the bone tables at the end of every pose-finalization pass and its state tasks move single guns within a pass ([hands-locomotion-and-state.md, "The attach-bone table is rewritten every pass"](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md#the-attach-bone-table-is-rewritten-every-pass)), so the mod writes both guns' wielding and dual-wielding entries before every placement (a pre-call detour on [`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)), after the end-of-pass rewrite (a post-call detour on [`Inventory::UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose)), so that the input tick, the gun aim, the per-shot seam, and the render-time late update all read the mod's bones, and at the weapon's per-tick update just before it fires. A pistol whose bone changed since its last placement snaps there through [`WeaponBase::SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame) instead of streaking across a frame. The layout is published from the input tick and republished at once when a hip grab draws or holsters one pistol; the detours compare the inventory's weapon with the owned pair's address before taking a lock. The weapon's state is never changed, so the game keeps the pair dual-wielded: the body animation, the reload, and the shared magazine are the pair's whether one pistol is out or two, and a pistol drawn alone fires from the shared magazine. With the game's reload, a reload refills it for both, and the undrawn pistol stays on the hip through the reload's own move of gun 0; the true reload splits it per pistol ([reload.md, "Dual-wield"](reload.md#dual-wield)). The game updates one gun per tick, the one at [`m_NextWeaponToFire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_NextWeaponToFire), and points that index at the other gun after every shot, so left to itself one held fire action fires both pistols in turn ([aim-pipeline.md, "Which gun fires"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#which-gun-fires)). The split therefore acts whenever the bindings inject and a pistol of the pair is in a hand, in every mode, and the mode decides only which triggers fire them: on foot, swimming, falling, under the parachute, and in a passenger seat each hand holding a pistol fires it with its trigger, and while the off-hand holds one its trigger, where it would retract the tethers, fires that pistol and the left stick click retracts instead; in the wingsuit and the driver's seat neither trigger fires them. Only the triggers of hands holding a pistol count as pulls, so an empty hand's trigger never fires the pistol waiting on the hip and never holds up the other hand's pull. On every update of the weapon in its wielding or dual-wielding state, the base weapon update's detour pins `m_NextWeaponToFire` before the aim point is chosen. With the fire action pulled, it pins the chosen hand's gun and hands the weapon that hand's pull, so that gun fires at its own hand's aim point. With no hand to fire, it withholds the fire input and pins the gun that fired last (or the dominant gun). With the fire action released, it pins the gun of the hand with a live pull, else the last to fire, else the dominant gun, and lets the game's input through. A buffered shot ([`m_InputBufferTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InputBufferTimer)) is dropped whenever no hand has a live pull, since it would otherwise fire the pinned gun for a pull that has ended. When the input tick is older than a quarter of a second, the dominant gun is pinned and the input passed through, so the worst case is the dominant pistol firing alone, never both. The split keeps a pull per hand: a semi-automatic weapon fires each pull once, the older pull first, clearing the weapon's own fired-this-pull, cancel, and buffered-shot state when a new pull is first presented; a fully automatic weapon ([`WeaponTuning::m_fully_automatic`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponTuning##field-m_fully_automatic)) fires the held hands in turn. Both triggers together fire both pistols alternately, not in the same tick: the engine starts at most one gun's shot per update, and the guns share the weapon's fire interval, trigger state, and magazine, so firing the second gun in the same tick would bypass the fire rate and the semi-automatic rule. Recoil, the fire rumble, and the split's shot count follow the gun that fired, read from that gun's attach bone at the per-shot seam. The Controllers tab's dual-wield readout shows whether the split is acting, the hand the last update handed the weapon, the pinned gun, and each hand's trigger, gun, and shot count, and, for the pistols' bones, whether the mod owns them, whether the pair is wielded, which hands have their pistol drawn, the hand of the first pistol, a hip grab's draw still waiting on the game, and the counts of table writes and snapped placements.

The anchored tier (`controllers.hands.anchor`). In the grapple reel and hang states the grapple hand keeps its controller position and turns so that its grip's pointing axis runs to the active wire's attach point ([`GrapplingHook::GetActiveWireAttachmentGraphicsWorldPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-GetActiveWireAttachmentGraphicsWorldPosition), read at the input tick). The mounted guns anchor the hands too (see "Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight"). A grapple hand that holds a gun of the wielded weapon, which is the second dual-wield pistol or a single gun in a left-handed layout, stays free instead of taking the line, so that its gun keeps aiming where the controller points. While hanging, a post-call detour on [`NStateTask_GrapplingHangTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::NStateTask_GrapplingHangTask##func-Update) takes the grapple hand's horizontal displacement from where it was when the hang began (relative to the head) and applies an impulse against it at the hips to the hang proxy's body, scaled from the game's own disabled continuous swing ([`GrapplingHangTask_SmallImpulseSwingMagnitude`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHangTask_SmallImpulseSwingMagnitude)). The hand's motion pumps the swing on top, capped together with the hold ([physical-grapple.md](physical-grapple.md#the-hang-swing)).

The yank (`controllers.bindings.yank`). At the input tick, the grapple hand's distance from a body point below the head is tracked over a short window; a fast enough drop of at least the configured length, while a grapple wire is live and the line is not already carrying the character (the reel-in, the wind-up to a hang, or the hang, where the grapple hand's pull swings the body), presses the grapple for one tick, which the game reads as the tap that zips. The pull also drives the reel's speed and the tether retract, and a yank during the reel boosts it ([physical-grapple.md](physical-grapple.md)).

## Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight

This is milestone 4, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/`, and the settings are under `controllers.vehicles`.

Driver weapons (`driver_weapons_by_hand`, on). The player's post-camera update hands the driven vehicle the gaze point through [`Vehicle::SetAimTargetPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-SetAimTargetPosition), and every built-in weapon aims from the stored point. With the option on, a pre-call detour substitutes the dominant hand's aim point from the same tick's aim update, for the vehicle whose local-player seat is the driver's. Turrets are left to the game: a turret turns toward its weapon's aim point at its own limited rate ([`TurretWeaponComponent::UpdateTurretAiming`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::TurretWeaponComponent##func-UpdateTurretAiming)), so a hand-aimed turret trails the hand as it trails the gaze. The motorcycle's and jetski's personal weapon is a wielded weapon on the hand's attach bone, so the per-hand gun aim already aims it by the dominant hand. Out of the driver's seat the dominant trigger fires the weapon in the hands or on the mount.

Mounted guns and gunner seats (`mounted.aim_by_hands` and `mounted.anchor_hands`, on). A mounted gun is the heavy slot's weapon in its mounted state. The hand tiers now classify the mounted-gun state before the vehicle states, because a gunner seat is a vehicle attach state outside the riding set and read as an entry, and the anchored tier is built for it. On the input tick the gun's origin, its barrel, and the handles the weapon's grip table places for the mounted state ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition)) are read. When the hands take the gun, a reference is taken: the hands' midpoint, the lever from the handles to the gun's origin in the body frame, and the turn that makes the lever lie along the barrel. The barrel then points from the hands' midpoint through the pivot, so pushing the hands left swings the muzzle right. The resulting point is handed to the gun through the per-weapon seam and becomes the character-level aim point, which the game draws the gun toward. The rendered hands sit on the handles with the controllers' rotation.

Physical controls (each on by default). The driver's seat offers a wheel (car and boat), handlebars (motorcycle, and the ATV, jetski, and duck, told apart by their steering fork), a cyclic and a collective (helicopter), or a flight stick and a throttle (plane), each behind its own toggle under `physical`. The wheel and the handlebars are placed where the vehicle's own are, read from its parts each tick, with the configured specs as the fallback, and the vehicle's rendered wheel turns with the steering ([steering-wheel.md](steering-wheel.md)); they are centred on the vehicle and never mirrored. The sticks and levers are placed in the cockpit frame relative to the seated character's eyes, which the head pose pins to the character's animated head as it rides the seat, with their sides mirrored for a left-handed layout. A control is a grab volume held only while the grip is: a grip press near it takes it, and releasing the grip, or moving further than `release_distance_m` from it, lets go. A hand that passes through a control, or rests on it with the grip open, takes nothing, and a grip that holds a control does not fire the vehicle's weapons. With both hands on controls (the wheel, or the cyclic and the collective) no grip is left to fire them; moving vehicle fire to another input while a control is held is not built. While held, the wheel turns by the hands' twist about its axis (or, as an option, their travel around its hub), accumulated so that it turns past half a turn and carries on across hand-overs, and steers by its angle over its lock ([steering-wheel.md, "Turning the wheel"](steering-wheel.md#turning-the-wheel)), the handlebars steer by their turn and lean by their roll, a stick's top follows the hand across the horizontal plane, and a lever follows the hand along its axis with a detent around neutral (the throttle stays where it is let go). Each held control replaces the tables' analog writes for the actions it drives, and the stick keeps every other axis. The holding hand is drawn on the control, recomputed from the latest controller pose at every rate. A highlight marks the control a free hand can grab.

Wingsuit arm steering (`arm_steering.wingsuit`, on). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/arm_steering.rs`. With the hands further apart than `min_spread` of the arm span (half of it by default), the difference in their heights banks, past `deadzone_m` and fully at `full_bank_m`, and `arm_steering.pitch_by` chooses what pitches. Everything is measured in the cockpit frame, whose vertical is the player's, with forward the horizontal normal to the line from the left hand to the right, so a player who turned since the last recenter still pitches by their own forward and back. The shoulders are placed below the tracked head by the body dimensions' eye-to-shoulder drop, which is the same seated and standing, and a little behind it.

- `WristTilt` (the default): both wrists' roll about the fingers' axis, from the pose with the back of the hand as near straight up as that axis allows. Tilting the leading edges (the thumb side of a palm held flat) down dives and up climbs, like a wing's angle of attack, past `tilt_deadzone_deg` (5°) and fully at `full_tilt_deg` (30°), measured from `tilt_neutral_deg` (0°, the palms flat). Because the roll is taken about the fingers, raising, lowering, or sweeping an arm turns the reference with it and reads nothing, so the pitch is independent of the bank. The headset test of the height pitch found it laggy against the stick: moving both arms up or down takes longer than turning the wrists, and it moves the hands' height, which the bank also reads.
- `Height`: both hands above or below the shoulders, lowered diving, fully at `full_pitch_m`.
- `Sweep`: the hands' midpoint ahead of or behind the shoulders' midpoint, swept back diving, fully at `full_sweep_m`.

The bank stays the height difference in every mode. Each wrist's tilt is the same gesture for both wings, so a bank read from a difference in tilt would turn the wingsuit whenever one wrist rolls more than the other while the player means only to pitch. `invert_pitch` reverses the pitch. Once steering, the arms keep steering until they come in past a share of the arm span narrower by a tenth, so a wing held near the threshold does not flicker. Nothing is filtered: each input tick's pose writes that tick's stick. The result is added to the move stick and written to the move half-axes the wingsuit task reads. With `arm_steering.show_guide` (on) and the overlay on, the overlay rings each hand in the wingsuit, faint while the arms are in and lit with a line between the hands while they steer, so that spreading the arms shows when they take over. With `arm_steering.show_readout` (off; `WINGSUIT ARMS READOUT` on the pause menu), a head-locked panel below the view (`payload/src/hud/arm_readout.rs`) shows, while the arms act, the spread against the share needed and whether the arms steer, the bank and the pitch input, the arms' stick vector, the player's stick, and the sum written. The input tick publishes the readout to a leaf slot that the panel reads on the draw task, and the panel repaints at most ten times a second. The Controllers tab's vehicles section reads out the same values, the tilt, height, and sweep together, and has the tuning. The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has the pitch measure, the full tilt, the inversion, and the readout. The wingsuit's rendered arms stay yielded to the animation.

Parachute handles (`parachute_handles.enabled`, on). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/parachute.rs`. The hands are not on the handles by default. One handle hangs a little above, ahead of, and outside each shoulder, where a hand raised to a canopy's toggle comes to rest standing or seated: its volume is centred `from_shoulder_m` (0.15 m out, 0.12 m up, and 0.12 m forward, below eye level) from the shoulder joint along the torso's heading (mirrored for the left), with radius `radius_m` (0.14 m), and only that side's hand takes it. The torso's heading is the torso estimate's while it is fresh ([alignment.md](../body/alignment.md)), so a player turned from the recenter's forward finds the handles by their own shoulders. While the estimate is stale (a moment's lost tracking), the last heading eases toward the head's over a two-second time constant, so the handles neither jump to the cockpit frame's forward nor stay on a stale heading; before any estimate, the head's heading places them. The handles used to sit 0.28 m above the shoulders, above eye level and out of sight, and nothing marked them; the first headset test did not find them. Like a physical control, a grip press inside the volume takes the handle, held only while the grip is: releasing the grip, or moving further than `release_distance_m` from it, lets go; the press is latched at its edge as for every grab volume, so a hand holding a handle neither fires the grapple nor draws from the shoulder holster, and a handle volume wins over a body volume it overlaps. A press outside every volume but within `reach_m` (0.30 m) of a free handle still takes it rather than firing the grapple, so a hand that misses the handle by a little is not thrown at the scenery; a grapple aimed with the arm out ahead is well clear of that reach. With `show_handles` (on) and the overlay on, each handle is marked while the handles act: a faint dot out of reach, a brighter dot and a ring the size of the volume in reach, and the same lit green while held, drawn where the hand holds it. The marks are anchored to the head at render time rather than to a world point placed at the tick rate, which a falling player would outrun. A held handle's pull is how far its hand has come down from where it took the handle, past `deadzone_m`, reaching a full pull at `full_pull_m`; raising it is slack. The right pull less the left banks toward the side pulled further, and the share both are pulled past `brake_threshold` (of a full pull) holds the stick back, which the parachute reads as its slow-down ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The stick's vertical axis"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-sticks-vertical-axis)). A handle let go or never taken is neutral. The handles' vector is added to the move stick and written to the same move half-axes the stick writes, so the game's own parachute physics flies the canopy, and the stick still steers and dives alongside; while neither handle is held, the tables' stick writes stand alone. The hands do not dive: a parachute's toggles only slow it, so diving stays on the stick. The holding hand is drawn on its handle, straight below where it took it and no further down than a full pull. The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has the switch, the marks, the handles' height above the shoulders, the full pull, and the brake threshold. The controls summary lists the handles on the grips under the parachute, with a mod string, since the game has none. The wingsuit and the parachute both have the head look-steer removed, so the head steers neither.

The wingsuit's head look-steer. The air movement core adds a camera look-steer computed by its own side-effect-free function, [`NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/input::parachute##func-NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering), for the local player. A post-call detour zeroes its output while the mode detector reads the wingsuit or the parachute, under the controller scheme, for a head-decoupled local player, behind the parachute's existing toggle (`movement.suppress_parachute_look_steer`). The core also serves the falls, which the gate leaves alone, and the wingsuit task's own update is never hooked.

## Grenades, aim assist, and reloads

This is most of milestone 5, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/` (`grenade/`), `payload/src/controllers/aim/` (`assist.rs`, `targets.rs`), and `payload/src/controllers/motion.rs`. The engine side is in [aim-pipeline.md, "Grenades"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#grenades) and ["Aim assist"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#aim-assist-updatealltargets). The scopes are the next section, and the sight lines are under "Aiming"; the hand-tier promotions are not built.

The hands' recent motion is kept for the throws. Every published controller frame adds each tracked grip's pose and velocities to a half-second history; an untracked frame adds nothing, so an inferred pose never feeds a throw. A velocity is the runtime's own where it reports one, and a difference of positions otherwise.

The grenade throw (`weapons.grenade_throw`, on; tuning under `weapons.grenade`). The grenade pouch on the chest (see "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier") exists only while the throw is on, whether or not the holsters are. Either hand may grab it with no gun drawn; while one hand holds a gun only the other may, so a grenade is never primed in the gun's hand, and while both hands hold pistols it is not placed. A grip press there asks the game's grenade weapon to prime a grenade in whichever hand grabbed it through its own prime request ([`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::m_PrimeGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##field-m_PrimeGrenade)), which the throw animation normally raises. The request is set before the weapon's update and refused when the game's grenade permission ([`Inventory::m_DoesAnimationAllowGrenades`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_DoesAnimationAllowGrenades)) is off, no grenades are left, or a throw of the game's own is under way; a refusal buzzes the hand that grabbed (`refused_pulse`) and is logged at info level with its reason. Priming consumes the grenade and starts its fuse. With the pin on (the default), the fuse is held until the pin is pulled, and from then a grenade held too long explodes in the hand ([physical-weapons.md, "Grenades as objects"](physical-weapons.md#grenades-as-objects), which also covers catching a live grenade). While it is held, the weapon's attachment update puts it at the controller's grip (`hold_offset_m`), and the fuse warns in its last `warning_s` seconds: a ring at the hand turns from green to red, and the hand pulses at a rate that rises toward the end.

Opening the grip throws. The release velocity is the average velocity of the held point over the samples within `release_peak_span_s` of the fastest moment in the last `release_window_s` before the release, because a throwing hand slows before the fingers open. It is turned into world axes by the body's rotation and the VR world scale, scaled by `velocity_scale`, and capped at `max_speed_mps`. A release with no tracked sample drops the grenade at the hand. The throw waits until the grenade has its physics body, one update after the prime, and then raises the weapon's fire request ([`WeaponBase::m_ForceFireProjectile`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_ForceFireProjectile)); a prime and a fire are never requested in the same update, because the weapon fires before it primes. A post-call detour on [`Grenade::PropelToward`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##func-PropelToward) moves the launch to the hand ([`Explosive::SetPhysicsTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-SetPhysicsTransform)) and replaces the solved [`Explosive::m_DeferredInitialVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DeferredInitialVelocity) with the hand's velocity plus the character's velocity that the launch recorded. When the throw stops acting (its toggle, a mode without free hands, the gamepad scheme), a held grenade goes back to the inventory ([`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::CancelPrimedGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##func-CancelPrimedGrenade)). No button throws a grenade: the dominant stick click, which carried the gamepad RB's grenade throw, now carries only the detonation (see below). While a throw is under way, any `THROW_GRENADE` is still held back so that a wind-up of the game's own cannot take the grenade, and with `hand_aimed_fallback` the dominant hand's aim point is still written into the aim control's grenade slot after the aim update, for the game's own throw.

The C4 throw (`weapons.c4_throw`, on; tuning under `weapons.c4`). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/c4/` (`mod.rs` and `stage.rs`), and the engine side is in [planted-explosives.md](../../engine/gameplay/planted-explosives.md). The C4 pouch sits on the front of the chest on the side the grenade pouch leaves (`pouches.c4`; see "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier"), exists only while the throw is on, and is taken from as the grenade pouch is: by either hand with no gun drawn, by the free hand while the other holds a gun, and not at all while both hold pistols. A grip press there primes a charge in whichever hand grabbed it, with a pulse (`prime_pulse`). The game's own planting goes through its planting mode: the explosives slot is selected, a ghost is aimed with the camera, and the charge is placed on the aimed surface by an animation. The mod bypasses all of it and never touches the selected slot. Before the C4 weapon's serial update ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::UpdateFromUserPostSim_Serial`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-UpdateFromUserPostSim_Serial)), which the inventory runs every frame whatever is wielded, it calls the weapon's own prime ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::PrimeExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-PrimeExplosive)), which spawns the charge as the planting mode's track message does. Priming moves the weapon to its wielding state, so the state it was in is put back at once. A grab is refused while as many charges are placed as may be ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::CanPlantExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-CanPlantExplosive)), while the game does not allow planting ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsPlantingExplosives`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsPlantingExplosives)), and while the game's own planting holds the weapon; a refused grab buzzes the grabbing hand (`refused_pulse`) and is logged at info level with its reason. Placing a charge consumes nothing, so the cap is the C4 count. While a charge is held, the weapon's attachment update puts its in-hand model and its transform at the controller's grip (`c4.hold_offset_m`) instead of the right-hand bone.

A charge pressed onto a surface sticks there instead of being thrown ([physical-weapons.md, "C4 pressed onto a surface"](physical-weapons.md#c4-pressed-onto-a-surface)). Opening the grip throws, with the release velocity estimated as the grenade's is (`c4.release_window_s`, `c4.release_peak_span_s`, `c4.velocity_scale`, `c4.max_speed_mps`). Once the charge has its physics body, the weapon makes it live ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::Fire_Projectile`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-Fire_Projectile)), which adds it to the placed charges the detonation reads, and the mod then drops it from the hand the way the game drops a charge under the parachute: it moves the charge to the grip ([`Explosive::SetPhysicsTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-SetPhysicsTransform)), drops it into the simulation ([`PlantedExplosive::DropExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::PlantedExplosive##func-DropExplosive)), pulses the throwing hand (`throw_pulse`), gives it the hand's velocity plus the character's [`m_MeasuredWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_MeasuredWorldVelocity) ([`PlantedExplosive::SetVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::PlantedExplosive##func-SetVelocity)), and plays its trail. Under the parachute the weapon has already dropped it from the right-hand bone, so the mod only moves it to the hand and replaces its velocity. The explosive's own collision handler sticks the charge to what it lands on. A release once the cap is reached takes the charge back rather than throwing one that nothing would detonate. When the throw stops acting, a held charge is taken back ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::DespawnPrimedExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-DespawnPrimedExplosive)).

The detonation goes through the game's detonate input ([`WeaponInput::E_INPUT_DETONATE_EXPLOSIVES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::WeaponInput##variant-E_INPUT_DETONATE_EXPLOSIVES)), so it detonates the placed charges exactly as the game's own detonation does, under the game's permission ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsTriggeringExplosives`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsTriggeringExplosives)). The dominant stick click, on foot, falling, and under the parachute, carries `DETONATE_EXPLOSIVE` alone, which the gamepad rules act on once it has been held past the game's hold threshold, after the stick click chord's window; a tap does nothing. The wrist detonator requests a detonation instead: the C4 weapon's serial update then raises the detonate input for its own update only, putting the game's value back afterwards, until the weapon starts the detonation ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::m_AlreadyDetonatedOnThisInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##field-m_AlreadyDetonatedOnThisInput) rises) or a short timeout passes. A detonation the weapon starts, from either source, pulses both hands (`detonate_pulse`) and flashes the wrist button.

The wrist detonator (`weapons.wrist_detonator`, on; tuning under `weapons.c4.detonator`; the code is `c4/wrist.rs` and `c4/press.rs`). A button sits on the inside of the wrist of the hand without the gun, at `offset_m` in that hand's grip frame with its face along `normal`, both given for the left hand and mirrored for the right, and the gun hand's index fingertip presses it; the gun hand is the one the pouches are laid out around (see "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier"), the dominant hand unless a gun rides the other. The button is placed on the off-hand's grip where the hand is drawn (the poses the rendered hands follow, after the body-scale remap and the two-handed pose), and the fingertip is read from the controller frame and carried with the dominant hand to where that hand is drawn, so the detector and the drawn button share one frame: the finger the player sees on the button is the one that presses it. The fingertip is the tracked hand skeleton's index tip when the runtime reports one, and otherwise a point in the dominant controller's aim frame: `fingertip_pointing_m` while the finger is off the trigger and `fingertip_resting_m` while it touches it. A press is a touch from any direction in front of the wrist: the fingertip within `touch_radius_m` of the face's centre and no more than `max_behind_m` behind its plane presses at once when it was closing on the centre at `min_entry_speed_mps` or more as it came in, and otherwise once it has kept touching for `dwell_s`. A fingertip further behind the plane has come through the wrist, which a real finger cannot, and is blocked; so is one first seen touching, so that regaining tracking never presses. After a press the button presses again only once the fingertip has moved away past `release_radius_m`. A press with charges placed requests the detonation and clicks both hands (`pulse`); a press with none detonates nothing, buzzes the pressing hand (`empty_pulse`), and flashes the button grey; both are logged at info level. The button is drawn on the wrist through the overlay (`overlay`), lying on the wrist facing out of it: a dim disc with a rim while no charges are placed, a red one while there are, with a pip beside it for each placed charge in a row along the forearm (up to eight), turning yellow as a slow touch dwells, flashing white on a press, and flashing orange and larger when a detonation starts. It acts in every mode outside the menus.

Melee has no controller binding. The game's melee input (`PUSH_GRAPPLE` under the hammer ability) is on no button and no gesture starts it, because the game's melee attack lunges the body toward its target, taking the body from the player. An empty hand's palm still meets the world ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md)), but passes through characters.

Per-hand aim assist (`aim.assist_strength`, 0 by default; `aim.assist_max_distance_m`, `aim.assist_occlusion_tolerance_m`). For each gun hand, the target sets that the aim control's last pass included are scored with the game's own weapon fitness ([`PlayerAimControl::GetFitnessScore`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-GetFitnessScore)), with the camera getters returning the hand's sight line so that the fitness cone is the hand's. Only live hostile targets ahead of the hand and within reach count. The best-scored one whose line from the hand is clear to within the tolerance of its body position is the candidate, and the hand's aim point turns toward it by the strength, its distance moving by the same fraction.

The true reload (`weapons.reload`, on) has its own page, [reload.md](reload.md): the game's own reloads of the hand weapons are stopped, and the player ejects with the reload button or a flick, loads from a universal belt pouch into the gun's real magazine well or by pushing the gun onto the pouch, and works the slide, charging handle, bolt, or pump after running dry (the bolt and the pump after every shot).

The empty weapon (`weapons.auto_switch_when_empty`, off; the pulse is `weapons.haptics.dry_fire`). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/empty.rs`, and the engine side is in [weapon-selection.md, "The switch away from an empty weapon"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-selection.md#the-switch-away-from-an-empty-weapon). The game switches to another weapon when the trigger of an empty weapon that cannot reload is pulled, which would take a gun out of the hand that drew it. A detour on [`WeaponBase::CheckIfReloadShouldBeTriggeredBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-CheckIfReloadShouldBeTriggeredBase) marks the check of the local player's weapon on its thread, and a detour on [`Inventory::TryToSwitchToValidWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-TryToSwitchToValidWeapon) reports, inside a marked check and with the setting off, that no weapon qualified, without switching. The game then handles the pull as it does with nothing to switch to: the out-of-ammunition notice and the gun's out-of-ammunition effect at the press edge, and no shot. The switch's other caller, the weapon-equip state task, runs outside a check and is unaffected, as are manual selection, scripted switches, and dropped weapons, which do not use it. Both act only under the controller scheme. The empty gun stays in the hand and can be holstered; the reload button does nothing until ammunition is picked up, and dual-wielded pistols, which share a magazine, both stay out. A press-edge pull of an empty gun that cannot reload also pulses the hand holding the pulled gun, whether or not the switch is suppressed. The weapon section shows the dry fires and the switches suppressed and allowed.

The Controllers tab's weapon section shows the throw's stage, the fuse, the last release velocity, and the throw's steps; the C4 throw's stage, the charges placed and the cap, the planting and detonating permissions, the throw's steps, and the detonations with the source of the last one (the wrist button or the detonate button) and whether the game started it; the wrist button's phase, the fingertip's distance from the button and height in front of its face, where the fingertip was read from, and the presses; the true reload's readout (see [reload.md, "The readout"](reload.md#the-readout)); and each hand's aim-assist correction.

## Scopes

This is the rest of milestone 5, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/scope/`, the shaders are `payload/src/shaders/scope_lens_vs.hlsl` and `scope_lens_ps.hlsl`, and the settings are `weapons.scopes` and `weapons.scope`. The engine side is [aim-pipeline.md, "Precision aim and sniper zoom"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#precision-aim-and-sniper-zoom).

Precision aim is suppressed (`scope.suppress_precision_aim`, on). It adds a main-camera zoom hint, and on a sniper rifle it shows the 2D sniper overlay and hides the character, which hides the hands. Every entry into it goes through [`Player::SetAimMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-SetAimMode): the precision-aim input, the sniper rifle's return after a bolt-action cycle, and the guided rocket launcher's shot. A detour there refuses precision aim under the controller scheme, so the mode, the hint, the aim control's aim type, and the weapon's own mode all stay on normal aim. When the scheme takes over while the player is already in precision aim, a post-call detour on the game's own per-tick exit check ([`Player::UpdateAimMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-UpdateAimMode)) leaves it through the same setter, which undoes the overlay and the hidden body as the game does.

The lens (`scopes`, on). A scoped weapon is one of the sniper class ([`WeaponBase::m_Class`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Class)), identified on the input tick. Its sight line comes from the gun aim, kept in the holding wrist's frame between aim updates and placed from the render-time wrist each frame, so the lens follows the controller at frame rate. The eyepiece is on the sight line `eyepiece_back_m` behind its origin, which with the weapon's sight correction is above the muzzle. The embedded sight table's scoped entries give every scoped weapon one; a weapon without one has its sight line along the barrel, below the scope, and the readout says so. While an eye is within `max_eye_distance_m` of the eyepiece and behind it, that eye sights, and the lens is drawn on it as a disc of `lens_radius_m` facing back along the sight line; the other eye sees the game's scope model.

The lens resamples a rendered eye image rather than rendering the scene again. At the end of [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw), each dispatch copies its eye's back buffer (both halves under the collapse) before the world-space overlay draws, while a scoped weapon is held, with the camera that rendered it. After the overlay, the sighting eye draws the lens: a point of the lens the eye sees at a tangent `t` off the sight line shows the scene at `t / zoom` off it, taken at the aim point's distance and projected into the source image, so the image is right at the target. The rim darkens from `vignette_start` of the radius, and a crosshair (`reticle`) marks the sight line. Outside the source image the lens is black.

The source image is the other eye's by default (`source`, `OtherEye`). The sighting eye's own image has the scope's model in front of exactly the region the lens magnifies, because the scope sits on the line from that eye to the target; the other eye, a few centimetres to the side, sees past it. In two-pass stereo, when the first eye sights, the second eye's image is the previous frame's, reprojected by its own camera, so the lens lags the scene by a frame there. `OwnEye` reads the sighting eye's own image of this frame, which shows the scope's model unless the weapon is hidden.

The narrow-field scene render (`path`, `SceneRender`) is not built; selecting it draws the resample, and the readout says so. It would be a third dispatch in the frame loop in `hooks/game.rs`, beside the far-field share's extra dispatch, and would need:

- a dispatch kind of its own, with the between-dispatch restores (effect info, frame counters, the constant-buffer ring, the SSAO and GI histories, the add-list parity) run before it as before the second eye, and prepass sharing to skip shadows and reflections;
- a narrow-field camera in the `SetupRenderCamera` hook: the sighting eye's position, the sight line's orientation, and a projection of the lens's true field, with no upscaler jitter;
- a smaller render: the scene targets' size is a re-initialisation, so the only cheap lever is a viewport in a corner of the full-size targets, with every full-screen pass (the post chain, the upscaler, the far-field composite, and foveation) either skipped or confined to it;
- the temporal effects kept out of it: the upscaler's history, auto-exposure, and motion blur would take the narrow view as the next frame of an eye;
- the `PostDraw` tail told apart: no HUD, overlay, capture copy, recorder, or screenshot for it, its image copied into the lens's texture instead, and its flip blocked;
- the gate on the scope being at the eye, so the cost of a nearly full scene render is paid only while sighting.

The Controllers tab's scopes section shows whether precision aim is suppressed and the refusals and exits, the scoped weapon and its hand, the eyepiece's distance from the nearest eye, and the lens's state: why it is not drawn, or which eye sights and which eye's image it reads, and the path in use.

### Arm reach

A fully extended player arm must ask for a fully extended character arm about the joint HumanIK actually reaches from. Three parts under `controllers.hands.reach` do this, and a fourth keeps the rendered hand with the controller when the solve still falls short. All four default on and act only where the hand IK or the late update acts.

At the pre-solve seam the hands measure the character's arms (`controllers/hands/rig.rs`) from the bones HumanIK drives for the shoulder, elbow, and wrist effectors, found through the solver's node-and-bone pairs rather than by name. HumanIK's shoulder node is the upper-arm joint, not the collar ([humanik.md, "Effector-id model"](../../engine/character/humanik.md#effector-id-model)), so the measured chain is exactly the one the wrist target is solved along. Each tick gives the upper-arm and forearm lengths and the shoulder joint's offset from the eye-bone midpoint, turned into the body frame's axes. The lengths settle over half a second and then hold, since the rig carries no scale; the offset follows the animation over `shoulder_smoothing_s`. A sample with a segment shorter than 5 cm or longer than 60 cm is dropped and counted.

With `measured_remap` set, the remap places the wrist at the character's shoulder, the head's cockpit position plus the measured offset, and scales its offset from the player's estimated shoulder by the character's measured shoulder-to-wrist length over the player's. The player's length is half the arm span less the shoulder width, less the hand, the figure the calibration splits into the segments; the saved segment lengths are not read. Both are divided by the world scale, since the character's arm is in world metres. The measured remap applies whether or not `controllers.body.remap_hands` is on; that setting governs only the estimated fallback, used for an arm not yet measured.

With `reach_pull` set, each wrist's position target carries a pull that ramps, by a smoothstep, from zero at `pull_start` of the character's arm length (the target's distance from his pre-solve shoulder joint) to `pull_max` at `pull_full` and beyond, so the shoulder and chest follow a reach the arm alone cannot make and the solve stays local otherwise. The flat `controllers.hands.pull` is the floor. Pull rides the position target ([humanik.md, "The four control values"](../../engine/character/humanik.md#the-four-control-values-and-which-list-drives-which)). When the body IK pinned that side's shoulder this tick, the pin's reach yields by the same ramp, so it does not hold the girdle back. On the procedural torso the pull is zero: the torso is the player's, and the arm reaches alone.

With `detach` set (and the late update on), the late update corrects against the solved wrist rather than the IK target. After the solve, each driven hand's shortfall, the tick's target less the solved wrist in the world, is published with the solved joints. At render time the reference the correction is taken from is the target moved back along the shortfall by the part the detach covers: the shortfall beyond `detach_threshold_m`, up to `detach_cap_m`. The rendered hand and the gun therefore leave the end of an arm that cannot reach by up to the cap, toward the controller. With `stretch_forearm` set, the forearm entries also scale along the corrected forearm's axis about the elbow, bounded to half again or two thirds of its length, so the forearm stays attached to the detached hand. Past the cap the hand stays at the cap. The detach rides the correction's tier weight, so while a hand blends toward the animation part of the intended offset from the target is detached too. The shot origin, the muzzle flash, and the sight line stay on the sim-rate transform, so with the hand detached they can be up to the cap away from the rendered gun.
