# Session capture

A session recording's video is made from the eye views inside the game: the payload draws both eyes into one frame on the GPU, reads it back without stalling, and streams it to a capture host, which encodes it with the default output's audio into the take's folder. The capture host is the injector, which stays running after it injects, on Windows and under Proton alike; on Linux the relay can take the stream instead and encode it with the host's own ffmpeg. With the in-process capture switched off, the relay records the desktop mirror window from the screen with gpu-screen-recorder instead. Either way the payload writes a per-frame pose sidecar that lines up with the video.

The payload side is `crates/jc3vr-tooling/src/recorder/`, the injector's host is `injector/src/capture/`, the relay is `scripts/capture_host.sh` running `tools/capture/relay.py`, and the post-processing tool is `tools/capture/reframe.py`. The module documentation of each covers the internals; this page covers what a take records, how the pieces fit, and how to run them.

## Session recording

F1 (or Numpad / through the director's keymap, or the Video feature's button) starts a session recording: the video take and a tracking recording ([testing/recorder.md](testing/recorder.md)) under one stamp, so both land in one `recordings/<stamp>/` folder ([sessions.md](sessions.md)). F1 again stops both, as it does when either runs alone. There is no video-only recording. When the capture host has not answered within a moment of the start, or refuses the take, the session carries on as tracking only, and the Video feature says why in red. The tracking half needs `testing.enabled` and a player character; its error shows there too. Neither half has a length limit or grows in memory with the session's length.

The two halves line up by the OpenXR predicted display time, which the sidecar's `predicted_display_time_ns` and the tracking frames' `display_time_ns` share; `tracking-synth dump --from xr:<ns>` prints the tracking around a sidecar frame.

F2 drops an unlabelled take marker into whatever records, and with no tracking recording running it also saves the tracking lookback (its window is `recorder.lookback_window_s` in the view crate's tuning) to `recordings/<stamp>/lookback.jc3trk`, so a moment is kept even when no session recording runs. The headset chord (left stick click, then right A, except in a vehicle; [input/controllers.md](input/bindings.md#injection-timing)) and the Video feature's "Mark (F2)" button do the same. The director's take-marker binding (Numpad 3) and the labelled Mark button write the marker only. A marker goes into the take's sidecar while a video take runs and into `markers.jsonl` in the session folder otherwise, and while a tracking recording or its lookback collects it is also written to the tracking timeline as a `take_marker` event, so the copies join by `index` and `stamp`.

## What the video shows

A streamed take (the default, `recorder.in_process`) shows both eyes as the headset receives them, left eye on the left, each scaled to `recorder.eye_width` (1920 by default) with its aspect kept, so the frame is about 3840 wide and 1900 to 2140 tall for current headsets. The other arrangement (`recorder.arrangement`, "Left eye, right at half size") puts the right eye at half size beside the left, at the top, with black below: about 40 percent fewer pixels, for a machine that cannot read back or send the full pair. The mirror window plays no part, so it can be anywhere or minimized.

The eyes are captured after the floating HUD, the panels, and the in-world overlays are drawn into them, so the clean view ([hud.md](hud.md)) at the everything scope takes those out of the footage. The desktop scope affects only the mirror window, so it changes nothing in a streamed take.

A mirror take shows the mirror window at the window's size, recorded from the screen ([vr-runtime.md](vr-runtime.md#the-desktop-mirror)), so the mirror view (one eye or the stereo pair, F10) decides what is recorded. The mirror must be on (`vr.mirror`) and the window visible for the whole take, on the active workspace and wholly inside its output: the recorder captures a fixed region, so a window moved, resized, or covered during a take spoils the footage. At the desktop scope the clean view keeps the flat debug overlay out of it.

## The in-process capture

On the render thread, in `render_engine_post_draw` on the frame's last near dispatch, once both eyes' captures hold the frame, `capture_frame` checks the pacing and, when a stream frame is due, draws one fullscreen pass (`i420_pack_ps`) that samples each eye into its rectangle and converts the display-referred RGBA to planar 4:2:0 YUV (BT.709, limited range) in a single `R8` texture (`recorder/convert/`). The packed texture is copied into a ring of staging textures, and the one written two captures earlier is mapped without waiting; if its copy has not completed, the previous image is repeated rather than stalling. While a streamed take runs, both eyes' captures are copied every frame even where the eyes would otherwise go straight to the headset's swapchain.

The stream holds a constant rate by the wall clock (`recorder.frame_rate`, 30 by default; `recorder/pacing.rs`). A rendered frame that owes no stream frame is not captured at all, and the frames after a slow one repeat the previous image. A frame the writer's queue cannot take owes its stream frames to the next one queued, up to a second's worth, so the stream stays as long as the take was and the host's audio, recorded against the same clock, stays in step.

The writer thread (`recorder/stream/`) sends the `yuv4mpegpipe` stream once the host confirms the take, and records each stream frame in `frame_map.jsonl` (below). The readback is the cost to watch: a side-by-side frame at 1920 per eye is about 12 MB, about 370 MB/s at 30 fps through the staging copy, the row copy, and the loopback socket. The Video feature counts frames captured, sent, repeated, dropped, given up, and queued; dropped and given-up frames growing during a take mean the host or the socket cannot keep up, and a narrower eye width or the half-size arrangement is the lever.

## The control protocol

The payload connects to the capture host over TCP (port 47172 by default) and speaks a line protocol: one line of UTF-8 JSON per message, at most 64 KiB. Either side ignores a message type it does not know, and a host refuses a start naming another protocol or a version it does not serve, so a change an old peer cannot read takes a new version number. `recorder/protocol/` is the payload's half; the injector's `capture/protocol.rs` and `relay.py` are the hosts'.

Version 2 is a streamed take. The start line carries the take's stamp, its folder as a host outside the game's Windows environment names it (`folder`, null on native Windows), the folder as the payload names it (`payload_folder`, which the injector uses), the window, and the `video` format: size, frame rate, pixel format, colour tags, the layout, and each eye's rectangle. The take's thread connects only once the render thread has captured the first frame, which fixes the size. The host answers `started` with the file, the encoder, and its audio source (or `none: <why>`), or `error` and a close. After `started` everything the payload sends is the stream; it ends the take by closing its writing half, and the host answers `stopped` with the encoder's exit code once the file is finalized. During the take the host may send `audio` when its audio capture changes.

Version 1 is a mirror take. The start line carries the stamp, the folder, and the game window's title and client size; the host answers `started` with the file, the video's start wall clock, the recorded region, and the output. The payload ends the take with a `stop` line or by closing the connection.

The injector serves only version 2 and refuses a mirror take with a message saying to turn the in-process capture on or run the relay; the relay serves both.

## The payload side

A take's thread (`recorder/take/`) creates the take's folder, opens the sidecar, connects with retries every half second until the host listens, sends the start line, and reads the host's answers without blocking. It retries only until the first connection succeeds: a host that refuses or drops the take ends the video for that take, and the error says why, while the sidecar keeps being written until the take stops. The host has 30 seconds to confirm, since the injector probes its encoders on the first stream of a size. At the stop the writer finishes the stream (or the thread sends the stop line), the sidecar is flushed, and the thread waits a bounded time for `stopped`; a disconnect or an expired deadline without it is reported as unconfirmed finalization. A streamed stop closes the writing half even when the host has not confirmed yet, so a slow host sees the end of the stream and can finish its replies.

The sidecar, `frames.jsonl`, is one JSON line per record in the order the records were made. A reader tells the kinds apart by the `marker`, `mirror`, and `present` keys, which frame lines never have:

- A frame line per rendered frame, written when every eye image of the frame is final: `frame` (the take's frame ordinal from zero), `unix_ms`, `game_run_time_us`, `predicted_display_time_ns`, and `views`, both eyes' raw located pose in the runtime's LOCAL space (orientation as `x, y, z, w`) and field of view as `left, right, up, down` angles in radians. Outside a VR session `views` and the display time are null.
- A marker line: `frame` (the frame it falls before) and `marker`, with its session ordinal `index`, the local `stamp`, `unix_ms`, `game_run_time_us`, and `label`.
- For a mirror take, a mirror line at the first mirrored frame and whenever the layout changes: `frame`, `unix_ms`, and `mirror`, holding `view` (`Left`, `Right`, or `StereoPair`), the client size `window`, and `draws`, each eye's whole image rectangle in window pixels in on-screen order. A single eye's rectangle extends past the window where the mirror crops it.
- For a mirror take, a present line per present of the mirror: `frame`, `unix_ms`, `present` (the mirror's present counter), and `stamped`, whether the frame stamp drew it.

The Video feature in the debug UI holds the in-process switch ("Capture the eye views in process"), the width of each eye, the arrangement, the frame rate, and the capture host's address and port, all applied to the next recording. Its readout shows the host's state, the encoder and the audio the host reports, the stream's size, and the frame counters.

## The frame stamp

While a mirror take runs, the mirror draws its present counter into the window's bottom-left corner as a strip of square blocks (`crates/jc3vr-view/src/vr/mirror/stamp.rs`), cleared straight into the back buffer just before the present. The present lines carry the same counter, so a run of identical video frames can be read: one stamp held across several video frames is the recorder or the compositor repeating a present, while consecutive stamps on identical images are the mod presenting a repeated eye image. It draws only during a mirror take; a streamed take has `frame_map.jsonl` instead.

## The injector's capture host

After it injects, the injector listens on the capture port on loopback and serves streamed takes one at a time until the game exits. `--no-capture-host` makes it exit after injecting instead, and `--capture-port` moves it. When the port is taken, another host (the relay, started before the injection) is serving, and the injector leaves the takes to it.

For each take it creates the folder from `payload_folder` and runs ffmpeg (`JC3VR_FFMPEG`, else `ffmpeg.exe` beside the injector, else one on `PATH`) through `ffmpeg-sidecar`, reading the stream from standard input and writing Matroska to `video.mkv`, so a take cut short by a crash is playable up to the cut. The encoder is the first of the hardware H.264 encoders, then libx264, then OpenH264, that ffmpeg lists and that encodes a short probe at the stream's size (`capture/encoder.rs`); ffmpeg lists what it was built with, not what the machine can run, hence the probe. The choice is cached per stream size for the injector's run, and `JC3VR_CAPTURE_ENCODER` forces one.

`scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.sh` (or `scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.ps1`) stages a pinned, checksum-verified LGPL ffmpeg build beside the built injector, with its licence. The LGPL build has the hardware encoders and OpenH264 but not libx264, which is GPL. Under Wine, ffmpeg.exe reaches no GPU encoder (the injector says so at start), so it encodes in software next to the game, which a 3840-wide stream is likely too much for; on Linux the relay is the better host.

The audio is a WASAPI loopback recording of the default output device, fed to ffmpeg as a second input over loopback TCP and muxed as stereo AAC. Loopback records everything the output plays, not the game alone, and delivers nothing while the output is silent, so each packet's capture time places it on the take's timeline, gaps becoming silence (`capture/audio/timeline.rs`). Wine implements loopback for its PulseAudio driver, recording the default sink's monitor; Proton is unverified. A device that does not open leaves the take silent, and the `started` answer and the log say why. `JC3VR_CAPTURE_AUDIO=none` records no audio.

## The relay

`scripts/capture_host.sh [fallback output dir] [-- extra gpu-screen-recorder args]` runs the relay on Linux; its header lists the environment overrides (port, codec, bitrates, audio source, ffmpeg, and a fixed window or region). The relay serves one take at a time and listens again after each, writing into the folder the start line names, or a stamped folder under the fallback directory (`captures/` by default) when it names none. Ctrl+C finalizes the take in progress and stops it. Start it before injecting, so it holds the port.

A streamed take is encoded with the host's ffmpeg, using the first of NVENC, VAAPI, and the software encoder for the configured codec (HEVC by default) that encodes a probe at the stream's size, at a bitrate scaled to the stream unless one is set. The audio is the default sink's monitor through ffmpeg's PulseAudio input, and `-shortest` ends the mux when the video ends, since the monitor never does.

A mirror take finds the game window under niri (by the title the payload sent, falling back to Proton's app id, then narrowing by client size and focus), computes its region on screen from the window's tile position and the output's logical position, and runs gpu-screen-recorder on that region with the default output's audio, confirming the start once the recorder's first frame arrives. It refuses a window not on the active workspace of its output or not wholly inside the output, and warns on a scaled output, where logical pixels are not screen pixels; each refusal names its reason, which reaches the in-game status. The video is HEVC at a constant 10 Mbps by default, about 1.1 GB for fifteen minutes of 1080p60. The relay strips the GPU-selection variables (`DRI_PRIME`, the Vulkan ICD and EGL vendor overrides) from the recorder's and ffmpeg's environment, since with them set gpu-screen-recorder finds no card to capture from.

After each take, the relay runs `scripts/rerun_take.sh` on the take's folder in its own session, so it runs alongside the next take and survives the relay stopping. It waits for the tracking recording's size to settle, writes `take.rrd` with the video ([testing/rerun.md](testing/rerun.md)), runs the view scan ([testing/view-scan.md](testing/view-scan.md)), and logs to `rerun.log`. `JC3VR_CAPTURE_RERUN=0` turns it off.

## Files and alignment

A take's files share its `recordings/<stamp>/` folder ([sessions.md](sessions.md)): the sidecar `frames.jsonl`; `frame_map.jsonl` for a streamed take, a line per stream frame with `stream_frame` (its number in the video), `frame` (the sidecar frame whose image it shows), and `repeat`; `video.mkv`, a streamed take's at the stream's size and constant rate or a mirror take's at the window's size and a variable rate; for a mirror take, `video.mkv.ts`, gpu-screen-recorder's first-frame time; and `stream.json`, the host's metadata (format `jc3vr-capture-stream`, version 2 for a streamed take and 1 for a mirror take), with the video's first-frame wall clock (`video_start_unix_ms`), the encoder or recorder, its exit code, the audio state, and the stream format or recorded region. A second connection for the same folder writes `video_1.mkv` and `stream_1.json` rather than overwriting.

In a streamed take, video frame `n` is `frame_map.jsonl`'s `stream_frame` `n`, at `n / frame_rate` seconds into the video. The audio starts when ffmpeg opens it, just after the first frame arrives; the offset has not been measured.

In a mirror take the sidecar lines up by wall clock: a line at `unix_ms` is at `unix_ms - video_start_unix_ms` into the video, since wine reads the host's clock. The video frame showing a rendered frame comes a frame or two after its line (the present, the compositor, and the recorder's interval); this has not been measured. The take markers give a second reference either way.

## Post

`tools/capture/reframe.py VIDEO.mkv OUT.mp4` turns a mirror take of a single eye into a flat video. It gives each video frame the pose of the last rendered frame at or before its wall-clock time and the mirror layout in effect then, builds the eye's pinhole matrix in video pixels, and warps the frame by the homography to a symmetric output camera of the requested field of view and aspect, so the crop is centred on the optical axis. `--smooth` makes the output camera follow a Gaussian-smoothed head orientation: rotation-only stabilisation from ground truth, nothing estimated, panning into the part of the eye image outside the crop. A take of the stereo pair is refused, and the tool does not read streamed takes. `--dry-run` prints the framing without decoding; both modes list the take markers with their time in the video.

## Operating it

On Windows, run `scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.ps1` once after building (`scripts/run.ps1` warns when `ffmpeg.exe` is missing beside the injector; any build with a hardware H.264 encoder or OpenH264 works). Start the game and run the injector, and leave its window open: it is the capture host and closes when the game exits, and its log names the encoder and the audio device at each take. Record with F1; the take's `video.mkv` is in its folder.

On Linux under Proton, start `scripts/capture_host.sh` (ffmpeg for streamed takes; niri and gpu-screen-recorder for mirror takes; Python 3 for the relay) and leave it running, then inject with `scripts/proton_run.sh`. The injector finds the port taken and exits after injecting; without the relay it stays as the host and encodes in software, after `scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.sh` has staged its ffmpeg. Record with F1; the Video feature and the Status page show the host's state, the encoder, the audio, the counters, and any refusal.

To record the mirror window instead, switch "Capture the eye views in process" off and run the relay; place the window where it can stay for the take and pick the view with F10. On an output where gpu-screen-recorder's region capture comes out squashed or offset, the display plane may hold a stale size from boot; switching the output's mode away and back clears it.
