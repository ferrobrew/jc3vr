# The procedural body

Rico's body has been the game's animation with corrections on top: HumanIK targets for the head, the torso, and the hands, a crouch that lowers the hips, and a stance that squares the feet ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md), [alignment.md](alignment.md)). The pelvis, the legs, and the shape of the spine stayed the clip's, so the player's waist and Rico's disagreed by whatever the clip did, and a hand on the player's hip landed beside Rico's. The procedural body builds the pose from the player instead. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/procedural/`, its settings are `controllers.body.procedural`, and the pause menu's `VR: BODY` page has the switch (`BODY FROM YOUR HEAD`).

It is off by default. The write seam has not been seen working in the headset yet; see the last section.

## Where the pose is written

The pose is written at the HumanIK solve gate: a pre-call detour on [`HumanIK::HasTargets`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##func-HasTargets) (`hooks::character::gate`), acting for the local player's solver and the `MAIN` pass. That call has one caller, inside the pose-finalization pass, after every game writer of IK targets and the first weapon attachment refresh, and before the solve, the second attachment refresh, the model-space sync, the hit shapes, the AI's view of the character, and the attached effects ([skeleton.md, "Who reads the pose before `UpdatePropEffects`"](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#who-reads-the-pose-before-updatepropeffects)). A pose written there is the one bullets hit and NPCs see, which a write at `UpdatePropEffects`, where the head override is, would not be. The solver is found from the character, which embeds it, and the gate runs inside the HumanIK gate the game checks first (the global switch and the reduced level of detail).

The writes go through [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) into the current pose, parents first, so each write carries the bones under it. The pose producer's pose pair ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#bone-store-hkapose-model-space)) makes the pose written this tick next tick's `T0`, so the skinning interpolates it like any other.

## The rig

The body drives the hips, the pelvis, both legs down to the foot, the four spine bones, the neck, the head, and the two clavicles (`LeftShoulder` and `RightShoulder`, measured for the chest's turn). `rig` finds them by name in the skeleton the animation controller holds ([`AnimationController::m_Skeleton`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##field-m_Skeleton)), and checks that each bone's parent is the one the solver assumes: the legs hang from `Pelvis`, a sibling of the spine under `Hips`. A skeleton that differs is refused with a warning (`procedural rig: ...`), and the body stays the animation's. The skeleton is logged once per session (`body: skeleton bone`, one line per bone), which is how a live Rico's hierarchy can be checked against the shipped one.

## Modes

- `Off`: nothing happens at the gate.
- `PassThrough` (`TEST` in the menu): the animated pose of the driven bones is read and written back unchanged, then read again. Every five seconds it logs `procedural body: pass-through wrote the animated pose back` with the largest read-back error in metres and degrees, which should be zero. Nothing on screen should change; if anything does (a twitch, a weapon off the hand, the head override fighting), the write path is wrong.
- `Full` (`FULL`): the body, below, standing and moving. Settings saved while this mode was named `Body` load as `Full`.

## The body

The body takes over while Rico is on foot on the ground with the headset driving the view and the movement task running, standing or moving. The air, vehicles, and every authored state keep the animation, and the hand-over between the two blends over `blend_s` seconds, eased. While the procedural pose has any weight, the pre-solve IK that would pose the same bones stands down: the body IK's shoulder, chest, and hand-pin targets, the roomscale crouch's hips and ankle targets, and the squared stance. The game's own MAIN-pass targets on the owned effectors (the hips, the waist, the chest end, the shoulders, the hip joints, the knees, the ankles, and the feet) have their reach and pull faded out by the same weight (`neutralize_targets`), so the solve after the gate leaves the pose alone. The head's own target stays: the body IK's head target closes what is left between the neck and the player's head.

The solver (`solver`, pure and unit-tested) works in Rico's model space from the animated pose:

1. The hips follow the head down: their height is the head's less the reference pose's head-over-hips height, at most the reference standing height (so legs are straight when the head is at standing height), and at least `min_hips_height` of it. Horizontally they sit over the middle of the feet, moved back by `counterbalance` of the head's lean ahead of its neutral place over the feet, so leaning forward hinges at the hips. They turn to the player's torso heading from the torso estimate ([alignment.md](alignment.md)), at most `pelvis_turn_limit_deg`, measured on the line between the hip joints. When a foot is further from its hip joint than the leg reaches (a long stride, a foot on a lower stair, a slope), the hips drop until it reaches (`reach_drop`), followed down quickly and back up at a third of the pace (`reach_drop_s`), so the body does not bob on every stride.
2. The chest twists to the torso heading, at most `chest_turn_limit_deg`, shared out up the spine (more of it higher), measured on the line between the clavicles. When the point under the player's head is closer to the spine's base than the spine is long, which happens once the hips are at their lowest and the head goes on down, the spine curls forward about the chest's sideways axis until the neck reaches it, as far as a back bends: a deep squat folds the back after the legs, and a head lower still is left to the head override. Then the spine swings so the neck points at the point under the player's head (the head position plus the animated head-to-neck offset turned by the head's rotation), shared out along the four spine bones and repeated three times to converge, never past `lean_limit_deg` from upright.
3. Each leg is solved with analytic two-bone IK so the ankle reaches its foot's place, bending the knee in the plane it already bent in, and the foot takes its place's orientation.

The rotations are composed onto the animated bones, so no bone's rest frame is assumed.

### Crouching and seated players

A crouch is the head going down: the hips follow it to `min_hips_height`, the knees bend, and the spine curls for the rest. The roomscale crouch's capsule request still runs, so the crouch also lowers the collision. A seated player's head is remapped to Rico's standing eye height (the posture's height remap, [head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)), so the solver gives a seated player straight standing legs; the seated crouch toggle lowers the remapped head by its preset and the legs fold the same way.

### The feet

The feet's places come from `solver::feet` (with `plant_feet`, the default). Each foot holds its place and orientation in the world while the body moves and turns above it, so the roomscale chase moving the root, the body turning to follow the torso, and the idle's own foot shuffle never slide it.

The feet stand around a neutral stance: the animated feet once Rico has stood still for half a second, measured once per rig and made symmetric about the root (`procedural body: measured the neutral stance (model space)`, info level). Until then, the animated feet stand in for it while standing, and the reference pose's while moving. A foot's wanted place is the stance carried by the root.

Standing, a foot steps to its wanted place once it is more than `step_threshold_m` away horizontally or `step_turn_deg` turned. One foot steps at a time, the worse one first, over `step_duration_s` on an eased arc lifted `step_height_m` at its middle, and lands wherever its wanted place has moved to meanwhile.

Moving faster than `gait_speed_mps`, the feet walk (`gait`, on by default; `solver::gait` sets the tuning). A phase advances by the distance the body travels over the stride, and each foot lifts when its half of the cycle comes round, the one furthest behind first. A foot in the air heads for its wanted place half a stance after it lands, so it passes under its hip at mid-stance, and that foothold moves on with the body every tick. The stride, the share of the cycle a foot spends on the ground (the duty factor), and the lift are set at a walking and a running speed (`walk_*` and `run_*`) and eased between them, so a faster walk takes longer strides at a quicker cadence and runs with a moment of both feet in the air. Below the walking speed, the stride shortens with the square root of the speed. The `WALKING LEGS` switch on the `VR: BODY` page turns the walk off, leaving the standing steps to chase a moving body. The body's velocity is the stick locomotion's commanded velocity ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md)), or the movement task's where the stick does not drive the body; the root itself moves only at that velocity, so the speed of the feet never moves the body. A stop finishes the steps in the air at the standing pace, and the standing steps then settle the feet under the body. A foot that ends up further than a few metres from its wanted place (a teleport, a respawn) is planted there afresh.

### The ground

The ground under a foothold is found with a scene ray straight down through it, from `max_step_up_m` above the root to `max_step_down_m` below it (`ground`, with `ground_casts`, the default). A step asks for it every tick it is in the air, since its foothold moves on; a planted foot keeps the height and the slope it landed on, so at most two rays are cast a tick. A foot lands at the ground's height plus the stance's ankle height, and tilts to the ground's slope, at most `ankle_tilt_limit_deg`. A surface steeper than about fifty degrees, or no hit, leaves the foothold at the root's height. The ray ignores the player, as the aim's does.

The rays are cast from the solve gate itself. The engine casts its own ground ray in the same pass, before the gate: [`Character::UpdateGroundInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateGroundInfo) reaches [`Character::CalculateHeightOverGround`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-CalculateHeightOverGround) whenever a character is off its support, which builds its hit record and filter on the stack and calls the same query ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay)) on the same layer as the mod's cast does ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#who-reads-the-pose-before-updatepropeffects)). The pass is queued for the parallel update of every character after the physics step, so such queries are made from the engine's worker threads against a settled world by design. The design's fallback, casting a tick ahead from the locomotion post-hook, is therefore not needed. The first cast of a session logs its thread (`procedural body: the first ground cast from the solve gate`, info level), and a tally of casts, hits, surfaces too steep, errors, and the largest ground offset is logged every ten seconds while casts run (`procedural body: ground casts`).

### The arms

The arms are the controllers' wrist targets ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md)), solved by HumanIK on the procedural torso after the gate. While the procedural body poses the torso, the hand IK does not queue them at the pass's entry; it keeps its plan for the gate (`hands::ik::queue_or_defer`), and the gate queues it right after writing the pose (`hands::ik::queue_deferred`). The rotation target is a delta from the wrist as it stands before the solve, and only after the write is that the wrist the procedural torso carries, so a target queued at the entry would turn the hand by however far the torso moved it. At the gate:

- The wrist target is kept out of the torso (`keep_out`, on by default; `HANDS OUT OF THE BODY` on the `VR: BODY` page). The torso is two capsules along the written spine: the chest from `Spine2` to the neck (`chest_radius_m`) and the abdomen from the hips to `Spine2` (`abdomen_radius_m`). A target inside either, widened by `hand_margin_m` for the hand's thickness, moves straight out of it, so a hand on the belly rests on it and an arm across the chest goes around it. The recorded target is the moved one, so the render-time late update corrects toward it.
- The pull is zero, and the body IK's shoulder pin does not yield: the torso is the player's, and the solve may not bend it toward a hand. A hand beyond the arm's reach stops at the arm's length.
- Each driven arm gets an elbow hint (`elbow_hints`, on by default): a weak position target on the elbow (`elbow_hint_reach`, scaled by the procedural pose's weight) at the middle of the shoulder-to-wrist line, out along a pole that points down, out to the arm's side, and a little back, as far as the arm's length bends it, and kept out of the torso the same way. It gives the solve a natural elbow without fixing it.

The first gate that queues the wrist targets logs `hand IK: queued the wrist targets at the solve gate` (info level, with whether a procedural torso was there), and a keep-out push is logged at most every two seconds (`hand IK: kept the wrist target out of the procedural torso`, debug level, with the distance). The hands readout on the Controllers tab shows, per hand, whether the target was queued on the procedural torso, how far it was kept out, and whether an elbow hint was queued. The physical hands' offsets are composed into the wrist before the keep-out, so a hand body pressed against the world and the torso's keep-out both apply.

### Diagnostics

The Controllers tab's "Procedural body" section shows the mode, the weight, whether the body owns the pose, a rigging error, the pass-through's read-back error, how far the hips sit below the animation's and the neck from its target, whether a foot is stepping, the body's speed and the walk's phase, the reach drop, whether the stance is measured, and the last tick's ground casts. While walking, `procedural body: walking` is logged every two seconds at debug level with the speed, the stride, the duty factor, the phase, the reach drop, and the neck's error.

## Not yet

- The clavicles do not yet rotate toward a hand reaching above the shoulder or far out; the arm alone reaches.
- The physical hands' keep-out for held objects uses its own body cylinder, not the procedural torso's capsules.
- In the air the body hands back to the animation; procedural landing and air legs are later work.

## What needs confirming in the headset

1. With `TEST`, the read-back error in the log is zero, and nothing moves.
2. With `FULL`, standing still: the readout's weight reaches one, the hips sit where the player's are (a hand on the hip lands on Rico's), the knees bend when the player crouches, the back curls in a deep squat, and the feet stay on the ground. Turning the torso turns the hips and the chest within their limits. The stance log line appears once.
3. The weapon stays in the hand, and shooting Rico's procedural body (from an NPC) hits where it is drawn.
4. Walking and running with the stick: the weight stays at one, the feet alternate without sliding, the cadence rises with speed, a run has a flight phase, and stopping settles the feet in one or two steps. Strafing and walking backward step sideways and backward. The debug line's speed matches the stick's.
5. On stairs and slopes the feet land on the treads and tilt to the slope, and the hips drop for the lower foot. The first ground cast's log line names a worker thread or the game thread, and the ten-second tally shows hits with no errors.
6. Turning the body in place (the right stick, or turning the torso past the follow's deadband) plants the feet and then steps them one at a time; walking in the room under the chase does the same; the readout shows `stepping` during a step.
7. A seated player gets a standing Rico, and the seated crouch toggle folds the legs.
8. With hands on: a hand held on the belly or the chest rests on Rico's body instead of sinking in, an arm folded across the chest goes around it, the elbows hang naturally, and the debug line `hand IK: kept the wrist target out of the procedural torso` appears while a hand is against the body. A weapon held at the chest still aims where the hand points; if two-handed aiming is pushed off, report the radii.
9. The engine's own reach for the owned effectors reads zero the tick after the gate (the body IK readout's engine values for the head stay non-zero).
