# Vehicle doors by hand

An empty hand opens a vehicle's doors as a real hand does, though more forgivingly: a grip anywhere on a closed door takes hold of it (on its handle when the hand is near the handle), a pull outward undoes the latch, and the door then swings on its hinge with the hand. Let go and a side door swings on, damped, and a lid stays up where it is left; shove it shut and it latches. Hoods, trunks, and the other hatches a vehicle's door controller keeps open the same way. The code is `crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/doors/`; the engine side is in [vehicle-doors.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-doors.md). It is always on under the controller scheme and builds on the pickup and the hand bodies ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#picking-things-up)). It has not yet been seen working in the headset.

The grapple snatch pulls a closed door open from range on the line, through the same reach and unlatch, with the grapple's aim point standing in for the palm ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md#phase-4-car-doors)).

## Finding a door

Panel acquisition requires an actual ray hit inside the door's model bounds. A nearby bounding box alone is not contact. Ordinary window-glass hits cannot acquire a car door, with impact sounds on or off. Queries continue past panes the glass feature has hidden without ignoring the rest of the vehicle; an empty window opening therefore supplies no door contact.

Known fighter, fighter-bomber, vintage fighter, and floatplane canopies are identified by their part entries in the embedded handle table. Their nearby scan probes the canopy surface independently of the estimated handle, and an intact canopy pane can supply a grip. Hidden panes cannot. A canopy grip stays at the actual hit point, and a grip on an inner door panel does not snap to its exterior handle.

Every door of every vehicle near the player is offered by its handle, whatever the reach's rays hit (`doors/scan.rs`). The vehicles are those the game's target system keeps within 6 m of a free palm, and, for each hand, the vehicles its reach rays hit that tick. The target system does not keep every vehicle: a parked civilian car can be missing from it, and without the rays its doors would never be scanned.

A door's handle comes from the door handle table when its part is in it ([`door_handle`](../../../crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/doors/geometry.rs)). The table is `tools/door_handles/door_handles.json`, embedded at build time (`doors/table.rs`) and keyed by the part's name hash, which the live part's entry in the parts resource carries ([`Part::m_name`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::Part##field-m_name)); every variant of a model shares its parts, so one entry serves them all. Each entry is a point and an outer-face normal in the part's own space, carried into the world as the door's box is. The tool places a front door's handle where the player's entry animation has its hand when it opens that door, on the door's outer surface (grade `clip`, or `clip_doubtful` when the hand is far off the door), and every other door's from its box with the hinge at its constraint point (grade `hinge`); [`tools/door_handles/README.md`](../../../tools/door_handles/README.md) has how, and the engine side is in [vehicle-doors.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-doors.md#entering-through-a-door). Every grade is used.

A door the table lacks (a DLC vehicle, or a part whose name hash is not in it) takes its handle from its part's model box alone ([`DoorBox::handle`](../../../crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/doors/geometry.rs)): the door is a panel, its thinnest extent through it; the hinge runs along one edge, and the handle is on the outer face (the face away from the vehicle's middle) at the opposite edge, a tenth of the door's run in from it, halfway along it (at mid-height for a side door, midway across for a lid). Which edge is the hinge's is read from the part's origin when it lies within a quarter of the door's run of one end, taken as the pivot; otherwise from the kind of door: a side door hinges at its front (the vehicle's -Z), a hood at its back, and a trunk, a rear door, or a hatch at its top (the front of a lid lying flat). A door part's origin is the vehicle's, not its hinge, so on the shipped vehicles the kind decides.

The first time the doors of a vehicle model are scanned, the log has, at info level, `physical hands: doors: door handles:` with the model and, for each door part, whether its handle came from the table (and the entry's grade) or from its box. A palm within the tuning's `grip.handle_reach_m` ([tuning.md](../tuning.md)) of a handle is offered that door, marked green on the handle. A shut door is merged into its vehicle's body, and a grip on it needs that body to prove the vehicle alive while the hand holds the latch, so a short cast from the palm to the handle, or failing that across the face at the handle, finds it; an open door's own body is taken directly. Each door near a palm is noted with its slot, whether it is shut, how far its handle is and where the handle came from, and why it was not offered (no model box, past the reach, no body found at the handle), and the notes are logged with the hand's next grip press ([grip.md](grip.md#readouts-and-logs)).

The pickup's reach rays ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#picking-things-up)) also ask about every hit on a vehicle or a loosened vehicle part, before anything else, so a player standing on a car can still open its door:

- A hit on a loosened part's object ([`VehiclePartGameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::VehiclePartGameObject)) that is one of its vehicle's doors, found through the object's vehicle and the door whose own body that object owns, is an open door, taken where the ray hit it.
- A hit on the vehicle's own body evaluates each door box in world space ([vehicle-doors.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-doors.md#the-loose-door)), using the part's model box, its root-space transform, and the vehicle's transform. The pure `doors::acquisition::panel` contract returns the reached box point, outward direction, and typed body: `PanelBody::LatchedHandle` for a closed door the palm reaches anywhere within the box reach (`grip.box_reach_m`), at the reached point, or at the handle when the palm is within the handle reach of it (`grip.handle_reach_m`, the reach the scan uses), or `PanelBody::OpenPanel` for an open door's own body within the box reach. The take away from the handle is there because the handles the table places are where the entry animation's hand goes, which on some vehicles is not where the drawn handle is (a fighter's canopy glass is well away from its table handle). A door wins over the climb's vehicle hold wherever it is reached, hoods and trunks included: a hand that closes on a door most likely means to open it, and a hand bracing on a door or hanging on to a riding car's side opens it only by pulling outward past the unlatch distance, which a car's own motion under the hand can do. The handle share is measured from the part's origin, its hinge pivot. Missing boxes, out-of-reach palms, and inconsistent door/body states refuse acquisition. The vehicle-body ray path maps those refusals to no door target; it does not alter the separate open-part ray path.

A hit on a part object that is not a door, such as a piece broken off, is taken as any loose object. A hit on the vehicle's own body that reaches no door takes nothing: however light, a vehicle is never carried (`bodies::classify` refuses it), so a grip near a handle that misses the door's reach does not lift the car. A reached door is marked green ([grip.md](grip.md#markers)).

## The latch

A grip on a closed door holds the hand on the grab point, where the hand took the door (the handle, when it was near it), carried with the vehicle (`doors::Latch::handle_palm`): the hand's body is held there as a climbing hand's is held on the wall ([hand-climbing.md](hand-climbing.md#gripping)), and a probed hand is drawn there, so a hand that took the handle from up to the reach away closes on it. The latch holds until the hand has pulled `grip.unlatch_pull_m` outward from where it took the handle, so the gap the hand reached across never counts as a pull, outward being from the vehicle's origin toward the door's centre (a side door's side, a hood's front and top, a trunk's back). Pushing in or sliding along the door never opens it. The door that is no longer latched when the grip looks (another hand, an animation, the AI) gives at once.

When the latch gives, the hand plays `Event::Unlatch` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)), and the door is loosened the game's own way without its motor (`doors::engine::unlatch`): [`Vehicle::OpenDoor`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-OpenDoor) puts the vehicle's body in the world and the vehicle in the update, so that the door controller keeps running for this door, the opening request it leaves is withdrawn at once so that no motor turns the hinge, and [`PfxBreakable::EnablePart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PfxBreakable##func-EnablePart) loosens the part onto its hinge, keeping its constraint. The door's entry is marked open, as the controller marks a door it spawns.

The hand then takes the door's own body (`doors::tearing::TearGrip::take`), held with the handle in the palm, so the door comes to the hand by the gap and the pull. Holding it at the offset the hand took the handle from left a hand that reached for the handle floating that far off the door for the whole hold: at t 332.75 in the 10 November session the private plane's door was taken from 151 mm, so the hand floated that far off the handle, and the held report showed the door a further 10 to 15 cm short of where that offset put it, at most of the hand's strength, until a yank tore the door off. An offset carried in the palm's frame swings with every turn of the wrist, often off the door's arc about its hinge, where the door cannot follow; a grab point in the palm can only be missed by as far as the hand leaves the arc. The vehicle body the reach captured at the handle need not survive the unlatch as the vehicle's own body: the reach may have hit another body the vehicle owns, and the loosened part may change it. So the vehicle is proven either by that captured body or by the loosened part's own reference to its vehicle, read through the held body, and the grip keeps the vehicle's current body for its later checks. Requiring the captured body to be the vehicle's own made the hand let go the moment the latch gave, leaving the door barely ajar. A refused take logs its reason with `the opened door's identity changed`.

## Seated doors and palm pressure

The seated path (`physical_hands/seated.rs`) acts only while the player occupies a seat, and only on the occupied vehicle's doors. It uses contact and reach rays, not ordinary physics hand bodies. Door grips use the existing latch and nonjoined carry drive. Generic pickups, character grabs, climbing, chassis shoves, and player-push locomotion remain disabled. Entering, leaving, changing vehicles, and teleporting clear the previous interaction and pressure history. Pending grabs and continued holds are restricted to attached doors of the occupied vehicle; a torn-off part is released.

An ungripped palm pressing a closed door's inner solid panel outward releases its latch after the same outward movement as a grip (`grip.unlatch_pull_m`). The gesture starts at first contact and measures displacement in the vehicle frame. A brief missed palm probe can retain progress for up to `pressure.contact_grace_s` only while a separate short ray still proves the same closed solid panel and the palm stays within `pressure.gap_drift_m` of its last contact position in that frame. No movement during the gap or the first reacquisition tick counts toward opening. Glass, an obstruction, a changed target, withdrawal, or invalid tracking clears the gesture. Existing penetration, vehicle translation, and vehicle rotation do not spend the opening movement. The contact query allows a wider pressure follow-through (`pressure.reach_m`) than a new grip. An inward push on a closed outer face or a slide along a panel does not unlatch it.

First valid inner-panel pressure contact plays one light tick. Continuing contact and a retained probe gap do not repeat it. Generic palm-touch and sustained palm/body-pressure feedback are suppressed for that hand while the door-pressure gesture owns it; impact physics and sounds remain active. A successful unlatch plays the stronger existing latch pulse. Neither cue needs a visible marker. Opening pressure applies a bounded impulse to the loosened door's own body. Continued pressure on an open panel can swing it either way, including shutting a canopy, without creating a grip. Ordinary car-window glass never supplies mechanical door pressure; a shattered opening supplies none either. Pressure probes stop at hidden glass shapes and clear the gesture rather than continuing to a farther panel. This conservative rule prevents the grace period from bridging an empty window or canopy opening. Grip queries retain their separate pane-continuation policy. Striking glass still belongs to the separate [glass feature](breaking-glass.md).

## Swinging and shutting

From then on the hand holds the door's own body as any loose object, where it gripped: through a joint to the hand's body with the hand bodies' joint grabs ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#holding-through-a-joint)), or carried otherwise. The hinge constraint removes whatever the hand asks that it forbids, so moving or turning the hand swings the door about its hinge and nothing else, with the hand's strength.

The game leaves a loose door on a bare hinge with no friction, and its latch merges a door that swings shut within [`DoorInfo::LATCH_ANGLE_DEG`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::DoorInfo##const-LATCH_ANGLE_DEG) of shut faster than [`DoorInfo::LATCH_SPEED_DEG_S`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::DoorInfo##const-LATCH_SPEED_DEG_S), a speed any rebound off the open stop, the player's capsule, or a lid's own weight reaches ([vehicle-doors.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-doors.md#the-latch)). So every door the hands opened (by hand or by the grapple snatch) is registered at the unlatch (`doors/hinge/mod.rs`), and has the controller's latch switched off: it is marked kept open ([`DoorInfo::FLAG_KEEP_OPEN`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::DoorInfo)) with its kept angle at the hinge's largest, which skips the latch and never asks the motor to reopen it. Each palm tick the mod then decides for it:

- A door a hand holds never latches, so it stays where the hand has it, wherever that is.
- A free side door is damped (`hinge.damping_per_s`), so a door thrown open slows and settles rather than bouncing back off its stop.
- A free lid (the hood, the trunk, a rear door, the hatches, and known aircraft canopies) above `hinge.strut_fraction` of its travel is held where it is against its weight each tick, as a gas strut holds it: a push down moves it, and it stays where the push leaves it. Below that share it falls freely. A lid thrown open carries on up, damped.
- A free door latches only when it swings shut within the latch angle faster than `hinge.latch_speed_deg_s` (six times the game's), or when it rests within that angle for `hinge.settle_s`. It latches through the game's own merge ([`PartInstance::MergePart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PartInstance##func-MergePart), then [`Vehicle::OnFinishedClosingDoor`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-OnFinishedClosingDoor)), and the log has `a free door latched` with the cause and speed. Merging destroys the door's body, so a hand on it loses the hold, as any hold whose body goes away.

Known aircraft canopies retain a supported pose relative to the chassis after they settle sufficiently open. Bounded velocity correction restores small drift instead of letting the canopy creep toward the face or lose support at the engagement boundary. Gripping the canopy releases that target. A successful ungripped palm push temporarily yields support. A clear closing movement from physical contact also yields support, while small drift remains corrected. Continued closing motion keeps support yielded until the motion settles. Support then captures the new pose if the canopy remains sufficiently open; below the engagement point it can close normally. Ordinary doors, hoods, and trunks keep their existing support policy.

Damping removes only motion relative to the chassis. The carrier velocity at the door's centre of mass includes the chassis's translation and rotation. A supported lid retains that carrier motion and compensates gravity in world space, so support does not stop the vehicle's motion. Missing or changed body ownership skips the velocity write.

A door the game asks to open or close (an entry or exit animation, the AI, a script) leaves the registry with the controller's latch restored, and so does one that latched or broke off by other means, and every door when the switch is turned off.

## Tearing an open door

Door tearing is always on, and its thresholds are the tuning's `tearing` section. An open grip must first settle: its lag (how far the grab point is from where the hand would have it), lag direction, and relative separating motion must hold within the settling tolerance (`tearing.settle_speed_mps`) for `tearing.settle_s`. Gestures are measured from that settled position, not from zero. Direction is measured in the vehicle frame, so turning the vehicle does not change the gesture. A heavy door held through a joint hangs a few centimetres off the hand, so a grip that had to come within a fixed few centimetres of the door might never settle. A grip that eases off settles lower at once, and one that creeps more slowly than the settling speed settles again where it is. The opening pull and its latch handoff cannot directly tear the door, since a hand moving the door has not settled.

After settling, three gestures tear. A yank opens the lag `tearing.yank_lag_m` above the settled lag while the lag grows, and the hand pulls away from the door, at `tearing.yank_speed_mps` or more: the pull a player makes on a door that does not give, since a held door offers the hand no resistance to yank against. A hard pull holds the lag `tearing.wrench_lag_m` above the settled lag for `tearing.wrench_s` however fast it got there, short of the 0.6 m at which a hand's body lets go of what it is joined to. A push holds a sustained grip load (`tearing.push_load`) and at least `tearing.push_lag_m` of additional outward tangential displacement from a fresh settled position at the authored hinge opening limit for `tearing.push_hold_s`. The opening direction comes from observed grip-point movement while the native hinge angle changes, in the vehicle frame. A yank or a hard pull whose lag runs mostly (`tearing.free_swing_share`) along a swing the hinge leaves free, opening away from the open limit or closing away from shut, does not count: the door would swing with the hand there, and the lag is a heavy door catching up. A door at rest has no observed swing, so any hard pull on it counts. Ordinary swinging, an under-threshold pull, and a closing shove do not request tearing. The rule is implemented in `doors/tearing/mod.rs`; the native hinge does not supply a force-break threshold.

Free swing clears tear intent. Arriving at or leaving the opening stop, or changing the pull direction by more than the angle whose cosine is `tearing.direction_cos`, requires fresh settling. An opening-aligned effort at the stop uses only the sustained-push gate; it cannot bypass that gate as a yank or hard pull. Reaching the stop in one continuous opening movement therefore does not tear the door. Same-direction positional lag from a wrist adjustment remains indistinguishable from a pull, so these gates reduce accidental tearing without proving intent.

In the 2 November session the player gripped an open door at rest through a joint and pulled it toward them at up to 0.8 m/s for about 30 cm. The yank then needed 1.8 m/s of hand speed and lag that had settled within 4.5 cm of the door, and the limit push needed an observed swing; none was met, and the joint ended when the hand's body fell 0.6 m behind. That pull is now a yank.

The pickup retains the vehicle, part index, native object, and body identity through opening. Each body is processed once even with two hands. Before requesting damage, it checks current ownership, vehicle and part protection, authored detachability, body-preserving break behavior, valid non-root part identity, representable nonnegative health, and the nonparallel update phase. It calls [`PartInstance::DamagePart_ToBreakOff`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PartInstance##func-DamagePart_ToBreakOff) with player attribution and forced damage only after those checks. Force permits the authored healthy-part restriction to be bypassed. Actual invulnerability remains protected. Locked and occupied vehicles have no additional mod-side immunity. No forced native work is queued across the parallel phase.

Confirmed detachment changes all matching held doors to ordinary props. The same native body, grab frame, and existing hand joint remain in use. Native door removal and the missing-door state come from the game's break notification. A closed or replaced native body ends the old hold instead of rebinding it to a new object. Each attempted tear requires the hands to settle again. Held-body impact feedback already accepts the surviving native object/body; it has no vehicle-part type exclusion.

The log has, at info level, `a hand asked to tear a door` with the hand, the gesture (a yank, a hard pull, or a push past the opening limit), and the gesture's lag, separating speed, opening push, load, and whether the door was at its limit; then either `a tear was refused` with the reason (the vehicle or the door is invulnerable, the door is not authored to break off or not allowed to now, it would disappear rather than come off whole, its part is not the one gripped, its health is out of range, or the vehicle is in its parallel update), or `the game was asked to tear a door off` with the result. A grip that never settles never asks. The debug readout counts confirmed tears and refused requests. Acceptance requires normal opening without tearing, a sharp yank, a sustained opening-limit push, under-threshold motion, two hands without duplicate damage, and refusal on invulnerable, mission-protected, non-detachable, disappearing, or invalid parts. Check that a torn body remains in hand, strikes scenery with impact feedback, and can be released and picked up as a prop. Check native missing-door state and vehicle interaction after tearing. Material/filter changes during native detachment and hinge-axis direction remain live acceptance risks until exercised in game.

## When the grip lets go

A grip on a latched door lets go with the grip, when the hand stands down, when the vehicle is gone (its body no longer belongs to it), when the door broke off, and when the latch did not give the door a body; each but the first logs `a grip on a latched door was lost` with the reason. A latch that gave a door whose own body then cannot be read within the body wait, or whose identity no longer matches the vehicle's door, lets go too, logging `the latch gave, but the door's own body could not be read` with the error or `the latch gave, but the opened door's identity changed`.

## Threading

The doors are found, gripped, and unlatched from the palms' tick (the player aim control's post-simulation update), on the game thread outside the physics step, as the door controller's own post-simulation update does it. The hand's place on the latch reaches the hand bodies' pre-simulation step through their own leaf lock, as the holds do. Every read of a vehicle's doors first checks that the vehicle is alive, through the body the reach found it on.

The hinge registry runs on the game thread before the hand-mode gate, including while seated or while the hands otherwise stand down. When the hands stand down (the gamepad scheme, or out of play), every door gets the native latch back.

## Settings and readouts

The doors have no settings: they are always on under the controller scheme. The reach, the handle reach, the pull that unlatches, the hinges, the pressure, and the tearing are tuning, in `crates/jc3vr-physical/tuning/doors.toml`. The debug UI's Characters and doors feature opens with the `VEHICLE DOORS` status row (under `HANDS IN THE WORLD`): the latched grips and unlatches, the last door's slot, and whether a hand is on a latch. Beneath it, the readout says which door a hand reaches (its slot and whether it is latched) and whether a hand grips a latch, and counts the tears and refused tears.

The pressure readout lists each hand's current door slot, outward displacement, required displacement, and result. Results distinguish no contact, rejected glass, no panel, an exterior or inward-facing contact, insufficient movement, contact loss, an open panel, a successful opening, an invalid sample, and native failure. Successful pressure openings log `door pressure: opened`. Native failures log `door pressure: native failure` with the underlying body error when available; an unchanged failure for the same target is logged only once.

A hold of a door that ends other than by the grip opening logs `physical hands: pickup: a hold was lost` with the reason: the door latched shut or broke off (its body went away), the door or its vehicle is no longer the one gripped, the hand's joint to it ended, it was torn from the hand, or the hand stopped acting ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#picking-things-up)). The log has, at info level, `physical hands: doors: a hand gripped a latched door` with the hand, slot, and part, `the latch gave, and the hand holds the door` with the door's body and whether it is joined, and `a grip on a latched door was lost` with the reason.

## Canopy audio investigation

Automatic outside-world sound deadening from canopy movement is not established. The inspected listener and sound-update paths contain no direct canopy-state or hinge-angle input. This does not exclude filtering in an authored FMOD event or another audio path.

The shipped fighter and fighter-bomber entities bind their engine-running sound to engine load, player control, engine timeline, speed, tilt, nitrous, steering, and engine RPM inputs. No explicit canopy-position input was identified in those bindings. Some authored input and output hashes remain unresolved, and the FMOD banks have not been traced, so this is not proof that cabin attenuation is absent. The aircraft engine-audio tuning describes engine response, not a verified cabin filter.

The generic attached-sound parameter dispatch and world-ambience mixing remain unverified as sources of cabin attenuation. No audio override is installed. Next-session check 72 compares manual opening, normal entry and exit, partial opening, and shattered glass while keeping the listener seated where possible.

- Which object a ray reports for an open door ([vehicle-doors.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-doors.md#unverified)). If it is the vehicle, the open door is found by its box, which is right only if the part's root-space transform follows the swing; if it is the part's raycast body, the hold takes the door's own body anyway.
- Whether the door controller marks the loosened door open on its own, and what an entry animation does with a door the hand holds.
- Seated inner-panel grips and ungripped pressure, canopy acquisition from both sides, canopy support after release, empty-window rejection, and behavior while driving or flying remain unverified in-game. The next-session checklist includes these cases.
- How the joint between a three-kilogram hand and a heavy door feels, and whether the hinge's limits hold against the hand's strength.
- The tear gestures' thresholds: that a firm pull on a door at rest tears it before the hand's body falls far enough behind to let go, that swinging a door open fast does not, and that a door torn off stays in the hand (its native body and owner survive the break).

## Headset checklist

With `GRIP MARKERS` on:

1. Walk up to a parked, empty modern saloon and reach toward the driver's door handle: a green marker on it, and the log's `door handles:` line for `modernsaloon` says its front doors come from the table (clip). Near the hinge: the door is taken there too, and a pull outward swings it little, the force acting near the hinge.
2. Grip: `a hand gripped a latched door`, a pulse, and the rendered hand stays on the door while the controller moves. The `VEHICLE DOORS` row says `ON A LATCH`.
3. Pull outward a few centimetres: the latch gives with a strong pulse, `the latch gave, and the hand holds the door`, and the door comes open with the hand.
4. Swing the door wide and back: it turns about its hinge only. Let go while swinging: it swings on, slows, and settles without bouncing shut off its stop. Stand in its swing: it does not latch against you.
5. Shove the door shut hard: it latches (`a free door latched: it swung shut hard`), and the hold ends if the hand was still on it. Ease it shut: it rests against the frame, then latches after a second (`it came to rest shut`). Hold it near shut and wiggle it: it never latches while held.
6. Open the hood from its front edge, and the trunk from its back edge. Let go of each a third of the way up or more: it stays there. Push it down: it moves with the hand and stays where left; push it below a third: it falls and latches.
7. Snatch a door open with the grapple: it swings wide and stays open. Have an NPC enter the car: the entry animation opens and closes the door as before.
9. Grip a door, then walk the car away (or have it driven away): the grip lets go with `the vehicle is gone`, or is torn away once the door trails past the break distance without following the hand ([physical-hands.md, "Holding"](physical-hands.md#holding)).
10. Grip the door handle of a parked civilian car, such as the vintage sports car: the door is taken, not the car, and the grip press notes the door with its handle distance. Near the handle but off it, nothing is taken; the car is never lifted as an object.
11. Open a door of a parked, empty, undamaged civilian car wide and let it come to rest. Grip it near its edge, hold still for a moment, then pull it hard toward you: it tears off (`a hand asked to tear a door` with `a yank` or `a hard pull`, then `the game was asked to tear a door off` with `Detached`) and stays in the hand as a loose object; swing it into a wall for impact feedback, drop it, and pick it up again.
