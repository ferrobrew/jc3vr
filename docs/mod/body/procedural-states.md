# The procedural body outside standing play

The procedural body ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md)) builds Rico's body under the player's head while he is on foot. In the other states the game's animation carries the body: the seat's pose, the swim, the harness under the parachute, and the wingsuit's flight pose are authored, and they are what the vehicle, the water, and the air move. This doc covers what the procedural body takes from them, region by region, and what it leaves. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/procedural/`, alongside the standing body's.

## The torso on the animation's hips

Where the body is not standing on its own legs, the hips belong to whatever carries Rico: the seat, the water, the harness. The standing body places the hips from the head, which would lift Rico out of the seat, so these cases use a second solve (`solver::anchored`, pure and unit-tested) that keeps the hips and the clip's posture and moves the spine from there by the player's own motion:

1. The chest twists toward the player's torso, by the torso's turn against Rico's facing ([alignment.md](alignment.md)), within `chest_turn_limit_deg`, about the clip's own spine and shared out up it as on foot. A spine far from upright takes less of the twist, and none lying flat, since the torso's heading is a turn about the vertical.
2. The spine swings so the neck reaches the point under the player's head, shared out along the four spine bones and repeated to converge, never past `lean_limit_deg` from the clip's own spine. A lean in the seat bends the back; the hips stay where the clip put them.
3. A look down pitches the chest forward by a share of it, as on foot, again by how upright the spine is, and the spine turns back onto the neck's line about its base.
4. The neck takes a share of the head's turn, which the head override composes onto the clip's head, so the neck's share is a share of that turn.

The clip's spine is not settled toward the reference pose first, as the standing body's is: a bike's lean into a turn and a driver's reach for the wheel stay, and the player's motion is added to them. A player whose head is where the clip has Rico's leaves the clip's pose exactly as it was. The clavicles then turn toward the hands as on foot, the hand IK queues the wrist targets at the gate against the written torso (kept out of it, with no pull, so the solve cannot bend the torso toward a hand), and the game's IK targets on the torso's bones fade by its weight ([procedural-body.md, "The arms"](procedural-body.md#the-arms)). The elbow hints point down and back from the chest, so they are queued only while the written chest is near upright.

The view's anchor in these states is the animated head ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)), captured before the gate writes the pose, so the head the solve reaches for is the clip's head moved by the player's tracked motion, with no feedback from the written spine.

A switch between the two solves while the body stays procedural would move the hips at once, so the last pose written before it is held and faded into the new one over `blend_s` (`solver::pose::SolveSwitch`). Coming from the animation needs no such fade: the region blend covers it. The first gate of each case that poses the torso this way logs `procedural body: the torso follows the head on the animation's hips` at info level, with the case, both weights, and the neck's distance from its target.

## Seated in a vehicle

Seated and riding, the torso is procedural on the seat's hips and the legs keep the seat's animation (`seated_torso`, on by default; `TORSO IN VEHICLES` on the `VR: BODY` page). The hand-over case is `seated in a vehicle`; it needs the hand tiers' classification of the seat as riding ([`Character::IsInRidingInVehicleState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInRidingInVehicleState), read by the input tick, so it lags by up to a frame), which an entry, an exit, a seat switch, and a mounted gun are not, and no stunt position. Those stay the vehicle's animation, and the torso blends between the two as the seat's state changes, so an entry blends the seat in after the entry's clip and an exit hands back before it.

The legs stay the seat's: the clip puts the feet on the pedals or the footrests and moves them with the vehicle's own animation (a bike's lean, the steering's turn of the hips), and nothing the player does with their own legs is tracked to replace it. The arms are the controllers' through the hand IK, on the wheel, the bars, or a held control ([steering-wheel.md](../input/steering-wheel.md)), and the clavicles reach with them. The driving view's correction moves the head the solve reaches for as it moves the view, so Rico leans by it as a player leaning the same distance would ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)).

The body IK's torso lean, which posed the torso in a seat before, stands down once the procedural torso has its full weight, as on foot. With `TORSO IN VEHICLES` off the seat's animation and the body IK's lean pose the torso as before.

The scenario signals `procedural.seated` and `procedural.neck_error_m` read the same, and `testing/scenarios/seated_torso.toml` is the template for a recorded check ([recorder.md](../testing/recorder.md)). The `VR: STATUS` page's `SEATED TORSO` row shows the torso's weight and the neck's distance from the point under the player's head; it is `OK` at full weight once a switch has faded, and `NOT SEATED` outside a seat. The Controllers tab's procedural body section says whether the last pose was the torso on the animation's hips or the body under the head, and whether a switch is still fading.

## The wingsuit

In the wingsuit the body keeps the flight pose (both regions stay the animation's; the hand-over case is `wingsuit`), and Rico's arms follow the player's (`wingsuit_arms`, on by default; `ARMS AS WINGS` on the `VR: BODY` page). The hand tier of the wingsuit stays yielded ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)): the hand IK cannot put Rico's hands where the player's are, since the player stands or sits upright while Rico flies prone, and a hand at the player's height relative to the head is nowhere near a prone flyer's shoulders. Each arm is instead carried over as the arm steering reads it ([`vehicles::arm_steering`](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)), in a frame that keeps the vertical and turns with the heading (`solver::wings`, pure and unit-tested):

- The player's wrist (the grip offset applied, not the body-scale remap) is measured from the player's estimated shoulder ([alignment.md](alignment.md)), in the frame of the player's torso heading. The cockpit frame's vertical is the player's real vertical.
- That reach is scaled from the player's arm (the body dimensions' upper arm and forearm) to Rico's (measured from the reference pose, [procedural-body.md, "The rig"](procedural-body.md#the-rig)), and placed from Rico's animated shoulder joint in the frame of his flight heading, with the world's up. The flight heading is the line from the hips to the neck laid flat, which a prone flyer holds along the flight, or the root's forward laid flat in a dive straight down.
- The wrist frame's orientation turns by the same turn between the two headings.

So the spread is the spread, a hand lowered for a bank is lowered toward the ground, a hand swept forward goes toward where Rico flies, and the wrists' tilt that pitches the wingsuit turns Rico's wrists the same way. The arms follow the player's whether or not the arm steering is switched on or engaged.

The targets are placed at the solve gate from the pose the animation wrote this tick, and queued once the gate has let go of its state (`hands::ik::queue_model_wrists`): a position and a rotation target on each wrist, at the hand IK's reaches scaled by the arms' weight, with no pull, so the flight pose's torso does not bend toward a hand. The weight eases in over `blend_s` in the wingsuit with both controllers read and out over `blend_out_s`, and while it eases out the last targets hold where they were. The body IK does not pin those wrists to the animation while the arms queue them (a tick behind, from the last gate). The first gate of each flight that places a target logs `procedural body: Rico's arms follow the player's in the wingsuit` at info level, with the hands placed.

The wingsuit's membrane is skinned between the arms and the body, so arms held far from the wing pose (by the sides, overhead) fold or stretch it as they would in the game's own arm animation; the arms spread as wings keep it as the flight pose has it.

The `VR: STATUS` page's `WINGSUIT ARMS` row shows the arms' weight and the hands queued, `OK` with both at full weight and `NOT IN THE WINGSUIT` otherwise. The scenario signal `procedural.wing_arms` reads the same, and `testing/scenarios/wingsuit_arms.toml` is the template for a recorded check. The Controllers tab's procedural body section shows the weight and the hands placed and queued.

## The parachute

Under the parachute the torso is procedural on the harness's hips and the legs hang from them (`parachute_body`, on by default; `BODY UNDER THE PARACHUTE` on the `VR: BODY` page). The hand-over case is `parachute`, from the mode detection, and the torso is solved as in a seat. The arms are the controllers' as before: the parachute's hand tier is free, so a hand follows its controller, or holds the handle it took ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)), and the clavicles reach with them.

The legs are the procedural body's, hanging (`solver::anchored::hang_feet`, pure and unit-tested): each ankle below its hip joint, along the hips' own down, by `air_hang` of the standing leg, the toes pointing down by `air_toe_drop_deg`, and solved with the two-bone IK with the knees bending toward the hips' forward. The handles' two-handed brake ([`vehicles::parachute`](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md), the share both handles are pulled past the brake threshold, read from the last input tick) swings the legs forward about the hips' sideways axis by up to `parachute_flare_deg`, shortens them a little so the knees come up, and brings the toes level, as a canopy pilot's legs come forward for a flare. The swing follows the brake over `parachute_flare_s`. A single handle pulled to turn does not brake, so it leaves the legs hanging.

The canopy and its lines are a separate model with its own skeleton; whether the lines ride the harness's bones that the torso solve moves (the spine and the clavicles) is not established, so a lean that moves the shoulders far from the clip's may part them from the lines a little.

Coming from a jump (the body under the head, legs in the air) the switch of solve fades from the last pose written; coming from a fall past the grace the region blend brings the body in from the animation. Landing hands back to the body under the head, which plants the feet afresh where the stance wants them.

The `VR: STATUS` page's `PARACHUTE BODY` row shows the torso's weight and the flare's share, `OK` at full weight with the legs hanging, `NOT UNDER THE CANOPY` otherwise. The scenario signals `procedural.parachute` and `procedural.parachute_flare` read the same, and `testing/scenarios/parachute_body.toml` is the template for a recorded check. The Controllers tab's procedural body section shows the flare.

## What needs confirming in the headset

1. Seated in a car: the log says the case changes to `seated in a vehicle` after the entry's clip, then `procedural body: the torso follows the head on the animation's hips`, and `SEATED TORSO` reads `OK` with the neck within a few centimetres. Lean forward, back, and to each side: Rico's back bends with you and his hips stay on the seat; the view does not dip into the chest. Turn your torso (both hands off the wheel): the chest turns, within its limit. Look down at the lap: the chest leans a little over it. The hands stay on the wheel while you lean. With `TORSO IN VEHICLES` off, the seat's animation and the body IK's lean pose the torso as before.
2. On a motorcycle, ride through turns: Rico's lean with the bike stays, the feet stay on the footrests, and leaning your head adds to it rather than fighting it. Report a jerk at the start or end of a turn.
3. Enter and leave vehicles, switch seats, and take a mounted gun: each logs the case `vehicle` while it plays, the torso blends without a pop, and a stunt position on the roof stays the animation's. In a helicopter and a plane, the torso follows the head the same way.
4. Get shot or thrown out of a seat: the ragdoll hands over at once, as on foot.
5. The wingsuit, watching the shadow: open it and spread your arms. The log says `procedural body: Rico's arms follow the player's in the wingsuit`, and `WINGSUIT ARMS` reads `OK, 100%, BOTH HANDS`. Rico's arms spread with yours; lowering the left hand lowers his left arm toward the ground as the wingsuit banks; sweeping both hands forward brings his ahead of his head, and back brings them toward the hips; tilting both wrists down tilts his. The membrane stays whole with the arms spread; report how it looks with the arms at the sides. Turn your body in the room while flying: his arms follow your torso's heading, not the room's. Open the parachute: the arms hand back to the animation without a pop. With `ARMS AS WINGS` off, the flight pose's own arms are back.
6. The parachute, watching the shadow: open it from a fall and from a jump. The case changes to `parachute`, the log says `procedural body: the torso follows the head on the animation's hips`, and `PARACHUTE BODY` reads `OK, 100%, FLARE 0%`. Leaning and looking around bends Rico's back over the harness; the legs hang straight down with the toes pointed, not the clip's legs. Take both handles and pull them all the way down: the legs swing forward and the knees come up, `FLARE` rises toward 100%, and letting the handles up lets the legs hang again. One handle pulled to turn leaves them hanging. Report whether the canopy's lines stay on the shoulders while leaning. Landing plants the feet without a pop. With `BODY UNDER THE PARACHUTE` off, the harness's animation has the body.
