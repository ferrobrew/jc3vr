# The procedural torso outside standing play

The procedural torso ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md)) poses Rico's spine, neck, and clavicles from the player on the hips the game's animation keeps. Standing on foot and in a jump the IK body poses the pelvis and the legs from the learned estimate ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md)); it stands down for every state here. Outside standing play the animation carries the body: the seat's pose, the swim, the harness under the parachute, and the wingsuit's flight pose are authored, and they are what the vehicle, the water, and the air move. The code is in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/body/procedural/`.

## The torso on the animation's hips

Outside standing play the hips belong to whatever carries Rico: the seat, the water, the harness. The torso is solved as on foot (`solver::anchored`; [procedural-body.md, "The torso on the animation's hips"](procedural-body.md#the-torso-on-the-animations-hips)), keeping the hips and the clip's posture and moving the spine from there by the player's own motion, with two differences. The clip's forward lean is not taken out, so a bike's lean into a turn and a driver's reach for the wheel stay, and the player's motion is added to them; a player whose head is where the clip has Rico's leaves the clip's pose as it was. The rig has a chest heading only while Rico is grounded ([rig.md](rig.md#the-yaw)), so in a seat, on a roof, under the parachute, and in the water the chest keeps the clip's facing.

The clavicles turn toward the hands as on foot, the hand IK queues the wrist targets at the gate against the written torso, and the game's IK targets on the torso's bones fade by its weight ([procedural-body.md, "The arms"](procedural-body.md#the-arms)). The elbow hints point down and back from the chest, so they are queued only while the written chest is near upright.

The view's anchor in these states is the animated head ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)), captured before the gate writes the pose, so the head the solve reaches for is the clip's head moved by the player's tracked motion, with no feedback from the written spine.

The first gate of each case that poses the torso logs `procedural body: the torso follows the tracked head` at info level, with the case, the torso's weight, and the neck's distance from its target.

Each state below has a status row in the debug UI's Body fit live status that shows the torso's weight and the neck's distance from the point under the player's head. It reads `Ok` at full weight and waits with a "Not ..." reason outside its state.

## Seated in a vehicle

Seated and riding, the torso is procedural on the seat's hips and the legs keep the seat's animation. The hand-over case is `seated in a vehicle`. It needs the hand tiers' classification of the seat as riding ([`Character::IsInRidingInVehicleState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInRidingInVehicleState), read by the input tick, so it lags by up to a frame), which an entry, an exit, a seat switch, and a mounted gun are not, and no stunt position. Those stay the vehicle's animation, and the torso blends between the two as the seat's state changes, so an entry blends the seat in after the entry's clip and an exit hands back before it.

The legs stay the seat's: the clip puts the feet on the pedals or the footrests and moves them with the vehicle's own animation (a bike's lean, the steering's turn of the hips). The arms are the controllers' through the hand IK, on the wheel, the bars, or a held control ([steering-wheel.md](../input/steering-wheel.md)), and the clavicles reach with them. The driving view's correction moves the head the solve reaches for as it moves the view, so Rico leans by it as a player leaning the same distance would ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)). The status row is `Seated torso`.

## Standing on a roof

Standing in a vehicle's stunt area (a car's roof, a boat's deck, a plane's wing; [stunt-positions.md](../input/stunt-positions.md)) is the case `standing on a vehicle`. The game's graph has no standing stunt state, so the animation state is the on-foot one, and the torso is procedural on the on-foot clip's hips with the clip's legs. The vehicle's interaction node carries the character without changing the animation. The case is a stunt area ([`Character::IsInStuntArea`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInStuntArea)), not a hang from a helicopter ([`Character::IsStuntHanging`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsStuntHanging)), and not a stunt traversal. It keeps its own case because the vehicle carries it: the view hangs from the clip's head, not from the root. A stunt traversal stays the vehicle's animation. The helicopter's stable hang is its own case, `hanging from a helicopter`, with the torso procedural on the animation's hips.

The animation set's `STUNT_COMBAT_MODIFIER` and `STUNT_NONCOMBAT_MODIFIER` select the stunt idle, movement, turn, and stop clip variants inside the on-foot states ([idle-stance.md](../../engine/character/idle-stance.md)). For the local player standing in a stunt area, `hooks::character::modifiers` drops those two modifiers on their way to the rule system, so the on-foot state selects its normal clips. The engine clears frame-bound modifiers and sets them again every tick, so nothing is left behind. The status row is `Roof torso`.

## The firm ragdoll

While the grapple lock holds the line (a swing or a hang) and under the canopy, Rico's hips and legs hang from the physics (`controllers::body::firm_ragdoll`, with a readout of the preset and the weight in the debug UI's Body fit feature), because the animated legs ignore the swing and hang stiff under the canopy. The game's ragdoll presets that keep the upper body keyframed to the animated pose and drive the hips and legs toward it with constraint motors do this without the character ragdolling ([ragdoll.md](../../engine/character/ragdoll.md)). On the line the mode asks for the grapple hang's preset, writing the character's preset id with its sync flag as the game's canopy update writes its own; under the canopy it keeps the canopy's preset, which the game already applies. Each tick it writes the ragdoll's blend weight just before the post-physics pass blends the simulated pose in, ramping in and out over the body [tuning](../tuning.md)'s `firm_ragdoll.blend_in_s` and `blend_out_s`, and once out it puts back the preset it found. A graph state that clears the preset while the mode wants its own is overruled on the next tick. The blend lands before the HumanIK solve, so the head, the hands, and the chest stay the mod's. The log marks the mode's entries and exits.

The ragdoll's own pre-physics step takes its muscle target from the animated pose before the solve, so the motors would drive the body toward the clip rather than the drawn body. The IK body composes a target from the drawn body instead ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#the-firm-ragdolls-muscle-target)), which a detour on the ragdoll's pre-physics step hands it in place of the clip's. That the motors hold the hips close enough to the drawn body for the holsters is unverified.

In the `grapple line locked` case, while the firm ragdoll does not own the legs, the gate relaxes them itself: each leg bone eases from the clip toward the reference pose, and the thighs hang along a damped pendulum from the root that lags the root's acceleration and settles back under it (the procedural tuning's `leg_swing`). The legs ease in and out with the torso's blend times.

## The wingsuit

In the wingsuit the body keeps the flight pose (the torso stays the animation's; the hand-over case is `wingsuit`), and Rico's arms follow the player's. The game's skydive, its free fall ([`SB_IS_FREE_FALLING`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_FREE_FALLING)), takes the same case: the game reaches it from a long, fast fall and straight from a scripted drop, and presents both belly down, so the view hangs from the head and the arms follow the player's as in the suit. The drop's lead-in clip, before the free fall's state, still reads as a fall.

The wingsuit's hand tier stays yielded ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)): the hand IK cannot put Rico's hands where the player's are, since the player stands or sits upright while Rico flies prone. Each arm is instead carried over as the arm steering reads it ([`vehicles::wingsuit::arm_steering`](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)), in a frame that keeps the vertical and turns with the heading (`solver::wings`):

- The player's wrist (the grip offset applied, not the body-scale remap) is measured from the player's estimated shoulder, in the frame of the player's heading. The body rig has a heading only while Rico is grounded, so in flight the arms read it from themselves (`procedural::wings::WingHeading`). With both wrists tracked at least `wings.spread_m` apart across the ground, the heading is the one the line from the left wrist to the right faces, unless it faces more than a right angle from the head, as crossed arms do. Otherwise it is a follower of the head (`wings.follow`) that no glance turns, and that turns slowly after the head has stayed well off to one side for a while, or settles slowly under a head held steady. The head's heading alone would turn the shoulders with every look at an arm and bend that arm. The same follower gives the torso's heading to every other estimate of the player's shoulders while airborne (falling, the wingsuit, and the parachute): the hands' shoulders, the arm remap, and the ripcord's place on the hip ([rig.md](rig.md#the-yaw)), so a glance under the canopy turns none of them. The estimated shoulders hang from the neck pivot (the eyes plus `body_rig::body::EYES_TO_NECK` turned by the head), with the eyes put back in front of it along the torso's heading, so a turn of the head that swings the eyes about the neck leaves the shoulders in place. The cockpit frame's vertical is the player's real vertical.
- That reach is scaled from the player's arm to Rico's and placed from Rico's animated shoulder joint in the frame of his flight heading, with the world's up. The player's arm is the body dimensions' shoulder-to-wrist length across a T-pose (`BodyDimensions::shoulder_to_wrist`), the pose the arm span is measured in and the one spread arms hold; the hands' reach remap reads the longer forward and hanging reach instead ([controllers.md, "Arm reach"](../input/controllers.md#arm-reach)). Rico's is measured from the reference pose ([procedural-body.md, "The rig"](procedural-body.md#the-rig)). The flight heading is the line from the hips to the neck laid flat, which a prone flyer holds along the flight, or the root's forward laid flat in a dive straight down.
- The wrist frame's orientation turns by the same turn between the two headings.

So the spread is the spread, a hand lowered for a bank is lowered toward the ground, a hand swept forward goes toward where Rico flies, and the wrists' tilt that pitches the wingsuit turns Rico's wrists the same way. The arms follow the player's whether or not the arm steering is engaged.

The targets are placed at the solve gate from the pose the animation wrote this tick, and queued once the gate has let go of its state (`hands::ik::queue_model_wrists`): a position and a rotation target on each wrist, at the hand IK's reaches scaled by the arms' weight, with no pull, so the flight pose's torso does not bend toward a hand. The weight eases in over `blend.in_s` in the wingsuit with both controllers read and out over `blend.out_s`, and while it eases out the last targets hold where they were. The body IK does not pin those wrists to the animation while the arms queue them. The first gate of each flight that places a target logs `procedural body: Rico's arms follow the player's in the wingsuit` at info level, with the hands placed.

The wingsuit's membrane is skinned between the arms and the body, so arms held far from the wing pose (by the sides, overhead) fold or stretch it as they would in the game's own arm animation; arms spread as wings keep it as the flight pose has it.

The `Wingsuit arms` status row shows the arms' weight and the hands queued, `Ok` with both at full weight. The Body fit live status shows the arm targets placed beneath it.

## The parachute

Under the parachute the torso is procedural on the harness's hips and the legs keep the harness clip's. The hand-over case is `parachute`, from the mode detection, and the torso is solved as in a seat. The parachute's hand tier is free, so a hand follows its controller or holds the handle it took ([controllers-and-roomscale.md](../input/controllers-and-roomscale.md)), and the clavicles reach with them.

The canopy and its lines are a separate model with its own skeleton. Whether the lines ride the harness bones that the torso solve moves (the spine and the clavicles) is not established, so a lean that moves the shoulders far from the clip's may part them from the lines a little.

Coming from a jump the torso stays procedural through the change of case; coming from a fall past the grace the torso's weight blends it in from the animation. Landing hands back to the torso on foot, with the legs the game's landing. The status row is `Parachute body`.

## The grapple's wall attach

A grapple that reels Rico to a wall can leave him attached to it in `S_GRAPPLE_ATTACH_0`, slumped back against the wall with his legs up, for as long as the player stays. None of the grapple's own predicates report it ([`Character::IsGrappleHanging`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsGrappleHanging), [`Character::IsStuntTraversing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsStuntTraversing), and the traversal mode), so the gate reads the body rule system's state by name (`procedural::state::grapple_attached`) and classifies it as a hang of its own, `grapple wall attach`, with the torso procedural on the clip's hips. The spine swings from the clip's reclining posture toward the point under the player's head within `torso.lean_limit_deg`, the chest keeps out of the near field, and the twist and the look down shrink with the spine's tilt from upright, as in the water. The view hangs from the animated head with the face clearance ([locomotion.md, "The steady view"](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)).

## Swimming

Swimming, the torso and the arms are procedural and the legs keep the swim's kick. The hand-over case is `swimming`, and the torso is solved on the swim's hips as in a seat. The swim lies Rico nearly flat, so the chest's twist toward the torso and its pitch for a look down fade out as the spine lies down (the torso's heading is a turn about the vertical, which a spine lying flat has no part in); the spine still swings toward the player's head within the lean limit, and the neck takes its share of the head's turn. The elbow hints are not queued while the written chest is far from upright.

The swimming hand tier is free (`HandTierTable::PLAN`), so the arms follow the controllers through the hand IK, queued at the gate on the written torso. The player's hands are placed from the player's head, as on foot, so a stroke the player mimes is the stroke Rico's arms make. The stick moves Rico through the water ([swim-yaw.md](swim-yaw.md)), and so do the player's strokes ([swim-strokes.md](swim-strokes.md)), which read the controllers rather than the drawn arms.

Walking into the water keeps the torso procedural throughout, on the hips of whatever clip plays, and the legs go to the kick as the game's animation blends them. Walking back out does the same the other way. The status row is `Swim torso`.

## The body mirror

In the headset Rico's body is mostly out of sight: the head is hidden, the view looks down onto the chest, and a seat, the wingsuit, or the water hides the rest. The body mirror is a diagnostic for checking his alignment with the player's (`controllers.overlay.body_mirror`, off by default; the `Body mirror` switch in the debug UI's visualization drawer). It is drawn by `controllers::body::mirror`.

It draws the pose rather than rendering the scene a second time: a second camera would be a third render dispatch per frame, doubling the scene's cost, with the render setups' state to save and restore. At the end of the local player's pose-finalization pass, after the head override and the limbs' twist, it reads a stick figure's bones from the finalized pose (the hips, the spine, the neck, the head, the clavicles, the arms to the wrists, and the legs to the feet, found by name once per skeleton), carries them into the world, and reflects them across a plane standing the controls [tuning](../tuning.md)'s `overlay.body_mirror_distance_m` ahead of Rico's facing (the root's forward laid flat, or its up where the forward points at the ground, as a prone body's may). The reflection is drawn through the world-space overlay ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md)), over the scene: lines between the bones (the spine white, the left side blue, the right orange), a ring at the head bone, the mirror's frame, and smaller rings at the reflected targets the body was posed toward, the player's head (green) and each controller's wrist target (in its side's colour). As in a mirror, the left stays on the viewer's left. A wrist ring away from the reflected hand, or the head ring away from the head, is a misalignment the player can see while moving.

The primitives are submitted from the pass's thread each tick into the overlay's `body mirror` slot, which expires a few ticks after the last submission, and are anchored to the head at an offset, so the figure rides with a player moving fast (a vehicle, the wingsuit) rather than trailing at the tick's rate. It costs a handful of joint reads and some forty primitives a tick while on, and nothing while off. The first pass on a skeleton logs `body mirror: found the figure's bones` at info level with any bone it did not find.

The `Body mirror` status row says how to switch it on while it is off, and how many of the figure's bones it found while it is on; finding none fails it.
