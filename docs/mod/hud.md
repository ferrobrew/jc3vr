# Floating HUD in VR

The flat game draws its HUD (health, ammo, minimap, reticle, objective markers, distance labels) straight onto the final image ([engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md](../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md)). In VR that would pin the whole HUD to the screen plane at infinity, which is unreadable and uncomfortable. The mod renders the HUD into its own texture instead and draws that texture as a quad floating in front of the head, damped so it follows the head without feeling nailed to it. World-anchored markers are reprojected rather than left on the panel (["World-to-screen"](#world-to-screen-split-the-hud)), and the parts that describe one world object leave the panel for their own depths (["HUD depth"](#hud-depth)). The code is `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/`, with the UI detours in `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hooks/ui/`; the settings are `hud` in the settings file (`hud::config`) and the tuning is `crates/jc3vr-hud/tuning/hud.toml`.

## Presentation

The panel is a quad the mod draws into each eye's scene at [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw), so it reaches the compositor inside the projection layer rather than as an OpenXR quad layer. The in-scene quad shows in the desktop stereo preview, so its distance, size, and follow can be tuned without a headset, and it shares the scene's depth with the world markers and the readouts that leave the panel. The cost is that the compositor resamples it with the scene, so text is a little softer than a quad layer would be, and it updates at the game's frame rate.

## Redirecting the HUD into the mod's texture

The UI manager renders the Scaleform UI through its render buffer ([`UIManager::m_RenderBuffer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##field-m_RenderBuffer)), which [`UIManager::InitPlatformRT`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-InitPlatformRT) builds over the engine's surface at startup and on every device or resolution reset. [`RenderTargetData::UpdateData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::RenderTargetData##func-UpdateData) swaps the buffer's render-target and depth-stencil views and refcounts them itself, so it is safe to call from outside.

The redirect (`hud/binding.rs`) creates the mod's HUD texture with its views, drives the movie's render rectangle, the cached viewport the safe area is computed from, and the movie viewport to the texture's size so the movie fills it at its own aspect, then re-runs `InitPlatformRT` and calls `UpdateData` to point the buffer at the texture. Nothing depends on `InitPlatformRT` running at a particular time, so a late injection works. The redirect installs lazily on the render thread and re-applies on a render-size change, since a reset re-runs `InitPlatformRT` and discards it. Its maintenance runs before the main [`UIManager::StartRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-StartRender) queues its worker, under the UI-deferred lock and then the graphics context lock; `PostDraw` runs it as a fallback only on a dispatch where it did not run. Turning `hud.redirect` off, or unloading, re-runs `InitPlatformRT` so the UI never renders into a freed texture.

The texture's longer axis follows the geometric mean of the per-eye render resolution, scaled by the tuning's `panel.render_scale` ([swapchain-ownership.md](stereo/swapchain-ownership.md)), since the panel is seen at a fixed apparent size.

Off the engine's working surface, the HUD drops out of the scene composite with no further patch. Before `PostDraw` samples the texture, composes the panel, or captures the eye image, it calls [`UIManager::SyncRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-SyncRender) and [`UIManager::Submit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-Submit) outside both locks, and only while the redirect is applied, not merely configured. The engine's own background, synchronization, and submission work stays in place, so the tracked world stays visible behind full-screen UI.

## Comfort: lazy follow

A HUD locked rigidly to the head amplifies head tremor and the eyes cannot settle on it; a world-locked HUD slides out of view at the first turn. The panel takes the middle: its position is the live head position, and its orientation eases toward the head's with a critically damped quaternion slerp (`alpha = 1 - 2^(-dt/halflife)`, the halflife being the tuning's `panel.follow_rotation_halflife_s`). There is no deadzone, so the panel never rests at an offset from the gaze, and a quick flick barely moves it. The slerp follows roll as well as yaw and pitch, which matters for the heavy banking of the parachute, the wingsuit, and vehicles.

The panel has two modes (`HudMode`, chosen per frame by `current_mode`), which differ only in aspect: the gameplay HUD at the tuning's `panel.hud_aspect`, and full-screen UI (movies, loading screens, and menus) at `MOVIE_ASPECT`. An open in-game pause menu or map latches the panel in place (`menu_world_lock`) so the head can look around it. The front end and loading screens keep the follow: outside gameplay the render camera has no valid pose to latch, and a loading screen has nothing else to look at.

While the player rides a vehicle the gameplay panel eases to the near distance `panel.depth_shift.near_distance_m`, and back to the configured distance after (`hud/depth.rs`); full-screen UI always sits at the configured distance.

The debug panel does not follow the head ([below](#where-the-debug-panel-lives)).

## Stereo depth: one panel, projected per eye

The panel's world-space corners are computed once per frame on eye 0, from the pose, the distance, and the effective aspect, and both eyes project those corners through their own view-projection, so the panel has correct disparity at a finite depth. Computing the corners per eye would cancel the world transform against each eye's view and collapse the panel to view space: head-locked, at infinity. Under collapsed single-pass dispatch, eye 0 prepares the atlas and marker textures once and the HUD texture is cleared only after eye 1.

The panel keeps a constant apparent size, so its distance (`hud.distance`) can change without the HUD growing or shrinking; its physical size follows from `hud.panel_scale`, the distance, and the aspect (`hud::panel_height`). Both are on the pause menu's `VR: DISPLAY` page ([pause-menu.md](pause-menu.md)).

## World-to-screen: split the HUD

The game places objective markers, enemy pips, and distance labels by projecting a world point through the camera's view-projection. Baked into the lagging panel, a marker meant to point 20 degrees left would land wherever the panel had drifted. So the HUD is split by meaning. The static HUD (health, ammo, minimap, reticle frame) has no world anchor and lags with the panel, which is correct. World-anchored markers mean a direction, so the mod reprojects them onto the panel's surface.

The engine takes the view-projection as a parameter: [`UIManager::Get2DInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-Get2DInfo) forwards a caller's matrix to [`Convert3DCoords`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-Convert3DCoords) and does the on-screen test and edge clamp ([ui-rendering.md, "Points of interest"](../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md#points-of-interest)). The `Get2DInfo` detour (`hooks/ui/mod.rs`) replaces the matrix and the camera transform with the panel's (`hud::compute_panel_vp`: the damped follow rotation, and the active camera's position and projection re-aspected to the panel), so a marker lands at the right spot on the panel despite the follow's lag. `Convert3DCoords` corrects for aspect from [`UIManager::m_CachedViewportRatio`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##field-m_CachedViewportRatio), so the detour sets it to the panel's height over width for the call and restores it after, leaving other projections in the frame alone. With the redirect off, the caller's matrices pass through.

The grapple reticle bypasses `Get2DInfo`: it projects through the default-matrix wrapper [`UIManager::Convert3DCoordsDefault`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-Convert3DCoordsDefault), whose callers are all in [`HUDUI::UpdateGrappleReticle`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-UpdateGrappleReticle). A separate detour routes it through the same reprojection and records the aim depth (below).

## World markers and the atlas

The on-screen point-of-interest markers leave the panel and are drawn as billboards at their world points, keeping the angular size they had on the panel. Whether a marker is on screen is decided with the headset's field of view rather than the panel's narrower one, so a marker visible in the world does not become an edge arrow; markers outside the headset's view stay arrows on the panel. Each sits on the nearest visible surface along its line of sight (the scene depth at its pixel, with a raycast fallback), so it never reads as farther than a wall it shows through.

With the HUD atlas on (`hud.atlas.enabled`, the pause menu's `HUD ON THE HANDS`), the markers move into cells of their own in the HUD texture, and the readouts that describe the player's own body leave the panel for the hands. Without it, markers are cropped out of the panel. How the parts are moved, placed, and shown, and the marker depth's limits, are in [ui-decoupling.md](rendering/ui-decoupling.md#the-atlas-as-built). Every panel composite blends premultiplied alpha, because the HUD texture and the egui panels hold premultiplied colour ([ui-decoupling.md](rendering/ui-decoupling.md#the-alpha-of-the-hud-texture)).

## HUD depth

The atlas is the only HUD depth path. With one HUD texture every element shares the panel's depth, and Scaleform has already composited overlapping clips into it, so nothing on the texture side can separate them; the atlas moves the parts that describe one world object into their own cells before the capture and draws each on its own quad.

The rule: an element tied to one specific world object gets depth, as a cell at the object's depth, a placement on the hands or body, or a placement at the aim depth. A general element stays on the panel. A group that mixes both is split per child, as the centre group is.

- World markers: a cell each, at the depth of the surface they label.
- The weapon readout: replaced by the mod's ammunition counter over each gun in a hand (`hud::anchored::hand_ammo`), the magazine and the reserve, red while the magazine is empty or out of the gun. It is the only per-hand ammunition readout, so with the atlas or the redirect off there is none; the game's readout keeps its cell, off the panel, and is not drawn.
- Under the controller scheme: the tether readout above the off-hand's forearm; the weapon selection cross at the hand steering the [weapon wheel](weapon-selector.md); and, on the inside of the off-hand's wrist, a health bar the mod draws (the game's health vignette is suppressed) over the game's heat and grenade readouts.
- The weapon and grapple reticles and the hit marker: one cell at the aim depth, under either scheme. Under the controller scheme the hand-aimed reticles are hidden and the world-space overlay draws a depth-tested gun dot or laser and the grapple beam instead, so the cell shows for the gamepad scheme and for driver and mounted weapons aimed by the head.
- The grab hint: the game's icon of what a hand near a body slot would take, on the panel's plane below its centre ([grip.md](input/grip.md#the-grab-hint)). Its object is on the body, out of view, so it goes where the player looks.
- The markers on weapons the player let go of: the game's icon of each at the weapon's world point ([two-handed-inventory.md](input/two-handed-inventory.md#markers-on-the-weapons-let-go-of)).
- Everything else stays on the panel: the centre notices (pickup counters, out of ammo, too far, the planted-explosives meter, the wingsuit notifications), the directional damage indicators, objectives, timers, notifications, the chaos feed, edge arrows, subtitles, and the cutscene skip prompt.

The directional damage indicators give only the attacker's direction. The movie creates them at runtime as children of the HUD document and places them on an ellipse around the stage centre, so they stay on the panel.

The full-screen overlay clips (the drowning tint, the omni-damage flash, the low-health paint, and the sniper scope overlay) are always held invisible, forced ahead of every capture in the capture detour (`hud::scaleform` holds the clip handles).

Aim depth: the `Convert3DCoordsDefault` detour records the aim point and its distance from the panel anchor (`hud/aim.rs`) from the first default-matrix projection of each game frame only. The game-thread tick clears a per-frame flag and the first projection claims it, so the frame's later projections (the wire's attach point and the grip-radius sample) are not recorded. The distance is smoothed with the tuning's `aim.halflife_s` and eases back to `aim.rest_distance_m` once nothing has been targeted for `aim.stale_after_s`. The atlas's aim cell is drawn toward the recorded point at that depth.

The render-root split and the single-panel warp that preceded the atlas, and the constraints their failure established, are in [the post-mortem](../issues/08-14-hud-overlays-and-depth.md#the-split-post-mortem).

## The interactive egui debug panel

In VR the flat egui overlay on the back buffer is invisible, and the desktop cursor lands in neither eye, so while an OpenXR session runs the debug UI renders into a floating panel of its own (`hud/egui_panel/`): its own render target, placement, and eye-0 corners, so it never touches `HUD_STATE`. egui is tessellated once per frame, on eye 0, into the panel texture (`EguiState::render_to`, which clears the target to transparent first, since the renderer neither clears nor draws on an empty frame); both eyes draw the same corners through their own view-projection, and the desktop mirror can composite the same texture as a letterboxed overlay. There is never a second egui pass.

The panel's visibility is `hud.egui_panel.enabled`, off at every launch and not saved. F8 or the controller chord (the left stick click held and the right B pressed) shows and hides it; opening it requests editing, and the debug UI's `Allow gameplay input` makes it a read-only overlay ([settings.md](settings.md#where-a-setting-is-exposed), [debug-ui.md](debug-ui.md#input-ownership)). While it is shown, `EguiState` lays egui out at the panel texture's size (`set_panel_mode`) and drops the window-pixel mouse messages that would reach egui in the wrong coordinate space.

### Where the debug panel lives

The panel lives in the player's play space, like a window in a VR runtime's dashboard: its pose is held in the cockpit frame, the tracked space the head and the controllers are reported in (`hud/egui_panel/slot.rs`). That frame travels with the player, so the panel rides along when Rico walks, drives, or falls, turns with a smooth or snap turn, and banks with the wingsuit, as the hands do; turning the head away and back finds it where it was left.

Each frame, on eye 0, the panel's cockpit pose goes into the world through the same composition as the hands and the head (`cockpit::CockpitTransform`), with the render-time head of the camera the pointer captures (`pointer::rendered_camera`). So the panel is anchored to the head as drawn, not as of the last tick, and does not swim at speed ([render-time-anchoring.md](rendering/render-time-anchoring.md)); the controller rays use the same basis, so pointer and panel agree.

The first time it is drawn the panel is placed in front of the head at the tuning's `egui_panel.summon_distance_m`, along the head's heading (`body_rig::math::angle::heading`) at the gaze's pitch, facing the head with no roll. Dismissed and shown again it comes back where it was left, unless it now lies behind the vertical plane square to the head's heading, in which case it is placed in front again. The debug UI's `Recall panel` button places it in front at any time, and a recenter, seat recenters included, re-bases the cockpit frame and so places it in front again. The placement lasts for the game session. The panel's physical size gives a fixed apparent size at the summon distance (`egui_panel.scale`) and stays fixed wherever it is moved, so it looks smaller pushed away, as a real window does.

### Moving the debug panel with the controllers

A hand grabs the panel by pressing its grip (the derived `SqueezePress`) with its aim ray on the panel (`crates/jc3vr-gameplay/src/controllers/bindings/pointer/egui_grab.rs`). The grip counts only after a released sample, so a grip already held, or squeezed off the panel and swept onto it, never grabs; one hand holds the panel at a time, and when both grip on the same tick the pointing hand takes it. While held, the panel keeps the pose it had in the hand's aim frame at the grab and follows the hand rigidly, unlevelled. Releasing the grip, or the hand losing tracking, leaves it where it is.

While a hand holds the panel, that hand's stick pushes and pulls it along the aim ray at a rate proportional to its depth (`egui_panel.push_rate_per_s`), between `egui_panel.min_distance_m` and `egui_panel.max_push_depth_m`; that stick does not scroll during the grab. The grip is not a pointer button, so a grab never clicks.

The input tick decides the grab and moves the stored pose with the hand. The draw task then carries a held panel with the grabbing hand's aim pose from the latest controller frame, a frame fresher than the input tick's, and stores that pose, so a released panel stays where it was last drawn. While the panel is enabled the controller mode is UI and the bindings idle with the mod-panel reason, so its grip presses reach neither the game nor the grip model.

### Pointing at the debug panel with the controllers

Either controller points at the panel (`crates/jc3vr-gameplay/src/controllers/bindings/pointer/egui_controller.rs`). The panel publishes its corners each frame with the camera basis that placed them, and the input tick intersects each hand's aim ray with them. The pointing hand's hit is egui's pointer, its trigger the primary button, its upper face button the secondary, and its stick the wheel, scrolling past a deadzone at a speed proportional to deflection. The cursor dot follows whichever source owns the pointer, and a line from the pointing hand to the panel is drawn through the world-space overlay ([controllers.md](input/controllers.md#the-world-space-overlay)).

The pointing hand starts as the dominant hand. It passes to the other hand when that hand pulls its trigger with its ray on the panel, or when only the other hand's ray is on the panel, never while the pointing hand holds a press. The pull that hands the pointer over does not click, since the player has not yet seen where that hand points. A press clicks only after a released sample and only when it begins on the panel; a press held as the ray leaves the panel drags along its edge while the ray still meets the panel's plane; and a tap shorter than an input tick still clicks once.

Every controller pointer (the debug panel's, the game UI's, and through it the map's) has an aim hold on its trigger (`pointer/aim_hold.rs`): a light pull past the gameplay tuning's `pointer.aim_hold.pin_press` pins the cursor where the ray was, and only a full pull past `pointer.aim_hold.click_press` presses, at the pinned point. The press releases below `click_release` and the cursor is freed below `pin_release`. Once the press begins the cursor moves with the ray again, offset by where it was pinned, so a drag starts from the pinned point. A press reported with the trigger already at rest is a tap between two ticks and clicks at the live ray. The trigger value comes with the menu set's `pointer_select` action, so a rebinding carries the hold with it.

A held press stays a click until the hit leaves a slop around where it began (`pointer.click_slop`, a fraction of the panel's height); until then the pointer and the release stay at the press point, so hand tremor never turns a press into a drag. egui stops counting a press as a click once held longer than its click duration, which while the panel is shown is the HUD tuning's `egui_panel.click_duration_s`. When the ray leaves the panel egui is told the pointer is gone, releasing a held button first.

The controller pointer steps on every input tick, also while the bindings stand down for the panel, and queues one update per tick, which the game thread drains before running egui. The desktop mouse and the controller are arbitrated as in the game UI (below): the mouse is used when it moves, clicks, or scrolls, the controller when its ray lands on the panel, clicks, scrolls, or moves beyond `egui_panel.controller_move_uv` from where it rested, so a resting controller's jitter never takes the pointer. Ownership changes only while the owner holds no button. The arbitration runs only while the floating panel is active; the flat desktop overlay (F6) is untouched.

The chord's presses are masked from the frame the panel pointer reads, as for the bindings and the game-UI pointer ([controllers.md](input/controllers.md#bindings-and-injection)), so the B that opens the panel never right-clicks on it. While the panel is enabled the bindings idle and the game-UI controller pointer is cleared, and while egui holds the game's input the `SendMouseEvents` route cancels every game-UI pointer source, so the trigger neither fires a weapon nor clicks a game menu behind the panel.

## The clean view

The clean view (`director.clean_view.on`, `crates/jc3vr-core/src/clean_view.rs`) takes the mod's overlays out of the picture without changing their settings, at a scope chosen in the debug UI's Director feature (`director.clean_view.scope`). At `Desktop` it hides the flat egui overlay from the desktop window only, both the direct back-buffer path and the mirror overlay (`hud/mirror_overlay.rs`). At `Everything` it also hides the floating HUD, the debug panel and its mirror copy, and the in-world overlays (the world markers, the reticle, the anchored readouts, the controls summary, the controller overlay, and the scope lens); with the redirect off the game's own HUD stays. F4 toggles it, releasing egui's input capture while hiding, and the debug UI's Hide button switches it on. Neither value is saved: the switch is on at every launch, so the desktop overlay starts hidden, and the scope starts at `Desktop`, so a launch never starts with the HUD hidden.

## Controller pointing in the game UI

The game's Scaleform UI takes the dominant controller's aim ray as its mouse while a menu action set is active (`crates/jc3vr-gameplay/src/controllers/bindings/pointer/`, `hud/pointer/`, `hooks/ui/mouse.rs`). It follows `hud.redirect` and does not depend on the debug panel.

Camera setup captures the un-offset centre camera with the frame's cockpit head pose and world scale. The HUD places the panel from that capture and publishes the corners it drew with the basis that placed them; a frame that draws no panel publishes none, and a panel drawn from the render-camera fallback without a capture is logged once, since the pointer cannot reach it. The pointer recovers the tracking-to-world rotation from the rendered and cockpit head rotations, composes the aim pose as the hands are composed, and intersects the ray with those corners. No beam is drawn; a controller hover shows the panel's cursor dot.

The trigger is armed only after a released sample, so a trigger held across a mode change, focus loss, tracking recovery, or a desktop drag never clicks, and a press that misses the panel cannot click by entering it. The UI binding table also uses the trigger to turn pages; while the pointer shows, its trigger is the pointer's alone, so a click just off the panel's edge clicks nothing rather than turning the page. The other hand's trigger still turns pages, and pulled with its grip held turns the pointer hand's page; the stick, face buttons, confirm, cancel, and tabs stay available.

One router picks the source for each delivery through [`UIManager::SendMouseEvents`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-SendMouseEvents). Taking over emits one neutral frame, a desktop drag keeps ownership through its release, and a desktop button held across a transition counts only after release. When the ray leaves the panel the mouse parks off the movie at the engine's own gamepad park position until the desktop mouse moves or clicks, so the hover never jumps to wherever the desktop cursor rests. A tracked release outside the panel after a press on it lands at the clamped hit.

Every cancellation goes through one path: the redirect turning off, egui taking input, a missing or replaced movie, shutdown, and tracking or focus loss. A button still held on the live movie is released off the movie, which Scaleform treats as a release outside, so a cancelled press on "Quit to main menu" does nothing. The tracker keeps only the movie's address for comparison and never dereferences it; with no destructor hook or generation token, a replacement at the same address between two deliveries is not distinguishable. Shutdown cancels on the game thread before the hook-removal worker starts.

[`UIManager::PreUpdate`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-PreUpdate) is the frame's input boundary. Its detour peeks the route and prepares the map's controller sample before the input dispatch, so the map and the Scaleform delivery see the same sample, and each is committed only when its consumer runs: the engine skips both on a frame with no input-priority UI, and such a frame consumes no transition.

[`UIManager::m_IsUsingGamepad`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##field-m_IsUsingGamepad) stays set under the controller scheme, and the engine forwards it to every menu movie as `OnUseGamepad`. The native delivery has no other gate, and the mod bypasses the gamepad park while the controller owns the UI, but whether each menu's ActionScript honours mouse rollover and press in gamepad mode cannot be read from the binary and needs checking menu by menu.

### The native map

The map ([`CommMapUI`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI)) reads the mouse only in its mouse branch, chosen by `m_IsUsingGamepad` ([engine/gameplay/input.md](../engine/gameplay/input.md#the-maps-cursor-and-selection)). While the controller drives the map, the mod (`crates/jc3vr-hud/src/controllers/map/`) clears that flag for the duration of [`CommMapUI::OnManageInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI##func-OnManageInput) and answers the map's effector lookups through [`InputActionMap::GetActionEffector`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::InputActionMap##func-GetActionEffector) with effectors it owns: [`MOUSE1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-MOUSE1) and `MOUSE2` for the first mouse device, the delta axes at rest, and the GUI zoom actions for the steering's own [`m_DeviceIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::steering::Steering##field-m_DeviceIndex). No physical action-map slot is written. The map's cursor comes from the prepared controller UV through the [`UIManager::GetMovieSpaceMouseCursor`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-GetMovieSpaceMouseCursor) detour. [`CommMapUI::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI##func-Update) runs later, from `UIManager::PreRender`, and clears the flag the same way on a frame whose dispatch the controller drove, so the gamepad reticle hides and the selection-relative elements and the context menu follow the mouse selection point. The two scopes share one saved value and restore it once.

The trigger is the map's primary button: after the aim hold pins the cursor, a full pull and release selects, as a left click does. The map turns a held press into a drag once its cursor leaves the press point by a few stage pixels, which tremor and the pull itself exceed, so the map's cursor is held at the press point while the ray stays within the HUD tuning's `map.click_slop_uv`, and the release is delivered there. Past the slop the map sees the live ray and drags. Only the map's cursor is held; the panel's pointer keeps following the ray.

A (`GUI_OK`) acts at the controller's cursor. In the mouse branch the map answers a clicked `GUI_OK` with the context menu at its last mouse selection point, without selecting, so A alone would open "Place waypoint" wherever the mouse last selected. The shim watches the engine's own lookup of `GUI_OK` on the steering's selector under the map's scope, and when the effector it returns is clicked, reports `MOUSE2` released in the same dispatch, which the map reads afterwards: a right click, which selects at the cursor and opens the context menu there. A dispatch that cancels the controller's ownership never converts.

The menu set's `map_zoom` action (the off-hand stick's vertical axis by default) zooms: a push past `map.zoom_engage` steps one zoom level, a held push repeats after `map.zoom_repeat_delay_ms` every `map.zoom_repeat_interval_ms`, and the release threshold `map.zoom_release` sits below the push so a resting stick does not chatter.
