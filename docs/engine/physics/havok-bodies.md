# Havok bodies, the physics step, and collision filtering

How the game's physics advances, where code can change the world before a step, how the engine makes and tears down a free dynamic body, how a placed object's body turns dynamic, how the collision filter decides what touches what, and how contacts are reported. The engine is Havok Physics 2014.1 in its newer form (`hknpWorld`, `hknpBody`, `hknpMotion`), not the older `hkpWorld` family. A loose object's body, its motion and owner, and the velocity setters are in [item-pickup.md](../gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world).

## The physics step

The physics system ([`PhysicsSystem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem)) owns one Havok world ([`PhysicsSystem::m_World`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##field-m_World)), made by its constructor at application start and kept for the session; loading a save, dying, and streaming change what is in it, not the world.

[`PhysicsSystem::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-Update) advances it. [`Game::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::Game##func-Update) runs the current game state's update (`CGame::UpdateGame`), and `CGameStateRun::Update`, like the loading and front-end states' updates, calls the physics update inline, which calls `PhysicsSystem::Update` and then dispatches the queued collisions, the vehicles' post-collision work, and the damage listeners' events. So the physics steps on the game thread, between the state update's pre-simulation work and the character updates that follow.

`Update` fires the pre-update signal, clears the collision array, and updates the bodies that do not simulate. It returns without stepping while either of its two suspend flags is set (`CGameStateRun::Update` sets one from its update context's pause). Otherwise, when the world exists and the time given is above a small threshold, it fires the pre-simulation callbacks and steps the world exactly once by [`PhysicsSystem::STEP_SECONDS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem), whatever time it was given; the collide and solve phases run as tasks on the physics thread pool, joined before it returns. The game does not sub-step.

### Pre-simulation callbacks

A pre-simulation callback ([`PfxPreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback)) has a three-slot virtual table: a destructor, [`preSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback##vfunc-preSimulationCallback) with the world and the time `Update` was given, and [`onWorldShiftCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback##vfunc-onWorldShiftCallback) with the shift of a world recentring. The ragdolls, the grappling hook's wire, and the AI world register one.

[`PhysicsSystem::AddPreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-AddPreSimulationCallback) appends to a `std::vector` of callback pointers; [`RemovePreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-RemovePreSimulationCallback) clears matching entries without erasing them. The walk before each step calls each non-null entry and compacts cleared ones away as it meets them, as does the world-shift walk, so a callback may remove itself or another while they fire, but an addition then could reallocate the vector under the walk. The engine never deletes a callback; its owner removes and frees it.

A callback runs on the game thread, outside the step, with the world otherwise idle: a velocity set there is what the step integrates, and a body made, added, or removed there is in or out of that step.

### World recentring

The Havok world is simulated about a moving origin: a Havok-space position plus [`PhysicsSystem::m_AccumulatedShift`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##field-m_AccumulatedShift) is the game-space position. When the camera strays far enough, `CPhysicsSystem::RecenterWorld` adds to the shift, moves every body and the broad phase by its opposite, and calls each callback's world-shift slot. Game-space poses do not change; only code that keeps Havok-space positions of its own has to move them.

## Making a free dynamic body

The grappling hook's hang proxy (`CGrapplingHangPhysicsProxy::Init`) makes a dynamic capsule that belongs to no game object, the template for any such body:

1. Construct a body construction info ([`hknpBodyCinfo::Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpBodyCinfo##func-Construct)): no reserved id, no motion, the default quality and material, a zero collision-filter word, no name or local frame, and the identity orientation.
2. Make the shape. [`hknpCapsuleShape::createCapsuleShape`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpCapsuleShape##func-createCapsuleShape) allocates a capsule from the calling thread's Havok heap from the segment's two body-space end points and the radius, returning it with one reference, the caller's. Set it as [`hknpBodyCinfo::m_shape`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpBodyCinfo##field-m_shape), with the Havok-space position and orientation.
3. Construct a motion construction info ([`hknpMotionCinfo::Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpMotionCinfo##func-Construct)) and fill its mass properties from the body info with [`initializeWithMass`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpMotionCinfo##func-initializeWithMass) (the inverse mass, the inertia from the shape, and the centre of mass and orientation from the pose, so those must be set first). The hang proxy overrides the inertia with a capsule's afterwards. [`hknpMotionCinfo::m_enableDeactivation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpMotionCinfo##field-m_enableDeactivation) starts set, letting a resting body sleep.
4. Create the body with [`hknpWorld::createDynamicBody`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-createDynamicBody), passing [`DO_NOT_ADD`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpAdditionMode##variant-DO_NOT_ADD) to leave it out of the simulation. It copies the body info, creates the motion and the body (taking its own reference to the shape), and writes the body id through its second argument. The disassembler names the body creation it calls `hknpWorld::setBodyShape`.
5. Allocate the wrapper with the engine's [`OperatorNew`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/memory##func-OperatorNew) and construct it ([`PfxRigidBody::Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-Construct)) with the body id, an empty data pointer, and a clear add flag. The wrapper is an engine physics instance ([`PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance)): it sets itself as the body's instance property, which every query, listener, and damage path reads to find a body's owner, with its owner ([`PfxInstance::m_GameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##field-m_GameObject)) null, as the hang proxy's stays. It also sets a body flag and writes the collision-filter word to [`NO_COLLISION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-NO_COLLISION).
6. Give the body its collision layer and system group ([`PfxRigidBody::SetCollisionLayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetCollisionLayer), [`SetCollisionSystemGroup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetCollisionSystemGroup)); the hang proxy takes a fresh group from the filter. Damping, quality, and gravity factor may be set here too ([`SetGravityFactor`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetGravityFactor) copies the motion's properties into the wrapper, changes them, and gives them back to the motion).
7. Release the caller's shape reference. A Havok object's count is the low half of [`hkReferencedObject::m_memSizeAndRefCount`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkReferencedObject##field-m_memSizeAndRefCount), and the release is inline in the game's code: an object whose allocation-size half is zero is not counted; otherwise the count is decremented atomically, and an object whose count was one is destroyed through its [`deleteThisReferencedObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkReferencedObject##vfunc-deleteThisReferencedObject) slot.

Shapes and other Havok heap objects come from the calling thread's Havok memory router (a thread-local), which the game thread and the physics workers have; the steps above run on the game thread outside the step. Creating a body takes its own reference to the shape in its construction info (`hknpBody::initialize`), so another body's shape ([`hknpBody::m_shape`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody##field-m_shape)) can be given to a new one with nothing to release; the two share it for as long as either lives.

### The wrapper's lifecycle

- [`AddToWorld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-AddToWorld) adds the body, with any body attached to it, to the simulation at once if it is not in it; [`IsAddedToWorld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-IsAddedToWorld) says whether it is, and [`RemoveFromWorld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-RemoveFromWorld) takes it out, the body living on to be added later.
- [`GetTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-GetTransform) and [`SetTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-SetTransform) read and place the body in game space, converting with the accumulated shift; the rows are the body's axes, and placing keeps the velocities.
- The deleting destructor ([`Destruct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-Destruct)) removes the body from the simulation, clears its instance property, destroys it, and with [`DESTRUCT_FREE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody) frees the wrapper to the engine allocator. Deleting the wrapper is the whole teardown.

The disassembler mislabels several of these (the pyxis items note it): the destructor as `CPfxRagdollProxyPhantom::Destruct`, `AddToWorld` after the ragdoll proxy phantom, `SetTransform` as `CPartInstance::ClearWindowDamage`, `SetCollisionLayer` as `boost::shared_ptr<CAnimationRuleSystem>::operator->`, and the filter's group counter (`CPhysicsSystem::GetNewSystemGroup`) as `CAnimatedModel::GetModel`.

## Placed bodies becoming dynamic

A rigid object ([`RigidObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObject)) carries a type in [`m_Flags`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObject##field-m_Flags) under `TYPE_MASK`: static, fixed, keyframed, or dynamic ([`RigidObjectType`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObjectType)). Its body is created from its collision resource with that resource's mass, and a static or fixed object's body is then made static, a keyframed one keyframed, through [`PfxRigidBody::SetMotionType`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetMotionType). That copies the body's motion into an unused motion kept in [`PfxRigidBody::m_SavedMotionId`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##field-m_SavedMotionId) and gives the body the world's static motion or an infinite-mass copy. So a placed object at rest has no [`IS_DYNAMIC`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody##const-IS_DYNAMIC) flag, but the saved motion keeps its mass and inertia (the inverse mass is the last entry of [`hknpMotion::m_inverseInertia`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpMotion##field-m_inverseInertia), an `hkHalf`). To the grappling hook a static rigid object is part of the world and a fixed one is a loose object.

[`RigidObject::MakeDynamic`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::RigidObject##func-MakeDynamic) sets the type to dynamic, moves the body to the dynamic collision layer, has `SetMotionType` build a new motion from the saved one's mass and inertia, give it to the same body, and destroy the saved motion, and wakes the body ([`PfxRigidBody::ForceActivate`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-ForceActivate), then [`hknpWorld::activateBody`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-activateBody)). The body id is unchanged and the change is immediate, on the game thread outside the step. Every call the game makes passes false for the new system group, keeping the body in its group. A body created without a mass has no saved motion and cannot leave the static type.

Damage does not make a fixed object dynamic: `CRigidObject::OnDamage` applies a hit's impulse only to an object already dynamic, and the explosion force pulse skips static and fixed objects. What does is the object's make-dynamic and stop-keyframe events, which its data wires to other objects (its damage controller, for example), a script call (`CRigidObjectModule::MakeDynamic`), or the code that drops held and attached objects. Waking a body, as the grappling hook does on attaching ([`WireEnd::SetupProxyForAttachment`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-SetupProxyForAttachment) calls `activateBody`), never changes its motion type. Destructible props are a different class (`CHavokDestructionObject`), not covered here; whether a given placed prop is a fixed rigid object or a destruction object is read from its type and has not been checked in game for every kind.

## Collision filtering

The world's filter is a constraint collision filter (which also keeps bodies joined by an enabled constraint apart, following the world's constraint-enabled and constraint-disabled signals) over the engine's group filter ([`CustomGroupCollisionFilter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::CustomGroupCollisionFilter)). It reads each body's collision-filter word ([`hknpBody::m_collisionFilterInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody##field-m_collisionFilterInfo)): a layer, a subsystem id, a no-collide id, and a system group in the high half.

- Two bodies in different system groups, or both in group zero, collide when the filter's layer table says their layers do.
- Two bodies in the same non-zero group collide only when their layers are equal, at least one has a non-zero subsystem id, and neither's subsystem id is the other's no-collide id. A ragdoll's parts use this to collide selectively.

Every character proxy gets a system group of its own, with the proxy on [`CHARACTER_COLLISION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-CHARACTER_COLLISION) and a zero subsystem id, so a body put in that group on another layer with a zero subsystem id never touches the proxy, nor anything else in the group with a zero subsystem id.

The layers are [`PfxLayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer), and the physics system's constructor fills the table pair by pair. Among the rows:

- [`WEAPONS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-WEAPONS) collides with the static, terrain, dynamic, destruction, character collision, character ragdoll, and character-attached dynamic layers, the three vehicle collision layers and the vehicle raycast layer, and the listener-effects layer. It does not collide with itself, with [`RAYCAST_EVERYTHING_PHYSICAL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-RAYCAST_EVERYTHING_PHYSICAL) (the player's aim query layer), or with the trigger and explosion shape-query layers. So a vehicle's wheel rays can hit a weapons-layer body; whether the explosion and trigger queries and the AI's obstacle tracking see one in other ways is unverified.
- [`CHARACTER_ATTACHED_DYNAMIC_RB`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-CHARACTER_ATTACHED_DYNAMIC_RB), the hang proxy's layer, collides with the static, terrain, dynamic, destruction, and character collision layers and the vehicle layers, but not with ragdolls.
- [`FULL_COLLISION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-FULL_COLLISION) collides with everything, queries included.

## Joining bodies with a fixed constraint

The magnet weapon (`CMagnetWeaponComponent::ConstrainBodiesToGravityField`, which the disassembler names `CPartInstance::AttachPartWithConstraint`) holds a body in its field with a fixed constraint:

1. It allocates a [`hkpFixedConstraintData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkpFixedConstraintData) from the calling thread's Havok heap: the thread's memory router ([`hkMemoryRouter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkMemoryRouter), in the thread-local slot [`s_memoryRouterSlot`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkMemoryRouter)) holds the heap allocator ([`m_heap`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkMemoryRouter##field-m_heap)), whose [`blockAlloc`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkMemoryAllocator##vfunc-blockAlloc) gives the memory. [`Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkpFixedConstraintData##func-Construct) leaves the data with one reference, a counted allocation size, identity frames, and the stabilized solving method, which the magnet sets again.
2. It sets the constraint's frame in each body's space ([`setInBodySpace`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkpFixedConstraintData##func-setInBodySpace), two [`hkTransformf`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkTransformf)s), which the constraint holds together. A body's space is the frame of its transform ([`hknpBody::m_transform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody##field-m_transform)), Havok space, whose rotation's columns are the images of the body's axes.
3. It fills a [`hknpConstraintCinfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpConstraintCinfo) with the data and the two body ids, flags clear, and creates the constraint ([`hknpWorld::createConstraint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-createConstraint)), which writes its id. A constraint between a body and itself, or with data the world does not support, is created disabled.
4. It releases its reference to the data; the world's constraint took its own when made (`hknpConstraint::init`, which the disassembler names `hknpWorldEx::handleConstraintActivationMode`).

[`hknpWorld::destroyConstraints`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-destroyConstraints) destroys constraints by id. Each releases its data reference, deleting data no one else holds back to the deleting thread's heap, and its slot in the world's constraint manager ([`hknpWorld::m_constraintManager`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##field-m_constraintManager)) goes onto a list of destroyed slots with its first body set to the invalid id ([`hknpConstraint::INVALID_BODY_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpConstraint)); the manager hands destroyed ids out again once its free ones run out. So a constraint id names a constraint only while its slot ([`hknpConstraint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpConstraint)) holds the same two bodies and data.

The world does not end a constraint whose body is destroyed, so the engine's own users destroy their constraints first (the damage controller's body replacement, the magnet's release). `hknpWorld::destroyBodies` removes the bodies from the simulation (`hknpWorld::removeBodies`, which touches no constraint), fires the body-destroyed signal for each, takes it out of its motion, releases its shape, and schedules its slot to be freed; the signal's only subscribers are the destruction system (`CHavokDestructionSystem`) and the destruction world (`hkndWorld::onBodyDestroyedSignal`). A constraint on a destroyed body therefore stays in the manager, naming a body id the world will free and reuse, until its owner destroys it; what a step does with it is unverified.

## Contact events

The physics system's collision listener (`CHavokCollisionListener`) subscribes to the world's manifold-processed and contact-impulse events. For each, it looks up both bodies' engine instances and, for each instance whose switch for that event is set ([`PfxInstance::m_IsManifoldProcessedEventActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##field-m_IsManifoldProcessedEventActive), [`m_IsContactImpulseEventActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##field-m_IsContactImpulseEventActive)), calls its event virtual ([`OnManifoldProcessedEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##vfunc-OnManifoldProcessedEvent), [`OnContactImpulseEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##vfunc-OnContactImpulseEvent)) with the event, the other body's instance (null without one), and whether the instance is the event's first body. The wrapper's own [`PfxRigidBody::OnContactImpulseEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-OnContactImpulseEvent) does nothing.

### Contact-impulse events

The solver raises a contact-impulse event ([`hknpContactImpulseEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpContactImpulseEvent)) only for a contact one of whose bodies has [`hknpBody::RAISE_CONTACT_IMPULSE_EVENTS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody). A wrapper's [`SetContactImpulseEventActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-SetContactImpulseEventActive) sets both the flag and the instance's switch, so one call makes a body report its contacts to its own instance.

The event names its two bodies, carries the contact's status, and carries the impulse applied at each contact point over the step, in newton seconds; the point count comes from the contact's solver Jacobian ([`hknpContactJacobianUtil::getNumContactPoints`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpContactJacobianUtil##func-getNumContactPoints)), and the lanes past it are not the contact's. For a contact with a collision cache, the status is "started" on the first step its impulse is positive, and "continued" and "finished" events come only for a contact a listener asked them of, as the characters' own listener does. A contact without a cache raises an "immediate" event every step its impulse is positive.

The solver writes the events into per-thread command streams, and the world dispatches them in its post-solve phase. The solve half of a step ([`hknpWorld::stepSolve`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-stepSolve)) runs a single-task graph on its calling thread, as the post-solve phase always is, so the listeners run on the game thread inside the physics update while the world is being stepped: a listener may read the world but must not change it, and the game objects it finds are mid-update.

Each character proxy also subscribes a listener (`CRigidBodyCharacterCollisionListener`) to its capsule's events: it asks for the continued events and records each point of a started contact as a hit for the character, reacted to after the step (`CPfxCharacterInstance::ProcessHitInfos`, through each game object's `DoReactToHit`; unverified, as is the reaction to a body whose instance has no owner).

## A loose object's mass

A loose object's mass is authored on its entity, not taken from its shape: each prop's entity properties carry a physics parameter block that keeps the Havok shape's own mass, picks one from a fixed table of mass categories, or gives one outright, and the chosen mass is left on the body for its motion to read. The shapes' own masses are of unit density (a fuel barrel's hull reads its volume in cubic metres), so the authored mass is the real one. How the physics game object chooses between the three is unverified. The authored masses are coarse and heavier than their real counterparts:

| Object | Mass |
| --- | --- |
| Cardboard box, can, bottle | 5 kg |
| Traffic cone | 10 kg |
| Gas canister, tyre, crate debris | 25 kg |
| Small wooden cargo crate | 40 kg |
| Large cargo crate, jerrycan, fire extinguisher | 50 kg |
| Wooden barrel, checkpoint barrel, ammo cache crate | 100 kg |
| Red fuel barrel, water-filled barrier barrel | 150 kg |
| Propane tank, circuit box, generator cylinder | 250 kg |
| Voltage box, stack of planks | 300 to 500 kg |
| Cable drum, hay bale, Hesco barrier, containers, wrecks | 1 t and up |

Nothing loose lies between 500 kg and 1 t.
