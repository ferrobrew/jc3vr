# Grenades

## Safe items

Taking a grenade from the chest pouch creates an independent loadout item and, with it, a native [`Grenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade) of its own. The grenade comes from the grenade weapon's own factory, [`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::PrimeGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##func-PrimeGrenade): the engine's pooled allocation, constructor parameters, owner, weapon provenance, and ammunition-manager update registration are all the game's. Its one lasting claim on the weapon, [`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::m_PrimedGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##field-m_PrimedGrenade), is released as soon as the grenade exists, so the weapon keeps no reference to the item and any number of items can exist at once without contending for it.

The draw consumes no ammunition and adds none. The factory deducts a round through the weapon's [`ConsumeAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-ConsumeAmmo) virtual; that deduction alone is suppressed, on that thread and for that weapon, for the duration of the call. Inventory values are never topped up or refunded, and nothing in the item's later life reads grenade stock.

A grenade's fuse counts down from the moment it spawns. Its own post-simulation update, [`Explosive::UpdatePostSim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-UpdatePostSim), ticks [`Explosive::m_DetonationTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DetonationTimer) only while [`Grenade::m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##field-m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer) holds. Drawing clears that flag and [`Grenade::m_CanUseUpgrades`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##field-m_CanUseUpgrades). Contact upgrades can zero the timer without checking the countdown flag, so disabling countdown alone is insufficient. The original upgrade flag is restored when a grenade whose pin is out leaves through the weapon's launch; an unsuccessful launch clears it again. Creation accepts only finite, positive start and remaining timers. An item whose actor the pool reclaims retires from the loadout rather than being kept alive by us.

The controller holds only a weak reference to each item's actor. A weak count check does not prevent forced pool erasure. The input fragment runs after the previous simulation completion waits and completes before ammunition pre-simulation. Queued releases, actor reconciliation, and removal run at the render boundary after simulation completion and ammunition post-simulation, not in the parallel weapon update. Only held and stowed items receive cached transform pairs. World items retain their simulation-written matrices, and pickup candidates use the actor's current simulated position. A non-simulating body follows the held or stowed transform. Held-body collision behavior still needs gameplay validation.

A safe grenade can move between hands and the supported hip and chest holsters, and it keeps its item and its actor throughout. The loadout table checks the expected item view before every move, so an occupied destination or stale move leaves the item unchanged.

## In the hand

A held grenade and a held C4 charge are each placed in the hand by a hold (`hands::held::HoldPlacement`): the model's origin and axes in the hand's grip frame, given for the right hand and mirrored for the left. The grip frame's origin is the centre of the closed hand, which is about where Rico's palm is drawn, so a model centred there with the grip's own orientation sinks into the palm. Each hold is derived from its model's geometry.

The grenade (`GRENADE.hold`) stands upright in the fist: its body runs up the finger tube with the fuse head out past the thumb, its lever lies against the palm, and its body sits one body radius out of the palm, level with the middle of the palm. The grenade palm pose closes the fingers as far as round a bar of the body's diameter, so they lie on the body.

The C4 charge (`C4.hold`) is a flat square brick. It lies with its top face on the palm, centred on it, so its sticking face looks out of the hand at whatever it is pressed onto ([physical-weapons.md, "C4 pressed onto a surface"](physical-weapons.md#c4-pressed-onto-a-surface)); the surface probes start at the brick's middle (`C4.centre_m`). The charge pose bends the fingers at the knuckles and middle joints over the brick's far edge onto its outer face, with the thumb along its side.

The magazine's hold is in [reload.md](reload.md#load).

## Locker sources

A free hand can draw a safe grenade from a registered native weapon display or a verified static collection display. Both source paths are enabled by default. Each hand receives its nearest qualifying display within reach. Completion checks the same source registration and distance before calling the safe-item draw used by the pouch. A missing or changed source refuses the grab instead of selecting another source. The draw leaves the display, native ammunition, the other hand, and holstered items unchanged, so either hand can draw repeatedly from the same display. A nearby gun rack, the player position, and the body pouch do not create a locker target.

Native weapon displays use read-only observations after [`WeaponBase::SetState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SetState), [`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms), and the world weapon render pass. The interaction-entry walk can seed the same registry. Registration requires the hand-grenade slot and a dropped or rack state. It never calls a native pickup predicate, starts native pickup, or changes native eligibility. The bounded registry retains weak references rather than keeping native weapons alive. Each input tick locks registered weapons briefly, prunes freed or changed world sources, and reads each visible [`WeaponData::m_BaseModelInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_BaseModelInstance) at its current weapon-data transform. Completion checks the weapon and control block, weapon-data record, visible model, and registration. Native ammunition capacity does not suppress this path.

The upper-shelf grenade bodies in the inspected Massos locations are static model-collection instances. They are separate from the finite native grenade placement rule. The static adapter observes [`Location::LoadModelCollection`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/location::Location##func-LoadModelCollection), validates the bounded dictionary against an explicit allowlist of the inspected Massos collection paths and their instance counts, and finds the grenade body by its exact model filename. The parser validates the names, model indices, transforms, LOD data, bounds, alignment, and complete buffer length. It does not assume a fixed grenade index across collections. A model hash near a rack is insufficient to register a source.

After native loading succeeds, the adapter checks [`ModelCollection::m_NumInstances`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::model_instance::ModelCollection##field-m_NumInstances) and reads the corresponding entries in [`ModelCollection::m_ModelInstances`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::model_instance::ModelCollection##field-m_ModelInstances). Each accepted entry is enabled, visible, static, not deleted, in the world, and backed by a non-fail model resource. [`ModelInstance::GetMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::model_instance::ModelInstance##func-GetMatrix) supplies its live world matrix. The adapter copies that matrix and its source identity while the loader keeps the collection live. Asset coordinates and the player's pose do not supply static source positions.

The static registry owns copied records only. It does not retain native pointers for later dereference or keep a collection alive. Its bounded entries have a collection address, a monotonic generation, an exact file-order instance index, and the captured instance and model-resource identities. The [`ModelCollection::Destruct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::model_instance::ModelCollection##func-Destruct) detour removes the collection's records before native destruction. Target publication, completion, and retirement use the same lock; completion holds it through the safe draw. Address reuse, eviction, or unload therefore invalidates an old offer. Static poses remain the captured static transforms until their collection retires. There is no per-frame scan or input-thread native collection dereference.

Leaving gameplay or losing input clears hand offers. It does not discard live static records, because a loaded location need not load again when gameplay resumes. Shutdown closes and clears both registries. The locker hook scopes expose an active-entry count to the shared image-retention check. Source registration and acquisition logs distinguish static collection records from weak native weapon displays.

The source family covers the inspected Massos collections. It does not claim coverage for every static grenade decoration or cabinet. The localized location title does not establish an internal collection number, so the adapter includes the complete inspected family rather than guessing that mapping. The adapter accepts each inspected full location path and its model-dictionary member path, with case and slash normalization. The native loader's resource-name spelling still needs runtime observation: an unrecognized name refuses registration rather than falling back to a global model search. A location loaded before the hooks were installed must unload and load again before the static adapter can capture it. For late injection, the runtime check must stream the location out and return, or reload the save after hook installation. There is no mapped enumeration of already loaded collections in this adapter. These paths implement source acquisition but do not establish game/headset acceptance of the recorded locker.

## The pin

A trigger press on a hand holding a grenade pulls its pin. Pulling the pin raises [`Grenade::m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##field-m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer), so the fuse counts down from that moment, plays a bump on that hand (`Event::GrenadePin`), and is logged at info level (`grenade: the pin is pulled, and the fuse runs`). The fuse warning then applies as it does to a caught grenade: the ring at the hand turns from green to red, and the hand pulses faster toward the end; with grenades in both hands it follows the fuse nearest to running out. A grenade held too long explodes in the hand. The pin comes out only once the grenade has its physics body, which it gains at the end of the tick it is drawn in, so a press in that tick leaves the pin in. No sound plays: no game sound for a grenade's pin has been bound. Nothing else pulls the pin, and nothing puts it back. A grenade whose pin is out can pass to the other hand but cannot be holstered.

## Releases

Opening the grip releases the grenade, with the motion a gun's let-go would give it ([The throw's motion](#the-throws-motion)), and when the actor has its physics body the release goes through at once, in the input tick's game stage. The pin decides what the release is, whatever the hand's speed; there is no arming on a throw.

A grenade with its pin in leaves the hand inert. The release queues the captured pose in [`Explosive::m_DeferredPhysicsTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DeferredPhysicsTransform) and raises [`Explosive::m_DeferredSetPhysicsTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DeferredSetPhysicsTransform), which the ammunition update applies before the pending launch, and writes the deferred launch fields with the release motion ([`Explosive::m_InitialAngularVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_InitialAngularVelocity), [`Explosive::m_RelativeVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_RelativeVelocity), [`Explosive::m_DeferredInitialVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DeferredInitialVelocity), and [`Explosive::m_DeferredPropelTowards`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DeferredPropelTowards)), so the body takes the release velocity on its next update, without the launch's trail effect. It lies in the world as the loadout's item ([Dropped grenades](#dropped-grenades)) and can never detonate.

A grenade whose pin is out is a live grenade. The release re-attaches it to [`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::m_PrimedGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##field-m_PrimedGrenade) and calls [`WeaponBase::Fire_Projectile`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-Fire_Projectile), so the engine's own launch owns the effects and the slot bookkeeping, and the launch hook replaces the launch's start and motion with the captured transform and the hand's release. When the launch cannot go ahead, the release falls back to the inert path, with the fuse still running. Either way the item and its table entry retire once the grenade is in the simulation: taking it up again as an item would give it a fresh, inert actor and lose its fuse, so a live grenade in the world is caught as any other is ([Live grenades](#live-grenades)). Neither the flag nor the timer it gates is ever reset.

A release waits for the physics body: when the body has not appeared within the wait the release is given, the item stays in the hand and the request is abandoned, because neither engine path can move a grenade that has no body. The wait happens because a grenade's body is created by the ammunition manager's pass at the end of the tick it spawns in, so a release in the same tick as the draw has nothing to launch yet; such a release is serviced at the render boundary once the body exists. A loss of tracking or input releases the hand's grenade at rest by the same rules.

A draw and a pickup allocate their actor in the input tick's game stage at the top of the world's pre-simulation queue, after the update-queue commit, so the actor's first world update is the next tick's, after its body exists. Anywhere else, the draw and the pickup are refused ([controllers.md](controllers.md#bindings-and-injection)).

### Dropped grenades

A dropped item keeps its table entry in the world and can be taken up again: a free hand closing on it takes it (the grip's live grenade target, at the grenade), and so does the grapple's snatch ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md)), which pulls it to the hand and takes it into the hand at the next input tick. The physical hands' reach never takes a grenade as a loose object, so the loadout always owns it. The landed body is simulating, so the engine no longer follows its transform; taking the item up gives it a fresh inert actor in the hand and removes the landed one from the pool. That is the only case where an item changes actors, and it happens on pickup rather than per frame.

The native factory normally permits player allocations to reclaim an existing pool entry. During a safe-item draw, the allocator hook forces the reclaim argument false under the allocator's critical section. A full pool therefore refuses the draw without deleting another item or live grenade. Pickup also needs room for the replacement before the old actor is removed. If allocation or exact-lease validation fails, the loose item remains unchanged. Stop, load, and reset callbacks clear pending releases and transfer old actors to a retirement list, which the serialized render boundary removes without touching newly drawn items.

## The throw's motion

A grenade leaves the hand the way a gun does when the hand lets go of it ([physical-weapons.md, "Letting go"](physical-weapons.md#letting-go)), and through the same code: `hold::act::release_at` is the estimate a gun's let-go takes, and `grenade::throw_release` caps it as a gun's is, at `hold::MAX_THROW_MPS`. The point it estimates is the held grenade's own centre, the hand's history is read only from when the grenade came into that hand, the hand's fastest moment in the last quarter second is used because a throwing hand slows before the fingers finish opening, and the player's own movement comes in once, through the rig's motion at that point. The release carries the hand's angular velocity as well as its linear velocity, so a flick of the wrist spins the grenade as it spins a thrown gun. A pouch grenade, a caught live grenade, and a C4 charge all take this estimate. There is no separate grenade window, peak span, scale, or cap.

Three differences remain. The first two are the engine's, and the third is the item's safety:

- A held grenade's body is not in the simulation, and only the engine's launch puts it there: [`Explosive::UpdatePostSimFromManager`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-UpdatePostSimFromManager) applies a pending launch at the end of the tick, putting the body into the simulation, setting its angular velocity from [`Explosive::m_InitialAngularVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_InitialAngularVelocity) and its linear velocity from [`Explosive::m_DeferredInitialVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_DeferredInitialVelocity), and disabling collision with the thrower. A grenade therefore takes its release through those fields instead of the owned-body setter a gun's body takes, and it first moves one tick after the release, where a gun moves in the same tick. The release's transform and the actor's current matrix are both set to the release pose, so the grenade waits where the hand let go of it rather than at the hand. A caught grenade's body is already simulating, so it takes the owned-body setter as a gun does.
- The engine gives a grenade's body a high angular damping when it creates it, so a grenade's spin dies away within a fraction of a second.
- The fuse: only the pin starts it ([The pin](#the-pin)); a release never does.

A held gun is also a body of its own joined to the hand bodies ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md)), which gives it weight and collision while it is held; that body does not take part in the let-go, which sets the motion on the gun's own body. A held grenade has no such joined body: its own body follows the hand's transform until the release, and the hand's body leaves the world while it holds the grenade.

## Live grenades

Catching an existing live grenade retains the native grenade and its running fuse. It remains dangerous in the hand, and the existing warning ring and haptics continue to reflect its fuse. Throwing it on does not create a replacement, and nothing in the catch path raises or resets its fuse. The inert-from-draw guarantees apply to grenades drawn from the pouch until their pins are pulled, not to captured explosives.

## Runtime checks

In the game, draw a grenade with no native grenade ammunition, move it into a hip or chest holster, draw another into each free hand, and re-draw the stowed item. Verify that the grenades remain independent, that each is the engine's own object, and that ammunition does not change. Open a hand slowly or stop controller input: the grenade must fall inertly, lie as a real body, and be re-grabbable, and neither dropping it nor losing tracking may arm it. Then release one with a deliberate throw and verify that only that item becomes a live grenade, that it leaves the hand inert for an instant before its fuse starts, and that the fuse counts down from the release. Repeat with both hands, with a native grenade caught in the other hand, and after a gameplay transition.

Draw until the ammunition pool refuses. The refusal must produce no item, no floating model, and no half-created item in the hand.

At the locker, repeat the draw with native grenade stock at zero, below capacity, and full. Test both free hands independently and together, with an occupied opposite hand and with stored safe items. Confirm that the visible grenade display is the target, that stock and existing items do not change, and that leaving the source or resetting gameplay cannot reuse a stale offer. Check slow release, re-grab, deliberate throw, and repeated draws. Native grenade displays must remain available at full stock even when the native pickup prompt is absent. Correlate the static collection registration and successful acquisition with the upper-shelf grenade bodies separately from any native grenade weapon. Stream the location out and back in, and confirm that the new registration cannot complete an old hand offer. Repeat across a gameplay transition and after injection into an already loaded location. Confirm the loader's resource-name spelling before claiming coverage of the recorded location. A missing target remains a source-coverage failure, not evidence of successful locker acquisition.

The factory, the fuse gate, the deferred launch, and the launch path are verified against the game database. The item bookkeeping has unit tests. Rendering, physical feel under the hands, pool-pressure behaviour, and transition timing still require live gameplay testing.
