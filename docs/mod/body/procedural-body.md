# The procedural torso

Without it, Rico's body is the game's animation with corrections on top: HumanIK targets for the head, the torso, and the hands ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)). The procedural torso poses the spine, the neck, and the clavicles from the player instead, on the hips the game's animation keeps. The hips, the pelvis, and the legs are the game's in every case: the body rig moves Rico by driving the game's own movement, so the legs walk, stand, and land the way the game's clips put them ([rig.md](rig.md)). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/procedural/`, its settings are `controllers.body.procedural`, and the debug UI's Body fit feature has the switch (`Procedural torso`, on by default).

## Where the pose is written

The pose is written at the HumanIK solve gate: a pre-call detour on [`HumanIK::HasTargets`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##func-HasTargets) (`hooks::character::gate`), acting for the local player's solver and the `MAIN` pass. That call has one caller, inside the pose-finalization pass, after every game writer of IK targets and the first weapon attachment refresh, and before the solve, the second attachment refresh, the model-space sync, the hit shapes, the AI's view of the character, and the attached effects ([skeleton.md, "Who reads the pose before `UpdatePropEffects`"](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#who-reads-the-pose-before-updatepropeffects)). A pose written there is the one bullets hit and NPCs see. The solver is found from the character, which embeds it, and the gate runs inside the HumanIK gate the game checks first (the global switch and the reduced level of detail).

The gate has no step of its own, so the pass's entry records the pass's step on the thread running it, and the blend and the followers advance by it rather than by the wall clock between gates.

The writes go through [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) into the current pose, parents first, so each write carries the bones under it. The pose producer's pose pair ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#bone-store-hkapose-model-space)) makes the pose written this tick next tick's `T0`, so the skinning interpolates it like any other.

## The rig

The torso drives the four spine bones, the neck, the head, and the two clavicles (`LeftShoulder` and `RightShoulder`). `rig` finds them, with the hips, the pelvis, and the legs it leaves alone, by name in the skeleton the animation controller holds ([`AnimationController::m_Skeleton`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##field-m_Skeleton)), and checks that each bone's parent is the one the solver assumes. It also measures each arm from the reference pose, for the clavicles: where the shoulder joint (`LeftArm`) sits on its clavicle, and the arm's length down `LeftForeArm` to `LeftHand`. A skeleton that differs is refused with a warning (`procedural rig: ...`), and the body stays the animation's. The rig is kept for the skeleton it was built from, and every bone index is checked against the skeleton's bone count before a joint is read or written, since [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) does not check it. The skeleton is logged once per session (`body: skeleton bone`, one line per bone).

## Modes

- `Off`: nothing happens at the gate. Switching to it from `Full` first blends the torso back to the animation at the hand-back's pace (`blend_out_s`), with the case `switched off`.
- `Full`: the torso, below. Settings saved while this mode was named `Body` load as `Full`.

The weight follows its case's with a critically damped spring: `blend_s` toward the procedural pose and the quicker `blend_out_s` back to the animation. A death or a ragdoll hands the torso over at once, since a ragdoll writes the pose itself. Handing back is continuous because the animation has been playing all along. Every change of case is logged at info level (`procedural body: the hand-over case changes`, with the case left and entered and the weight), and the readout shows the case and the weight.

The steady view reads the same table with a tracker of its own, and hangs the on-foot view from Rico's root only in the cases where the player's movement carries the body: on foot on the ground, a jump, and climbing with the hands ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)).

The predicates the table calls at the gate walk the character's own rule system and interaction graph; the engine calls the same ones from the same pass. The vehicle seat, the traversal, and the airborne modes come from the mode detection the input tick publishes, so they lag by up to a frame; the state bits, the motion state, the health, and the proxy's support are read at the gate. The ledge climb keeps no state on the character, so a post-call on its motion task stamps each tick of it for the local player (`hooks::character::signals`, `procedural::signals`), and the first tick of each climb logs `procedural body: the grapple's ledge climb starts` at info level. A hit reaction keeps no state either: [`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction) queues the stumble or the knock-back straight on Rico's animated model, so a pre-call on [`AnimatedModel::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-QueueAct) stamps the local player's, matched by the model's address, logging `procedural body: a hit reaction starts`. The reaction then holds the torso on the animation for `hit_reaction_hold_s`, renewed by each new act.

## The torso on the animation's hips

`solver::anchored` poses the torso from the clip's own pose, so the clip's posture is the base the player's head moves from: a walk's stride, a bike's lean into a turn, a driver's reach for the wheel. In order:

1. The chest twists toward the player's torso, by the body rig's chest heading against Rico's facing, within `chest_turn_limit_deg`, about the clip's own spine and shared out up it (more of it higher). A spine far from upright (lying in a swim) takes less of the twist, none lying flat.
2. The spine swings so the neck reaches the neck pivot under the player's head, shared out along the four spine bones and repeated to converge, never past `lean_limit_deg` from the clip's own spine. A lean the body rig reads as a bend therefore bends Rico's back, and the hips stay where the clip has them.
3. A look down pitches the chest forward by a share of it, again by how upright the spine is, and the spine turns back onto the neck's line about its base.
4. The chest is kept out of the camera's near field: a point of the chest (the upper spine, the sternum, or a shoulder) less than `chest_clear_below_m` below the camera's eyes is swung back with the whole spine about its base until it is at least `chest_clear_back_m` (8 cm) behind the eyes along the view's level forward, a frame that turns with the head's yaw alone, so looking down never brings the chest forward. BIMOS, an open VR rig, holds its chest target about 22 cm behind and 11 cm below the eyes for the same reason; the rule here measures the chest's front points (the sternum and the shoulder joints, which stand about 9 cm behind the eyes in a neutral pose) rather than a chest centre, and only pushes when they come closer. The rule holds whatever the clip and the rig do, so a camera that sits low or a spine bent back never puts the chest in front of the eyes, and a look down at a chest further below is left alone.
5. The neck takes a share of the head's turn; the head override sets the head itself after the solve.

The player's head is read at the neck pivot, below and behind the eyes in the head's frame (`procedural::head`): looking down turns the eyes about the neck, so the eye point drops and comes forward although nothing below the neck moved. The head is measured against the root at the height the steady view hangs it from, so a staircase does not read as a crouch.

The rotations are composed onto the animated bones, so no bone's rest frame is assumed. The game's own IK targets on the torso's effectors (the waist, the chest's end, and the shoulders) have their reach and pull faded by the torso's weight (`neutralize_targets`); the hips' targets stay the game's. The head's own target keeps its reach, so the body IK's head target closes what is left between the neck and the player's head, but its pull fades, so it cannot drag the procedural spine after the head. The pre-solve IK that would pose the same bones (the body IK's torso) stands down once the torso has its full weight.

## The arms

The arms are the controllers' wrist targets ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md)), solved by HumanIK on the procedural torso after the gate. While the procedural torso poses the torso, the hand IK does not queue them at the pass's entry; it keeps its plan for the gate (`hands::ik::queue_or_defer`), and the gate queues it right after writing the pose (`hands::ik::queue_deferred`), since the rotation target is a delta from the wrist as it stands before the solve. At the gate:

- The wrist target is kept out of the torso: two capsules along the written spine, the chest from `Spine2` to the neck (`chest_radius_m`) and the abdomen from the hips to `Spine2` (`abdomen_radius_m`), widened by `hand_margin_m`. A target inside either moves straight out of it.
- The pull is zero: the torso is the player's, and the solve may not bend it toward a hand.
- The clavicles reach with the hands: up for a hand above the shoulder, at most `clavicle_elevation_deg`; forward for a hand reaching far ahead or across the body, at most `clavicle_protraction_deg`; and down a little for a hand reaching far below, at most `clavicle_depression_deg`, starting at `clavicle_reach_start` of the arm's length and followed with the time constant `clavicle_follow_s`.
- Each driven arm gets an elbow hint (`elbow_hints`, on by default): a weak position target on the elbow (`elbow_hint_reach`, scaled by the torso's weight), out along a pole that points down, out to the arm's side, and a little back. The pole is chosen from the reach: a hand reaching up or across the body lifts the elbow further out to the side, as a person's does, instead of leaving it hanging under a raised arm. Where the reach runs along the pole (a hand reaching down, out, and back, toward a hip holster), the pole's part across the reach vanishes, so a second pole, straight back, takes over smoothly as it shrinks rather than at once, and the elbow does not jump.

The gate's torso keep-out (`body::procedural::torso_keep_out`) is also the capsule geometry the physical hands' held-object keep-out queries ([physical-hands.md](../input/physical-hands.md#the-players-own-body)).

## The limbs' twist

The skeleton's twist bones carry part of the skin along the arm ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md): `df_LeftArmRollA01` under `LeftArm` and `LeftForeArmRoll` under `LeftForeArm`, and the right side alike), so a hand turned far about the forearm does not wring the skin at the wrist. `body::procedural::twist` runs at the end of the pose-finalization pass, in the post-call `UpdatePropEffects` detour after the head override and the finger curls, and shares the hand's twist against the forearm over the forearm's twist bones by where each sits, and holds the upper arm's roll back near the shoulder, by the torso's weight. A forearm can only pronate or supinate so far before a real arm rolls the upper arm at the shoulder, so a hand turned more than a right angle from its forearm (`solver::twist::FOREARM_TWIST_LIMIT`) passes half of the excess (`UPPER_ARM_SHARE`) up to the upper arm: its twist bones take the passed turn rising toward the elbow, and the forearm's start from it at the elbow, so the skin turns continuously from the shoulder to the wrist and the forearm no longer carries a 130 degree turn alone (27:17 of the 2 November 2026 session, a gun held out of a car window with the hand turned 104 to 132 degrees from the clip's wrist). The bones of the arm themselves are not turned, so the elbow stays where the solve put it. The legs' twist bones are the animation's. The `TWIST BONES` status row shows how many twist bones were found and the largest twist they carried.

## Diagnostics

The debug UI's Body/tracking > Body fit live status shows the mode, the weight, whether the procedural torso owns the torso, a rigging error, the hand-over case and how far the neck sits from its target, each clavicle's turn, and the wingsuit's arms.

## What needs confirming in the headset

1. Standing still: the readout's weight reaches one, the game's idle plays in the legs, and a hand on the hip lands on Rico's. Leaning over a table bends Rico's back toward the head while the legs and the hips stay the game's. Standing relaxed, the camera sits at Rico's eyes (the camera height readout's difference within a few centimetres), and neither the scarf nor the shoulders come into the lower view unless the player looks down.
2. The weapon stays in the hand, and shooting Rico's body (from an NPC) hits where it is drawn.
3. Walking with the stick and stepping in the room: the legs play the game's walk without the torso tearing from the hips.
4. With hands on: a hand on the belly or the chest rests on Rico's body, an arm folded across the chest goes around it, and the elbows hang naturally.
5. The hand-over: a scripted sequence logs the case `scripted sequence`; a stumble logs `procedural body: a hit reaction starts` and hands back about a second later; a vehicle, the wingsuit, a grapple reel, a stunt, swimming, and the ledge climb each log a change of case and blend without a pop; a ragdoll and a death hand over at once; a jump keeps the torso with the game's legs.
6. Raising a hand overhead lifts that shoulder, and reaching across the body brings it forward.
7. Twist bones: `TWIST BONES` reads `OK`; a hand turned palm up and down spreads the twist along the forearm, and turned past a right angle (a gun held out of a car window, the palm turned out) the upper arm's skin takes part of it rather than the forearm wringing. Raising a hand overhead or reaching across the chest lifts that elbow out; reaching down and back to the hip moves the elbow back without a jump.
