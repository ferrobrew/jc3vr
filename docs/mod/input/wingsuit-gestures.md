# Wingsuit pose controls

The arms open the wingsuit, brake and land it, and close it. The recognizer is `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/wingsuit/gestures/`, which returns commands without writing game actions; the bindings' flight state (`crates/jc3vr-gameplay/src/controllers/bindings/flight/`) feeds it and turns its commands into actions. Its thresholds and dwells are the `wingsuit_gestures` section of the vehicles' [tuning file](../tuning.md), in shares of the calibrated arm span. The arm steering, the barrel roll, the stall flip, the parachute handles, and the ripcord are in [flight.md](flight.md).

## Observations

The recognizer works from one coherent head-and-controller observation. `crates/jc3vr-view/src/vr/input/flight_pose.rs` publishes a complete packet only when the head and controllers belong to the same input sampling operation, and unavailable tracking, origin changes, recentering, and runtime replacement invalidate it. The recognizer requires fresh observations, and the flight state keeps the grip offset and body dimensions it interprets them with: a change to either resets arming, dwell, and braking without rewinding the observation watermark, so an old packet repeated after a calibration edit cannot add dwell or revive held output.

The arms are measured against the body's facing, read from the line between the wrists, because a flier looks around, down, and along the turn while the arms stay spread. The palm is each wrist's roll about the fingers, the measure the arm steering's wrist tilt reads, so raising or sweeping the arms does not change it.

## Opening and closing

A fall's start is a fresh start: a stable flight pose held for `flight_dwell_s` requests deployment, whether the arms were spread before the player stepped off or are still spread from cutting a canopy. Only a fall out of the suit needs a neutral (both arms lowered or drawn in) first, so arms spread as the suit closes do not open it again, and a canopy the player opened needs another pose before the spread, so the arms that opened it do not reopen the suit. In a fall, and on a grapple line that can yield, the flight pose is the arms spread alone, however the palms turn, since a player spreading their arms to fly a reel turns the palms into the wind as often as flat. Under a canopy the flight pose also needs flat palms, since arms out with the hands on the toggles are the canopy's own pose.

In the suit, a stable flight pose with flat palms arms the closure, the suit's entry being the fresh start, and a deliberate withdrawal of both arms then requests it. One withdrawn arm, tracking loss, menu capture, or an interaction claim is not a closure gesture, and a button deploy with the arms lowered does not close the suit at once.

The recognizer watches from the moment Rico leaves the ground on foot, as the view anchor reads it (from the jump's grace on; [locomotion.md](../body/locomotion.md)), not only once the mode detection reports a fall. Off a ledge the game plays its jump for a while before it reports a fall, and its jump states take the suit's opening as its falls do once Rico is descending and high enough ([engine: wingsuit-opening.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-opening.md)), so the dwell is met during the jump and the deploy goes in there. A request the game refuses (still rising, or too near the ground) is asked again after `deploy_retry_s` while the pose is held, longer than the suit's entry takes to reach the mode detection, so a repeat never lands in the suit, where the same action closes it. A fall keeps its start through a suspension, so a claim or a menu mid-fall does not ask for a neutral afterwards.

## Hand claims

Finalization checks packet currency, availability, and current per-hand interaction claims again after the other handlers run. A contextual canopy request (the ripcord's among them) suppresses a pending suit contribution, and newly acquired grip, trigger, mechanical, or grapple ownership cancels it. Blocked weapon actions do not, since the game blocks them around a draw or holster and through some of a fall's start, and the grip and slot claims already cover a draw.

A hand has the grapple while the grapple button is held, while its line is attached, or during a traversal; tethers left in the world claim neither hand, so a fall after tethering a car still opens the suit. A grip claims a hand only when it grips something: neither the line's finger pose, which outlasts the line by a few ticks, nor a flat palm, a passive contact the fingers also report in free fall with the arms spread, claims it. A hand claims a body slot only while its grip is squeezed within the slot's reach, so the lowered arms of the neutral and the closure, which bring the hands to the hip sockets, do not stand the gestures down. A deploy out of a grapple traversal spends the grapple button's press, and the spent press ends as soon as the button reads up in any mode, so the grapple works again for the rest of the flight.

## Braking and landing

The game lands the wingsuit through its close act, which a held air brake queues on every tick ([engine: wingsuit-landing.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-landing.md)). Once the suit is slow and low enough, the act turns into a slide onto the feet; faster, it does nothing, and the glide that meets the ground ends in a ragdoll. So the brake is the landing: wide arms with both palms to the wind, held as the suit comes in low over level ground, slow it until the game closes the suit into the slide. Held higher up, the same brake closes a slowed suit into a fall, so the landing's brake belongs in the last few metres. Both need the game's gear mods (`upgrade_wingsuit_air_brake` and `upgrade_wingsuit_cancel`); without the second, the close act never fires. The `air_brake` action holds the same pair of actions from a button, by default the left controller's lower face button whichever hand is dominant.

The brake gesture latches. It engages once both arms are spread past the flight pose's span and both wrists are rolled toward palm-forward by `brake_roll_deg` to `brake_roll_max_deg`, held for `brake_dwell_s`; no flight pose or arming comes first. Once engaged it holds whatever the palms do until either arm comes in past the spread's hysteresis (`pose_hysteresis_span` inside `flight_lateral_span`) or both palms are back within `flight_roll_deg` of flat. A flare that turns the palms on past palm-forward toward palm-up, and through the roll's wrap at palm-up, keeps braking; the band's upper end only keeps a palm turned past it from entering the brake from the far side of the wrap. A released brake takes the whole dwell again.

The brake engages only from a pose seen in the suit that releases it, once per suit: the arms drawn in, or both palms flat, for one sample. A fall takes the spread arms alone as the deploying pose, palms turned any way, so a suit opened with the palms already to the wind would otherwise brake as it opens, and the close act would turn the suit, slowed by its opening, back into a fall. A suit opened with the button and the arms down is ready at once. Leaving the suit asks for the release again; an interruption within it (a claim, lost tracking, a menu, recentering, a calibration edit) drops the brake but not the readiness.

The brake weakens while the suit turns, since the game's brake gives up part of its drag and lift for the steering's sideways input, which the arm steering's bank is; level wings brake hardest. The stall flip's removal ([flight.md, "The stall flip"](flight.md#the-stall-flip)) keeps the brake and the close act open as the suit slows nose-up, where the game's stall would close both. The arm steering's pitch stays live while braking, and wrists rolled to the wind read as a full climb under the wrist-tilt measure, so the brake is also a flare: the game's drag and lift, which follow the climb input, slow the plain suit to the landing's speed.

## Take-off from the ground

The boost wingsuit's take-off from the ground (the use button held on foot, the rule states `S_JC_WINGSUIT_BOOST_TRANSITION_FROM_GROUND_INTO` and then `..._FROM_GROUND`) launches the suit along the character's facing. In VR the body faces where the grounded rig puts it, not where the head looks, so a take-off, being a movement action, turns the body (`crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/wingsuit/take_off.rs`): the head's heading relative to the view stands in for the torso's as the body's follow target ([rig.md](../body/rig.md)), so the game's own turn brings the body round under the view and the view does not move.

The game turns the body only while its movement task runs, and the wind-up stops it, so the turn starts with the hold: once the use button (read from its `OPEN_WINGSUIT` action) has been held on the ground for `take_off.turn_after_s`, the target tracks the head's heading, and the body has the rest of the hold to come round. On entering the take-off the heading is held until the take-off ends. Letting go before the wind-up lets the target go, so a tap to pick something up turns nothing. A hold that enters a vehicle's gunner seat also turns the body toward the gaze until the entry starts. The turn needs the body to be following, which it is on foot with the follow setting on. The log has `wingsuit: the use button held on the ground turns the body to the head's heading` and `wingsuit: the take-off from the ground holds the body on the head's heading`, each with the heading and whether the body was following.
