# The VR inventory

The game's inventory holds one weapon per slot. The VR inventory treats every weapon as an object that belongs to the world, a hand, or the body, so the player can carry more than the slots hold and pick up again what they put down. The game stays the source of truth for its slots, which the save, the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the missions read; the mod mirrors them and owns everything beyond them. The engine side is in [weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md), and the hands' part (drawing, holstering, picking up, handing over) in [physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md). The code is `payload/src/controllers/weapons/inventory/`.

None of this has been run in the headset. It acts while the bindings inject, under the controller scheme with a controller active.

## The registry

With `weapons.inventory.enabled` (on; it and the inventory's other switches are in the debug UI's Controllers tab, under "VR inventory"), the registry (`inventory::registry`) keeps one entry per weapon object the player owns, holds, or has let go of nearby, and where it is: in its slot, in a hand (wielded by the game, or driven by the mod as the second weapon), lying in the world, or held by another character. It covers the pistols, the two-handed weapons, and the special weapons; the heavy weapons, the grenade, the planted explosive, the beacon, and the sun gun stay the game's alone.

Each input tick it reads the slots, the hands, and, for every weapon it knows that is in no slot, what became of it, through a weak reference to the weapon ([`WeaponBase::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeakThis)) that tells whether it still lives without keeping it alive: a weapon whose holder ([`WeaponBase::m_Holder`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Holder)) is another character is that character's; any other is lying in the world, with its distance from the player. A weapon is keyed by its object while it lives; one whose object is gone leaves the registry.

Every change is logged at info level: `inventory: tracking a weapon of the two-handed slot, in the left hand`, `inventory: a weapon of the two-handed slot is in the world`, `inventory: no longer tracking a weapon of the two-handed slot: the object is gone`, each with the entry's number and the weapon's spawn tag hash.

## Keeping dropped weapons

The game removes a dropped weapon by its distance from the player and the view, and never one that something else holds a strong reference to. With `weapons.inventory.pin_world` (on), a weapon the player let go of is pinned (`inventory::pins`), held by a strong reference, while it is within `pin_radius_m` (8 m), and once pinned until it is past `unpin_radius_m` (12 m), so one at the edge does not flicker. At most `max_pinned` (6) are pinned, the nearest first, since a pinned weapon keeps its resources loaded. An unpinned weapon past `forget_m` (60 m), or beyond the most the registry keeps, is left to the game. Weapons the player never touched are never pinned, so the game's own clean-up of the world goes on as before.

The spawn system's reset (a load) releases its references to every object and replaces the slots' weapons. At its pre-reset event ([`Inventory::m_SpawnSystemPreReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemPreReset), seen through the local player's [`Inventory::HandleEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HandleEvent)) the inventory releases every reference it holds and forgets every entry (`inventory: the spawn system is resetting`, `inventory: released every weapon`), so no pinned weapon outlives the reset outside the spawn system. Switching the registry off does the same.

## Carrying more than the slots

With `weapons.inventory.carry` (on), the player carries weapons beyond the slots on sockets of the body (`inventory::sockets`, `inventory::carry`). A carried weapon is held by a strong reference of the mod's, holstered, with the player as its holder, and in no slot, so the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the saves do not list it.

The sockets: three on the back for long guns (the two-handed and special weapons), two on the hips for pistols (the dominant hip first), and one on the chest for a submachine gun (then the hips). How many back and hip sockets there are, and whether there is a chest socket, are settings (`back_sockets`, `hip_sockets`, `chest_socket`). Each socket is a bone of the character's (the back's two dock bones, the hip holster bones, and the chest's explosive bone, where the slots' own weapons dock) with an offset in the character's frame (`offsets`, x across, y up, z backward), so a carried weapon sits beside the slot's weapon rather than inside it. The bones follow the body, as the slots' weapons do.

A carried weapon is placed after each placement of the inventory's own weapons ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms), the hold's detour): its guns' holstered attach-bone entries name the socket's bone, the weapon's own placement ([`WeaponBase::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) puts them there, and the offset moves them. It is shown and hidden whenever the inventory's weapons are ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponVisibility`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponVisibility), detoured), so it hides and shows with them in vehicles and the wingsuit.

How weapons come onto a socket and off it:

- A weapon picked up into a slot that holds another weapon (a pickup into the hand, a take from a character's hands, a take off a rack, or a rack's copy) carries the slot's weapon on the first free socket that fits it, rather than dropping it: the weapon is unequipped if it was wielded and taken out of its slot without a drop ([`Inventory::RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RemoveWeapon)), so it stays holstered on the player. With no socket free, the game's behaviour stands (`weapons.hold.drop_replaced`). The log lines are `inventory: carried a weapon of the two-handed slot on the back (first)`, or `inventory: no socket is free for the weapon the pickup replaces`.
- Each carried weapon has a grab volume on its grip (`GrabVolume::Socket`, radius `socket_radius_m`, 0.12 m) for a hand without a gun. A grip press there, with the other hand empty, draws it: the slot's weapon, if any, is carried on the socket it leaves, the carried weapon goes into its slot and is equipped ([`Inventory::AddWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-AddWeapon) with [`E_EQUIP`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::EquipMode##variant-E_EQUIP)), the hold puts it in the grabbing hand, and it slides there from the socket. The HUD and the weapon wheel then show it. With the other hand holding a gun, the grab is refused. The log line is `inventory: drew a carried weapon off the back (first) into the left hand`.
- Each free socket that fits the wielded weapon has a grab volume where its weapon would sit, for the hand holding it. Opening the grip there (with `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD GUNS`), or a grip press there, puts the weapon onto the socket: it leaves its slot, which is then empty until a weapon goes into it, and is carried. The dual-wield pair drawn in both hands stays together. The log line is `inventory: put the weapon from the right hand onto the back (second)`. This differs from the design's first sketch, which kept such a weapon in its slot and owned its holstered bone: moving it out of the slot keeps one rule, that a socket holds only carried weapons, and drawing it again puts it back into its slot.

With separate pistols ([physical-weapons.md, "Separate pistols"](physical-weapons.md#separate-pistols)), the second pistol of a pair the game hands over is a carried weapon from the start: it is spawned and taken into the carry set on the hip the slot's pistol does not take. A hip socket on a hip the slot's pistol does not take (the slot holds a single pistol whose home is the other hip, or no pistol) puts its pistol in that hip's holster rather than beside it, so the second pistol sits where the pair's second gun sat. A carried single pistol moves into the pistol slot, unequipped, once the slot has been empty half a second, and a carried single pistol drawn into the slot swaps hips with the slot's pistol where they sit.

Taking the slot's weapon out leaves the HUD's weapon list to be rebuilt from the slots ([`HUDUI::InitWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-InitWeapons)). Switching `weapons.inventory.carry` or `weapons.inventory.enabled` off lets every carried weapon fall where it is ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop), `inventory: dropped the carried weapons`). The spawn system's reset releases them.

## A carried weapon in either hand

A carried weapon can be held beside the wielded one, in the other hand, as the second weapon ([physical-weapons.md, "A second weapon"](physical-weapons.md#a-second-weapon)), so two rifles, or two weapons of any one slot, can be held at once, one per hand. With `weapons.hold.either_hand` and `weapons.hold.second_weapon` on, a grab of a carried weapon's socket by the hand without a gun, while the other hand holds the wielded weapon, takes it as the second weapon (`inventory: drew a carried weapon off the back (first) into the left hand beside the wielded one`). It stays holstered and carried: its guns ride the hand's attach bone, which the carry set's placement puts them on (`inventory::carry`), the rigid grip pins it to the hand as it does a slot's second weapon, and the mod fires it from the hand's trigger, with its own magazine (the pips over the hand) and the player's reserve. Its socket is kept for it.

What the hands do with it (`inventory::decide`, pure):

- with `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD GUNS`, opening its grip at its socket, or at a free socket that fits it (which becomes its socket), docks it there; at any holster puts it back on its socket; anywhere else lets it fall with the hand's motion, out of the carry set, a weapon in the world that the inventory keeps while near;
- a grip press at a free socket by its hand docks it there too;
- with `weapons.hold.promote_second` (on), once nothing else has been wielded for half a second, after the wielded weapon was holstered, put onto a socket, or dropped, it goes into its slot and is equipped in the same hand, the slot's weapon taking its socket, so the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the game's reload take it over.

A slot's second weapon (the shoulder's long gun, the existing case) does the same: put onto a free socket it leaves its slot into the carry set, and let fall it leaves its slot.

## Across resets, saves, and loads

The game's save stores only the slots, and the spawn system's reset (a load, and possibly a death or a fast travel) replaces the slots' weapons by type and lets every other object go. With `weapons.inventory.persist` (on), the carried weapons come back too (`inventory::persist`, pure, and `inventory::restore`):

- At the reset's pre-reset event, the carried weapons are recorded (each one's spawn tag hash, magazine, socket, and whether it is a single pistol) with whether the pistol slot's weapon is a single pistol, before the carry set lets them go (`inventory: the spawn system is resetting`).
- Each time the game stores the player's save data ([`StorePlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-StorePlayerSaveData), detoured), the save sidecar is written: `inventory.toml` in the per-user configuration directory beside `settings.toml` ([settings.md](../settings.md)), on a writer thread, through a temporary file moved over the old one. It holds a format version, the carried weapons, and a fingerprint of what the store writes: each slot's weapon type (the heavy slot's always zero, as the save has it) and every reserve (`inventory: wrote the save sidecar`). While pistols are made separate it also says whether the pistol slot's weapon is a single pistol (`slot_pistol_single`), and each carried single pistol says so (`single`).
- Each time the game loads the player's save data ([`LoadPlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-LoadPlayerSaveData), detoured, after the original), the sidecar is read and its fingerprint compared with what the load put into the inventory: the respawn table's types ([`Inventory::m_WeaponToSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawn)) and the reserves. A slot whose type the game does not know now (content not installed) loads empty and still matches. With a match, the sidecar's carried weapons of known types are kept for the respawn (`inventory: the sidecar matches the save; its carried weapons come back`); a missing, unreadable, other-version, or other-save sidecar carries nothing extra, and the load is otherwise untouched (`inventory: the load carries nothing extra`, with the outcome, and a warning for an unreadable file).
- After the reset's post-reset event, the first input tick with the player respawns the carried weapons: from the load, when there was one since the reset, and otherwise from the reset's own record, when it is less than two minutes old and for the same character (a quit to the menu followed by a new game should not carry them in). Each is asked of the spawn system by its type at the player ([`SpawnSystem::SpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-SpawnTagIndex), the way the inventory respawns a slot), and when it comes back it is taken into the carry set on its socket (or another free one that fits) with its magazine as it was ([`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup), then [`WeaponBase::SetMagazineAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetMagazineAmmo)), a single pistol made single again. The pistol slot's weapon the game respawns is made single when the record says it was, and is otherwise split as a pair the game hands over; a carried single pistol that never comes back makes it the game's pair again. The log lines are `inventory: respawning the carried weapons` and `inventory: restored a carried weapon on the back (first)`; a spawn that has not come within ten seconds is given up.

The game keeps one save per user, and the mod cannot name which save a load reads, so the sidecar is not keyed to a save: the fingerprint is what ties it to the save it was written with. A load of an older save, written before the last store, does not match, and nothing extra is carried.

## Other characters and missions

A weapon the player let go of that another character then holds (its holder is that character) is that character's: the registry shows it as with another character (`inventory: a weapon of the two-handed slot is with another character`), never pins it, and forgets it once the character is as far away as `forget_m`. Taken back (a take from the character's hands, or picked up after the character drops it), it is the same entry again.

A mission that takes the player's weapons away caches the slots in a graph script's weapon cache, which forces the slots' weapons out ([`NodeWeaponCache::CacheAllWeaponSlots`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-CacheAllWeaponSlots), detoured). The detour notes whether the pistol slot held a single pistol, and the next pistol to arrive in the slot is one pistol again when it did, rather than a pair to split. The carried weapons are then stashed (`inventory::missions`): a carried second weapon goes back to its socket, and every carried weapon is hidden, keeps no grab volume, and stays carried (`inventory: a mission took the player's weapons; the carried weapons are stashed`). The give-back has no seam of its own, since the game inlines it into its callers, so the carried weapons come back when a tracked slot holds a weapon again after the slots were seen empty (`inventory: a slot holds a weapon again; the carried weapons are back`): when the mission gives the slots back, but also when the player picks up any weapon in the meantime. The challenges' own weapon handling, which does not go through the cache, is not seen, and a reset unstashes them.

A weapon an area clean-up takes out of the spawn system while it is carried lives on through the carry set's reference, outside the spawn system, and is recorded and respawned at the next reset as usual.

## Settings and readout

The settings are `controllers.weapons.inventory`. The debug UI's Controllers tab has a `VR inventory` section listing every entry (its number, slot, tag hash, place, magazine, and distance) and the counts, and its settings under `VR inventory`. The debug UI's Status tab has an `INVENTORY` section: `TRACKED WEAPONS` (the weapons owned and in the world, and the resets), `CARRIED` (each carried weapon and its socket, and the draws, the weapons put away, and the weapons kept from pickups' drops), `SAVE SIDECAR` (the writes and the carried weapons in the last, or a failed write), `LAST LOAD` (what the last load made of the sidecar, and how many weapons the last respawn put back), and `KEPT IN THE WORLD` (the weapons kept of those in the world, the pins since load, and the weapons left to the game). The debug UI also has the socket counts, the socket radius, and each socket's offset. The scenario signals `inventory.kept`, `inventory.pins`, `inventory.resets`, `inventory.carried`, `inventory.draws`, `inventory.stows`, `inventory.restored`, and `inventory.sidecar_writes` read them (`testing/scenarios/inventory_keep_dropped.toml` and `inventory_two_rifles.toml`).

## In the headset

1. Draw a rifle and drop it (with `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD GUNS`, open the grip away from the body): `inventory: a weapon of the two-handed slot is in the world`, then `... is in the world, pinned`. `KEPT IN THE WORLD` shows `1 OF 1 KEPT`.
2. Walk 15 m away: the pin goes (`... is in the world` again). Walk back: it is pinned again, and the rifle is still there.
3. Set `Kept within` to 2 m in the debug UI, drop the rifle, walk 30 m away and turn around a few times, and walk back: the game may have removed it (`no longer tracking ...: the object is gone`). With the default 8 m and the pin held, it stays.
4. Pick the rifle up again: `... is in the left hand` (or right), and the pin's count does not change.
5. Let a soldier pick up a dropped rifle, if one does: `... is with another character`.
6. Save and load: `inventory: the spawn system is resetting` and `released every weapon`, then the slots' new weapons are tracked anew. Nothing the inventory kept is left lying around after the load.
7. Watch that nothing about the game's own weapons changes with the registry on or off.
8. Holding an assault rifle, pick up a second assault rifle with the free hand: `inventory: carried a weapon of the two-handed slot on the back (first)`, the first rifle sits on the back beside where the slot's rifle would, and the HUD shows the new one. `CARRIED` shows `TWO-HANDED ON BACK (FIRST)`. Look at the rifle on the back by turning the head: note whether the offset puts it beside the shoulder rifle or through the body, and tune `offsets` in the debug UI if not.
9. Put the held rifle away at the shoulder holster (it goes into its slot), then reach for the carried rifle with the right hand: its volume highlights, and a grip press draws it (`inventory: drew a carried weapon off the back (first) into the right hand`); it slides off the back into the hand, the HUD switches to it, and the rifle that was in the slot is now on the back.
10. With a pistol in the hand, open the grip at a free hip socket: `inventory: put the weapon from the right hand onto the right hip`, and the pistol sits on the hip beside the slot's place. Draw it again by grabbing it.
11. With three long guns carried and the back full, pick up a fourth: `no socket is free for the weapon the pickup replaces`, and the slot's weapon falls as before.
12. Enter a car, swim, wingsuit, and parachute: the carried weapons hide and show with the slot's weapons, and stay on the body.
13. Switch `weapons.inventory.carry` off: the carried weapons fall to the ground (`inventory: dropped the carried weapons`) and can be picked up.
14. Two rifles, one per hand: with a rifle drawn in the right hand and a second rifle carried on the back, reach back with the left hand and grab it: `... into the left hand beside the wielded one`. Each trigger fires its own rifle with its flash and sound, the pips over the left hand count the carried rifle's rounds, and `SECOND WEAPON` shows `TWO-HANDED (CARRIED) IN LEFT`. Fire the left rifle dry: the true reload turns to it. Check that the left rifle's shots alert soldiers and heat it as the wielded one's do.
15. Open the left grip at the back: the rifle docks on its socket again. Take it again, open the grip away from the body: `hold: dropped the second weapon from the left hand`, it falls with the swing, and `KEPT IN THE WORLD` counts it.
16. Holding a rifle in each hand (the second a carried one), holster the wielded right rifle at its shoulder. Half a second later: `hold: the second weapon in the left hand is now the wielded weapon`, the HUD shows the left rifle, and the right rifle is on the back. The left rifle does not move in the hand.
17. Two pistols of different kinds: carry a pistol on the hip, draw the pair's right pistol, and take the carried pistol into the left hand. Each fires its own.
18. Carry two extra rifles, fire one partly empty, and save at a safehouse (or wait for an autosave): `inventory: wrote the save sidecar` with `carried=2`, and `inventory.toml` beside `settings.toml` lists them. Quit to the menu and continue: `the sidecar matches the save`, then `respawning the carried weapons` and two `restored a carried weapon` lines; both rifles are on their sockets, the partly empty one with its magazine as it was. Watch for a rifle seen at the feet for a frame before it goes onto the back.
19. Load an older save, from before the extras: `the load carries nothing extra` with `another save's`, and none appear. `LAST LOAD` shows `ANOTHER SAVE'S SIDECAR`.
20. Edit `inventory.toml` into nonsense and load: a warning names the file, the load is clean, and `LAST LOAD` shows `SIDECAR UNREADABLE`.
21. With extras carried, die and respawn, and fast travel: note whether the log shows `the spawn system is resetting` for each, and whether the extras come back (`respawning ... origin=Reset`). Start a new game from the menu after a session with extras: none should come with it.
22. Let a soldier pick up a rifle you dropped, if one does: `... is with another character`, and `TRACKED WEAPONS` counts it with others. Take it back from his hands: it is the same entry (the same number in the debug UI).
23. For the pistols split in two, follow the checklist in [physical-weapons.md, "In the headset: separate pistols"](physical-weapons.md#in-the-headset-separate-pistols).
24. Start a mission that takes the player's weapons (identify one first) with extras carried: `the carried weapons are stashed`, they vanish from the body, and their sockets cannot be grabbed. When the mission gives the weapons back: `the carried weapons are back`. Note whether picking up a weapon during the mission brings them back early.

## Unverified

- Which missions use the graph script's weapon cache to take the player's weapons, whether every such mission removes the slots' weapons through it, and how the challenges strip and restore weapons.

- Which game states pass through the spawn system's reset (a death, a fast travel, the frontend), whether the post-reset event is always handled after the save data's load, and whether a new game loads player save data at all; the two-minute and same-character limits on the reset's record are a guard for the new game, not a finding.
- On which thread the game stores and loads the save data; the hooks take only the mod's own locks and read what the game's store reads.
- Whether a respawned weapon lying at the player's feet for the frame before it is adopted is seen, or collides.

- Whether a carried weapon fired from a hand, in no slot, plays its effects and sounds, heats, and alerts the AI as the slot's second weapon does.
- Whether the promotion's equip, which plays no draw act, leaves the upper body in a wielding pose, as the draw without act does.

- Whether the sockets' default offsets put a carried weapon beside the slot's weapon or through the body: the bones' frames are not read, so the offsets are in the character's frame and are a first guess.
- Whether anything of the game's (the weapon's own updates, its effects, its level of detail) expects a holstered weapon with a holder to be in that holder's inventory.
- Whether the AI's weapon search ever sees a carried weapon: it is holstered, so its pickup is off.

- Whether every load, death, and fast travel passes through the spawn system's reset, and whether the reset's pre-reset event reaches the local player's inventory in each.
- Whether a soldier picks up a weapon the player dropped, which the registry would show as held by another character.
- Whether a weapon the area clean-ups take out of the spawn system while it is pinned lingers in the world until the pin goes.
