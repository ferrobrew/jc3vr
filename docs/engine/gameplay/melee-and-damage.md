# Melee and damage

How the player's melee lands, the damage message every hit is made of, how a struck character reacts, and how a body is pushed. The aim side of melee (the melee target slot and its scoring) is in [aim-pipeline.md](aim-pipeline.md), and the input in [input.md](input.md).

## The damage message

Every hit is a damage message, [`DamageMsg`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg): who dealt it ([`m_Damager`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##field-m_Damager), a weak reference), where it struck and along which direction ([`m_Position`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##field-m_Position), [`m_Direction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##field-m_Direction)), the damage and the impulse it carries ([`m_Damage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##field-m_Damage), [`m_Impulse`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##field-m_Impulse)), its type ([`m_DamageFlags`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##field-m_DamageFlags)), the damager's faction, and what the damager was doing ([`Character::GetCurrentDamageSourceStateInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetCurrentDamageSourceStateInfo)).

The engine builds one on the stack per hit: the virtual table pointer, its smart pointers cleared, then [`DamageMsg::Clear`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##func-Clear). It fills the fields, hands the message to the target, and destroys it with [`DamageMsg::Destruct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageMsg##func-Destruct), which releases the references. The target is any physics game object, [`PhysicsGameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsGameObject), whose damage virtual [`OnDamage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsGameObject##vfunc-OnDamage) every kind overrides: a character takes the health and reacts, a rigid prop damages its damage controller, a vehicle damages its parts.

The type bits are a closed set of sources (bullets, explosions, impacts, fire, melee, and others). Only [`DMG_TYPE_MELEE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/damage::DamageFlags##flag-DMG_TYPE_MELEE) and the reset type are bound; the other bits are unverified.

## The player's melee

Melee happens in three steps.

1. The melee input task ([`NStateTask_InputMeleeTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_InputMeleeTask##func-Update)) runs on the melee input when the character can melee and the hammer ability is unlocked. It reads the melee target slot. With a target character, it asks whether the player's melee damage ([`Player::m_MeleeDamage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##field-m_MeleeDamage)) would kill it, picks one of the four melee stumbles or its killing variant ([`ACT_HITREACT_MELEE_STUMBLE_LEFT_0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable::ACT_HITREACT_MELEE_STUMBLE_LEFT_0) and its siblings), tests it with [`AnimatedModel::TryAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-TryAct), and queues it on the target. It then links the player to the target and queues the player's own attack act.
2. The attack animation sends a melee hit message, which [`Character::HandleTrackMessage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HandleTrackMessage) handles. With a linked character, it sends that character a melee damage message with no impulse: the stumble the input task queued is the reaction. With none, it finds the objects in a box in front of the chest ([`PhysicsSystem::CollideBox`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CollideBox)), sends each a melee damage message (a vehicle only once), and pushes each body forward by the player's melee impulse ([`Player::m_MeleeImpulse`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##field-m_MeleeImpulse)).
3. The struck character's damage virtual takes the health, plays the hit effects, and runs its hit reaction.

## The hit reaction

A character's `OnDamage` ends in its hit reaction (`CCharacter::HitReaction`, unbound and unverified). The reaction scales the message's impulse by a per-type factor (chosen by the lowest type bit set), adds it to an impulse it accumulates over a short window, and compares the result against the character's stumble and ragdoll thresholds: past the stumble threshold the character stumbles, and past the ragdoll threshold it goes to ragdoll. A message with no impulse leaves the reaction to whatever act the attacker queued, which is what the player's melee relies on. The thresholds and factors are per-character tuning read from data; their values are not established.

## Hit objects and run-time types

A physics query reports the owner of the hit body as a physics game object ([`CastRayInfo::m_GameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::CastRayInfo##field-m_GameObject)): for a character, the character itself. Its `CGameObject` subobject sits at [`PhysicsGameObject::GAME_OBJECT_BASE_OFFSET`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsGameObject::GAME_OBJECT_BASE_OFFSET), and the engine's run-time type test goes through it ([`GameObject::IsType`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##vfunc-IsType)) against a class's type id ([`Character::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-TYPE_ID)). The melee hit's box query tests its hits the same way.

A character's own weak reference ([`Character::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WeakThis)) is what the engine hands out as the damager, and [`Character::GetFaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetFaction) gives the faction it records.

## Pushing a body

[`hknpWorld::applyBodyImpulseAt`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-applyBodyImpulseAt) applies a linear impulse to a body at a point, changing both its linear and angular velocity, and wakes the body. It acts only on a dynamic body; a static or keyframed body is left alone, so it is safe on any hit. The point is in Havok space: the Havok world is simulated about a moving origin, and a game-space position less [`PhysicsSystem::m_AccumulatedShift`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##field-m_AccumulatedShift) is the Havok-space one. [`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay) takes and reports game-space positions; the box query reports Havok-space ones.

## Threading

A character's `OnDamage` reaches the HUD, the statistics, and the vocals, so it is main-thread code. The player's aim control update ([`PlayerAimControl::UpdatePostSim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-UpdatePostSim)), where the engine casts its own aim ray, runs from the player's post-camera update, a serial pass of the character update (`UpdatePassPostCameraPlayer_Serial`, unbound); which thread runs a serial pass is unverified.
