# Hand climbing

An empty hand whose body touches the static world, or a moving body large enough, grips it with the grip, and while it grips, the player's body moves against the hand's motion: pulling a gripping hand down lifts the body, pushing it away from a wall pulls the body toward the wall, and with both hands gripping the body follows their average. Letting go keeps the climbing speed, so a hard pull flings the player up and over. Near the top of a ledge whose top the hands grip, the mantle carries the body over it.

The code is `crates/jc3vr-physical/src/controllers/physical_hands/climb/`; the engine side is in [root-motion-and-proxy.md, "The proxy's support and the fall"](../../engine/character/root-motion-and-proxy.md#the-proxys-support-and-the-fall). It needs the hand bodies ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#hand-bodies)) and is always on under the controller scheme, with its tuning in the `climb` section of `crates/jc3vr-physical/tuning/climb.toml` ([tuning.md](../tuning.md)).

## Gripping

Each tick, the palms' tick casts the pickup's reach rays ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#picking-things-up)) from the body of each hand its body drives, where it was before the last step, out to `climb.reach_m` past the palm, and keeps the nearest hit on a body a hand climbs: a static body ([`hknpBody::IS_STATIC`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody)), or a moving one, either keyframed ([`IS_KEYFRAMED`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody)), such as a moving platform, or dynamic of at least `climb.min_moving_mass_kg` or infinite mass, such as a vehicle or a helicopter's skids. A character is never climbed.

A hand with a surface to climb in reach offers it to the grip as a fallback target (`GrabVolume::Climb`, [grip.md](grip.md)), which every other target in reach wins over: a grip near a crate on a ledge takes the crate, and a vehicle's closed door at its handle area opens ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)). A hand whose body only presses into something no reach ray finds offers nothing. The input tick asks the next physics step to grip, and a released grip asks it to let go.

The step takes the grip only for a hand whose body acts, on a body that is still one a hand climbs. It remembers where the player's body and the palm were and pins the hand's body where it is: from then on the body's goal is that pin, so the rendered hand stays on the wall while the controller moves. The hand plays `Event::ClimbGrip` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)), `Event::ClimbRelease` when it lets go, and `Event::ClimbFling` for a release that flings, in proportion to the fling's speed against `climb.max_fling_speed_mps`. While the hands move the body, each gripping hand sends `Sustain::ClimbStrain` in proportion to the climbing speed, full at `climb.tension_full_speed_mps` and not below `climb.min_tension`.

## Climbing

While a hand grips, the hand bodies' pre-simulation step moves the player. The palms' tick publishes each palm in the rig: the controller's tracking-space place, turned by the body's facing and scaled into world metres, but not carried by the head's world position. Each gripping hand would have the body where it was at the grip, moved back by how far the palm has since moved in the rig, and the hands' targets are averaged. The target depends only on the controllers, not on where the body or the view is now, so the climb is a closed loop on the body's position: a palm composed into the world from the head, whose place is smoothed and published a tick late, would chase the body it moves and could overshoot without end. A rig jump ([physics-substrate.md, "The rig's jumps"](../body/physics-substrate.md#the-rigs-jumps)) moves every palm at once without the hands moving, so it re-takes the grips from where the body is then.

The step gives the player's proxy the velocity that closes `climb.gain` of the gap within the step, capped at `climb.max_speed_mps`, through the proxy body's own velocity setter ([`hknpCharacterRigidBody::setLinearVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpCharacterRigidBody##func-setLinearVelocity)). The callback runs after every character's proxy update, so that velocity replaces the one the game computed, gravity and the stick's included, for the step. The step's collisions still apply: a body pulled into a wall stops at it, and one pulled up a wall slides along it.

On a face that leans back, a pull that lifts the body also carries it toward the face (`solve::slope_follow`), by the lift times the face's slope (the grip normal's upward share over its level share, at most one), so Rico rises along the face rather than straight up and away from the rock at head height. A vertical face, an overhang, and a ledge's top (a normal whose level share is below `climb.min_face_level`) add nothing.

A gap past `climb.break_distance_m` lets every grip go. A gripped static body that is gone or no longer static, or a gripped moving body that is gone, lets its grip go, since the step writes it as the proxy's ground every step.

## Moving bodies

A grip on a moving body remembers the body's transform when the hand took hold ([item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world)) and is held in the body's own frame from then on. Each step the gripped point and the hand's pin are carried as the body has moved and turned, so the rendered hand stays on the vehicle; the place the hands would have the player moves with the gripped point; and the velocity given adds the gripped point's own velocity (the body's linear velocity plus its spin about its centre of mass), averaged over the gripping hands and capped at `climb.max_carry_speed_mps`. So a player hanging from a moving truck travels with it, and climbs it as a wall. The support written for the game names the moving body as the ground, as dynamic, with the carried velocity as the surface's. A mantle onto a moving body adds the same carry, and letting go keeps the smoothed velocity, carry included, so the player leaves a vehicle with its speed.

A vehicle grip stays at the actual handhold, a helicopter's skid included ([stunt-positions.md](stunt-positions.md)). While a grip owns locomotion, the climb refuses the game's automatic entry into a vehicle's roof stunt area (`climb::native`), and only a completed mantle onto that vehicle hands over to its roof stunt position; a normal grapple can still enter the game's animated helicopter hang. While the game holds the player in a stunt position, the climb moves no character: a grip only pins its hand, and a release keeps no fling. A stunt transition the game makes discards older grips with `the game took the player into a stunt position`.

## Keeping the game on foot

Hanging from a wall, the proxy has no support, so the game would start a fall: the falling states, their animations, fall damage, and the parachute and wingsuit. The game decides from the proxy's support ([`PfxCharacterInstance::m_GroundInformation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxCharacterInstance##field-m_GroundInformation)), which the proxy's update finds before the physics step and the state machines read on the next tick before the proxy's next update. So while a hand grips, the step writes the proxy as supported on the gripped surface: level, at rest on the static world or moving with a moving body, with the gripped body as the ground body ([`m_GroundBodyId`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxCharacterInstance##field-m_GroundBodyId)). Every reader until the next proxy update sees a character standing: the ground-contact condition, the fall bookkeeping, and the ground raycasts, which then clear the fall distance instead of measuring it. The game keeps the player on foot, so the hands keep acting, the root-fixed view stays anchored ([locomotion.md](../body/locomotion.md#the-steady-view)), and nothing lands when the climb ends. The proxy's update finds the real support again each tick before the write, so the write never outlives the grip by more than a tick.

That this keeps the player out of every falling state rests on the readers the disassembly shows; which conditions Rico's state machine uses to start a fall is not established from the shipped data. If the game ever leaves on foot on the ground while a hand grips, the log warns (below).

## Letting go

The total velocity and the inherited point velocity are smoothed over `climb.fling_smoothing_s`. When the last grip opens, the proxy keeps the inherited part plus `climb.fling_scale` of the relative part, with only the relative part capped at `climb.max_fling_speed_mps`; a change of gripping bodies resets both filters together. A grip that ends for any reason other than opening does not fling. The game may take the velocity back before its own air handling has the player in the air, so for `climb.fling_carry_s` after the release the step keeps giving the proxy the fling's velocity as a throw under gravity (`GRAVITY_MPS2`), until the proxy finds support or a hand grips again.

## Mantling

A gripped surface whose normal is at least `climb.min_ledge_normal_y` upward is a ledge's top, unless the player stood on something when the hand took hold and the surface was no more than `climb.mantle_start_m` above the feet then: the floor, or a table gripped while standing, is never a ledge. The mantle takes over once the feet come within `climb.mantle_start_m` below the highest ledge gripped, while the hands pull the body up steadily (the smoothed climbing speed at least `climb.mantle_min_speed_mps` upward) and the feet are off what they stood on, or have climbed `climb.mantle_min_climb_m` since the climb started.

It lifts the feet straight up to `climb.mantle_clearance_m` above the grip, then carries the body over to `climb.mantle_forward_m` past it at up to `climb.mantle_speed_mps`, keeping the support written; once the feet are `climb.mantle_land_past_m` past the top it lets them down at `climb.mantle_land_speed_mps`. As soon as the proxy's own update finds it standing with the feet within `climb.mantle_landed_below_m` below the grip, or the feet come within `climb.mantle_arrived_m` of where the mantle carries them, every grip lets go without a fling and the player stands on the ledge, still in the on-foot states the climb kept, so no landing plays. A mantle that has not landed within `climb.max_mantle_steps` steps gives up and lets go.

## The body

Climbing is a case of its own in the procedural torso's hand-over table ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)): the torso stays procedural on the animation's hips, and a hit reaction, a vehicle, and the other cases above it in the table still win. The IK body takes the legs, each foot bracing on the face or hanging relaxed ([ik-body.md](../body/ik-body.md#feet-on-the-face)). The arms reach the gripping hands' bodies, which stay where they took hold. The body rig does not step while a hand grips ([rig.md, "Who moves the root"](../body/rig.md#who-moves-the-root)). When the climb ends in the air, the game's own fall takes over as after a jump.

## When the grips let go

Besides the grip itself, every grip lets go when the player is teleported, when the palms' tick stops publishing for more than `climb.max_stale_steps` steps or the hand input for longer than `climb.stale_after_ms` (the hands stand down: a drawn gun, a vehicle, a lost controller), when the player has no proxy, and when the body could not follow the hands. One hand's grip lets go when its body stops acting for more than `climb.max_stale_steps` steps, so a flicker of the mode or a dropped tracking sample keeps it. The hands act while the player falls on foot, so a hand flung at a ledge can grip it. The game's pause stops the steps and the palms' tick alike, so a grip holds through it.

## Threading

The grips are taken and ended, and the player moved, only in the hand bodies' pre-simulation callback, on the game thread outside the step and after the characters' proxy updates ([threading.md](../threading.md)). The palms' tick publishes the palms, the reach, and the player's place, and pulses the hands for the grips the step took or ended; the input tick asks for grips and releases. They share the climb's own leaf lock, and the step reads the hand bodies' poses before it is called, so the climb never takes the hand bodies' lock.

## Readouts and logs

The debug UI's Climbing feature opens with the `Moving climbs` and `Stunt positions` status rows, then shows whether the hands climb or mantle, the velocity given, the gap, whether the game judges the player in the air ([`Character::IsInAir`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInAir)), the counts of grips, releases, mantles, and dropped grips with the last reason, the last fling's speed, and what each hand reaches and grips.

The log has, at info level under `hand climbing:`, `a hand gripped the world` with the body, whether it moves, the point, and the normal, `climbing starts`, `mantling over a ledge`, `mantled onto the ledge`, `let go of the world` with the fling, `a grip was let go` with the reason, and `climbing ends`; once a second while climbing, `hand climbing: climb` at debug level. If the game leaves on foot on the ground while a hand grips, it warns `hand climbing: the game left on foot on the ground while a hand gripped the world` with the mode and the in-air verdict.
