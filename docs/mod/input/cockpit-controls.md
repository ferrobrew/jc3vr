# Flight controls and cockpit buttons

The physical controls of the driver's seat ([controllers.md, "Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight"](controllers.md#vehicles-mounted-guns-and-flight)) include the plane's flight stick (or its yoke, where the cockpit models one) and throttle and the helicopter's cyclic and collective, and the cockpit's buttons are worked by hand alongside the controller's. This covers how the sticks, the yoke, and the levers move and feel, and the horn, the nitrous, and the landing gear. The wheel and the handlebars are in [steering-wheel.md](steering-wheel.md). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/physical/`: `sticks.rs` for the sticks, `yoke.rs` for the yoke, `levers.rs` for the levers, `cues.rs` for their haptic cues, and `control_haptics.rs` one level up for the pulses. The switches are under `controllers.vehicles.physical`, and the placement and tuning are fixed in `PhysicalControlsTuning` (`controllers::vehicles::tuning`).

A hand holding anything of the loadout table's takes no stick, lever, or button, as for the wheel ([steering-wheel.md](steering-wheel.md)).

## The sticks

A stick is grabbed like every physical control: a grip press with the hand's grip within `PHYSICAL.grab_radius_m` of the stick's top takes it, and only releasing the grip lets go: a holding hand that drifts off the stick keeps moving it from where it is, however far it goes. The grab is relative. The hand's offset from the top is taken when it grabs, wherever the top is at that moment, so a hand that takes the stick off-centre, or takes it again while it is still springing back, moves it only by moving. The holding hand is drawn on the top at that offset.

The top follows the hand across the horizontal plane of the cockpit frame, up to `max_deflection_m` from centre (0.10 m for the flight stick, 0.12 m for the cyclic). The output is the deflection's share of the full deflection past `deadzone` (5%), rescaled so that it still reaches one, so the tremor of a resting hand flies nothing. Pushing forward pitches the plane's nose down and the helicopter forward, and pushing right rolls right.

Let go, a centring spring returns the stick: its deflection decays with the time constant `return_s` (0.12 s), and the stick keeps writing its value on the way back, so the plane comes back with it rather than snapping level. A thumbstick pushed out of its deadzone while the stick returns snaps the stick to centre, so the thumbstick flies at once. Before the spring, a released stick snapped to centre on the tick it was let go.

### The rudder

`physical.twist_rudder` (on) works the plane's rudder and the helicopter's yaw by the held stick's twist about its shaft, the cockpit frame's vertical: turning the top to the right yaws right. The twist is measured from the hand's orientation when it took the stick, so a wrist held at any angle starts from no rudder. Twists within 6° work nothing, and 35° gives a full rudder (`PHYSICAL.flight`). Tilting the wrist about the other axes does not count. While the stick is held with the twist on, it replaces the tables' rudder writes, as it replaces their pitch and roll; let go, the stick's rudder stops and the thumbstick's rudder stands. The cyclic's twist works the helicopter's yaw the same way, written as the helicopter's yaw actions rather than the plane's rudder; the thumbstick's yaw still works alongside.

## Where the aircraft controls sit

Where the cockpit's model has its controls in the airframe's body mesh, the controls are placed from the handles measured in that mesh and carried by the body part's live transform (`native::cockpit_asset`, fitted in `fit.rs`), so they ride the airframe: the utility helicopter, the fighter, the fighter-bomber, and the Urga U-17 Akrobat (`privatepropeller`), the twin-engine private plane with two yokes. The pilot's seat, read from the seated character's eyes in the part's space, picks which side's controls are offered. Elsewhere the controls fall back to the fixed specs, placed in the cockpit frame relative to the seated character's eyes. That frame turns with the cockpit's yaw, which is re-based at the seat lock by where the player's head points then, so fallback controls land in a different place from one entry to the next, and with the stick 0.75 m below the eyes they sit low in a cabin built round a yoke. The Akrobat was such a cockpit until its yokes were measured.

The heavy troop helicopter (`heavytroop_body`, the dark military one at 3879.97 s) is measured from its separate control parts, which lie in the body's space: a cyclic for each seat and one collective, on the centre console by the right seat. A helicopter's collective the mesh does not model, as the utility helicopter's (its body mesh has none) and the heavy troop's left seat's, is placed from that seat's measured cyclic, by the pilot's left thigh (`native::COLLECTIVE_FROM_CYCLIC_M`, a placement rather than a measurement). Otherwise an airframe the table does not know, or a lever its mesh does not model, falls back to fixed places from the pilot's eyes, which on the 14 November take put the utility helicopter's collective by the door and left the second helicopter's controls in the open (3791 s, 3879.97 s). The `driving position` log line names the cockpit's body part and gives the pilot's eyes in that part's own space.

A cockpit's controls can be set from the hands (`vehicles::physical::calibration`). The debug UI's Air feature has "Set the cockpit controls from the hands": press it, close the panel, and sit at the controls, and a three-second countdown ticks on both hands (it starts only at the controls, since the open panel holds the controllers). When it ends, the right hand's grip becomes the stick's (the cyclic, the flight stick, or the yoke's right horn, the yoke moving whole) and the left hand's the lever's (the collective or the throttle), in the cockpit part's own space, for that part and seat; both hands buzz, and the controls move there at once, without the check that they lie inside the part's bounds. Each capture is written to `cockpit_calibration.toml` in the configuration directory, beside the settings, keyed by the part and the seat (`utility_body#0`), and the file loads on start, so a calibration holds across sessions and its numbers can be read back into the table. "Clear this cockpit's calibration" returns the current cockpit to the table. A request lapses after a minute away from any cockpit's controls. The readout shows the current cockpit, whether it is calibrated, the countdown, the last capture, and the file's path. Only the merged cockpits the table knows take a calibration; a separate stick part's aircraft does not.

Opening the debug panel or otherwise standing down the vehicle controls resets an armed calibration countdown. Returning to the controls starts the full countdown again; the original request still expires at its normal deadline.

## The yoke

A plane whose cockpit models a control wheel offers the yoke in place of the flight stick (`ControlKind::Yoke`): so far the Akrobat. Either hand or both take it by its horns, and the yoke turns about its column for the roll and moves along the column for the pitch: turned clockwise it rolls right, and pulled toward the pilot it pitches the nose up. The grab is relative, as the stick's is: each hand's angle about the column and its place along it are taken when it grabs, so a hand taking the yoke while it is turned or springing back moves it only by moving; with both hands on it the yoke takes the mean of what each would make it. It turns up to 75° either way and travels 8 cm either way along the column (`PHYSICAL.yoke`), each axis's output past a 5% deadzone. Let go, it springs back to centre with the stick's time constant, flying the plane on its way. The holding hands are drawn on the horns, riding the yoke as it turns. The rudder stays on the thumbstick: a yoke has no twist to give it.

## The levers

A lever's handle follows the hand along its axis, the collective's up and the throttle's forward. The collective's position over half its travel is its value, `-1..=1`, with `detent` (10%) of the travel around neutral giving nothing, and it returns to neutral when it is let go. The throttle stays where it is let go and keeps writing its value. A trigger pressed while the throttle is parked flies the plane for as long as it is pressed, and the throttle takes over again, where it was, once the trigger is let go.

### The throttle's zero gate and reverse

The throttle's travel is split by a gate at zero (`PHYSICAL.flight`): the back third (`reverse_share`) is reverse, and the front two thirds are forward thrust from nothing to full. The throttle's rest, where the measured handle or the spec puts it, is the zero; its travel runs a third behind that and two thirds ahead. Pulled back to zero, the throttle stops there with a hard haptic stop; a hand that pulls on past zero by `zero_pull` (12% of the travel) takes it through the gate with a buzz into the reverse section, which is proportional, not a toggle: the farther back, the more reverse. Pushing forward through zero by the same distance takes it back out with the same buzz, and short of that the throttle waits at zero. Within `zero_snap` (6%) of either section next to zero it gives nothing, and a throttle let go there snaps to zero, so a hand that means zero does not leave a little thrust on. Reverse is what the gamepad's left trigger writes, so on the ground it brakes and then backs the plane up.

### The afterburner gate

The plane has no nitrous input of its own: its input reader ([`AirPlane::UpdateVehicleInputsFromActionMap`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::AirPlane##func-UpdateVehicleInputsFromActionMap)) feeds the nitrous whenever the thrust input is above [`AirPlane::NITROUS_THRUST_THRESHOLD`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::AirPlane) ([input.md, "The plane's thrust and nitrous"](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#the-planes-thrust-and-nitrous)). A throttle that ran straight to full would light the afterburner in the last stretch of every push. The throttle's forward half has a gate starting at 85% of its travel (`PHYSICAL.flight.gate_at`). Below the gate the thrust rises from the detent to just under the game's threshold; in the gate it is full, and the afterburner burns where the game allows it (the nitrous upgrade). Without the upgrade, the gate is simply full thrust. The gate's share is of the forward section.

## Haptic cues

The held sticks, the yoke, and the levers give their hand a cue of the vehicles category ([haptics.md](haptics.md)), each firing once as the control reaches it and re-armed only once the control has left it by a tenth of its travel, so a hand resting at a stop or wobbling about centre does not buzz:

- a bump (`Event::ControlStop`) when a stick reaches its full deflection, the yoke the end of its turn or its pull, or a lever the end of its travel;
- a tick (`Event::ControlNeutral`) when the collective comes back to its middle or passes it, and when a held stick or the yoke comes back to centre on an axis, so the hand finds centre without looking;
- a light tick (`Event::ThrottleStep`) at each tenth of the throttle's forward section (`thrust_step`);
- a thud (`Event::ThrottleZero`) when the throttle comes back to zero and stops at the gate;
- a buzz (`Event::ThrottleReverse`) when the throttle goes through the zero gate either way;
- a firm bump (`Event::ThrottleGate`) when the throttle goes into its afterburner gate and when it comes back out, with a little hysteresis on the way out.

One cue fires a tick: the zero gate's first, then the zero stop's, the afterburner gate's, a stop's, and a step's. The wheel's own lock and centre cues are in [steering-wheel.md](steering-wheel.md).

## Cockpit buttons

The horn and the nitrous are worked by hand (`buttons.rs`, with the fixed `PHYSICAL.buttons`). The nitrous is also on the dominant stick click; the horn is on no controller button by default (its `horn` action ships unbound), so the cockpit's horn is how it sounds; it also toggles a vehicle's magnet (`TOGGLE_MAGNET_1`), as the gamepad's left stick click does:

| Seat | Horn | Nitrous |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Car and boat, with the wheel | Push a free hand into the wheel's hub | Push a free hand into the button on the dash beside the wheel |
| Motorcycle, ATV, jetski, and duck, with the handlebars | The button | The button (with `physical.bars_twist` on, the bars' grips are the throttle and the brake, below) |
| Helicopter, with the collective | The button | Twist the collective's grip either way |
| Plane | The button | Push the throttle through its afterburner gate (above) |

A push button is a disc facing the driver: the horn is the hub, 35% of the wheel's radius across, and the nitrous button sits 12 cm right and 2 cm up from the wheel's right edge, 4.5 cm across, facing the driver as the wheel does. Both ride the wheel as it is fitted to the vehicle ([steering-wheel.md](steering-wheel.md)). A hand arms a button by coming to it from the front within its radius, and presses it by pushing on past its face by 2 cm; the button stays pressed, giving a centimetre before it lets go, until the hand comes back out or slides off its edge. A hand that reaches it from behind, as one through the dash does, or from the side, as one sweeping across the wheel does, presses nothing, and a hand holding a control presses nothing either, so the hands on the rim cannot sound the horn. The nitrous button is only on a vehicle with nitrous, the vehicles whose game HUD shows the nitrous meter: [`Vehicle::GetCurrentEffectiveNitrousUpgradeLevel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-GetCurrentEffectiveNitrousUpgradeLevel) is above none (the vehicle takes mods, its physics has the nitrous action, and the player has the nitrous upgrade for its class), read from the driven vehicle each input tick. Elsewhere the button is neither marked nor pressable, so the dash has no disc that does nothing. The vehicle's model has no button, so the mod marks it, but like the horn only once a hand comes in front of it, with "Nitrous" written above the marker (`hud::mark_labels`, from the marker's label); both buttons are ringed while a hand is in front of them and lit while pressed.

With `physical.bars_twist` on (off by default), the handlebars' right grip is the throttle, as a motorcycle's is, and the left grip is the brake, twisted the same way: rolling a grip's top forward, away from the rider (clockwise seen from the bars' right end), about the line across the bars, from the orientation the hand took it in, works it from nothing within 4° to full at 30°. It is off because on the 14 November take the player rolled the wrists 50 to 120 degrees, far past the full twist, the left wrist rolled with the right and braked against the throttle, and each regrab reset the reference, so the bike started and stopped at what felt like random; the triggers are the pedals. The throttle reaches the game as `ACCELERATE` and the brake as `REVERSE`, each alongside its trigger, the larger of the two winning, so the triggers still work with the hands on the grips; rolling a grip back closes it. `REVERSE` is what the left trigger writes, so the brake grip brakes a moving bike and, held once it has stopped, reverses it, as the trigger does. A grip clicks in the hand as it opens and ticks as it closes. Neither burns the nitrous: the nitrous is the dominant stick's click, as on every land vehicle. The collective's grip burns the nitrous while the hand holding it has twisted it 40° (well past the roll a wrist makes while it raises the lever) either way about the fore-aft axis, and stops once the twist comes back by 8°. The handlebars' and the collective's own motion is untouched by either: the bars turn by the grips' positions, and the collective moves along its axis.

A pressed horn holds `SOUND_HORN_SIREN` and `TOGGLE_MAGNET_1`, and the nitrous holds `USE_VEHICLE_MOD`, which the game reads as the nitrous while it is held and as the turbo jump when it is tapped ([input.md](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#land-vehicle-generic-car-and-motorcycle)), so a short press of the nitrous button is a turbo jump, as a tap of the gamepad's button is. Both need the vehicle's upgrades as the gamepad does; a vehicle with the turbo jump upgrade but not the nitrous one has no button, and jumps from the stick's click. Each press and twist clicks in its hand, and each release ticks. A hand pressing a button takes the flat palm's finger pose ([grip-poses.md](grip-poses.md)); a twisting hand keeps the pose of the control it holds.

### The landing gear

The game has no input for the landing gear. While the local player drives, [`Vehicle::UpdateLandingGearVariables`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-UpdateLandingGearVariables) chooses the gear's state itself on every update ([vehicle-parts.md, "Landing gear"](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-parts.md#landing-gear)), so a gear lever would have nothing to drive. Overriding the choice would mean taking over that update for the player's plane; it is not built.

## Settings

The switches are `controllers.vehicles.physical.sticks` (the cyclic and the flight stick), `physical.levers` (the collective with its nitrous twist, and the throttle with its afterburner gate), `physical.nitrous_button`, `physical.bars_twist` (the bars' throttle and brake grips), and `physical.twist_rudder`; the horn comes with the wheel (`physical.wheel.enabled`). They are in the debug UI's Vehicles feature, under Movement/vehicles. Everything else is fixed in `PhysicalControlsTuning`: per stick the top, the full deflection, the deadzone, and the centring time; per lever its neutral, axis, travel, and detent; the rudder and yaw twist's deadzone and full twist and where the gate starts; and the hub's share, the nitrous button's place and size, the press depth, the collective's twist, and the bars' grips' full twist.

## What is logged and shown

The input tick logs `the throttle went through its afterburner gate` and `the throttle came back out of its afterburner gate` at info level. The debug UI's Status page has a FLIGHT CONTROLS row in its VEHICLES section: `WAITING, NOT FLYING A PLANE` outside a plane's driver's seat, and in it whether the stick is held, returning, or free, the rudder, the throttle with `AFTERBURNER` while it is through the gate (or `THROTTLE BY TRIGGER` while the trigger has it), and the counts of cues and burns. The Vehicles feature's live status opens with the VEHICLES section's rows, and adds the written rudder and the last cue beneath them.

Each press of a button and each twist that opens a grip logs `the hand worked the horn`, `the hand worked the nitrous button`, `the hand worked the handlebars' throttle grip`, `the hand worked the handlebars' brake grip`, or `the hand worked the collective's twist grip` at info level, with the hand. The VEHICLES section's COCKPIT BUTTONS row names what the seat has (`HORN, NITROUS BUTTON` with the wheel, `THROTTLE AND BRAKE GRIPS` with the handlebars, `TWIST GRIP` with the collective, `WAITING, NONE IN THIS SEAT` in a plane), whether the horn or the nitrous is on, and the count of presses; out of the driver's seat it reads `WAITING, NOT DRIVING`.

## Unverified

- The Akrobat's yoke measurements against the drawn yokes in both seats, and the throttle's place by the centre pedestal, which the model does not have.
- The yoke's 75° turn and 8 cm travel against the plane's response, and the roll and pitch signs.
- The throttle's zero pull, snap, and step against the hand's feel, and that the reverse section's third is long enough to control reverse with.

- That the plane's afterburner lights through the gate with the nitrous upgrade, and that the dry throttle's top, just under the threshold, does not light it.
- That the rudder's sign is right: the top turned right yaws the plane right.
- The flight stick's throw, the centring time, and the twist's range against the game's own response.
- Whether the throttle's brake half (pulled back past idle) is the brake the plane wants on the runway.
- That a hand resting near the hub does not sound the horn while the driving animation sways the fitted wheel, and the hub's share against the drawn hubs.
- The nitrous button's place beside the wheel, which has no drawn button to match, in cars with the wheel on the right.
- The twist grips' engaging twist, and that twisting a bars' grip does not turn the bars.

## Headset checklist

1. In the Akrobat (the private twin-prop, `privatepropeller`), enter by the pilot's door and then again from the right door, shuffling across: both times the virtual yoke sits on the modelled pilot's yoke, not below it, and the throttle stands by the centre pedestal within reach. The FLIGHT CONTROLS row reads `STICK HELD` while either hand holds the yoke.
2. Take the yoke by one horn and turn it clockwise: the plane rolls right; pull it toward you: the nose comes up. Take it with both hands and fly: no jump at either grab. At the end of the turn and of the pull, a bump; coming back to centre on either axis, a tick. Let go: the yoke springs back to centre.
3. Take the throttle at zero and push it forward slowly: a light tick every tenth of its forward travel. Pull it back: at zero a hard thud, and it stays at zero while the hand pulls a little farther. Pull on deliberately: a buzz, and the plane reverses (on the ground it brakes, then backs up), more the farther back. Push forward a little: the throttle waits at zero; push on: a buzz, and forward thrust again. Let go just in front of zero: it snaps to zero and gives no thrust.
4. In a plane's driver's seat, take the throttle and push it forward: the thrust rises, and near the front a firm bump marks the gate. Through it, with the nitrous upgrade, the afterburner lights; the log has `the throttle went through its afterburner gate`. Pull back out: a click, and the afterburner goes out. Push on to the stop: a bump.
5. Let go of the throttle at half: the plane keeps that thrust. Press the right trigger: the trigger flies the plane; let go of the trigger and the throttle's thrust comes back.
6. Take the stick off-centre and fly: no jump at the grab; a small tremor does not move the plane; full deflection gives a bump, and bringing it back to centre a tick. Let go mid-roll: the stick and the plane come back level over a fraction of a second. Take the stick again while it returns: no jump.
7. Twist the held stick to the right: the plane yaws right, and the FLIGHT CONTROLS status row shows a positive rudder. In a helicopter, twist the cyclic to the right: the helicopter yaws right. With `flight.twist_rudder` off, twisting does nothing and the thumbstick's rudder and yaw work.
8. In a helicopter: the cyclic springs back the same way, and the collective clicks at its middle and bumps at its ends.
9. In a car, hold the wheel with one hand and push the other into the hub: the horn sounds with a click, and stops as the hand comes back out. Rest a hand on the hub without pushing, and sweep a hand across the wheel to take the far rim: no horn. With both hands on the rim, no horn.
10. With the nitrous upgrade, bring a hand right of the wheel: the button's marker appears with "Nitrous" above it, and goes when the hand leaves. Push it: the nitrous burns while it is held, and a short press is a turbo jump. The COCKPIT BUTTONS status row shows `NITROUS ON` while it is pressed. In a vehicle without nitrous (no nitrous meter on the game's HUD, or before the upgrade), there is no marker, no label, and pushing there does nothing (the COCKPIT BUTTONS row still names the button the settings offer). Check that the label reads at that distance and does not cover the wheel.
11. On a motorcycle and on a jetski, twist the right grip forward (its top rolling away from you) while riding: the throttle opens from a few degrees to full at about 30°, a click marks it, no nitrous burns, and twisting back does nothing. Twist the left grip forward the same way: the bike brakes, and held at a stop it reverses. In a helicopter, twist the collective's grip either way: the nitrous burns.
12. In a plane, check that the gear still comes down and goes up by itself.
13. Take the flight stick, the cyclic, or the collective and pull the hand well off it with the grip closed: the control stays held and keeps following the hand, and only opening the hand lets go, with `vehicle control: let go of the control` and the reason `grip released` in the log.
