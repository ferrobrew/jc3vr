# Decoupling HUD parts

The HUD renders into one texture shown on one floating panel ([hud.md](../hud.md)). Several VR features want a part of it somewhere else: the reticle at the aim point, the readouts on the hands and the wrist, the weapon selection cross at the steering hand ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)), and the world markers at their own depths. The HUD atlas does this: at each capture it moves the parts into their own cells of the HUD texture, outside the stage, and moves them back straight after, and each cell is drawn on its own quad. Markers that find no cell are cropped from the panel instead. How the engine captures and draws the UI is in [ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md).

## Why the atlas

Scaleform hands the renderer one snapshot per capture, a capture is safe only on the game thread inside `PreRender`, and the renderer consumes only the latest snapshot. That rules out the alternatives:

- Rendering the movie once per part, with only that part visible, needs one capture per part, and several captures in a row collapse to the last one. Toggling visibility on the render side without a capture leaves the renderer's cached bundles stale.
- A second movie instance kept in sync with the HUD would have to mirror invokes, display-info writes through handles into the root movie's objects, and engine-side tweens, and its ExternalInterface calls would land on the same native handlers twice ([scaleform.md](../../engine/scaleform.md#standalone-movies-crendertotextureui)). A part the mod rebuilds anyway is better as a mod-owned movie.
- The depth-split mechanisms tried for the HUD's depth (a multi-pass render, a time-multiplexed refresh, and a render-root partition) fail on capture timing or on structural edits to the render tree; [the post-mortem](../../issues/08-14-hud-overlays-and-depth.md#the-split-post-mortem) has the constraints they establish. The partition is deleted. One lead on its pause wedge was never followed: the engine's own off-screen render ([`RenderOffScreenTexturesImpl`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-RenderOffScreenTexturesImpl)) calls the manager's `Submit` before popping each target, and the partition's render did not.
- Cropping one render takes whatever else the game draws into the rectangle, cuts filters that spill past it, and is bounded by the HUD texture's sharpness. It remains the fallback for markers without a cell.

The atlas keeps one capture per frame on the game thread at the seam the engine already uses, so every part updates at the full rate. Its only edits are node-local display-info writes, undone before the next advance, so the display side, hit tests, ActionScript, the engine's tweens, and the next `Advance` all see the original layout, and the engine's own `Render` runs unchanged. A part in its own cell cannot be overdrawn by a marker or a centre notice. The glyph and filter caches see the same transform every frame, so they settle.

## The alpha of the HUD texture

The HUD texture holds premultiplied colour with coverage alpha, which the Scaleform HAL's blend states establish ([ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md#colour-alpha-masks-and-filters)). Compositing it as straight alpha would darken every partly transparent pixel a second time, so antialiased text edges, glows, drop shadows, and fading elements would come out dark. Every composite of a HUD-style texture blends premultiplied (`ONE, INV_SRC_ALPHA` on colour and on alpha): the panel quad (`crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/quad/mod.rs`, which the egui debug panel, the controls summary, the readouts, and the world markers share) and the desktop mirror's egui overlay (`crates/jc3vr-view/src/vr/mirror/mod.rs`). egui renders premultiplied too. The one quad shader that computes its own colour, the cursor dot (`cursor_ps.hlsl`), premultiplies its output. Anything that fades a quad in its shader must scale all four channels, not alpha alone.

## World markers

The code is `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/world_markers/`; the engine side of the markers is in [ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md#points-of-interest).

Classification. Inside [`HUDUI::UpdatePOIs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-UpdatePOIs) (bracketed by a detour in `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hooks/ui/hud.rs`), the `Get2DInfo` detour sees every point of interest's projection through the panel's view-projection. A marker the panel projection puts off screen but that lies inside the headset's field of view (both eyes' together, from the frame's located views, shrunk by the HUD tuning's `world_markers.fov_inset_deg`, or a symmetric `world_markers.fallback_half_fov_deg` without a headset; [tuning.md](../tuning.md)) is reclassified on screen and its stage position clamped into the safe area. The HUD then draws its on-screen icon, not an edge arrow, at that clamped spot. The panel's subtense is much narrower than the headset's view, so without this a marker plainly visible in the world would show as an arrow at the panel's edge. Markers outside the headset's view stay edge arrows on the panel. The final classification is recorded by the world point's exact bits.

Snapshot. At the capture seam, in the gameplay HUD mode and outside the cinematic and hidden HUD states, the record list is walked under its lock. For each shown record whose point of interest is alive (a strong reference is taken and released around the read), not invisible, and classified on screen, the snapshot keeps the point of interest's id, its world point (the transform's translation, which `UpdatePOIs` has just refreshed), its 2D offset, and the clip's origin from its display info, up to a fixed number of markers. The snapshot is published after the original capture, keyed by the movie's snapshot id, and the render side picks the layout whose key matches the displayed snapshot, falling back to the newest. One HUD update precedes every capture, so the classification and the snapshot always come from the same update.

Render. Stage coordinates map to the HUD texture through the show-all visible frame (`jc3vr_view::hud::stage_frame::StageFrame`): the texture is taller than the stage's aspect at the default gameplay aspect, so the stage spans the texture's width and sits centred vertically between two bands. On eye 0 each marker gets a crop around its clip origin (`CROP`, and `LABEL_CROP` for the labelled marker, which extends left for its label; both in `world_markers/mod.rs`), clamped to the texture; a marker in an atlas cell is cropped at its cell. After the UI has rendered into the HUD texture and before the panel draws, each crop is copied into its own slot texture and the crop's rectangle in the HUD texture is overwritten with transparent black, so the panel no longer shows it. Multipass repeats the crops and cuts on every dispatch and clears the HUD texture after each panel draw; under the single-pass collapse they run only on eye 0, both eyes reuse the slots and the cut panel, and the HUD texture is cleared only after eye 1. Each slot is drawn through the shared quad pass as a billboard at its marker's world point, facing the head with the head's up (so it rolls with the view like the panel), at the angular size per texel the panel shows the texture at, so the marker looks the size it did on the panel at any distance. The game's own distance shrink is already in the clip's art. Billboards draw farthest first, before the panel.

Depth. Each marker is drawn on the nearest visible surface along the line of sight to its point, so it never reads as farther than a wall it shows through, which would be a stereo conflict. The scene depth at each marker's pixel is the primary source: on eye 0, at the end of the eye's draw, a compute pass (`marker_depth_cs.hlsl`) reads the main depth buffer at each marker's projected pixel, keeping the farthest of a small cross of taps so that a surface counts only when it covers the whole footprint. The depth is linearised through the projection's z-row (`hud::depth::projection_z_row`) and read back asynchronously a couple of frames later; a readback whose dispatch is more than a few frames old is discarded. The linear depth and the marker's own clip `w` are both depth along the eye's forward axis, so their ratio is the fraction of the way to the marker where the line of sight meets a surface. Depth sees everything that was rendered (foliage, decals, LODs) that physics proxies miss. A marker with no recent depth result falls back to a physics raycast from the head (`crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/aim/marker_rays.rs`), cast on the game thread in the player aim's simulation update, outside the physics step, with the player aim's filter. The `world_markers` tuning sets the rest: a surface nearer than `surface_min_m` is ignored, so the player's own gun and hands and foliage brushing the head do not pull every marker onto them; a marker moves onto a surface only when the surface is nearer than `surface_engage` of the marker's distance, and leaves it only once the surface reaches `surface_release` of it, so a surface near the threshold does not make the marker hop; a blocked marker is placed `surface_pull_m` in front of the surface, never nearer than `min_distance_m`; and the drawn distance eases toward its target in log space with `depth_halflife_s`, capped at `max_distance_m`. Markers are drawn without a depth test, as in the flat game.

A crop, as opposed to a cell, takes whatever the panel drew in its rectangle (overlapping markers and centre notices), cannot be sharper than the HUD texture, cuts filters that spill past the rectangle, and, for markers reclassified on screen that pile up at the panel's edge, may include their neighbours.

## The atlas as built

The code is `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/atlas/` (the layout, the seam, and the render side), `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/anchored/` (the hand and wrist readouts), and `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/reticle.rs` (the aim part). Everything is behind `hud.atlas.enabled` (on by default; `HUD ON THE HANDS` on the pause menu's VR display page), and the readouts have no switches of their own. The fixed extents and capture scales are in `atlas/seam.rs`, and the placements in the HUD tuning's `anchored` tables.

Room. With the atlas on, the gameplay HUD's safe area hugs the stage: the atlas feeds [`ComputeSafeArea`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-ComputeSafeArea) the largest stage-aspect rectangle inside the texture (the stage-shaped safe-area input of `hud::binding`), so the bands the show-all scaling exposes above and below the stage hold nothing the HUD lays out. The render viewport, the panel's geometry, and the pointer mapping are unchanged, and menus get the full layout back because the safe-area input switches with the HUD mode. The capture seam acts only once the applied redirect reports the stage-shaped layout, so a mode change never puts cells over the edge containers.

Layout. `atlas/layout.rs` first gives the grenade readout (`MCI_safe_area_bottom_right.MCI_grenade_parent`) the right end of a band, then packs cells into the bands in shelves: the weapon readout (`MCI_safe_area_bottom_right.MCI_weapons_parent`), the heat readout (`MCI_safe_area_bottom_right.MCI_heat_container`), the weapon selection cross (`MCI_weapon_selection_wheel`, its extent from [`HUDUI::m_SelectionWheelBB`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##field-m_SelectionWheelBB) plus a margin), the tether readout (`MCI_safe_area_bottom_right.MCI_dual_tether`), the aim part, the labelled marker's wide cell, then as many marker cells as fit, each with a gutter for glows. A part taller than a band is captured scaled down to fit it; markers are never scaled, so their crops keep the size they have on the panel. The extents of the weapon, heat, and tether readouts around their clips' origins are fixed, read from the movie's shapes, so animations that reach past them (the heat widget's restricted-area and reinforcement banners) are cut at the cell. The grenade readout lays its icons out for the player's grenade capacity and parks the unused icons far to the right of the readout, off the stage; at the right end of a band they fall past the texture's right edge instead of into another cell. Its content lies below and to the right of its clip's origin, so its extents to the left and above are negative. The layout is a function of the configuration and the stage, so consecutive captures agree, and markers keep their cells across captures (`MarkerSlots`), so a frame that pairs a snapshot with a neighbouring capture's layout still shows each marker in its own cell.

Seam. In the [`MovieImpl::CaptureImpl`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::scaleform::MovieImpl##func-CaptureImpl) detour, in the gameplay HUD mode and outside the cinematic and hidden [`HUDUI::m_State`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##field-m_State) states, each readout's position and scale are read through its cached handle ([`GetDisplayInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::scaleform::ValueObjectInterface##func-GetDisplayInfo)), its parent container's placement converts the cell's stage position and scale into the parent's space, and one [`SetDisplayInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::scaleform::ValueObjectInterface##func-SetDisplayInfo) write selecting position and scale ([`DisplayInfo::VarsSet`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::scaleform::DisplayInfo##field-VarsSet)) moves it. Scale is in percent in both directions. The world markers' snapshot, taken under the point-of-interest list's lock, moves each marker that gets a cell to the cell's origin and records that origin as the marker's, so the render side's crop takes the cell. After the original capture every moved clip is written back with the exact values read; the markers are found again under the list's lock by their clip value, so a record removed during the capture is skipped (its pooled clip gets its position from the HUD before it is shown again). The atlas layout is published keyed by the capture's snapshot id, like the markers'. The selection cross is moved through the HUD's own value for it ([`HUDUI::m_SelectionWheelUi`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##field-m_SelectionWheelUi)), and only while the weapon wheel publishes a steering hand; otherwise it stays where the wheel or the game placed it on the panel. The readouts move only under the controller scheme, where something draws them; under the gamepad scheme they stay on the panel. A readout that is moved but not drawn (the hand untracked) is not shown at all.

Render. On eye 0 the capture matching the displayed snapshot is chosen (the newest one otherwise) and each moved readout's cell is converted to texels. After the UI has rendered the snapshot into the HUD texture and the world markers have taken their crops, each readout's cell is copied into its own texture and both bands are overwritten with transparent black, so the panel shows the stage only. Multipass repeats the copies and band clears on every dispatch; under the collapse they run once on eye 0, as for the markers. The readouts are drawn after the panel, being nearer than it. Redirect maintenance runs before the main [`UIManager::StartRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-StartRender) worker is queued, under the UI-deferred lock followed by the graphics context lock. [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw) consumes the matching scheduled job, with fallback maintenance when none ran for that dispatch. Only when the redirect is applied does it call [`UIManager::SyncRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-SyncRender) and [`UIManager::Submit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-Submit), outside both locks, before sampling, composition, or eye capture. The engine's late background, synchronization, and submission work remains, preserving the tracked world behind full-screen UI. None of this repeats the movie capture.

### The aim part

The aim part gathers the clips that mark the aim point into one cell: the weapon reticle group (`MCI_safe_area_center.MCI_reticles.MCI_weapon_reticle_group`), the grapple reticle's base, ghost, and attached clips (`MCI_reticle_grapple_base`, `_ghost`, and `_attached` in `MCI_grapple_reticle_group`), and the hit marker (`MCI_safe_area_center.MCI_inflict_damage`). It moves under either control scheme. The cell's origin stands for the aim point. The weapon reticle group and the hit marker stay at the screen centre in the movie, the point the camera aims through, so they are carried with their offsets from the centre container's origin. The movie places the grapple base, and its ghost while a wire is attached, at the projected grapple aim point, so each is carried with its own origin onto the cell's origin. The attached clip sits at the base until a wire attaches and then marks the attach point, so it is carried only while it sits at the base. The placements of the centre container, `MCI_reticles`, and the grapple group are composed to find each clip's parent in stage coordinates, and each clip keeps its own scale (the grapple reticle pulses) times the cell's scale. The extents (`AIM` in `atlas/seam.rs`) hold every weapon reticle shape and the hit marker at either height the movie gives it, so the cell is captured at about two thirds scale in the default bands. These clips are positioned by the movie on every HUD update, so they are left out of the drift count.

`hud/reticle.rs` draws the aim part after the panel, as a billboard that keeps the angular size the reticles have on the panel. It stands toward the aim point the grapple-reticle hook records ([hud.md, "HUD depth"](../hud.md#hud-depth)) at the smoothed aim depth (`hud::aim::current`), and along the head's forward while no aim point has been recorded for half a second. While the controller scheme holds the grapple reticle hidden, the recorded point follows the grapple's hand aim. Under the controller scheme the hit marker hides with the weapon reticle group, since it plays at that reticle, so the cell shows reticles only where the gaze aims: the gamepad scheme, and driver or mounted weapons with their hand aim off.

### The readouts

Each gun in a hand has an ammunition counter the mod draws (`hud::anchored::hand_ammo`): the gun's magazine and its user's reserve of its ammunition, in red when the magazine is empty, sampled each input tick of the weapon handling. Each hand holds a weapon of its own under the two-handed inventory, and the game's weapon readout (`MCI_weapons_parent`) shows only the wielded one, and nothing in the seats whose update skips the wielded weapon. The counter is therefore drawn for every gun, the wielded one included, and the game's readout keeps its atlas cell, which takes it off the panel, but is not drawn, so two guns read alike. A counter stands over the middle of the gun as drawn this frame (the gun's render-time matrix, `controllers::hands::late::rendered_guns` in the body crate, applied to the middle of its model's box): it rises along the gun's up axis until its lower edge is `anchored.ammo.gun_clearance_m` above the box's highest point however the gun is turned, so it sits over a rifle's receiver rather than over the arm behind the grip. Without a drawn matrix or a model box, it rises from the hand's grip until its lower edge is `anchored.ammo.clearance_m` above it. Either way its text runs along the gun's own right and up axes, so it turns with the rendered gun, with no view-facing turn and no follow filter. It is `anchored.ammo.width_m` wide with the height of its texture's aspect. Like every HUD quad it is drawn without a depth test and placed clear of the hand and the gun, since a readout the scene's depth could hide would vanish into the gun when held near the face, or into a wall the gun is pushed against. Raised to the eye to sight along it (the gun hand's grip within `anchored.sight_reach_m` of the eye and its aim within `anchored.sight_cone_deg` of the gaze), the gun would put the counter into the sight picture, so the counter fades out over `anchored.sight_fade_s` while the gun stays raised. The pistols are separate weapons, one magazine each, so each pistol has its own counter; the game's own dual-wield pair, where it is kept, is one weapon with one magazine, which both hands' counters show.

The tether readout is the game's own count of tethers in use out of the most the grapple holds, with its retract animation; the movie shows it while tethers are attached and fades it out after they are released, and the quad carries that fade. It sits `anchored.tether.back_m` back along the off-hand's forearm from Rico's wrist, where the grapple fires from (the grapple ray's origin, the aim tuning's `ray.grapple_origin_m`, is in the same wrist frame), raised until its lower edge is `anchored.tether.clearance_m` above the forearm, standing upright and facing the eye. The movie also builds a list of the tethers with their tension in `MCI_dualtether_stage` beside it, but holds that stage invisible, so the atlas leaves it alone.

The selection cross is drawn where the steering hand was when the wheel opened, pulled a little toward the head so the hand does not cover it, and held there relative to the head (so it travels with the player but not with the hand that steers it), facing the head. After the wheel closes it stays there and fades out over the cross's own hide animation.

The wrist readout sits on the inside of the off-hand's forearm, toward the elbow, facing out of the palm, read with the palm turned up; the controls summary sits on the back of the same wrist, so the two never face the head together. It stacks the health bar over the heat readout's cell, and the grenade readout's cell below that. The grenade readout is a narrow column of icons, so it is drawn at a fixed height (`anchored.wrist.grenades_height_m`) with the width of its cell's aspect. The health bar is the mod's own (`anchored/health.rs`): the player character's health ([`Character::GetHealth`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##vfunc-GetHealth)) over its [`m_MaxHealth`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_MaxHealth), sampled at the capture seam (the HUD's own health update reads the same on that thread), painted on the CPU into a small premultiplied texture when a texel changes, with a trail that holds where the bar stood before a hit and then drains. It is the only health readout in VR, since the game's own is the full-screen vignette, which the floating HUD suppresses. It shows on a glance (the controls summary's glance, with its dwell, linger, and hysteresis) and also while health is low, for a few seconds after damage, and while the player has heat ([`HeatManager::m_Level`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/heat_manager::HeatManager##field-m_Level)). The panel fades with the summary's fade, through the quad pass's fading pixel shader, which scales all four channels so the texture stays premultiplied.

### Hardening and cost

Pause and the comm-link map put the HUD in a hidden state or full-screen mode, so nothing moves; the history is cleared and the render side drops its frame, so a stale cell is never drawn. Cutscenes run in the cinematic state and are skipped the same way, and the mod's clean view at the everything scope hides the readouts. Every move is undone within the same capture, so nothing persists across a pause, a death, or a vehicle entry; the game's own timers decide what the readouts show in vehicles and the wingsuit. The debug UI counts moves, restores, failures of either, and drift (a moved clip found somewhere other than where its last restore put it), with the layout's snapshot match, the bands, each readout's cell and scale, and the readouts' hand, health, heat, glance, and fade.

Per capture the atlas costs one display-info read and two writes per readout, one write and one write-back per marker in a cell, and a second walk of the point-of-interest list; per dispatch in multipass, or once on eye 0 under the collapse, up to six small region copies and two band clears; and per eye up to seven extra quads. No capture, render pass, or render target is added to the UI's own render.

### Limits and unverified assumptions

A marker that leaves the list plays its hide fade in the band, which is cleared, so it vanishes rather than fading. Occluded markers are neither depth-tested nor dimmed. The explosive reticle has no cell. Unverified: that [`ClampToScreen`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-ClampToScreen) keeps edge arrows inside the stage-shaped safe area (if it does not, edge-clamped markers wander into cells, and the `Get2DInfo` hook can clamp them to the stage explicitly), that nothing in the HUD's ActionScript lays out against the visible frame during gameplay or reads render-side bounds of a moved part, and the placement and size defaults, which need the headset: that the counter reads at arm's length, sits over a rifle and a pistol without covering the sights or the other hand on a handguard, stays steady when the gun is swung, and stays legible over two guns held close together.
