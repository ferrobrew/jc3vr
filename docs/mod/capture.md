# Session capture

A recording's video is made from the eye views inside the game: the payload draws both eyes side by side into one frame on the GPU, reads it back without stalling, and streams it to a capture host, which encodes it with the default output's audio into the take's folder. The capture host is the injector, which stays running after it injects, on Windows and under Proton alike; on Linux the relay can take the stream instead and encode it with the host's own ffmpeg. With the in-process capture switched off, the relay records the desktop mirror window from the screen with gpu-screen-recorder, as every take did before. The payload also writes a per-frame pose sidecar that lines up with the video. The payload side is `crates/jc3vr-tooling/src/recorder/`, the injector's host is `injector/src/capture/`, the relay is `scripts/capture_host.sh` and `tools/capture/relay.py`, and the post-processing tool is `tools/capture/reframe.py`.

## What the video shows

A streamed take (the default, `recorder.in_process`) shows both eyes as the headset receives them, left eye on the left, each scaled to the configured width (`recorder.eye_width`, 1920 by default) with its own aspect kept. A 1920-wide eye holds a 1920x1080 crop of one eye with the eye's full height around it, which is everything the mirror window used to show and the 830 to 1050 pixels of height it cut. For the headsets in use that makes a frame of about 3840 by 1900 to 2140: 3840x2134 for the Index at its recommended eye size, 3840x2054 for the Quest 3, 3840x1908 for the Bigscreen Beyond. The other arrangement (`recorder.arrangement`, "Left eye, right at half size" in the debug UI) keeps the left eye at the full width and puts the right eye beside it at half size, at the top, with black below: 2880x2134 for the Index, about 40 percent fewer pixels, for a machine that cannot read back or send the full pair. The mirror window plays no part, so the desktop cannot bleed into the footage and the window can be anywhere or minimized.

The eyes are the final images, after the floating HUD, the panels, and the in-world overlays are drawn into them, so the clean view ([hud.md](hud.md)) decides what of the mod's own drawing is in the footage: at the everything scope it takes the HUD and the in-world overlays out of the eye images, and with them out of the video. The desktop scope affects only the mirror window, so it changes nothing in a streamed take.

A mirror take shows the mirror window at the window's size, recorded from the screen ([vr-runtime.md](vr-runtime.md#the-desktop-mirror)). The mirror view therefore decides what is recorded: a single eye filled into the window, or the stereo pair, toggled with F10. The mirror must be on (`vr.mirror`) and the window must be visible on screen for the whole take, on the active workspace and wholly inside its output, since the recorder captures a fixed region and records whatever is drawn there; a window moved, resized, or covered during a take spoils the footage. At the desktop scope the clean view keeps the flat debug overlay off the window, so it stays out of the footage.

## The in-process capture

The render thread does the minimum that needs the engine's immediate context. In `render_engine_post_draw`, on the frame's last near dispatch, once both eyes' captures hold the frame, `capture_frame` checks the pacing and, when a stream frame is due, draws a fullscreen pass with `i420_pack_ps` that samples each eye's capture into its rectangle of the frame and converts the frame's display-referred RGBA into planar 4:2:0 YUV (BT.709, limited range) packed into a single `R8` texture of `width x height * 3 / 2` (`crates/jc3vr-tooling/src/recorder/convert/mod.rs`). Each footprint is four bilinear taps, so the downscale from the render size averages every texel under it. The packed texture is copied into a ring of three staging textures, and the staging texture written two captures earlier is mapped with the no-wait flag. By then its copy has completed, so the map costs no GPU stall; if it has not, the map refuses and the previous image is repeated. The mapped rows are copied into a pooled buffer and queued to the take's video writer. While a streamed take runs, both eyes' captures are copied every frame even where the eyes go straight to the headset's swapchain, since the capture reads them.

The stream holds a constant rate by the wall clock (`recorder.frame_rate`, 30 fps by default; `crates/jc3vr-tooling/src/recorder/pacing.rs`). The game renders at about 40 fps in the headset, so most frames owe one stream frame and some none; a frame that owes none is not captured at all, which spares its readback. After a slow frame the extra stream frames repeat the previous image. A frame the writer's queue cannot take is not lost from the count: its stream frames are owed to the next frame that is queued, as repeats, up to a second's worth, beyond which they are given up and the video runs short of the take. The stream is therefore as long as the take was, which keeps the host's audio, recorded against the same clock, in step with it.

The writer (`crates/jc3vr-tooling/src/recorder/stream/mod.rs`) receives the connection from the take's thread once the host has confirmed the take, writes the `yuv4mpegpipe` header and then each frame, a repeat being the previous image's bytes again. The stream is self-describing and every frame is the same size, so a repeated frame cannot desynchronise the host's parser. Each stream frame gets a line in `frame_map.jsonl`: `stream_frame`, its number in the video, `frame`, the take frame ordinal of the image it shows (the sidecar's `frame`), and `repeat`, whether it repeats the previous stream frame's image. When the take ends, the writer sends what is queued within five seconds and closes its half of the connection, which is the stop.

The readback is the cost to watch. A side-by-side frame for the Index is 12.3 MB, 369 MB/s at 30 fps through the staging copy, the row copy on the render thread, and the loopback socket; the half-size arrangement is 9.2 MB a frame. The Video feature shows frames captured, sent, repeated, dropped, given up, and queued; dropped and given-up frames growing during a take mean the host or the socket cannot keep up, and the narrower eye width or the half-size arrangement is the lever.

## The control protocol

The payload connects to the capture host over TCP (port 47172 by default) and speaks a line protocol (`crates/jc3vr-tooling/src/recorder/protocol/mod.rs`, and the injector's and the relay's module documentation). Each message is one line of UTF-8 JSON, at most 64 KiB. Either side ignores a message of a type it does not know, and a host refuses a start that names another protocol or a version it does not serve. A change that an old peer cannot read takes a new version number.

A streamed take is version 2:

- The payload opens with `{"protocol": "jc3vr-capture", "version": 2, "type": "start", "take": <stamp>, "folder": <path or null>, "payload_folder": <path>, "window": {...}, "video": {"format": "yuv4mpegpipe", "width", "height", "frame_rate", "pixel_format": "yuv420p", "color_space": "bt709", "color_range": "limited", "layout": "side_by_side" or "left_with_half_right", "eyes": [{"eye", "x", "y", "width", "height"}, ...]}}`. `folder` is the take's folder as a host outside the game's Windows environment names it ([sessions.md](sessions.md#the-sessions-root)), null on native Windows; `payload_folder` is the folder as the payload names it, which the injector uses. The take's thread connects only once the render thread has captured the first frame, which fixes the size; a take that never captures one (no VR session) says so and has no video.
- The host answers `{"type": "started", "file": <path>, "encoder": <ffmpeg encoder>, "audio": <what it records the audio from, or "none: <why>">}` once its encoder runs, or `{"type": "error", "message": <string>}` and closes.
- After `started`, everything the payload sends on the connection is the `yuv4mpegpipe` stream. The payload ends the take by closing its writing half; the host lets its encoder finish and answers `{"type": "stopped", "exit_code": <integer or null>}` once the file is finalized. During the take the host may send `{"type": "audio", "state": <string>}` when its audio capture changes, or an error when its encoder fails.

A mirror take is version 1:

- The payload opens with `{"protocol": "jc3vr-capture", "version": 1, "type": "start", "take": <stamp>, "folder": <path or null>, "window": {"title": <string or null>, "client_size": [w, h] or null}}`, `window` being the game window's title and client size as the payload reads them.
- The host answers `{"type": "started", "file": <path>, "video_start_unix_ms": <number or null>, "region": {"x", "y", "width", "height"}, "output": <name or null>}` once it records, or an error and a close. It may also send an error and close during the take, when the screen recorder exits on its own.
- The payload ends the take with `{"type": "stop"}`, or by closing the connection. After a stop line the host answers `{"type": "stopped", "exit_code": <integer or null>}` once the video file is finalized.

The injector serves only version 2 and refuses a mirror take with a message saying to turn the in-process capture on or run the relay; the relay serves both.

## The payload side

F1 (or the Video feature's button) starts a take. The payload creates the take's folder, opens `frames.jsonl` in it (and `frame_map.jsonl` for a streamed take), and starts the take's thread, which connects with retries every half second until the host listens, sends the start line, and reads the host's answers without blocking. The connection is retried only until the first one succeeds: a host that refuses the take or drops it ends the video for that take, and the error says why, while the sidecar keeps being written until the take stops. The host has 30 seconds to confirm, since the injector probes its encoders on the first stream of a size. At the stop the thread lets the video writer finish the stream (or sends the stop line for a mirror take), flushes the sidecar, waits for the host's confirmation (30 seconds for a streamed take, five for a mirror take), and exits. A take that never reached a host finishes with an error saying it has no video.

A streamed stop closes the connection's writing half after the writer finishes, even if the host has not yet confirmed startup. A take with no frame sent reports that failure before waiting for finalization. This lets a delayed host observe EOF and complete its start and stop replies without waiting for video that will not arrive. A disconnect or deadline expiry without a `stopped` reply reports unconfirmed finalization as an error.

The sidecar is one JSON line per record, in the order the records were made:

- A frame line per rendered frame, written from `render_engine_post_draw` on the frame's last near dispatch, when every eye image of the frame is final: `frame` (the take's frame ordinal, from zero), `unix_ms`, `game_run_time_us`, `predicted_display_time_ns`, and `views`, both eyes' raw located pose (position, orientation as `x, y, z, w`) in the runtime's LOCAL space and field of view as `left, right, up, down` angles in radians. Outside a session `views` and the display time are null.
- For a mirror take, a mirror line whenever the mirror's layout changes, and at the first mirrored frame of the take: `frame` (the frame it applies from), `unix_ms`, and `mirror`, holding `view` (`Left`, `Right`, or `StereoPair`), the window's client size `window`, and `draws`, each eye's image as `eye` and `rect`, the whole eye image's rectangle `x, y, width, height` in window pixels, in on-screen order. A single eye's rectangle extends past the window where the mirror crops it.
- A take marker line (F2, the director's hotkey, Mark in the Video feature, or left stick click then right A in the headset): `frame`, the frame it falls before, and `marker`, holding its session ordinal `index`, the local `stamp`, `unix_ms`, `game_run_time_us`, and `label`.
- For a mirror take, a present line for each present of the mirror: `frame` (the take's frame ordinal at the present, so the last rendered frame is one before it), `unix_ms`, `present`, the mirror's present counter since the payload loaded, and `stamped`, whether the frame stamp drew it into the window.

A reader tells the kinds apart by the `marker`, `mirror`, and `present` keys, which frame lines never have.

The Video feature in the debug UI holds the switch ("Capture the eye views in process"), the width of each eye, the arrangement, the frame rate, and the capture host's address and port; they apply to the next recording. Its readout shows the host's state, the encoder and the audio the host reports, the stream's size, and the frame counters above.

## The frame stamp

While a mirror take runs, the mirror draws its present counter into the window's bottom-left corner as a strip of eight-pixel blocks (`crates/jc3vr-view/src/vr/mirror/stamp.rs`): a white start block, then the counter's low sixteen bits, least significant first, white for a one and black for a zero, then a black end block. The blocks are cleared straight into the back buffer with `ID3D11DeviceContext1::ClearView` rectangles, sized so they are square on the window whatever the back buffer's size, after the eye images and the overlay and just before the present. The sidecar's present lines carry the same counter, so a run of identical video frames can be read: one stamp held across several video frames is the recorder or the compositor repeating a present, while consecutive stamps on identical images are the mod presenting a repeated eye image. It is on by default (`vr.mirror_frame_stamp`, the "Frame stamp while capturing" switch under the desktop mirror in the debug UI's VR feature) and draws only while a mirror take runs, so the window is clean otherwise; a streamed take has `frame_map.jsonl` instead. Reading the stamp out of a video is not automated yet; the blocks survive the default bitrate, but that is unverified on a real take. With no recording running, markers go to `markers.jsonl` in the session root instead, one JSON object per line with the same marker fields. Either way, while a tracking recording or its lookback collects, the same marker is also written to the tracking timeline as a `take_marker` event with the same fields ([testing/recorder.md](testing/recorder.md)), so the copies join by `index` and `stamp`.

## The injector's capture host

After it injects, the injector listens on the capture port on loopback and serves streamed takes one at a time until the game exits (`injector/src/capture/`). `--no-capture-host` makes it exit after injecting instead, and `--capture-port` moves it. When the port is taken, another capture host is already serving (the relay, started before the injection) and the injector leaves the takes to it.

For each take it reads the start line, creates the take's folder from `payload_folder`, and starts the ffmpeg beside it (`ffmpeg.exe`, or the executable `JC3VR_FFMPEG` names, or one on `PATH`) through `ffmpeg-sidecar`, reading the stream from its standard input and writing `video.mkv` into the folder. Matroska is written as it goes, so a take cut short by a crash is playable up to the cut. Closing ffmpeg's input at the stop lets it drain its encoder and finalize the file, and its exit code goes into the `stopped` answer. ffmpeg's warnings and errors go to the injector's log.

The video encoder is the first of `h264_nvenc`, `h264_amf`, `h264_qsv`, `h264_mf`, `libx264`, and `libopenh264` that ffmpeg lists and that encodes a three-frame probe at the stream's size. ffmpeg lists the encoders it was built with, not the ones the machine can run, hence the probe; the candidates are probed at once, and the choice is cached per stream size for the injector's run. `JC3VR_CAPTURE_ENCODER` names one encoder to use instead. The bitrate is about 0.12 bits a pixel, about 30 Mbps for the Index's side-by-side frame at 30 fps, with a keyframe every two seconds and BT.709 limited-range colour tags.

`scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.sh` (or `scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.ps1` on Windows) stages a pinned, checksum-verified LGPL ffmpeg build from BtbN's FFmpeg-Builds beside the built injector, with its licence as `ffmpeg-LICENSE.txt`. The LGPL build has the hardware encoders and OpenH264 but not libx264, which is GPL, so on a machine with no working hardware encoder the fallback is OpenH264; an ffmpeg with libx264 put in its place is used as it is. Under Wine, ffmpeg.exe reaches no GPU encoder (the injector says so at start), so the injector's host encodes in software next to the game, which a 3840-wide stream at 30 fps is likely too much for; on Linux the relay is the better host.

The audio is a WASAPI loopback recording of the default output device, fed to ffmpeg as a second input over a loopback TCP connection and muxed as stereo AAC. Loopback records everything the output plays, not the game alone. The capture opens on a thread of its own before ffmpeg starts and begins once ffmpeg connects to it. Loopback delivers nothing while the output is silent and can deliver late, so each packet's capture time places it on the take's timeline: a gap becomes silence, a packet overlapping silence already written has the overlap skipped, and a timestamp more than two seconds off is taken as contiguous (`injector/src/capture/audio/timeline.rs`). Wine implements loopback for its PulseAudio driver from Wine 9.12, where it records the default sink's monitor; it worked under Wine 11 in testing, and Proton is unverified. A device that does not open leaves the take without audio, and the `started` answer and the log say why; one lost mid-take is reported with an `audio` message and the rest of the take is silent. `JC3VR_CAPTURE_AUDIO=none` records no audio.

## The relay

`scripts/capture_host.sh [fallback output dir] [-- extra gpu-screen-recorder args]` runs the relay on Linux. For each connection the relay reads the start line and creates the folder (a stamped folder under the fallback directory, `captures/` by default, when the start names none). It serves one take at a time and listens again after each, so one host serves every take of a session. Ctrl+C stops the relay, finalizing the take in progress; the recorder runs in its own session so the interrupt does not cut it off first. Start it before injecting, so it holds the port and the injector leaves the takes to it.

A streamed take is encoded with the host's ffmpeg (`JC3VR_FFMPEG`, default `ffmpeg` on `PATH`), using the first of NVENC, VAAPI, and the software encoder for the configured codec (`JC3VR_CAPTURE_CODEC`: `hevc_nvenc`, `hevc_vaapi`, or `libx265` for the default HEVC) that encodes a probe at the stream's size. The bitrate scales with the stream, about 20 Mbps of HEVC for the Index's side-by-side frame at 30 fps, unless `JC3VR_CAPTURE_STREAM_BITRATE` sets one in kbps. The audio is the default sink's monitor through ffmpeg's PulseAudio input (`JC3VR_CAPTURE_AUDIO`, a PulseAudio source name, `default_output` for the default monitor, or `none`). The relay copies the stream into ffmpeg until the payload ends it, then closes ffmpeg's input and waits for it to finalize.

When a streamed take has live audio, ffmpeg uses `-shortest` to end the mux when the video stream reaches EOF. The PulseAudio monitor can continue indefinitely; finalization does not wait for that input to end.

A mirror take finds the game window, runs gpu-screen-recorder on the window's region with the default output's audio, writing `video.mkv` into the folder, and waits for the recorder's first frame before confirming the start. At the stop it sends the recorder SIGINT, waits for it to finalize the file, and answers.

After each take, including one finalized by Ctrl+C, the relay runs `scripts/rerun_take.sh` on the take's folder in its own session. The export runs alongside the next take and finishes even if the relay has stopped. The script waits until the tracking recording's size has held for a few seconds, because the payload stops the video and the tracking writer together. It then writes `take.rrd` with the take's video beside the recording, and logs to `rerun.log` in the folder ([testing/rerun.md](testing/rerun.md)). `JC3VR_CAPTURE_RERUN=0` turns the export off, and the script can be run by hand on any take's folder.

The relay finds the window with `niri msg --json windows`: the window whose title equals the title the payload sent (`Just Cause 3` when it sent none), falling back to the Proton app id `steam_app_225540`. Several matches narrow by that app id, then by a window size equal to the payload's client size, then by focus. The region is the window's tile position in its workspace view plus the window's offset in the tile, offset by the output's logical position from `niri msg --json outputs`. niri reports the tile position for floating and tiled windows alike, so both work; the relay refuses a window whose workspace is not the active one on its output, and one that is not wholly inside the output (a tiled column scrolled partly off screen). The region is in logical pixels, which match the screen's pixels only on an output at scale 1; the relay warns on a scaled output. It refuses with a message naming the reason when the window is not found, several windows match, the window is off screen, niri or gpu-screen-recorder is missing, or the recorder exits at start, and the message reaches the in-game status.

A mirror take's video is HEVC at a constant 10 Mbps by default (`-k hevc -bm cbr -q 10000`), about 1.1 GB for fifteen minutes of 1080p60. gpu-screen-recorder's own default is constant quality, which with H.264 at the `high` preset ran to about 36 Mbps on the game, 4.8 GB for fifteen minutes. AV1 at the same bitrate looks at least as good where the GPU can encode it; `gpu-screen-recorder --info` lists the codecs the GPU offers (the capture host's NVIDIA card lists H.264, HEVC, and AV1). A static desktop comes out well under the bitrate, so a short test clip of the desktop says little about the size of a game take.

The relay removes `__EGL_VENDOR_LIBRARY_FILENAMES`, `DRI_PRIME`, `VK_DRIVER_FILES`, and `VK_ICD_FILENAMES` from the recorder's and ffmpeg's environment. A terminal wrapped to render on another GPU passes them to everything it starts, and with them set gpu-screen-recorder finds no card to capture from.

The environment overrides are `JC3VR_CAPTURE_PORT` (default 47172, the payload's default), `JC3VR_CAPTURE_FPS` (a mirror take's frame rate, default 60), `JC3VR_CAPTURE_CODEC` (`hevc` by default, `av1`, or `h264`), `JC3VR_CAPTURE_BITRATE` (a mirror take's constant video bitrate in kbps, default 10000, or 0 for constant quality), `JC3VR_CAPTURE_STREAM_BITRATE` (a streamed take's, default 0 for scaled to the stream), `JC3VR_CAPTURE_QUALITY` (gpu-screen-recorder's quality preset for constant quality, default `high`), `JC3VR_CAPTURE_AUDIO` (above, default `default_output`), `JC3VR_FFMPEG`, `JC3VR_CAPTURE_WINDOW` (a niri window id to record instead of looking the game up), and `JC3VR_CAPTURE_REGION` (a `WxH+X+Y` region to record without asking niri, for other compositors).

## Files

A recording's files share its folder, `recordings/<stamp>/` in the session folder ([sessions.md](sessions.md)):

- `frames.jsonl`: the payload's sidecar (above).
- `frame_map.jsonl`: a streamed take's record of each video frame's source frame (above).
- `video.mkv`: the video. A streamed take's is H.264 (from the injector) or the relay's codec at the stream's size and constant frame rate, with the default output's audio as stereo AAC where it was recorded. A mirror take's is the screen recording at the window's size (rounded up to even dimensions) with the default output's audio, at a variable frame rate.
- `video.mkv.ts`: for a mirror take, gpu-screen-recorder's record of the first frame's monotonic and wall-clock time.
- `stream.json`: the host's metadata, format `jc3vr-capture-stream`. Version 2, a streamed take's, has the video file's name, the `host` (`injector` or `relay`), `video_start_unix_ms` (the wall clock when the first frame reached the host), the encoder's spawn and stop times, its exit code, the `encoder`, the `audio` state, the `stream` object from the start line, and ffmpeg's command line. Version 1, a mirror take's, has the video file's name, `video_start_unix_ms` (the first frame's wall clock), the recorder's spawn and stop times, its exit code, the recorded `region`, the `output` and its logical layout, the niri `window` (id, title, app id, and whether it floats), and the recorder's command line. The host writes it when the take starts and completes it at the stop.

A second connection for the same folder (the payload reconnecting to a restarted host) writes `video_1.mkv` and `stream_1.json`, and so on, rather than overwriting.

In a streamed take the video and the sidecar line up by frame: video frame `n` is `frame_map.jsonl`'s `stream_frame` `n`, showing the image of the sidecar's frame `frame`, and it is at `n / frame_rate` seconds into the video. The image is two captures older than the frame it was queued with, which the record accounts for. The audio starts when ffmpeg opens it, just after the stream's first frame arrives; the offset between them has not been measured and is expected to be within a few frames.

In a mirror take they line up by wall clock: a sidecar line at `unix_ms` is at `unix_ms - video_start_unix_ms` milliseconds into the video. The payload and the host read the same clock, since wine reads the host's. The video frame that shows a rendered frame comes a little after its line, by the mirror's present, the compositor, and the recorder's own frame interval; the offset has not been measured, and is expected to be a frame or two. The take markers give a second reference either way.

## Post

`tools/capture/reframe.py VIDEO.mkv OUT.mp4` turns a mirror take into a flat video. It reads `frames.jsonl` and the `stream.json` beside the video, gives each video frame the pose of the last rendered frame at or before its wall-clock time and the mirror layout in effect then, builds the eye's pinhole matrix in video pixels from the eye's field of view and its rectangle in the window, and warps the frame by the homography to a symmetric output camera of the requested horizontal field of view and aspect, so the crop is centred on the optical axis rather than the window centre. With `--smooth` above zero the output camera follows a Gaussian-smoothed head orientation instead of the recorded one, a rotation-only stabilisation from ground truth: a homography, no depth, nothing estimated. The part of the eye image visible in the window outside the crop is what the stabilised view pans into. The mirror must have shown a single eye; a take of the stereo pair is refused. `--dry-run` prints the framing and the first frame's corner mapping without decoding, and both modes list the take markers with their time in the video.

The tool does not read streamed takes yet. Everything it needs is in them: the frame mapping in `frame_map.jsonl` and each eye's rectangle in `stream.json`'s `stream.eyes`, which take the place of the wall-clock lookup and the mirror layout.

## Session recording

F1 starts a session recording: the video recorder and a tracking recording ([testing/recorder.md](testing/recorder.md)) started together under one stamp, so both halves land in one `recordings/<stamp>/` folder, the tracking recording as `tracking.jc3trk` beside the sidecar and the video. F1 again stops both, as it does when either one runs on its own. The Video feature has the same button and a "Session recording" line saying which halves run and under which stamp, with the tracking recording's error if it did not start (it needs `testing.enabled` and a player character). Neither recording has a length limit: the sidecar is appended by the take's thread and tracking batches by the tracking writer, so an hour-long session holds no more in memory than a short one.

The two halves line up by the OpenXR predicted display time, which the sidecar's `predicted_display_time_ns` and the tracking frames' `display_time_ns` share; `tracking-synth dump --from xr:<ns>` prints the tracking around a sidecar frame, and the sidecar's frame maps to the video as above.

F2 is the take-marker action: an unlabelled marker into whatever records (above); in the headset the left stick click and then right A does the same, except in a vehicle ([input/controllers.md](input/controllers.md#bindings-and-injection)). With no tracking recording running it also saves the tracking lookback, the last ten seconds, to `recordings/<stamp>/lookback.jc3trk`, so a moment is kept even when no session recording was started.

## Operating it

On Windows:

1. Build, then run `scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.ps1` once to put `ffmpeg.exe` beside the injector (`scripts/run.ps1` warns when it is missing). A prebuilt injector needs an `ffmpeg.exe` beside it; any build with a hardware H.264 encoder or OpenH264 works.
2. Start the game and run the injector. Leave its window open: it is the capture host for the session, and it closes itself when the game exits. Its log names the encoder and the audio device at each take's start.
3. Start the recording with F1 (a session recording), or the video alone from Tools > Video in the debug UI or its hotkey (Numpad / by default), and stop it the same way. The take's `video.mkv` is in its folder in the session folder.

On Linux under Proton:

1. Start `scripts/capture_host.sh` and leave it running for the session. A streamed take needs ffmpeg on the host; a mirror take needs niri and gpu-screen-recorder. Python 3 runs the relay.
2. Inject with `scripts/proton_run.sh`. The injector finds the port taken and exits after injecting; without the relay running it stays as the host and encodes in software, after `scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.sh` has staged its ffmpeg.
3. Record with F1 as above. The Video feature and the Status page show whether the host is connected, the encoder, the audio, the stream's counters, and the host's error if it refused.

To record the mirror window instead, switch "Capture the eye views in process" off in the Video feature and run the relay; place the window where it can stay for the take, on the active workspace, wholly on screen, uncovered, and pick the mirror view with F10. On an output where gpu-screen-recorder's region capture comes out squashed or offset, the display's plane may still have a stale size from boot; a modeset (switching the output's mode away and back) clears it.
