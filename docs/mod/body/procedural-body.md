# The procedural torso

Without the procedural torso, Rico's body is the game's animation with HumanIK corrections for the head, the torso, and the hands ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)). The procedural torso poses the spine, the neck, and the clavicles from the player instead, on the hips the game's animation keeps. The hips, the pelvis, and the legs stay the game's: the body rig moves Rico by driving the game's own movement, so the legs walk, stand, and land the way the game's clips put them ([rig.md](rig.md)). Standing on foot and in a jump, the IK body poses the pelvis and the legs from the learned estimate ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md)). The code is in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/body/procedural/`, and its tuning is in `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/procedural.toml` ([tuning.md](../tuning.md)), whose keys this page names. It has no settings. The states outside standing play are in [procedural-states.md](procedural-states.md).

## Where the pose is written

The pose is written at the HumanIK solve gate: a pre-call detour on [`HumanIK::HasTargets`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##func-HasTargets) (`hooks::character::gate`), acting for the local player's solver and the `MAIN` pass. That call has one caller, inside the pose-finalization pass, after every game writer of IK targets and the first weapon attachment refresh, and before the solve, the second attachment refresh, the model-space sync, the hit shapes, the AI's view of the character, and the attached effects ([skeleton.md, "Who reads the pose before `UpdatePropEffects`"](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#who-reads-the-pose-before-updatepropeffects)). A pose written there is the one bullets hit and NPCs see. The gate runs inside the HumanIK gate the game checks first (the global switch and the reduced level of detail).

The gate has no step of its own, so the pass's entry records the pass's step on the thread running it, and the blend and the followers advance by that step rather than by the wall clock between gates.

The writes go through [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) into the current pose, parents first, so each write carries the bones under it. The pose producer's pose pair ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#bone-store-hkapose-model-space)) makes the pose written this tick next tick's `T0`, so the skinning interpolates it like any other.

## The rig

The torso drives the four spine bones, the neck, the head, and the two clavicles (`LeftShoulder` and `RightShoulder`). `rig` finds them, with the hips, the pelvis, and the legs, by name in the skeleton the animation controller holds ([`AnimationController::m_Skeleton`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##field-m_Skeleton)), and checks that each bone's parent is the one the solver assumes. It also measures each arm from the reference pose for the clavicles: where the shoulder joint (`LeftArm`) sits on its clavicle, and the arm's length down `LeftForeArm` to `LeftHand`. A skeleton that differs is refused with a warning (`procedural rig: ...`), and the body stays the animation's. The rig is kept for the skeleton it was built from, and every bone index is checked against the skeleton's bone count before a joint is read or written, since `SetJoint` does not check it. The skeleton is logged once per session (`body: skeleton bone`, one line per bone).

While the grapple lock holds the line, a traversal, a wall attach, a fall past the grace, or on foot without the movement task reads as its own case, `grapple line locked`, unless a clip owns the body (a ledge climb, a swim, the wingsuit, the parachute, a hit reaction, a climb by the hands, or an authored motion). The torso is the animation's there, and the legs hang from the line ([procedural-states.md](procedural-states.md#the-firm-ragdoll)).

A flight keeps the torso for `blend.flight_hold_s` of the simulation's time. After a jump or a spell under the parachute, the cases a flight passes through (a fall past the jump's grace, a grapple traversal, and on foot without the movement task) keep the torso procedural until the hold runs out, and each jump or spell under the parachute renews it. A jump that outlasts its grace, which a slowed time scale stretches, and a canopy closed and opened again around a grapple's reel therefore keep the torso throughout, instead of handing it to the animation and back with each change of case. A flight that outlasts the hold hands the torso over once, and any other case ends the hold at once. The hold does not change the case, so the steady view and the readout still report the flight's.

The weight follows its case's with a critically damped spring: `blend.in_s` toward the procedural pose and the quicker `blend.out_s` back to the animation. A death or a ragdoll hands the torso over at once, since a ragdoll writes the pose itself. Handing back is continuous because the animation has been playing all along. Every change of case is logged at info level (`procedural body: the hand-over case changes`, with the case left and entered, the torso's target, and the weight), and so is the end of a flight's hold, as a change from the case to itself with the target dropping to zero.

The steady view reads the same table with a tracker of its own ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)).

The predicates the table calls at the gate walk the character's own rule system and interaction graph; the engine calls the same ones from the same pass. The vehicle seat, the traversal, and the airborne modes come from the mode detection the input tick publishes, so they lag by up to a frame; the state bits, the motion state, the health, and the proxy's support are read at the gate. The ledge climb keeps no state on the character, so a post-call on its motion task stamps each tick of it for the local player (`hooks::character::signals`, `procedural::signals`), and the first tick of each climb logs `procedural body: the grapple's ledge climb starts`. A hit reaction keeps no state either: [`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction) queues the stumble or the knock-back straight on Rico's animated model, so a pre-call on [`AnimatedModel::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-QueueAct) stamps the local player's, matched by the model's address, and logs `procedural body: a hit reaction starts`. The reaction holds the torso on the animation for `hit_reaction.hold_ms`, renewed by each new act.

## The torso on the animation's hips

`solver::anchored` poses the torso from the clip's own pose, so the clip's posture is the base the player's head moves from: a walk's stride, a bike's lean into a turn, a driver's reach for the wheel. In order:

1. On the ground (on foot, and standing on a vehicle's roof) the clip's forward lean is taken out first. Every gait clip leans the torso forward, and swinging the neck under the head from there, with the pelvis kept, bends the upper spine back: the belly pushes forward and the chest tips up into the view. So the spine turns about its base until its base-to-neck line goes the share `torso.delean` of the way to vertical (`SolveInput::delean`, eased in and out over `torso.delean_blend_s` as the case changes, so a jump's takeoff and landing do not move the torso in one tick).
2. The chest twists to face the player's torso: the body rig's chest heading against Rico's facing, less the heading the clip's own shoulder line faces, so a clip that stands with its shoulders turned (the unarmed idle stands bladed) is squared to the player rather than turned further. The twist is limited to `torso.chest_turn_limit_deg`, taken about the clip's own spine, and shared out up it (more of it higher, `torso.twist_shares`). A spine far from upright (lying in a swim) takes less of the twist, and none lying flat.
3. The spine swings so the neck reaches the neck pivot under the player's head, shared out along the four spine bones (`torso.swing_shares`) and repeated to converge, never past `torso.lean_limit_deg` from the clip's own spine. A lean the body rig reads as a bend therefore bends Rico's back, and the hips stay where the clip has them.
4. A look down pitches the chest forward by a share of it (`torso.chest_pitch_share`, at most `torso.max_chest_pitch_rad`), again by how upright the spine is, and the spine turns back onto the neck's line about its base.
5. The chest is kept out of the camera's near field. A front point of the chest (the upper spine, the sternum, or a shoulder joint) less than `chest_clearance.below_m` below the camera's eyes is swung back with the whole spine about its base until it is at least `chest_clearance.back_m` behind the eyes along the view's level forward. That frame turns with the head's heading alone: its forward axis on the ground blended with its up axis as it pitches, which stays defined looking straight down. The swing never passes `chest_clearance.limit_rad` in all, so a head passing the vertical while bent over cannot fold the drawn spine over backwards. The rule holds whatever the clip and the rig do, so a camera that sits low or a spine bent back never puts the chest in front of the eyes, and a chest further below is left alone. BIMOS, an open VR rig, holds its chest target behind and below the eyes for the same reason; the rule here measures the chest's front points rather than a chest centre and only pushes when they come closer.
6. The neck takes a share of the head's turn; the head override sets the head itself after the solve.

The player's head is read at the neck pivot, below and behind the eyes in the head's frame (`procedural::head`): looking down turns the eyes about the neck, so the eye point drops and comes forward although nothing below the neck moved. On foot in the headset the pivot is the camera's own, its eye point plus `EYES_TO_NECK` turned by the head (`procedural::head::camera_neck_pivot`), the same point the rig reads as the neck. The body IK's head target and the spine's swing therefore put Rico's head bone there whatever frame the anchor and the body's turn under the view are in. In the cases where the view hangs from a clip's head, the steady view's weight blends the pivot with the published head carried back by the head's turn. The chest clearance measures against the camera's eye point too. With the headset's eyes hung by the neck model ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)) the head bone lands within about a centimetre of the camera's pivot standing on foot. The head is measured against the root at the height the steady view hangs it from, so a staircase does not read as a crouch.

The rotations are composed onto the animated bones, so no bone's rest frame is assumed. The game's own IK targets on the torso's effectors (the waist, the chest's end, and the shoulders) have their reach and pull faded by the torso's weight; the hips' targets stay the game's. The head's own target keeps its reach, so the body IK's head target closes what is left between the neck and the player's head, but its pull fades, so it cannot drag the procedural spine after the head. The pre-solve IK that would pose the same bones (the body IK's torso) stands down once the torso has its full weight.

## The hips

The idle clip stands bladed, its hips turned right of the root ([idle-stance.md](../../engine/character/idle-stance.md#the-idles-clip-variants)), and the yaw follow steers the root to the legs' heading ([rig.md](rig.md#the-yaw)), so the hips would face off the player. The gate therefore measures the heading of the line across the clip's upper legs against the root, before the IK solves, while the hand-over case is `on foot`, and follows it with the time constant `hips.smoothing_s` so a walk's swing of the hips from stride to stride averages out (`procedural::hips`). The follow steers the root by the legs' heading less it, so the clip's hips rather than its root face the legs' heading.

A case the gate passes through briefly on the ground (a hit reaction, a one-off move, a jump within its grace, or a tick without the movement task) holds the followed heading without measuring. Its publication lapses after half a second, so a brief pass leaves the root where it was, and a return to on foot carries on from the held heading rather than taking the next measurement whole. Only leaving the ground resets it. The view does not turn: the body turns under it as for any body turn. The feet stay where the clip puts them under the turned root until the stance squares them.

## The stance

While the IK body owns the standing body, its planted contacts place the feet and the stance yields to them by their weight ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#feet)). The stance below places the standing body's feet whenever the IK does not have them: without a fresh estimate, in gamepad-only play, and with the IK body switched off.

No idle in Rico's set stands square ([idle-stance.md](../../engine/character/idle-stance.md#the-idles-clip-variants)). The game's locomotion IK task queues a target on each ankle and each toe every on-foot tick, at the clip's own joints moved only up or down to the ground under them, before the solve gate. While Rico stands still in a standing idle (the hand-over case `on foot`, the body's state one of the base graph's standing idles, `STANCE_IDLES`, the movement task fresh, and the stick and the task's velocity at rest), the gate moves those targets across the ground to a square stance (`procedural::stance`). Each target keeps its height, so the ground's adjustment stays the game's; an ankle or toe the game queued nothing for gets a target of its own at the clip's height. A standing-still state outside `STANCE_IDLES` is logged once (`procedural body: standing still on foot in a state the stance's idle list misses`).

The stance faces the clip's followed hip heading, which the yaw follow turns to face the player ([The hips](#the-hips)). The ankles stand `stance.width_m` apart square across it, centred across it under the hips and along it where the clip's two ankles stand on average, and each toe stands ahead of its ankle by the clip's own foot length, turned out by `stance.toe_out_deg`. The stance blends in over `stance.blend_s` (a follower's time constant) once Rico stands still and out the same way as soon as he moves, so a step and the walk's stop start from the clip's feet. The state check matters because a landing reads as on foot once grounded: a fast fall's landing and get-up turn the hips and place the feet themselves, and a stance squared under them slides the feet across a slope at their old height.

## The arms

The arms are the controllers' wrist targets ([hands-and-aiming.md](../input/hands-and-aiming.md#the-wrist-targets)), solved by HumanIK on the procedural torso after the gate. While the procedural torso poses the torso, the hand IK does not queue them at the pass's entry; it keeps its plan for the gate (`hands::ik::queue_or_defer`), and the gate queues it right after writing the pose (`hands::ik::queue_deferred`), since the rotation target is a delta from the wrist as it stands before the solve. At the gate:

- The wrist target is kept out of the torso: two capsules along the written spine, the chest from `Spine2` to the neck (`keep_out.chest_radius_m`) and the abdomen from the hips to `Spine2` (`keep_out.abdomen_radius_m`), widened by `keep_out.hand_margin_m`. A target inside either moves straight out of it. The same capsules (`body::procedural::torso_keep_out`) are what the physical hands' held-object keep-out queries ([physical-hands.md](../input/physical-hands.md#the-players-own-body)).
- The pull is zero: the torso is the player's, and the solve may not bend it toward a hand.
- On foot, in a standing stunt position, in a jump, under the parachute, and in flight, each clavicle is moved before it turns, so its shoulder joint sits on the arm remap's character shoulder, the joint the wrist target was remapped about ([controllers.md, "Arm reach"](../input/hands-and-aiming.md#arm-reach)). The chest clearance has already run by then, so the move's part toward the camera is held to it too (`solver::anchored::clear_clavicle_offset`): neither the clavicle's root nor its shoulder joint is carried into the near field, and a point the solve already left closer is not pushed further back than it stood. What remains of the move comes from Rico's head standing off the camera's neck pivot and, looking steeply down, from the chest clearance swinging the chest back; only moving the chest as a whole under the camera would close those.
- The clavicles reach with the hands: up for a hand above the shoulder, at most `clavicles.elevation_deg`; forward for a hand reaching far ahead or across the body, at most `clavicles.protraction_deg`; and down a little for a hand reaching far below, at most `clavicles.depression_deg`, starting at `clavicles.reach_start` of the arm's length and followed with the time constant `clavicles.follow_s`.
- Each driven arm gets an elbow hint: a weak position target on the elbow (`elbow_hint.reach`, scaled by the torso's weight), out along a pole that points down, out to the arm's side, and a little back. A hand reaching up or across the body lifts the pole further out to the side, as a person's elbow does. Where the reach runs along the pole (a hand reaching down, out, and back, toward a hip holster), the pole's part across the reach vanishes, so a second pole, straight back, takes over smoothly as it shrinks, and the elbow does not jump.

The hint shapes the elbow the tick solves and the simulation reads (the hit shapes, the shot origins). The drawn elbow is placed again every rendered frame by the presentation layer, which bends a controller-driven arm toward its own pole: the hint's pole, leaned toward the little-finger side of the wrist and smoothed across frames ([presentation-layer.md, "The arms"](../rendering/presentation-layer.md#the-arms)).

## The limbs' twist

The skeleton's twist bones carry part of the skin along the arm (`df_LeftArmRollA01` under `LeftArm` and `LeftForeArmRoll` under `LeftForeArm`, and the right side alike; [skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md)), so a hand turned far about the forearm does not wring the skin at the wrist. `body::procedural::twist` runs at the end of the pose-finalization pass, in the post-call `UpdatePropEffects` detour after the head override and the finger curls. It shares the hand's twist against the forearm over the forearm's twist bones by where each sits, and holds the upper arm's roll back near the shoulder, by the torso's weight. A real forearm can only pronate or supinate so far before the upper arm rolls at the shoulder, so a hand turned further than `twist.forearm_limit_rad` from its forearm passes the share `twist.upper_arm_share` of the excess up to the upper arm. Its twist bones take the passed turn rising toward the elbow, and the forearm's start from it at the elbow, so the skin turns continuously from the shoulder to the wrist. The arm bones themselves are not turned, so the elbow stays where the solve put it. The legs' twist bones are the animation's.

## Diagnostics

The debug UI's Body/tracking > Body fit live status opens with the procedural body's status rows (a rigging error, the hand-over case, the `Twist bones` row with the twist bones found and the largest twist they carried, and the seated, wingsuit, parachute, swim, and roof torsos), then shows the weight, whether the procedural torso owns the torso, how far the neck sits from its target, each clavicle's turn, the square stance's weight and the foot targets it moved, and the wingsuit's arm targets placed.

## What needs confirming in the headset

1. Standing still: the readout's weight reaches one, the game's idle plays in the legs, and a hand on the hip lands on Rico's. Leaning over a table bends Rico's back toward the head while the legs and the hips stay the game's. Standing relaxed, the camera sits at Rico's eyes, and neither the scarf nor the shoulders come into the lower view unless the player looks down.
2. The weapon stays in the hand, and shooting Rico's body (from an NPC) hits where it is drawn.
3. Walking with the stick and stepping in the room: the legs play the game's walk without the torso tearing from the hips.
4. A hand on the belly or the chest rests on Rico's body, an arm folded across the chest goes around it, and the elbows hang naturally.
5. The hand-over: a scripted sequence logs the case `scripted sequence`; a stumble logs `procedural body: a hit reaction starts` and hands back about a second later; a vehicle, the wingsuit, a grapple reel, a stunt, swimming, and the ledge climb each log a change of case and blend without a pop; a ragdoll and a death hand over at once; a jump keeps the torso with the game's legs, a jump past its grace keeps it until a long fall hands it over once, and closing and reopening the canopy around grapple reels keeps it throughout. Stopping a walk logs no `jump` case for the proxy's settle.
6. Raising a hand overhead lifts that shoulder and that elbow out, reaching across the body brings the shoulder forward, and reaching down and back to the hip moves the elbow back without a jump.
7. `Twist bones` reads `Ok`; a hand turned palm up and down spreads the twist along the forearm, and turned past a right angle (a gun held out of a car window, the palm turned out) the upper arm's skin takes part of it rather than the forearm wringing.
8. Standing idle unarmed with a level gaze: Rico's hips and chest face where the player does; stopping a walk turns the root slowly under the hips with no turn-in-place animation playing each time.
9. Bending over and looking straight down at the feet: the drawn spine does not fold over backwards, and stays put while the head passes the vertical.
10. With the estimator switched off, so the IK body stands down ([ik-body.md](ik-body.md#headset-checks) has its own checks), standing still on foot, armed and unarmed, watching the shadow or the body mirror: within a fraction of a second Rico's feet come side by side, about a shoulder's width apart, toes a little out, and stay planted on slopes and steps. Walking off and stopping again blend the feet out and back in without a snap. The square stance's weight reaches one standing and falls to zero walking, with four foot targets moved.
11. Standing idle, a stumble, a hop within the jump's grace, or a one-off move: the drawn body does not swing to the side and back, and the yaw follow keeps facing the player through them.
