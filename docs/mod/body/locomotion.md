# Stick locomotion and the steady view

On foot the game moves Rico by his clips: the movement task's speed is the animation's root speed ([engine: the locomotion task](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md#41-the-locomotion-task-runs-at-idle-and-writes-the-proxy-input-directly)), so a start ramps up through a wind-up clip, a stop decays through a stop clip, a turning start swings the body, and a run surges with every step. The view hung from Rico's animated head as well, so all of that, plus the walk's bob and the idle's sway, reached the headset. Neither matches what the player's own body does, and both are discomforting.

The stick locomotion takes both away from the animation. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/locomotion/`. Every part has its own switch under `controllers.locomotion` and is on by default; the pause menu's `VR: COMFORT` page has `SMOOTH STICK MOVEMENT`, and the debug UI's On foot feature, under Movement/vehicles, has the other switches and the run speed.

## The velocity

`locomotion::after_task` runs from the post-call detour on [`NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask##func-Update) (`rig::root`), on the ticks where the task wrote the proxy's velocity, before the palms' push and the chase ([rig.md](rig.md)) add theirs. It replaces the task's horizontal velocity, through [`Character::SetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetWantedWorldVelocity), with its own, and keeps the task's vertical component.

- The direction is the move stick, read from the player action map's move half-axes, turned into the world by the view's heading on the ground plane. The bindings already rotate the stick into the off hand's frame when `controllers.locomotion.direction` asks for it, so the view is the frame either way. Rico's facing never steers the movement: the body follows the torso estimate ([rig.md](rig.md#the-torso)).
- The target speed is the game's own (`controllers.locomotion.stick.speed = "Game"`): the float the input tasks write at [`ObjectBlackboard::SPEED_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::SPEED_ID), the walk, run, or backpedal speed for the stick's deflection, and zero where the game would stop Rico at a drop or a wall ([`SetupTargetSpeeds`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_InputLocoSetTargetSpeedsTask##func-SetupTargetSpeeds), [engine](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md#41-the-locomotion-task-runs-at-idle-and-writes-the-proxy-input-directly)). Distances and chases therefore stay balanced, and the ledge stop keeps working. `Fixed` uses the stick's magnitude times `run_speed_mps` instead.
- The velocity moves toward the target at a fixed rate (`accel::step`): straight toward it, by at most the fixed acceleration times the step while speeding up and the deceleration while slowing or turning away (`accel::Rates::FIXED`). A start, a stop, and a change of direction all take a time proportional to the change and never overshoot, so there is nothing for the view to feel but a steady push. The rates reach the run speed in about half a second and stop from it in about a third.
- After a gap in the task (a jump, a fall, a vehicle exit), the integrator starts from the proxy's real velocity, so a landing carries its speed.
- While the animation carries the body, the task's velocity passes through untouched: an authored motion (the state bits the procedural body's hand-over table reads for it: a locomotion one-off, a hard landing, a looping context action, a full-body pickup, and cover) and a hit reaction's hold ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)). Their root motion is the motion, and a stumble flattened to the stick's velocity would go nowhere. The integrator then starts from the proxy's velocity again.
- The procedural body does not infer a ground sit or suppress the stick for one. Low head poses retain ordinary stick locomotion ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-lower-bodys-poses)).

The legs play the game's clips, at the clips' own speed and cadence, so they slide a little against the ground when the two speeds differ, unless the procedural body is on: its walk ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-feet)) keeps the feet at the root's own pace and leads them along the commanded velocity.

The task's velocity, the target, the written velocity, and the proxy's velocity are logged once a second while moving (`locomotion: stick velocity`, debug level on the `controllers` target), and the readout is the On foot feature's live status.

The game's speeds are measured once each: for every target speed the stick holds for two and a half seconds, the root speed of the clips is averaged after the first second and logged at info level (`locomotion: the game's on-foot speed`, with `target_mps` and `root_mps`).

## The turning animations

With the stick driving the velocity, the animations that turn the body are left with nothing to do but swing it. Most of them write the angle correction that turns the root, and so the view. The act filter (`locomotion::acts`, called from the `Character::QueueAct` detour) handles the local player's acts while the headset drives the view and the player is on foot on the ground:

- the rotate-on-spot acts and their warp flavors, the standing rotation starts, the quarter and half turns, and the heavy weapon's aim-focus rotations ([`QueueAimRelativeOnSpotActions`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueAimRelativeOnSpotActions)) are dropped. The body still turns: the orientation executor yaws the root toward the torso's heading.
- the turning run and walk starts are replaced by the straight start, the turning stops by the straight stop (warp and non-warp alike), and the turning flicks by the straight flick: the running flicks a start plays when a stop is called for inside its flick segment ([`QueueFlickStick`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueFlickStick)), and the aiming flicks ([`QueueAimFlickStick`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueAimFlickStick)). The state machine still passes through its start, stop, and flick states. Each replacement goes through the game's act pre-flight ([`AnimatedModel::TryAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-TryAct)) first, and the original act is queued when the state machine would not take the straight one.

Standing in a vehicle's stunt area, the stick turns the body toward it when the state machine would not take a stunt transition that way ([`QueueStuntTurns`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueStuntTurns)), and the view rides the body there. The filter replaces all three stunt turns, the straight one too, since its angle correction still turns the root, with `ACT_TO_IDLE`: the act the same function queues in their place when the stick points nearly straight ahead, so the state the turn was queued from moves on as it would with no turn to make. The replacement goes through [`AnimatedModel::TryAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-TryAct) like the others, but a refusal drops the stunt turn rather than queuing it. The game queues them only while it applies its stunt modification to the character ([`Character::ShouldApplyStuntModification`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-ShouldApplyStuntModification)), which is being attached to a vehicle, where the mode detection may read a vehicle rather than on foot. So the stunt turns have their own scope: the headset drives the view, gameplay owns the character, the controllers are on, and the game's own test holds when the act is queued, called on the same thread just after the input task called it. They need neither the on-foot mode nor `stick.enabled`, since the stick locomotion does not run on a roof. With nothing turning it, the body faces where it stood, and the stick still steps it to the next stunt area where a transition goes. The input tasks queue a stunt turn every tick the stick is held toward a side with no stunt area, so the stunt scope is kept out of the watchdog below: since its replacement is the game's own act for no turn, nothing is left waiting for a release to unstick, and a release would let a turn through.

The mortar's rotate-on-spot states are left alone: the game's code registers no mortar turning act, only the mortar's aim and fire acts, and what drives those states in the shipped state machine is not established.

The act names are bound in the pyxis defs ([`EventIdSymbolTable`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable)); they are the `NCharacter` ids the game registers at startup, so resolving them never adds a name to the table.

A filter that removes acts can leave the animation state machine waiting for one it never gets. The watchdog guards against that on foot: the filter normally acts in short bursts, at a start or a stop, so when it has acted with no gap longer than 400 ms for `WATCHDOG_S` seconds, it lets every act through for `RELEASE_S` seconds (both in `locomotion::acts`) and logs `locomotion: the act filter acted without a break; letting every act through` at warning level. The readout counts the dropped, straightened, and refused acts and the releases; a refused stunt turn counts as refused and is dropped.

## The steady view

`locomotion::anchor::stabilize` runs at the anchor capture at the entry of the pose-finalization pass (`hooks::character::body_ik`), once per sim tick, and decides what the view hangs from:

- on foot, the root-fixed anchor: Rico's root ([`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1), and `T0` for the previous tick of the pose pair) carrying a neutral standing head, neck, and eye midpoint in model space. The head and the neck ride the root only at their neutral heights, straight above it: their small horizontal offset would swing around the root whenever the body turns under the view (the torso follow, [rig.md](rig.md#the-torso)) and carry the view sideways, and the recenter takes up the constant offset instead. No clip moves it, so the view moves only with the root, at the stick's velocity, and with the player's own head.
- everywhere else (a vehicle, the parachute, the wingsuit, a grapple traversal, swimming, a ragdoll, and a fall that lasts longer than `locomotion::anchor::JUMP_GRACE_S`), the animated head as before. A jump keeps the root-fixed anchor through the grace period.

On the ground, the root-fixed anchor also needs the on-foot movement task, which moves the root the neutral head stands on: it holds while the task ran within `locomotion::view::MOVEMENT_TASK_GRACE_S`, or while Rico is in the air ([`Character::IsInAir`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInAir), or [`SB_IS_JUMPING`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_JUMPING)), since a jump stops the task too. The task stopping with Rico still on the ground means an animation the mode detection has not classified yet carries the body. A wingsuit takeoff from the ground is one: the mode stays on foot for over half a second while the clip pitches Rico forward into the suit, and a standing head held over the root left the view above and behind his back, looking down on the weapons slung there. The anchor hands over to the animated head then, as it does on entering the wingsuit. The task's stamp is written only while the headset drives the view, so outside the headset the mode alone decides.

The neutral pose is measured, not assumed: while Rico stands still on the ground, not aiming, the animated head, neck, and eyes are averaged over `CAPTURE_S` seconds (`locomotion::anchor`) of standing, once per session (`locomotion: measured Rico's neutral standing head (model space)`, info level). Until then the view keeps the animated head. The readout's button measures it again.

The anchor hands over between the two over `locomotion::anchor::BLEND_S` seconds, eased, so leaving the ground or entering a vehicle never jumps the view.

While the proxy stands on the ground that tick (not [`Character::IsInAir`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInAir)), the root's height is smoothed by a critically damped follower (`HEIGHT_SMOOTHING_S` in `locomotion::view`), so the steps of a staircase, which the proxy climbs in small pops, read as a ramp. Leaving the ground winds whatever the smoothing still hides down to nothing over a tenth of a second, so a jump's rise reaches the view at once and without a jump of its own. The smoothed height trails by at most `MAX_HEIGHT_LAG_M`, and a change beyond `HEIGHT_SNAP_M` (a drop, a teleport) passes at once. The readout shows the current offset.

Because the camera's anchor does not contain the animation, everything that composes onto it does too: the roomscale cockpit, the head-height remap, and the body IK's head target all measure the player's head against the root-fixed head.

## What needs confirming in the headset

- Standing still after the measurement, the view does not move when the anchor switches on (the handover log line, and a root weight of 1 in the readout).
- Walking and running with the stick: no bob, no surge, constant-rate starts and stops. In the debug log, `commanded` follows `target` at the configured rate and `proxy` follows `commanded`; a proxy velocity that lags well behind means the proxy's own smoothing is adding to the ramp.
- With the act filter on, the state machine still enters and leaves movement, starts, stops, and flicks look straight, and the watchdog's warning does not appear in normal play. Reversing the stick during a start, and aiming well to the side with a heavy weapon while standing, do not swing the view.
- On a car's roof, holding the stick toward a side with no stunt area, for as long as it is held, does not turn the body or the view, and the stick still steps the player onto a neighbouring area. Rico does not get stuck mid-stop or mid-step after letting go of the stick; the readout's refused count shows how often the state machine would not take the return to the idle.
- A hit stumble carries Rico as the game authored it, and a ledge stop stops him. A melee lunge that is not an authored motion comes through the movement task as root motion and is flattened to the stick's velocity; if it stops working, turn `SMOOTH STICK MOVEMENT` off and report it.
- A jump from a run carries its speed, and landing re-seeds the velocity from the proxy.
- Stairs and slopes feel smooth, and a long drop does not lag the view.
