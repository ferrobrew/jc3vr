# Grapple snatch

This is the plan of record and the description of what is built. All four phases are built ([Phases](#phases)): weapons on the ground and in characters' hands, long guns on racks and in lockers, guns holstered on characters and loose objects, and car doors pulled open on the line. A rack with unlimited stock, and a rack's pistols, are still the hands' alone ([Phase 2](#phase-2-racks-and-lockers)). None of it has been run in the headset. The code is `payload/src/controllers/snatch/`, with the aim patch in `payload/src/controllers/aim/grapple_weapons.rs`; the engine side is "Weapons and the grapple" in [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md#weapons-and-the-grapple).

It is built on the [two-handed inventory](two-handed-inventory.md), whose takes it ends in: the snatch's arrival lands the item in the loadout table and the grabbing hand, never through the game's equip. It is the first feature built on the inventory, and its test: a snatch takes an item from anywhere into the grabbing hand while the other hand holds whatever it holds, which exercises the table, the hands' independence, and the reconcile end to end.

The grapple hand pulls things to itself. The grapple button keeps doing what it does in the game (a tap fires, a hold tethers to and between targets), and the grip pulls: with the empty grapple hand pointed at something out of reach, a squeeze and a yank back away from it fire the game's hook at it and snatch it from wherever it is (the ground, a rack or a locker, a character's hands, a holster on a character's body) into the hand, in the held position. From there it is an ordinary held thing: hand it to the other hand, holster it, carry it, or drop it. Weapons are the core of the feature. Props and car doors follow, the doors pulled open on the line rather than into the hand, and an object too heavy for a hand to carry, such as an explosive barrel, swung on the line and slung into something ([Swinging on the line](#swinging-on-the-line)).

The game offers nothing like it: its grapple never hooks a weapon, has no pull of a light object to the player, and cannot reach into a character's hands. The snatch keeps the game's own grapple wherever it can, so it reads as the same device doing one thing more: the game's aim and target highlight, the game's hook, line, and flight from Rico's left forearm, and the game's own pickup and steal rules at the end. The mod adds the trigger, the pull, and the arrival.

## Why the game's grapple ignores weapons

Verified against the release IDB unless noted.

- The player's aim ray (`PlayerAimControl::UpdateDirectAim`) and the hook's own in-flight cast (`CWireEnd::TryCollide`) both query the `RAYCAST_EVERYTHING_PHYSICAL` layer, and the `WEAPONS` layer does not collide with it ([engine: havok-bodies.md](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md)). A dropped weapon's body is on `WEAPONS`, so both rays pass through it.
- Everything after the ray supports weapons. A weapon's grappling mode is "reel in, no attach", not "ungrapplable", and `CWireEnd::SetupProxyForAttachment` has an explicit weapon branch that builds the same object attachment proxy as for any loose object.
- The release has no usable pull of a light object to the player. The symbol dump's `CGrapplingHook::HandleGrapplePull` pulls one only behind two developer menu flags, and the release function bearing that name does not match it.
- A weapon in a character's hands has no body in the world, and `PlayerAimControl::CheckDirectTargets` resolves a hit on a held weapon to its holder.

So the game was built to hook weapons, and only its rays keep it from finding one.

## Targeting: the game's aim, scoped to the player's grapple

The snatch aims with the grapple's own aim: the off-hand's ray that the mod already feeds to the game's grapple ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "The grapple"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#the-grapple)). To make that aim see weapons, the patch is scoped to the grapple's pass of the direct-aim raycast, never the physics system's layer table, which every query on the layer shares (the gun's aim among them, which would then stop on guns lying on the ground). Other characters' grapples, the camera's pass, and every other query are untouched.

As built (`aim::grapple_weapons`): inside the grapple's pass, a detour of [`PlayerAimControl::CheckDirectTargets`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-CheckDirectTargets), the distribution of the ray's hits, first casts again along the same ray on [`VEHICLE_RAYCAST`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-VEHICLE_RAYCAST), which meets weapons, out to the ray's closest hit, with the aim's own filter ignoring the player and its vehicle. It steps past what that layer meets and the aim's does not (a character's capsule, a projectile, a vehicle's collision body) a few times. A weapon lying in the world that it finds is written into the ray's closest hit ([`CastRayInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::CastRayInfo)), and a holey hit beyond it is dropped from the [collector](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimRaycastCollector##field-m_HasHoleyHit), so the game does everything it does with a hit with the weapon: the slot's game object, the grapplable flag, the last-valid grapple cache, and the latch of a fire. The weapon found is published for the snatch, or, when there is none, the object the aim's own ray hit, which the snatch takes as a loose object or a car door. Because the line is the aim's own cast, nothing behind a wall is ever found.

With the patch, a gun on the ground is a grapple target like any other ([The grapple button at a gun](#the-grapple-button-at-a-gun)). For the snatch, a character holding a gun is a disarm target: the guns in characters' hands are the steal's ([physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-gun-from-a-characters-hands)), noted out to the snatch's range while the snatch and the either-hand hold (`weapons.hold.either_hand`) are on, and so are the guns holstered and visible on their bodies. The gun whose holding hand's grip, or whose holstered origin, is nearest the grapple's ray within the cone (`SNATCH.cone_deg`) and the range (`SNATCH.range_m`, both in the snatch's fixed tuning, `controllers::snatch::SNATCH`) is chosen, with a line-of-sight cast to it on the aim's layer that ignores the player and the character. A weapon lying in the world is a candidate off the ray too: a gun lying flat subtends a fraction of a degree at range, so the ray's exact hit is more than a tracked hand can hold on it. The snatch finds the dynamic bodies whose boxes lie near the ray (`physical_hands::remote::dropped_weapons`, reading only the broad phase's boxes) and keeps those that are dropped weapons. Their cone is the same cone widened in proportion to the distance, up to `SNATCH.floor_cone_widen_deg` more at the range (`snatch::rules::floor_cone`), since tremor and the ground's grazing angle throw the ray farther off a far gun; the angle is measured to the body's centre, and the same line-of-sight cast applies. What the aim hits itself (a weapon on the ground, a loose object, a car door's handle), at the ray, wins over a candidate off it, since the choice takes the smallest angle. The chosen target shows one marker, a ring in its kind's colour around it (a weapon's, an object's, or a vehicle's), which stands in for the game's highlight: the game's reticle is hidden under hand aim. What the patch cannot reach, a rack's or a locker's weapon, which has no body in the world, is found among the weapons the mod notes as the game renders them, around the same ray and within the floor's widening cone ([Phase 2](#phase-2-racks-and-lockers)).

In the grip's one targeting rule ([grip.md](grip.md)) the snatch is the lowest tier, `Tier::Remote`, taken only when nothing at all is in reach (not even a surface to climb or a reach margin), so anything within real reach always wins. It is offered only to the grapple hand, empty (no gun, foregrip, grenade, charge, or prop), on foot with no traversal, with the hook free (no active or attachment wire, no fire pending, the hook inactive) and the grapple button up. A fist never takes it: only a press aimed at it arms the snatch.

The grapple hand is the off-hand of the acting hand. The game's grapple device is on Rico's left forearm, so an ordinary snatch in a left-handed setup still leaves the opposite arm. While the line swings its target, the native device proxy follows the tracked grapple hand's wrist frame.

## The interaction

1. Aim: point the empty grapple hand at the target. The marker shows it.
2. Grip: squeezing arms the snatch on the target under the aim. Nothing flies yet, and the target is held while the grip is. The target's ring gives way to the latch: a solid cyan dot ringed on the target and a line from the gripping hand to it, in-world, through the scene, for as long as the snatch is armed (`snatch::latch`, the overlay's `SnatchLatch` slot, shown with the grip markers, `overlay.markers`). The hand feels `Event::SnatchArm`. The latch goes when the yank fires or the snatch is cancelled.
3. Yank: a pull of the hand back along its aim, faster than a threshold, while the grip is held, fires it. The pull is measured along the grapple hand's aim at the grip edge, in the cockpit frame (`bindings::yank::reach_along`), not as the hand nearing the body: seated, a hand already close to the body pulls back and out past its side, which neither nears the chest nor the head, and a measure toward a point below the head misses such a pull entirely. The speed is measured from whichever sample in the detector's window gives the pull its length, so a hand held still at the grip before it pulls is not slowed by the pause, and the snatch's threshold is lower than the grapple zip's, since only a hand already gripping a remote target can fire it. The yank is the confirmation, as with the gravity gloves of other games, and the motion matches what it does. Opening the grip before the yank disarms it, and a punch or a swing that closes the hand does not snatch, since the grip comes first. The grapple's line already uses a yank to zip ([physical-grapple.md](physical-grapple.md)); the snatch's yank is its own detector (`weapons.snatch.yank`, the bindings' yank gesture with its own thresholds), armed only while the grip holds a remote target with no line out.
4. Flight: the hook flies from the left forearm to the target and bites, and the line yanks the target off its feet toward the hand, a real body flying with real velocity and spin that collides with whatever is in the way. Over the last couple of metres it slows a little and comes straight at the hand, and a gun turns in the air so that it arrives in the pose the catching hand holds it in, grip first and pointing the way the hand points ([The aligned landing](#the-aligned-landing)).
5. Catch: the line steers the target to whichever free, empty hand is nearest its incoming path, the line from the target to the player's eyes, whether or not that hand's grip is squeezed, so the player can pull with the grapple hand and hold the other hand out in front to receive it. A couple of ticks out the line goes slack and the target flies on by its own momentum, and a hand catches it when its grip closes on it within about 0.4 m of the palm, or up to about 0.15 s after it came that near, so a squeeze a moment late still catches. The grapple hand's grip holds the line all along, so it catches only when the line steers to it; the other free hand catches whenever its grip closes on the target. If no hand closes on it, it flies past or hits the player, and drops.

Hold to hold holds: opening the grip at any point cancels. Mid-flight, the line comes away and the target keeps its momentum and falls, rather than snapping back to where it came from.

While the line is out, the other hand's grip, when that hand is free and empty, is held for the catch (`snatch::reserved_hands`): a press of it closes the hand on nothing, so it takes nothing in reach (a holster, a pouch, a ledge, a prop) and drives no game action, and a hand already closed on nothing stays so. A hand whose grip holds something else never catches. When the other hand catches, its grip holds the target from then on as the grapple hand's grip would have, so the closed hand takes nothing more it comes to.

Haptics: `Event::SnatchArm` (a bump) when the grip arms it, `Event::SnatchFire` (a stronger thud) when the yank fires it, the grapple's fire cue on the snatching hand, `Event::SnatchHit` (a thud) when the hook bites, the line's tension (`Sustain::GrappleTension`, by the line's share of its strongest pull) while it yanks, and `Event::SnatchCatch` (the hardest, longest thud) on the catching hand; a refused or failed snatch, a missed catch among them, plays `Event::SnatchRefused` on the grapple hand ([haptics.md](haptics.md)).

Sound: the snatch plays the grapple's own sounds and effects ([The snatch's sounds](#the-snatchs-sounds)).

Leaving the feet (a vehicle, a fall, a ragdoll, a reel) or turning the snatch off cancels it as opening the grip does.

## Swinging on the line

The line can hold its target at a length instead of pulling it in, and the grapple hand swings it: drag it, whirl it around, or lift it into an arc, then open the grip to let it go with its current velocity. The game's own physics and damage do the rest, so an explosive barrel slung into a wall or a soldier explodes on impact as it would from any hit. Three things swing:

- A loose object too heavy for one hand to carry (`physical_hands::remote::carry_max_kg`, the pickup's one-hand strength against gravity, about 36 kg) and no heavier than the swing's ceiling (`weapons.snatch.swing.ceiling_kg`) is offered as a heavy object (`HEAVY OBJECT`, marked as an object) and is always swung (`rules::prop_handling`). It never comes to the hand: once the hook bites, the line holds it. Above the ceiling nothing is offered. Vehicles are never offered, however light, nor a standing character.
- A character that ragdolls, knocked down, thrown, or dead ([engine: ragdoll.md](../../engine/character/ragdoll.md#ragdolling)), is offered as a body (`BODY`, marked as an object) when the whole ragdoll is no heavier than the swing's ceiling (`physical_hands::remote::ragdoll_target`, `snatch::target::ragdoll_offer`). It is always swung, by the body of its ragdoll the aim hit, or the one nearest the hit when the aim met the character's capsule or hit proxy instead ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md#reaching-a-character)); the hook latches the character's bone nearest that body. The line's strength and pull are those of the whole ragdoll's mass, and its pull is given to the one body as an impulse, which the ragdoll's constraints share with the rest, so the body comes limb first and the rest drags behind; a body's velocity alone would move one limb as if it were the whole ragdoll.
- A weapon or a light object swings while B (the grapple semantic button) is held: grip the target, hold B, and yank. Releasing B while the grip stays closed resumes the ordinary pull to the hand with a fresh stall watch.

The line (`snatch::sling`) is a stiff, tension-only spring-damper from the grapple hand to the target's centre of mass at a held length: the rope the grapple's lock also uses (`grapple::rope`, [grapple-lock.md](grapple-lock.md#the-shared-rope)), with the hand as its kinematic end. Each tick it asks the target's velocity along the line, relative to the moving hand, for the line's stiffness times its stretch past the held length plus its damping times the target's speed away from the hand, and gives no more tension than the line's strength. Velocity across the line and angular velocity are untouched, a slack line never pushes, and gravity and collisions stay the engine's. The change is written through the same body-velocity path every hand's hold uses, so the target is never placed or keyframed.

The line's strength scales with the target's mass (`sling::line_strength_n`): `strength_n` at a 10 kg reference, times the mass over the reference to the power `mass_scaling`. At the default 0.5 the line pulls a heavy target harder than a light one but speeds it up less, so a 150 kg fuel barrel takes a real swing to move but does move, and a rifle swings briskly. At 1 every mass swings alike; at 0 the line pulls every mass equally hard.

The held length starts as the distance at the bite and reels in at `reel_mps` toward `swing_length_m`, so a target hooked far off is drawn in and then swung around the player rather than hanging off the end of a long line. The reel takes in line no faster than the target comes, keeping it at most 0.3 m inside the target (`sling::reel_in`), so a heavy target that lags is pulled at the line's strength rather than left far beyond the line. It never pays line out. A rope cannot push, so a target reeled in keeps its speed once the reel stops and coasts on toward the player until friction, gravity, or a swing takes it; the default reel is slow enough that a barrel dragged along the ground stops within a step or two. A reel speed of zero keeps the length the hook bit at.

The swung target collides with everything, the player's own body included. The line's anchor is the tracked wrist frame with `GRAPPLE_ORIGIN_M` (`controllers::aim`), and the native device proxy's visual getters receive that frame during the owned wire update, so the game's line runs from the tracked forearm. Excessive stretch (3 m past the held length, "the target is beyond the line"), an invalid sample, tracking loss, a lost line, changed target ownership, an occupied hand, a hand-role change, leaving the supported mode, opening a menu, or turning the swing off ends the line. A stationary swing does not use the arrival pull's stall timeout.

The owned wire's native constraint creation and tension callbacks are excluded while swinging, so the target does not receive a second rope force and Rico is not pulled toward a heavy one. The exclusion starts during hook flight and ends before an ordinary pull resumes. The input tick owns target references and body access outside physics work. Hooks copy the published anchor and compare wire and proxy identities; they do not resolve target references or drive target bodies on a worker.

`GrappleButton` owns a fresh B press only after the grip has armed a remote target; on a heavy object the owned press does nothing, rather than firing the game's grapple from under the armed snatch. It consumes that press and release through cancellation and stand-down until a usable neutral observation on the original hand and on-foot route. B before grip remains an ordinary grapple. The native custom-wire state ignores ordinary fire and reel input, and the input owner prevents delayed zip, tether, or release gestures from the consumed episode.

### The two-handed brace

While the line swings, the other hand can brace the grapple hand: squeeze its grip with its wrist within `SNATCH.brace_take_m` of the grapple hand's wrist (`snatch::brace`). The other hand must be free and empty, its grip closed on nothing; while the line is out that grip is held for the snatch already, so the squeeze takes nothing in reach. Braced, the line's strength is `brace_factor` times one hand's, and the line is anchored at the average of the two hands, keeping the grapple hand's offset to the line's origin; the anchor's move between the hand and the pair is not read as a motion of the hands. The brace holds until the grip opens, the hand takes something, a wrist loses tracking, or the wrists drift more than `SNATCH.brace_break_m` apart, and the line returns to one hand's strength.

### Feedback

Haptics: the grapple hand feels the line's tension (`Sustain::GrappleTension`, by the tension's share of the line's strength), and so does the bracing hand while it braces ([haptics.md](haptics.md)).

In-world, the latch's slot (`snatch::latch`, `SnatchLatch`, shown with the grip markers, `overlay.markers`) draws the line while it swings: from the grapple hand to the target's centre, its colour running from the latch's cyan when slack to red at the line's strength and its width growing with the tension, a ring on it where the held length ends (a target beyond the ring is stretching the line), and a dot at the anchor between the hands while braced.

The `SNATCH` status row, which heads the Grapple feature's readout, shows `SWINGING` with the target's mass, the held length, the tension of the line's strength in newtons, and whether it is braced. While nothing is under way, the readout beneath it shows the mass of the loose object under the grapple's aim whatever the snatch would do with it, so an object above the ceiling can be told apart, and an offered object's mass rides with its kind. The log has `snatch: swinging on the held line` with the length, mass, strength, and whether the target is too heavy to come to the hand, `snatch: the other hand braced the line` and `let go of the line` with the strength, and `snatch: armed` with an object's or a body's mass.

### Tuning

`weapons.snatch.sling` (on by default) switches the swing, heavy objects' offer with it; its switch and the line's tuning (`weapons.snatch.swing`) are in the debug UI's Snatch feature:

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ceiling_kg` | 300 | the heaviest object offered |
| `strength_n` | 1500 | the line's strength on a 10 kg target, newtons |
| `mass_scaling` | 0.5 | how the strength grows with the mass |
| `brace_factor` | 1.75 | the braced strength as a multiple of one hand's |
| `stiffness_per_s2` | 400 | the pull per metre of stretch, per second squared |
| `damping_per_s` | 30 | the damping of the speed away from the hand, per second |
| `swing_length_m` | 3 | the length the line reels in to |
| `reel_mps` | 4 | how fast it reels in |

The ceiling comes from the game's prop masses ([engine: havok-bodies.md, "A loose object's mass"](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#a-loose-objects-mass)): 300 kg admits every barrel (fuel barrels and water-filled barrier barrels at 150 kg), crate, jerrycan, chair, and the 250 kg propane tank, and stops short of voltage boxes and stacks of planks, with nothing loose between 500 kg and 1 t. The 25 kg gas canister is carried rather than swung. At the defaults the line's strongest pull on a fuel barrel is about 5800 N, 39 m/s² (68 m/s² braced), enough to whirl it at about 10 m/s on the 3 m line; on a propane tank it is 30 m/s² (52 m/s² braced); on a 4 kg rifle the stiffness, not the strength, sets the pull.

In the headset:

1. Aim at a red fuel barrel 10 m off: a heavy object's marker, and the readout names it with its 150 kg. Aim at a hay bale: no marker, and the readout shows its mass.
2. Squeeze and yank: the hook bites, the line reels the barrel in along the ground to about 3 m and holds it there; it never comes to the hand. The in-world line runs from the hand to the barrel, reddening and thickening as it pulls, with its ring at the held length.
3. Drag it by walking back, then whirl the hand in circles or turn on the spot until it lifts and circles, then open the grip as it points at a wall or a soldier: it flies on and explodes on impact. The hand feels the line's tension throughout.
4. While it swings, squeeze the other grip at the grapple hand's wrist: the readout says braced, the strength rises, a dot shows between the hands, both hands feel the tension, and the barrel answers the swing harder. Pull the hands apart or open that grip: back to one hand.
5. Swing it into Rico: it hits him. Walk away from a barrel snagged on something: the line breaks with "the target is beyond the line".
6. Grip a rifle on the ground, hold B, and yank: it swings on the same line and reels in to about 3 m; whirl it and let go, then repeat and release B to bring it to the hand.
7. Turn the swing off in the debug UI: the barrel is no longer offered, and a swing under way is cancelled.
8. Knock a soldier down or kill one, and aim at the body on the ground: an object's marker labelled `BODY`, with the whole body's mass, several tens of kilograms. Aim at a standing soldier: no `BODY` marker.
9. Squeeze and yank at the body's arm: the hook latches that arm, and the line reels the body in by it, the arm leading and the rest dragging. Whirl it and let go: it tumbles on. A soldier who was only knocked down gets up once the line lets go and the body comes to rest.

`testing/scenarios/grapple_hammer_throw.toml` records the acceptance sequence for weapons and light props on B, and `testing/scenarios/grapple_heavy_swing.toml` the heavy swing's. Both still need a headset recording and live-game acceptance.

## The snatch's sounds

The snatch plays the grapple's own sounds and effects, from the hook's effect container (`snatch::juice`; the engine side is "The hook's sounds and effects" in [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md#the-hooks-sounds-and-effects)). The custom fire plays no fire animation, and the hook's state stays custom, so of the game's own only the wire's flight loop plays by itself.

- The fire: the device's fire sound and muzzle effect, the container's `GrappleFire` usage ([`GRAPPLE_FIRE_USAGE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##const-GRAPPLE_FIRE_USAGE)), played as the game's track message plays it. The container's `GrapplePreFire` usage, a sound only, is not played: which point of the game's fire it belongs to is unverified, and the snatch's yank fires at once.
- The bite: the hook's material impact, [`WireEnd::PlayImpactEffect`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-PlayImpactEffect), fed by a cast from the hand through the hook's end on the layer that meets weapons, kept only when it hits the target.
- The yank: the reel's loop ([`m_ReelingInEffectUsage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##field-m_ReelingInEffectUsage)), held on while the line pulls by a detour of [`GrapplingHook::UpdateLoopingEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-UpdateLoopingEffects), which otherwise plays it only while the hook reels in. When the line goes slack or the snatch ends, the game's own update stops it softly.
- The line coming away (the catch, a miss, a cancel, a failure): the retract's one-off ([`m_RetractingActiveWireEffectUsage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##field-m_RetractingActiveWireEffectUsage)).

## The grapple button at a gun

With the aim seeing weapons, the grapple button acts on a gun as the game acts on any light object, with one difference (`snatch::hooks`):

- A hook fired at a weapon flies as a ghost wire: a detour of [`WireEnd::SetExtend`]({V}equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-SetExtend) sets the ghost flag when the latched object is a weapon. The hook's own casts never hit a weapon's body, so a plain hook would attach to whatever lies under or behind the gun and zip Rico there; a ghost hook attaches to the gun itself ([`WireEnd::ForceAttachToTarget`]({V}equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-ForceAttachToTarget)). It covers the first end of a tap or a hold and the second end of a tether, so a hold tethers a gun to a car, or two guns together, through the game's own tethers.
- A tap at a gun reels the gun in, not Rico. A weapon's grappling mode is "reel in, no attach", and the release has no pull of a light object, so where the game would start its reel on an attached line to a weapon (in [`GrapplingHook::OnPreSimUpdate`]({V}equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-OnPreSimUpdate), the local player's hook inactive, the button up, no tether pending), a detour ends the wire ([`GrapplingHook::CancelActiveWire`]({V}equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-CancelActiveWire)) and the next input tick tosses the gun on a ballistic arc to land a little in front of the player. The tap is the grapple's way of fetching a gun for a hand that is not free, or for the gamepad's habits; the snatch is the hand's.

Both act only while the snatch's switch is on and the bindings inject, since the aim sees weapons only then.

## How a weapon is snatched

1. A weapon without a body is first freed into the world at its source. A gun in a character's hands goes through the steal's drop and stagger ([physical-weapons.md, "Taking a gun from a character's hands"](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-gun-from-a-characters-hands)), which leaves it a dropped weapon with bodies (`steal::release`, through the hands' take from a source, `source::free`, shared with the steal's take, [physical-weapons.md, "Taking a weapon from a source"](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-weapon-from-a-source)); the check that the hand can take it comes first, so a refused take leaves the gun in the character's hands. A holstered gun on a character goes the same way without a selection change (phase 3).
2. The game's hook is fired at the weapon through [`GrapplingHook::FireHookCustom`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-FireHookCustom), the entry point the game's own vehicle grapple and stunt-position grapple use, with a target info built on the weapon's game object ([`HookTargetInfo::SetupTargetFromGameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::HookTargetInfo##func-SetupTargetFromGameObject), latched at the weapon's origin, with the world position, the in-range flag, and no bone set by the mod) and the ghost-wire flag on. A ghost wire flies to the target and, when its last casts miss, as they do on a weapon, attaches to the target object itself. The player sees the game's hook and line. Before a weapon on the ground is fired at, the pickup's rules are checked (not a grenade or an unlimited-stock copy, the animation allows a pickup, and the table can take it into the hand), so a snatch the hand could not finish never flies. A freed gun is fired at on the next tick its body can be read, within `SNATCH.free_timeout_s`.
3. When the wire's hook end is attached ([`WireEnd::m_WireEndState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##field-m_WireEndState) and its proxy), the hook has bitten (`pull::bite`): the mod notes where the hook's end sits in the target body's frame, takes the native rope off the line (its constraint removed, and its re-creation and tension skipped while the line pulls, through the same exclusion the sling uses, `sling_hooks::exclude`), and plays the hook's impact. Then the line yanks (`pull::pull`): each tick the physical hands' hold solve, the physics every hand's hold on a body goes through, pulls the body toward the grapple hand's wrist, with a strength of the target's mass times `SNATCH.line_accel_mps2`, no twist of its own, and gravity in the solve. Beyond `SNATCH.approach_m` of the wrist it pulls at the hook's point at `SNATCH.line_speed_mps`, aiming `SNATCH.arc` of the distance still to go above the wrist so a far target clears the ground, and the pull at the hook rather than the centre spins a target hooked off its centre, up to `SNATCH.max_spin_rps`. Over the final approach, within `SNATCH.approach_m`, the line's speed falls in proportion to the distance to `SNATCH.arrival_speed_mps` at the wrist (`rules::line_speed`), the arc fades to nothing (`rules::pull_point`), and the line pulls at the body's centre, so the target keeps its spin but its centre flies at the wrist. The velocities the line sets are only the solve's change to the body's own. The game's line follows the target, since the hook is attached to it. A hook that flies longer than `SNATCH.attach_timeout_s`, or whose wire the game ends, fails the snatch.
4. The line steers to its receiver (`catch::receiver`): of the free, empty hands, the one whose wrist is nearest the segment from the target's centre to the eye point, whatever its grip is doing, kept until the other is nearer by `landing.hysteresis_m`. It is chosen afresh each tick while the line pulls and kept once the line is slack, and the line's speed, slack, and aim are all measured against the receiver's wrist. When the target is as near that wrist as it flies in `SNATCH.coast_s`, kept between `SNATCH.slack_min_m` and `SNATCH.slack_max_m` (`rules::slack_radius`), the line goes slack (the phase reads `INBOUND`): the line stops pulling and the target flies on by its momentum, still on the hook, for about two ticks. The catch is swept (`catch::CatchWatch`): the target is within a hand's zone when its centre passed within `SNATCH.catch_zone_m` of the hand's wrist anywhere along its path since the last tick (for a gun the landing steers, the gun's grip point against where that hand will hold the grip), and the hand catches it when its grip is closed, on nothing or on the snatch, while the target is within the zone or up to `SNATCH.catch_grace_s` after it was last there. The hands that may catch are the receiver and the free, empty hand other than the grapple hand; the grapple hand, whose grip holds the line throughout, catches only as the receiver. Of two that catch on one tick, the one the path passed nearer catches it. The wire comes away with the line's retract, and the weapon is taken into the catching hand through the direct pickup (`pickup::take`, with the source the weapon was freed from, so the table and the hand's readout keep its provenance, and `Delivery::Remote`, which the readout shows as snatched) by its grip, with a slide into the held pose as long as the landing left work for it (`landing::arrival_slide`). Every rule of the pickup applies again, and the weapon lands in the catching hand through the table, the other hand untouched. A slack target that recedes from the receiver and from every catching hand, or stops (the stall rule), while no hand with its grip open still has it within its zone or grace, or that flies longer than `SNATCH.max_slack_s`, has been missed: the wire comes away, the snatch fails as "the hand missed the catch", and the target flies on or hits the player and drops. Nothing draws the target into a hand once the line is slack.

### The aligned landing

A gun lands in the hand ready to shoot (`snatch::landing` for the math, `snatch::arrival` for the engine side). At the bite, while the gun lies still, the landing reads its body's frame (the centre of mass and the body's rotation) in the gun's own frame, the frame whose origin is the grip's attach point. Each tick of the flight then knows where the grip is, and for each catching hand, where the hand will hold the gun: the hand's IK wrist target, extrapolated by its motion to the catch (at most `landing.max_extrapolate_s`), composed with the rigid grip's canonical grip on that wrist frame (`grip::canonical_on_wrist_frame`, the right hand's chosen grip included).

The hand it steers for is the line's receiver, so the player can hold the other hand in front to receive the gun, its grip squeezed or not. The drive starts at the final approach (`SNATCH.approach_m`), or at the bite inside it, and comes to full strength over `landing.ramp_m` or one tick.

The rotation steers the flying body itself, through the same velocity setter the line uses (`physical_hands::set_body_velocities`): each tick the body's angular velocity moves toward the hand's own turn rate plus the turn still to make over the time left until the gun reaches the catch radius (`landing::turn_drive`, `landing::time_to_go`), held to `landing.max_spin_rps` and `landing.max_angular_accel`. Its gain grows as the catch nears, so the turn is closed by the catch, and it keeps running after the line goes slack, since it cannot change whether the catch happens. The position is corrected only while the line pulls: the line's aim moves off the palm to where the hand will hold the gun's centre, so the grip rather than the centre heads for the hand, by the part across the line only, held to `landing.lateral_window_m` (`landing::aim_shift`). Once the line is slack nothing moves the gun toward a hand, so a hand that moves away still misses.

At the catch the log says how far the gun arrived from where the hand holds it (`snatch: the gun arrived 2 degrees and 0.18 m from where the left hand holds it`), and the take's slide is the longer of the snap's length in proportion to the angle left over `landing.full_slide_rad` and the time the gun takes to fly the distance left, with none under `landing.settled_rad` and `landing.settled_m`.

For 300 ms after a catch the snatch watches the gun in the hand (`arrival::ArrivalWatch`): each tick it logs at info level `snatch: the caught weapon in the hand` with the weapon's state, whether its first gun's model instance is drawn ([`ModelInstanceFlags::VISIBLE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::model_instance::ModelInstanceFlags##flag-VISIBLE)), whether the rigid grip pinned it, and the slide's progress, and it shows a model found hidden while the gun is in the catching hand ([`WeaponBase::SetVisible`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetVisible), forced). The session of 2026-11-02 showed a catch with about 100 ms in which no gun was drawn, between the take (which takes the weapon out of the world and holsters it) and the first placement on a settled hand body; whether the model was hidden or drawn somewhere unseen is not settled, and the watch's lines settle it.

The mod holds a strong reference to the weapon from the fire to the end, so the despawn never takes it mid-flight. A stall while the line pulls (the body staying within `SNATCH.stall_distance_m` over `SNATCH.stall_window_s`, snagged on the world) or a weapon farther from the hand than the wire's [`m_MaxLength`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHookWire##field-m_MaxLength) fails the snatch: the wire is cancelled and the weapon is left where it is. A refused take at arrival leaves the weapon falling, logged with the reason.

The hook's state machine serialises snatches: `FireHookCustom` refuses while a line is out or the hook is busy, and the mod refuses before it while a normal fire is pending, which `FireHookCustom` does not test. While the snatch's line is out the bindings hold the grapple button back from the game, and the snatch ends its custom wire itself on every exit and every stand-down of the bindings, checking first that the wire is still the hook's active one.

## Settings

`weapons.snatch.enabled` (on by default) switches the snatch, the aim's patch, and the grapple button's ghost fire and tap at a gun together; phases 3 and 4's targets are always offered while it is on. The debug UI's Snatch feature has it, the swing's switch and tuning ([Swinging on the line](#swinging-on-the-line)), and the yank's speed and length; the yank's window stays in `weapons.snatch.yank` without a widget. The rest of the tuning is fixed in `controllers::snatch::SNATCH`: the range, the cone for guns on characters and weapons on the ground and its widening for the latter, the line's speed, arrival speed, acceleration, final approach, arc, and spin, the slack's coast and its bounds, the catch zone, its grace, and the slack flight's limit, the stall rule, the hook's flight limit, and the door's handle reach, fastest vehicle, and swing. A gun in a character's hands also needs the either-hand hold (`weapons.hold.either_hand`).

The Status page's `GRAPPLE` section has a `SNATCH` row: the snatch's state (idle, armed, freeing, hook out, pulling, inbound once the line is slack, swinging), the target offered, an object's mass, and its distance or why none is offered, the held line while it swings (not on foot, the hand not empty, the button held, a line out, the hook busy), and the snatches taken of those fired, with the last refusal as a note. The Grapple feature's readout shows the row as its headline and adds the failed snatches and the tosses. The log, at info under `controllers`, has `snatch: armed; a yank fires`, `snatch: yanked; firing` with the yank, `snatch: freed the weapon from its rack` (or `locker`), `snatch: the gun is free of its holder`, `snatch: the hook holds the weapon; pulling`, `snatch: the weapon came into the … hand` with the slot and the catching and line hands, `snatch: the object came into the … hand's hold`, `snatch: the line pulled a car door open`, `snatch: failed` with the reason (`the hand missed the catch` for a missed catch), `snatch: cancelled` with why, `snatch: a tap of the grapple reeled a weapon in`, and `snatch: tossed a weapon to the player's feet`; each grip press's log notes the remote target offered.

## Phases

### Phase 1: weapons on the ground and in characters' hands

Built, as described above, except the headset checks below.

- Engine: the scoped patch that lets the player's grapple aim see weapons, with the RE of the aim cast it patches and of what the grapple's tap and hold do with a weapon under the aim (a tap at a weapon must not zip Rico to it); bindings for `FireHookCustom`, `CancelActiveWire`, the active wire's far end and its attached state, and the weapon's grappling mode; a "Weapons and the grapple" section in [engine: grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md).
- Payload: the snatch module (armed, freeing, hook out, pulling; the arrival is the pull's last tick, and done or failed returns it to idle), `Tier::Remote`, the snatch grab volume, the yank detection, the grapple button held back during a snatch, the steal's character notice widened to the snatch's range, the setting, and the Status row.
- Tests: the yank detection, the tier's place in the resolver, and the capture and stall rules, as pure functions.

In the headset:

1. Point the empty grapple hand at a gun on the ground 10 m away: the marker shows on it. Move the hand onto a crate within reach: the crate's marker wins.
2. Squeeze, then yank: the device fires with the game's fire sound and muzzle flash, the hook flies from the left forearm, bites the gun with its impact sound and a thud on the hand, the reel's whine plays as the line yanks the gun, spinning, toward the hand, and with the hand held still it smacks into the hand with a hard thump, the line comes away with the retract, and the gun sits in the held pose. Yank again and pull the hand away as the gun arrives: it flies past or hits Rico and drops, and the snatch fails as a missed catch. Holster it, hand it over, drop it.
3. Squeeze without yanking: a bump, and the cyan latch dot and line on the gun until the hand opens. Open the hand: the latch goes and nothing flies. Hold the grapple button on a gun, aim at a car, and let go: the gun is tethered to the car.
4. Yank, then open the hand mid-flight: the line goes and the gun falls near you.
5. Snatch a gun lying a step or two away (1 to 1.5 m) a few times, holding the grapple hand still after the yank: it comes up into the hand rather than past it. Then yank and keep pulling the hand back hard: it can still miss. Snatch a rifle from 1.5 m, 5 m, and 15 m, each time holding the hand as if to shoot: it turns in the air and arrives grip first and pointing where the hand points, with no visible turn in the hand after the catch; the log's `the gun arrived ... degrees` reads under 10. The rifle is drawn throughout the catch, with no frame where the hand is empty; if one shows, the `the caught weapon in the hand` lines name the state and whether the model was drawn. Pull the trigger at once: it fires.
6. Snatch with the grapple hand, then hold the right hand out in front, between the gun and your face, its grip open: the line steers the gun to the right hand. Squeeze as it arrives, even a moment late: the right hand catches it, the thud is on the right hand, the log names the right hand, and the gun arrives aligned to the right hand's grip. Keep the right grip open: the gun flies past, and the left hand does not take it. Squeeze the right grip on the chest holster or a pouch while the line is out: it takes nothing and fires nothing. Keep the right hand down by the hip: the line steers to the left hand, which catches it.
7. Snatch a gun from a soldier at 10 m: the soldier staggers, and the gun flies in.
8. Point at a gun behind a wall: no marker. Tap the grapple button at a gun on the ground: the hook takes hold of it, Rico stays put, and the gun lands at your feet.
9. With tethers attached, try a snatch: it works with the tethers untouched, or is refused cleanly.
10. Swing a closed fist past a highlighted gun: no snatch.
11. Watch the frame time with many weapons and characters near.

### Phase 2: racks and lockers

Built for every gun a hand takes off a rack or out of a locker: the long guns, a rack's pistols, and a rack with unlimited stock. A rack's weapon has no body, the aim never hits it, and the game offers it only through the player's valid interaction entries, which hold only what is within reach. So the mod notes the weapons a placement rule holds out as the game renders them (`hold::rack::remote`, from the weapon render detour), each by a weak reference: a weapon in the rack state, and a weapon lying where its rule set it down (a locker's), known by its rule reference ([`WeaponBase::m_SpawnedFromRule`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_SpawnedFromRule)) with no holder. The render pass only notes; everything that changes a weapon runs in the input tick. The snatch offers the noted weapons whose gun shows near its ray, each gun of a rack's pistol pair on its own, labelled `WEAPON ON A RACK`, with the floor's widening cone and the same line-of-sight cast; one already lying in the world with its body is the floor's candidate instead. Up to 64 are noted at once, and the oldest is forgotten for a new one.

Firing frees the weapon through the hands' take from a source ([physical-weapons.md, "Taking a weapon from a source"](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-weapon-from-a-source)), after the same admission a hand's take makes, so the only difference from a hand's grab is the delivery:

- A rack that hands its own weapon over has [`WeaponBase::PickupStart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupStart) called for the player, which has no distance test: the weapon enters the picking-up state and sends its picked-up event, which tells its rack, so a finite rack is left as empty as a hand leaves it. The weapon then drops into the world where it hangs ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop), whose world addition places each body at its gun's model frame). A locker's weapon drops the same way.
- A rack with unlimited stock ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup) in the rack state) is asked for a copy as a hand's take asks (`hold::rack::copy`, [physical-weapons.md, "Taking a weapon off a rack"](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-weapon-off-a-rack)): the rack's placement rule hears of the one pickup, the rack's weapon is hidden, and the copy spawns a few frames later and is placed where the rack's weapon hangs. For the snatch it is then shown and dropped into the world there, and the snatch waits for it (`FREEING`, up to three seconds) before the hook flies. The rack's weapon fades back in a moment later, restocked. A locker weapon with unlimited stock is not offered: its copy would be dropped inside the bodies of the weapon still lying there.
- A rack's pistol pair gives up the pistol aimed at, or the other while the aimed one has already left ([paired-rack-weapons.md, "From range"](paired-rack-weapons.md#from-range)): the snatch observes the pair as a hand's reach does, so its staged pistol is asked for, and once it is staged the pistol is claimed from the pair, shown alone, and dropped into the world where it hung. The claim is settled at once as a take that left: a finite pair's first claim commits its ammunition split, so a missed catch leaves one loose single pistol holding its share, and the other pistol stays on the rack with the rest.

From there it is the freeing of a stolen gun: the hook fires once the body lies in the world, the line pulls it in, and the aligned landing seats it in the catching hand ready to shoot. The catch takes it through `pickup::take` with its source kept (a rack, a rack's copy, or a locker), delivered remotely.

Not offered:

- A locker weapon with unlimited stock, as above. A hand in reach takes its copy.

In the headset:

1. Aim the empty grapple hand at a rifle on a finite weapon rack 10 m away: the marker shows on it and the row reads `WEAPON ON A RACK`. Squeeze and yank: the log has `snatch: freed the weapon from its rack`, the rifle drops off the rack and flies in on the line, and it lands grip first in the hand, ready to fire. Walk up to the rack: the slot is empty, as after a hand's take.
2. Do the same at a locker's long gun: `freed the weapon from its locker`, and the same flight and landing.
3. Snatch from a rack with the hand's take refused (both hands full, or the slot's rules against it): nothing leaves the rack, and the snatch fails with the take's refusal.
4. Point at a rack behind its own cabinet door or at a wall: no marker, or a marker that a yank still reaches; note any rack whose weapon the line of sight never clears.
5. Snatch a rifle from a rack with unlimited stock: `hold: asked a rack for a copy for the right hand`, the rack's rifle disappears, the phase reads `FREEING` for a few frames, then a rifle drops from where it hung and flies in, landing grip first; the rack's rifle fades back in. Walk up and snatch or grab again: the rack restocks every time, as a hand's take leaves it. Check the copy arrives with a full magazine, that the reserve gains only its stored rounds, and how long `FREEING` lasts. Watch whether the dropped copy appears exactly where the rack's rifle hung, or jumps (where its bodies are placed for a staged copy is unverified).
6. Snatch the left pistol of a finite pistol pair (the Guardia Massos racks of the Wraith or the Spectre): `snatch: claiming a pistol of a rack's pair`, then `snatch: freed a pistol of a rack's pair`, only the left pistol leaves and flies in as one pistol, and the right stays on the rack. Snatch the right one too: the rack is left empty, as two hand takes leave it. Check the two pistols' magazines and the reserve add up to what the pair held, and that the rack's placement rule heard one pickup (the rack stays empty after leaving and returning). Miss a catch on purpose: the pistol lies where it falls with its share, and the other stays on the rack.
7. Where to find a rack with unlimited stock is not recorded in the code, the docs, or the placement-rule defs, and none was seen in the 2 November 2026 session. Try the racks of a liberated settlement or outpost, the rebel bases, and any resupply point you pass: a take of one logs `hold: asked a rack for a copy` rather than `took the weapon off a rack`. Note where one was found.
8. Note how the AI and the rack react to a weapon taken from range.

### Phase 3: holstered weapons on characters, and props

Built. The steal notes, with each character it notes, the guns holstered and visible on its body (the pistol, two-handed, and special slots in the holstered state), and the snatch offers them as it offers a gun in hands. Firing at one drops it as the player's own replaced weapon is dropped ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop), then [`Inventory::DropWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DropWeapon) without a selection change), the character staggers, and the gun is snatched as one lying in the world; the take's rules are checked before it leaves the character.

A loose object under the aim is offered by the hand's own classification of what it reaches ([physical-hands.md, "Picking things up"](physical-hands.md#picking-things-up), `physical_hands::remote::loose_mass`), so whatever a hand would take as a loose object the snatch takes too: not a character, a vehicle or a part still on it, or a weapon, with a finite mass. That includes a door torn off a car and a placed object at rest whose body is not yet dynamic, such as a crate or a tyre set in the world. It is offered as an object when one hand could lift it, no heavier than the hand's strongest pull holds up. A heavier one, up to the swing's ceiling, is offered as a heavy object and swung on the line instead ([Swinging on the line](#swinging-on-the-line)). A placed object stays where it was placed until the hook bites, when it is turned loose as the game turns one loose (`physical_hands::remote::loosen`, [havok-bodies.md, "Placed bodies becoming dynamic"](../../engine/physics/havok-bodies.md#placed-bodies-becoming-dynamic)) and the line pulls or swings it as any other. The hook takes hold of it as of a weapon, the same line yanks it in, and on the catch the wire ends and the object is handed to the hand's ordinary hold (`physical_hands::remote`), taken at its surface on the line from the palm to its centre: the next palms' tick takes hold of it as of a grab in reach, and the grip's release lets go of it, thrown with the hand's motion.

A grenade lying inert in the world (dropped with its pin in, and still the loadout's) is offered as a grenade rather than as a loose object (`OfferKind::Grenade`, marked as an object, labelled `GRENADE`). It is a candidate off the ray as a weapon lying in the world is, within the same widening floor cone and with the same line-of-sight cast (`grenade::inert_lying`), since a grenade is smaller still than a gun lying flat and the ray's exact hit is more than a tracked hand can hold on it. The hook takes hold of it and the same line yanks it in, and on the catch the wire ends and the grenade is handed to the weapon handling, which takes it into the hand through the loadout at the next input tick, as a hand closing on it would ([grenades.md, "Dropped grenades"](grenades.md#dropped-grenades)); the take clicks the hand. A grenade whose pin is out is a live grenade and not a snatch target: a free hand catches it.

A grenade a character drops is normally made one of the mod's own as it drops, and is snatched as the dropped grenade above ([grenades.md, "Grenades lying in the world"](grenades.md#grenades-lying-in-the-world)). A hand grenade the game left lying in the world as its own pickup (a native grenade weapon, such as one a character dropped, which the game collects by proximity only while the grenade reserve has room) is one grenade, and the snatch treats it as the physical grenade it is (`OfferKind::WorldGrenade`, labelled `GRENADE`, offered only for a finite one, not [`m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)). The pickup refuses it, since the game never picks a hand grenade up, so it is not taken as a weapon: the line yanks the native grenade's own body in, and the catch takes that grenade into the hand at the next input tick (`grenade::take_world_pickup`), the same take a free hand closing on it makes ([grenades.md, "Grenades lying in the world"](grenades.md#grenades-lying-in-the-world)). A missed catch leaves it lying where it falls. A grenade on a display (a locker's or a rack's) is not a snatch target: it stays where it is until a hand in reach takes it ([grenades.md, "Locker sources"](grenades.md#locker-sources)). Offered as a weapon on the ground, a hand grenade failed at the yank with "a hand grenade is collected by the game".

In the headset:

1. Snatch the pistol on a soldier's hip while it holds its rifle: the soldier staggers, keeps the rifle, and the pistol flies in.
2. Drop a grenade with its pin in a few metres off and aim near it rather than at it: the `GRENADE` marker comes up, and the snatch brings it to the hand.
3. Snatch a gas canister or a box a hand can carry: it arcs in and stays in the hand's hold; let go and it falls or flies with the hand. Aim at a fuel barrel: it is offered as a heavy object (the swing's checks are under [Swinging on the line](#swinging-on-the-line)). Aim at a hay bale or a container: no marker.
4. Tear a car door off and walk a few metres away from where it lies: aim at it and it is offered as a heavy object (`HEAVY OBJECT`, its mass in the readout), and the hook swings it. A door still hanging on its car is offered as a car door only while it is closed, as before.
5. Aim at a placed explosive crate and a tyre that nothing has disturbed: each is offered by its mass, and on the bite the log has `physical hands: a placed object was turned loose for the snatch` and it comes off its spot. Aim at a building or a wall: no marker.

### Phase 4: car doors

Built, and offered while the doors' own `physical_hands.doors.enabled` is on. A closed door of the vehicle under the aim whose handle area is within `SNATCH.door_reach_m` of the aim's point is the target, found by the doors' own reach ([vehicle-doors.md](vehicle-doors.md)) with the aim's point standing in for the palm; a vehicle moving faster than `SNATCH.door_max_vehicle_speed_mps`, read from the body the aim hit, is refused. The yank fires the hook at the vehicle, latched at the handle in the vehicle's frame. Once it holds, the door's latch is undone as a hand's pull undoes it, the door's own body is pushed outward at the handle by its mass times `SNATCH.door_swing_mps`, and the wire ends. The door swings; nothing comes into the hand.

In the headset:

1. Aim at a parked car's closed door near its handle, squeeze, and yank: the hook takes hold, the door comes open and swings out, and the line comes away.
2. Aim at a moving car's door: no marker. Aim at the middle of a door, far from its handle: no marker.

## Decisions

- A tap of the grapple button at a gun tosses the gun to the player's feet ([The grapple button at a gun](#the-grapple-button-at-a-gun)): the game's "reel in, no attach" read as the object reeled in, not the player, and the hook's line ends there, so nothing zips and the gun comes within a step.
- The hook latches the weapon at its origin rather than at its grip: a dropped weapon's grip has no cheap object-space point, and the pull and the capture work on the body's centre of mass either way.
- The line's yank is the hold solve with the line's own speed and strength and no twist: the target keeps its momentum, the pull off its centre spins it, and nothing places it. The catch is the hand's: a swept test along the target's path, so a fast target is not stepped over, with a radius of the hand's own reach and no pull toward the hand. A pull whose speed fell to nothing with the distance eased the target into a capture radius and a slide, which read as placed rather than thrown, so the line slows only to `SNATCH.arrival_speed_mps` and still goes slack before the hand rather than braking: the target arrives hard, as a yanked thing does.
- The landing's tuning comes from the same replay, with the gun's rotation added: each target starting at a random orientation and the hook's spin up to `SNATCH.max_spin_rps`, its grip 10 to 20 cm off its centre, and the hand at a random orientation turning slowly. Today's landing caught 44.9 of 48 with the gun arriving a median 133° (90th percentile 171°) off the held pose, which the take's slide turned in 0.12 s. With the landing, the catches stay at 44.9 of 48, the arrival is a median 0.2° off (90th percentile 1.1°, 98% under 10°), the slide a median 14 ms, and a path knocked 0.4 m off the hand is still caught 2% of the time, as before. Most of the session's snatches start 1 to 1.6 m out and fly three or four ticks, so the turn has to be fast: under the line's own 25 rad/s and a 600 rad/s² acceleration, those landed a median 7° and a 90th percentile 62° off, which is why the landing has its own caps of 40 rad/s and 1200 rad/s². A double-integrator law with gains of 6/t² and 4/t left 10° to 40° over the same few ticks, so the drive asks for the rate that closes the turn by the catch instead.
- The catch's tuning comes from the recorded session of 2026-11-02 (05:51), whose 48 pulled snatches the recording's wrists and snatch phases let a model of the line replay, each target's start fitted to the recorded moment its line went slack (`INBOUND`). The first tuning (a flat 18 m/s with no final approach, 160 m/s², slack at a fixed 1 m, and the line pulling at the hook to the end) missed 7, all but one from under 1.7 m, where the line had little time to speed the target up. Two things lose the catch there. The line fixed the target's path when it was still a metre out, so wherever the hand went during the slow flight that followed (the misses' hands still moved at about 1.6 m/s from the yank, the catches' at about 0.4), the arc's rise above the hand, and the drop under gravity all ended up as miss distance. And the pull at the hook spins the target, so the solve that matches the hook's velocity to the line leaves the centre's off it by the spin at the hook, and the slack target's centre carries that sideways. A model with that spin error, sized so it misses as often as the session did (41 of 48), catches about 45 of 48 with the current tuning, the short-range misses about all, and a target whose path is knocked 0.4 m off the hand at the slack is still caught 5% of the time against 20% before. No bend of the slack target toward the palm is added: within the slack's reach, a cone small enough to keep the catch physical covers less than the catch radius already does.
- Either hand catches, and the other hand's grip is held for the catch while the line is out, so a squeeze to catch never takes what the hand happens to be near. The session of 2026-11-06 caught almost nothing in the right hand: only a hand whose grip was already held counted, the line always aimed at the grapple hand's wrist, and the grapple hand's own held grip took every target that reached it. So the line steers to the hand held out to receive whatever its grip does, the grapple hand catches only when it is that hand, and a squeeze up to `SNATCH.catch_grace_s` late still catches.
- The snatch's yank has its own thresholds, gentler than the zip's (10 cm at 0.7 m/s), since the grip is already the arming and the yank only confirms. Recorded seated pulls peak at 0.8 to 1.5 m/s along the aim. A settings file that already holds `weapons.snatch.yank` keeps its saved values over these defaults.
- The hook's impact is played at the bite through a real cast at the target ([The snatch's sounds](#the-snatchs-sounds)): [`WireEnd::PlayImpactEffect`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-PlayImpactEffect) wants a cast hit's material, which the cast supplies.
- The switch is in the debug UI only, as the plan says, and not on the pause menu's VR pages.
- A holstered gun is dropped without a selection change, so the character keeps what it wields and the stagger is its whole reaction; a gun in its hands goes through the selection, as the steal's does.
- A caught object becomes the hand's ordinary hold where the hand caught it, at its surface nearest the palm: the hold's own solve takes its momentum, within the hand's strength, as for an object taken in reach.
- A car door's target is its handle area near where the aim hits the vehicle, not a cone around the ray, since a door is a large surface the aim lands on directly.
- A heavy object swings on the line rather than coming to the hand, and the line is a rope rather than a rod: it pulls but never pushes, so it reads as a line and needs no rule for a target pushed away. The reel to a swing length is what makes a far target swingable at all, since a hand's motion turns a 15 m line by a few degrees.
- The line's strength grows with the square root of the target's mass by default rather than holding a fixed acceleration, so a heavier target takes a bigger swing, and the ceiling, not a weak line, keeps vehicles and wrecks out. Vehicles are excluded outright for now, however light.
- The brace is taken by holding the other grip near the wrist, not only by a fresh squeeze there: that grip is already held for the snatch while the line is out, and a hand that squeezed early and then came to the wrist should brace.

## Open questions

- Whether the AI reserves a dropped weapon it walks toward, and what it does when the weapon leaves.
- Whether the game's fire plays `GrapplePreFire` before `GrappleFire`, and whether the snatch should play it on the arming squeeze ([The snatch's sounds](#the-snatchs-sounds)).
- Whether a target caught close to the body collides with Rico's collision proxy before it reaches the hand. A hand held near the chest may see it bounce off, which reads as a miss.
- Whether every placed barrel and crate is dynamic before it is disturbed. A prop the game keeps static or keyframed until it is hit reads as no loose object, and is offered only once it has been knocked loose.
- Whether a swung barrel's explosion on impact needs a harder hit than a swing gives, and whether the game counts the player as its instigator.
- Whether the hook's state is ever left parked off inactive with no wire, which would refuse the custom fire ("the hook is busy" in the `SNATCH` row).

Settled by the phase 1 RE: the custom fire is accepted while dual tethers are attached (they are not the active wire), and the hook ignores the grapple input in its custom state; a ghost wire passes through walls, so the line of sight is the aim's own cast for a weapon on the ground and the snatch's cast for a gun in hands.
