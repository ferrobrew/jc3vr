# Physical weapons

A weapon behaves as one physical object: it is held in whichever hand took it, it moves between hands, it is picked up off the ground by the hand that touches it, and it goes onto the body and comes off it as the same object. The loadout table says where every weapon is, in which hand or on which socket ([two-handed-inventory.md](two-handed-inventory.md)), and the game's slots follow it; the game keeps owning the weapon objects, the reserves, and the HUD of the selected weapon, and the mod owns where each gun is and which hand fires it. The engine side is in [weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md) and [item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md), and the attach bones in [hands-locomotion-and-state.md](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md).

None of this has been run in the headset. Everything acts only while the bindings inject, under the controller scheme with a controller active. The code is `payload/src/controllers/weapons/hold/`.

## Either hand

With `weapons.hold.either_hand` (on; it and the other switches in this doc are in the debug UI's Controllers tab, mostly under "Weapons"), a weapon rides the hand the loadout table holds it in rather than always the right hand ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The live table"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-live-table)), so the hold needs the VR inventory on (`weapons.inventory.enabled`), which runs the table; without it the game's own layout stands. The table's hands change by the hands' own moves at once: a draw off a socket, a dock onto one, a direct pickup or a take, a handover, a let-go. A weapon the game wields on its own lands in a hand of the table's once per input tick:

- A direct pickup or a take (below) leaves the grabbing hand expecting that one weapon, so another weapon the game wields first does not take it. The expectation lasts up to three seconds before it lapses.
- A weapon the game wields on its own (the weapon wheel, a switch, an automatic draw) lands in the acting hand (`controllers.acting_hand()`) when the table has it empty, otherwise in the other hand.
- A weapon the game puts away on its own (its holster act, a vehicle's entry) leaves its hand in the table, docked on the socket the game docks its slot on, or let go of when it left the slots.

A weapon lands only in a hand the table has empty: while both hands hold something, a weapon the game wields is put away again.

The log line `hold: the weapon is in the left hand` (or right) names the hand each time a weapon the game wields comes into the hands, with the cause (`pickup`, a take's source, or `the game's draw`).

With `weapons.holsters.long_guns_dominant` (on), a long gun is drawn off the back only by the dominant hand while neither hand holds a weapon (`volumes::reserve_long_guns`): the back's sockets and the long guns docked on them are offered to that hand alone, and marked for it alone. The layout follows the gun hand (the trigger that fires, the tethers' retract, the pouches, and the wrist detonator), so a rifle drawn by reaching to the near shoulder with the off-hand used to swap it. A right-handed player reaches across to the left shoulder for the special weapon. With a weapon already in a hand, either hand takes a long gun.

A single pistol ([below](#separate-pistols)) is a weapon of one gun and is moved as a rifle is. The game's pair, kept whole while the pistols are not made separate, is one weapon of the table's, held in one hand with its second gun in the other.

## The owned attach bones

A gun is placed at the world matrix of the bone its attach-bone table ([`WeaponData::m_lBoneAttachementPerWeaponState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_lBoneAttachementPerWeaponState)) names for the weapon's state. The game rewrites the tables at the end of every pose-finalization pass ([`WeaponBase::UpdatePostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdatePostFinalizePose)) and its state tasks move guns between bones within the pass, so the mod writes the holding hand's bones at three points: before each placement ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)), after the end-of-pass rewrite ([`Inventory::UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose)), and at the weapon's own per-tick update, before it fires. Both detours live in `hold/mod.rs`.

A single gun on the other hand's attach bone is moved to its counterpart on the holding hand (the right hand's primary or secondary attach bone to the left hand's primary or secondary, and back), whatever the slot: a long gun, or a pistol. A weapon of two guns (the game's pair) keeps a gun in each hand, its first in the holding hand and its second in the other, so held in the left hand its layout is mirrored rather than gathered into one hand (`hold::layout::placed_bone`). Either way the write is the same however many times it is made, so the three write points agree. The game's own moves within a hand, and a gun it puts on a holster or back bone, are kept. Both the wielding and the dual-wielding entries are written, and the weapon's state is never changed. A gun whose hand changed since its last placement skips its render interpolation for that placement ([`WeaponBase::SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame)), so a handover moves the gun in one frame rather than streaking across.

Everything that derives a gun's hand from its attach bone follows without change: the rigid grip pins the gun to the holding wrist, the gun aim casts the holding hand's sight line, the shot's hand drives the recoil, the heft, and the fire haptics, the reload works with the free hand, and the render-time late update corrects the gun by the holding hand. The magazine pouch is laid out on the side away from the holding hand, since it follows the gun's hand (`volumes::gun_side`). The character-level aim point is the dominant hand's while it holds a gun, and the other hand's otherwise.

## The right hand's grip

The rigid grip pins a gun to its hand's wrist at the hand's canonical grip, the hand's attach bone's pose on its wrist in the skeleton's bind pose ([controllers.md](controllers.md)). The skeleton relates its left and right bones with every local axis flipped rather than as mirror images: the two hands' grips have the same rotation and opposite offsets, as Rico's finger bones do. One gun model hung off both attach bones therefore sits in one hand turned half round from the other hand's mirror. The left hand's grip is the one the game's own animation holds a gun at in the left hand, and it holds the game's pistols the right way round; the game's animation holds a gun in the right hand about 150 degrees from the right hand's grip, so that grip on its own holds the gun backwards. The left hand always takes its own grip. With `weapons.hold.right_grip`, every gun in the right hand, the game's pair's right pistol included, takes one of:

- Automatic (the default): the right hand's own grip, unless it is turned more than 45 degrees from the left hand's grip mirrored onto the right wrist, which it then takes;
- The right hand's own: the right hand's own grip always;
- The left hand's mirrored: the left hand's grip (its attach bone's bind pose) mirrored across the body onto the right wrist's bind pose always. The body's lateral axis is the axis the two wrists' bind positions differ most along, and the mirror keeps the gun right-handed by flipping its own lateral axis, which is the mirror image of a gun symmetric across its own lateral axis.

The mirrored grip is read once per skeleton and logged with the angle between the two (`rigid grip: the left hand's grip mirrored onto the right wrist`), and the debug UI's Status tab shows which grip the right hand took and that angle (`RIGHT HAND GRIP`), so the two can be compared in the headset; a small angle would mean the skeleton's right attach bone already mirrors the left.

## Firing

Only the holding hand's trigger fires a gun. With a gun in the off-hand, that hand's trigger presses the off-hand fire action (`fire_off_hand_pistol`), and the tether retract moves to the off-hand's stick click (`Controls::dual_wield` is set for any gun in the off-hand, in a mode whose triggers fire the hand weapons). Both triggers drive the one fire action, so while the wielded gun is in one hand and only the other hand's trigger is pulled, the weapon handling holds the fire inputs back (`weapons::primary_trigger_idle`).

With `weapons.hold.per_hand_tick` (on), the fire action reaches no gun: every gun in a hand fires from that hand's own trigger, the wielded weapon too, whose update the inventory's fan-out runs with its hand's fire input in place of the game's, and each gun armed beside it, which the mod updates ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The per-hand tick"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-per-hand-tick)). A gun's hand fires it while its trigger is pulled and nothing holds it back: the hand carries it by a part other than its grip, its dock's slide runs, a reload act runs with it, the true reload holds it, or the animation forbids shooting. The game's pair fires from the game's own fire input, which alternates its guns.

## Handing a gun over

With `weapons.hold.handover` (on), a grab volume (`GrabVolume::Handle`) sits on the grip of the gun held in one hand while the other is free, wielded or armed there ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)), at the holding hand's grip point in its weighted pose (the foregrip and the heft applied), with radius `weapons.hold.handle_radius_m` (0.07 m), for the free hand only. A grip press there hands the gun to the free hand: the table moves it at once, the attach-bone tables follow on the next placement, and a foregrip hold ends. The grab pulses the grab cue. The volume is placed only while the hold owns the weapon's bones and the free hand holds nothing, and the deepest volume wins where it overlaps the weapon's action or the foregrip, which sit further along the gun. The log line is `hold: handed the weapon to the left hand` (or right).

With `weapons.regions.enabled`, a long gun's handover volume sits on its grip wherever the gun is, and the foregrip is wherever the free hand closes on the gun rather than only the gun's own foregrip point; a hand can also carry a long gun by another part, unable to fire it ([physical-guns.md, "Grabbing a gun anywhere"](physical-guns.md#grabbing-a-gun-anywhere)).

## Picking a weapon up into the hand

With `weapons.hold.direct_pickup` (on), a grab of a weapon lying in the world ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md)) takes that weapon straight into the grabbing hand instead of clicking the use-item action. On the input tick, with the hand's reach from the last simulation tick, it checks that the reached body still belongs to the weapon (a weapon's body leaves the world when it is picked up or despawned, so this proves the object is alive), that the weapon is still dropped, that it is not a hand grenade and does not give a copy ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)), and that the animation allows a pickup ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsPickup)). It then leaves the hand pending for that weapon and calls [`WeaponBase::PickupItem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupItem) with the character's game-object subobject, the call a swimming character's pickup makes: the weapon goes into the inventory and is equipped there and then, its magazine as it was, with no pickup animation. The weapon already in the slot leaves the slot first and stays where the table has it on the body ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The weapons beyond the slots"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-weapons-beyond-the-slots)); a weapon the table does not have on the body (with the VR inventory off) is dropped or despawned by the game's own inventory add, as with the game's pickup, unless `weapons.hold.drop_replaced` (on) lets it fall first, as a drop below does it, so it lands rather than vanishes (`hold: dropped the weapon the pickup replaces`). The log line is `hold: picked the weapon up into the left hand` (or right), with the cause. A weapon picked up this way is followed into its hand at once, so the next placement already puts it there. With `weapons.hold.take_slide` (on), the gun then slides from where it lay into the hand over `weapons.hold.snap_s`, the same slide a draw off a socket uses, starting from the first gun's pose in the world as it was before the pickup, so it is seen to leave the ground rather than appear in the hand.

A refused pickup falls back to the use-item click, so the game picks a weapon up its own way, and the readout counts it with the refusal. The one refusal that does not fall back is a pickup that ran but left the weapon out of the hands, since the call already happened.

## Taking a gun from a character's hands

With `weapons.hold.steal` (on), a free hand takes a gun out of a living character's hands by grabbing it (`hold::steal`). A gun in a character's hands has no body in the physics world, so the hands' reach never finds it. Instead the inventory's end-of-pass detour, which runs for every character, notes each other character within a few metres of the player that wields a gun of the pistol, two-handed, or special slot: the gun's segment from its holding grip to its other grip ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition)), its first gun's pose, and a weak reference to the character taken from its own ([`Character::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WeakThis)), which says whether it still lives without keeping it alive. The input tick carries the segments into the cockpit frame (through the head's pose in the world and the cockpit, and the view frame) and offers each hand without a gun the point of each segment nearest it, as a grip target within `weapons.hold.take_radius_m` (0.1 m) of the hand as it is drawn (`GrabVolume::WorldWeapon`, `hold::take`), marked gold where the hand would take the gun. A grip there takes the gun whose segment is nearest the hand:

1. the character is locked alive for the whole take ([`WeakPtrGameObject::Lock`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/types::shared_ptr::WeakPtrGameObject##func-Lock)), still wields the gun in the slot noted, and the player's animation allows a pickup;
2. the gun is dropped at once while the character's inventory still holds it ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop)), which unequips it from the character;
3. the character's inventory lets the slot go with its selection on ([`Inventory::DropWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DropWeapon)), as its death does, so it selects another weapon it carries; the gun is already dropped, so no second drop is deferred;
4. the character staggers toward the player ([`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction)), with a melee impulse just over its stumble threshold and under its knockdown threshold, so it reacts to the yank without falling;
5. the gun goes into the grabbing hand as a weapon off the ground does (the direct pickup above, with its checks), and slides there from where it was in the character's hands.

What the character does next is the game's handling of a character with nothing wielded ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "A character that loses its gun"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#a-character-that-loses-its-gun)): it draws another weapon it carries, and may go looking for one lying in the world. The log line is `hold: took the gun out of a character's hands into the left hand` (or right), and a refused take logs `hold: no weapon taken` with the reason. The direct pickup must be on, since the take ends in it.

## Taking a weapon off a rack

With `weapons.hold.racks` (on), a free hand takes a weapon off a rack by grabbing it (`hold::rack`). A rack's weapon has no body in the physics world either, so the input tick walks the player's valid interaction entries as a swimming character's pickup does ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "Racks"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#racks)), takes each weapon an entry offers in the rack state or lying in the world (held alive while it is read), and offers it as a gun in a character's hands is offered. A locker's weapon is offered the same way but is not in the rack state; that it lies in the world, set down at a spot by its placement rule, is unverified, and such a weapon is taken as a weapon off the ground is, without the game's pickup start. One a character holds or wields, one already being picked up, and a mounted gun are left out, and an offered weapon in any other state is refused with its state in the log. A grip there finds the weapon again among the entries, holding it alive through the take, and:

- a weapon the rack hands over goes into the grabbing hand as a weapon off the ground does, after the call that starts the game's own pickup of it ([`WeaponBase::PickupStart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupStart)), which takes it out of the rack's state and tells the rack, and slides there from the rack. The log line is `hold: took the weapon off a rack into the left hand` (or right);
- a rack with unlimited stock ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)) gives a copy instead, which the game spawns a few frames later and equips. The take leaves the hand pending for the next weapon wielded, starts the game's pickup, which asks for the copy, and hides the rack's own weapon ([`WeaponBase::SetVisible`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetVisible)), holding it alive. When the copy comes into the hand, it slides there from where the rack's weapon was, so the weapon is seen to leave the rack, and the rack's weapon fades back in a moment later, restocked. A copy that has not come within three seconds lets the rack's weapon show again (`hold: the rack's copy never came`). The log lines are `hold: asked a rack for a copy for the left hand` and `hold: the rack's copy is in the left hand`.

The copy is a new object, which the brief's illusion cannot avoid with the game's racks; the hiding and the slide are what keep it from showing. The rack's own weapon keeps its pickup, so the game's use-item action still works there.

## The sockets

Every weapon on the body sits on a socket of the loadout table's ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The places and their invariants"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-places-and-their-invariants)): each hip, each shoulder, the outer back, and the chest. What a grab at a socket does is the table's decision ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The grabs against the table"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-grabs-against-the-table)), with `weapons.holsters.enabled` (on): an empty hand draws the weapon on the socket, and a hand holding a weapon docks it on a free socket that fits it. Nothing selects a slot or asks the game to holster.

### Their volumes

The sockets' volumes are seated from the table each input tick, and reached by the hand as it is drawn (`weapons::docks`), so what the player sees on the body is what they grab, wherever the animation, the procedural body, or the chase puts the bone a socket places its weapon on:

- a socket holding a weapon has a volume on the weapon's grip ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition), or the gun's origin without one), for each hand that may draw it;
- a free socket that takes the weapon a hand holds has a volume where that weapon would sit there, for that hand: where the weapon's grip was on the socket's bone when it was last seen there, or else at the bone, moved by the socket's offset when the game does not dock the weapon's slot there itself;
- a socket of the body that does not take the weapon a hand holds (taken, or too small for it) is marked for that hand with a small red disc while the hand is near, never taken, so the hand knows before it opens.

Each volume's reach is its kind's: `holsters.hip.radius_m` (0.12 m) for the hips, `holsters.shoulder.radius_m` (0.17 m) for the shoulders and the outer back, and `holsters.chest.radius_m` (0.09 m) for the chest. With `weapons.holsters.follow_docks` (on) off, the hips, the shoulders, and the chest are reached at the body's landmarks instead ([controllers.md](controllers.md#sockets-pouches-the-foregrip-and-the-action-runs-tier)). The volumes on the parts of a long gun docked on the body ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md)) are reached by the drawn hand too. The grip's place on its bone is taken from the gun's last placement and the bone's pose now, so a gun noted while the body moves fast sits slightly off; an offset longer than 0.6 m is taken for a bad read and not noted. The debug UI's Status tab shows how many sockets' volumes sit on their weapons (`SOCKETS`).

### Drawing and docking as one object

A draw and a dock take no act: the gun is the same object throughout, and slides between the socket and the hand over `weapons.hold.snap_s` (0.12 s), eased in and out (`hold::act`).

A draw checks that the animation allows one ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsDrawing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsDrawing)), measures how the gun sits on the socket's bone (its placement relative to the bone's current pose in the skeleton), and tells the table, whose reconcile wields the weapon when the hand is now the primary ([`WeaponBase::OnEquip`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnEquip)) or arms it in the hand beside the other hand's weapon (below). Its gun rides the hand's attach bone at once; for the snap's duration the rigid grip places that bone part of the way from where the gun sat (the bone's current pose composed with the measured offset, so the start follows the body) toward the hand's grip (`hands::grip::docked_grip`). The log line is `hold: drew the weapon off the right shoulder into the left hand`.

A dock of the wielded weapon checks [`GetAnimationAllowsHolstering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsHolstering). The gun slides from the hand to where it sat on the socket at its last draw off it, held back from firing meanwhile, and when the slide ends the table takes the dock, the wielded weapon is unequipped ([`WeaponBase::OnUnequip`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnUnequip)), and the reconcile wields the other hand's weapon when that hand is the primary now, or disarms a weapon armed in the docking hand. A weapon not drawn off that socket since the payload loaded has no measured pose there, and docks at once without the slide. The log line is `hold: docked the weapon from the right hand on the right hip`. A dock by a grab and a dock by opening the grip over the socket both pulse the grab cue (`Event::VolumeGrab`) on the docking hand as the dock begins. A heavy weapon docks nowhere yet: the game drops a heavy weapon it unequips, so every socket is marked as refusing it and opening the grip lets it go.

Each hand has its own slide, so both hands draw and dock at once. The rigid grip shows a slide only while the hand's controller weight is above zero; a hand the controllers do not drive shows the switch at once. The holstered scale of a long gun on the back, when it is not one, still pops at the switch.

## Letting go

With `weapons.hold.grip_to_hold` (on; `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD GUNS`), a gun is held only while its grip is. Each hand watches the weapon the table has in it (`hold::dock::ReleaseWatch`). A gun taken by a grab (a draw, a direct pickup, a take, or a handover) is watched from the tick it is in the hand with the grip held. A gun that came into the hand some other way (the weapon wheel, a draw the game made) is watched from the tick the grip closes on it: a press, not a grip already held when the gun arrived, so a hand that held a vehicle's control while the game drew the gun does not drop it on letting go (logged at info, `hold: the grip closed on the gun in the hand`). Opening a watched grip does, as the table decides (`loadout::grab::release`):

- over a free socket that takes the gun, the nearest under the hand, it docks the gun there;
- anywhere else, it lets the gun fall, unless the other hand braces a long gun, which then stays in that hand, carried where it holds it ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md));
- for the game's pair, which cannot fall, it docks the pair on the first free socket the body prefers for it, or keeps it in the hand with none free.

A stand-down (a tracking or focus loss, the debug panel, a load) stops the watch but leaves the gun in the hand. The returning hand is masked until its controls rest, so a grip held through the loss reads open until the hand opens; the gun is kept through that, and is the grip's again once the grip closes on it. Entering a vehicle lets nothing fall: the watch acts only in the modes the hand weapons are used in, and the game holsters the gun as the player gets in. With `grip_to_hold` off, a gun stays in the hand until a grab of a socket docks it.

A drop of the wielded weapon reads the first gun's measured velocity and angular velocity, which the rigid grip makes the hand's, runs the weapon's drop at once while the inventory still holds it ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop)), the call a swimming character's pickup makes on a slot's occupant, then sets the gun's body ([`WeaponData::m_PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_PfxInstance)) to the hand's motion, the linear part capped at `weapons.hold.max_throw_mps` (15 m/s), through the hand bodies' owned-body velocity write (which checks that the body is dynamic and still the weapon's), and finally clears the slot ([`Inventory::DropWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DropWeapon), whose own deferred drop does nothing to a weapon already dropped). The game's drop alone would give the weapon the character's motion rather than the hand's, since it stops using the weapon first and so drops it as if from the holster ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "Dropping"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#dropping)). A heavy weapon is dropped through the slot alone, which already gives it the hand's motion. A weapon armed in a hand is disarmed and dropped the same way, out of its slot or the carry set. Either way the table takes the let-go, and the weapon lies in the world, where the VR inventory keeps it while near ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The weapons in the world"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-weapons-in-the-world)). The log line is `hold: let go of the weapon in the left hand` with the speed and whether the mod set it.

## A weapon in each hand

The game wields one weapon, the selected slot's, which the reconcile makes the primary hand's ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The slots as a projection"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-slots-as-a-projection)). A weapon the table holds in the other hand is armed there by the mod (`hold::armed`, per-hand runtime state beside the table):

- It stays in the holstered state, so the inventory, the selection, the HUD, and the saves see it as holstered, and its bodies stay out of the world. Its guns' holstered entries in the attach-bone table name the hand's attach bone, written at the same three points as the wielded weapon's, and its holstered scale ([`WeaponData::m_HolsteredScaleValue`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_HolsteredScaleValue)) is one while it is in the hand; both are restored when it is disarmed.
- It gets the character as its user ([`WeaponBase::StartUsing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-StartUsing)), so its fire code runs as the player's, and loses it when it is disarmed ([`WeaponBase::StopUsing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-StopUsing), which leaves a holstered weapon holstered).
- After the inventory's own weapon updates ([`Inventory::UpdateWeapons_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeapons_Parallel) and [`UpdateWeapons_Serial`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeapons_Serial)), the per-hand tick (`hold::tick`) updates it through its [`UpdateFromUserPostSim_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateFromUserPostSim_Parallel) and `_Serial` virtuals, on the same thread and in the same phase, with its hand's own fire input while the animation allows shooting ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsShooting`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsShooting)), and its own sight line's aim point, cast in the aim update like the wielded weapon's; without a current one the weapon is not updated. The base update has no state check and runs the guns' attached effects, so the muzzle effects are its own.
- The rigid grip pins it to the hand, the per-shot seam gives its shots its hand (so the recoil, the heft's kick, and the fire haptics follow), the heft weighs it as its hand's gun ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)), its hand's fingers take the gun's grip, and the render-time late update corrects it by its hand, as for the wielded weapon.
- When the primary moves to its hand (the other hand docked or let go of its weapon), the reconcile disarms it and wields it in the same hand (`reconcile: the primary hand's weapon is wielded`), and the HUD and the game's reload take it over; the weapon the other hand then takes is armed in turn.

With `weapons.hold.second_weapon` (on), a hand may draw or take a weapon while the other hand holds one; off, a hand draws only while the other holds no weapon. Mixing a pistol with a rifle, two long guns, or two weapons of one slot are all a weapon in each hand, whichever slots they come from: a weapon beyond the slots is kept by the carry set ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The weapons beyond the slots"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-weapons-beyond-the-slots)).

Its magazine is its own, and its reserve is the character's. The game's reload and the HUD's ammunition readout stay the selected weapon's, so:

- the true reload ([reload.md](reload.md#each-hands-gun)) acts on both weapons at once, each hand's gun with its own magazine, chamber, and action. With a gun in each hand there is no free hand for the pouch, so each is reloaded with a flick of its hand to eject and the belt push to load, and the game's own reload is stopped while either is reloaded by hand;
- with `weapons.hold.magazine_pips` (on), a row of pips floats above the hand holding it (`hold::pips`), anchored to the hand's grip on the overlay: a filled pip for each round, or each share of a magazine larger than the row, a ring for each spent, and the whole row red when the magazine is empty or out.

While the true reload holds it from firing, its trigger fires nothing. Its ammunition check asks the character to reload the wielded weapon whenever its own magazine runs low, and cancels the character's reload every tick for a gun that does not reload by magazine; with the per-hand tick both requests are refused for the local player, so it fires until it is empty and clicks dry like the wielded weapon. With `per_hand_tick` off, its trigger fires nothing once its magazine is empty, since the request would reload the wielded weapon.

## Separate pistols

The game's pistols are one weapon with two guns ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "The dual-wield weapon"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#the-dual-wield-weapon)), so neither pistol could be dropped or held apart from the other. With `weapons.separate_pistols` (on), each pistol is a weapon object of its own, a single pistol (`weapons::dual_wield::single`), and an item of the loadout table's like any other weapon: two pistols are a pistol in each hand, each on its own hip. It needs `weapons.inventory.enabled` and `weapons.inventory.carry`; without them, or with it off, a pair the game hands over stays the game's pair, one item with a gun in each hand. Pistols already single stay single.

A single pistol is the game's dual-wield object kept to its first gun, as the game keeps a pistol that a character wields rather than dual-wields:

- its second gun and that gun's model parts are hidden after every show the game makes (the detour of [`WeaponBase::SetVisibleBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SetVisibleBase)), and each input tick for the pistols the player owns and the ones the VR inventory keeps in the world;
- its next gun ([`WeaponBase::m_NextWeaponToFire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_NextWeaponToFire)) is held on the first at each of its updates and after each shot, so it fires only the gun that is shown, from that gun's muzzle;
- everything of the mod's that walks a weapon's guns sees one gun (`aim::gun::guns`): the hold places it in the holding hand as it places a rifle, and only the holding hand's trigger fires it ([Firing](#firing));
- its magazine holds half the tuned size, rounded up, and the reserve is shared. A magazine over the half (after the game's own reload, which fills to the tuned size, or a pistol taken full from the world) is brought down to the half at the next input tick, the rounds over it going back to the reserve;
- dropped, it is one pistol, as the game makes any dual-wield weapon it drops, and it stays one when picked up again.

The slot's pistol stays in the dual-wielding state when equipped, the state the game's draw gives the player's pistols; only its first gun is in a hand.

### Where the pistols come from

- A pair the game hands over (a new game's loadout, a supply drop, a mission's give-back of a pair, or the respawn after a load whose sidecar knows nothing of separate pistols) is split. The slot keeps it as a single pistol, and a second pistol of its kind, the twin, is asked of the spawn system at the player ([`SpawnSystem::SpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-SpawnTagIndex), the call the inventory's respawn makes). When it comes, the carry set keeps it, the table restores it onto the hip the slot's pistol does not take (or, with that hip taken, the socket the table prefers for it), and the pair's magazine is dealt between the two, the slot's filled first (`single pistols: split the pair; the second pistol is on the left hip`). A pair split while it is in a hand keeps a pistol in each hand: the slot's pistol stays where the table has it, and with the other hand empty and a weapon in each hand on, the twin comes into that hand at once.
- Until the twin comes, the pair is the game's. It stays the game's when no socket would take the twin, while the carried weapons are stashed for a mission, or when the twin has not come within ten seconds (`single pistols: the pair stays the game's`, with the reason). A twin that is no longer wanted when it comes (it is late, the pair left the slot, it is of another kind, or it could not be carried) is not left in the world, where the spawn system would keep it as a pistol too many: it is taken into the player's keeping, which turns its pickup off and its bodies out of the world, hidden, and kept until the next reset (`single pistols: a second pistol is not wanted`).
- A pistol taken from the world (a direct pickup, the game's own pickup, a take from a character's hands, a take off a rack, or a rack's copy) is one pistol. It goes into the empty slot; with the slot's pistol there and the other hand holding the wielded weapon, the carry set keeps it and the table takes it into the grabbing hand, its stored rounds going to the reserve as the game's pickup gives them (`hold: picked the pistol up into the left hand beside the wielded weapon`); otherwise it takes the slot, and the slot's pistol stays where the table has it on the body. Pistols never merge back into a pair.
- A weapon of the pistol slot with one gun is one weapon already, and is never split.

### The hips

Each hip is a socket like any other, and which pistol sits on which hip is the table's. The game docks the pistol slot's first gun on the right hip, so a single pistol in the slot is the game's to place there; on the left hip the mod places it, as it does any weapon on a socket other than its slot's own. A pistol on a hip sits in that hip's holster, with no offset, unless the slot holds the game's pair, whose two pistols take both holsters (`inventory::sockets::placed_offset`). A grab at a hip draws the pistol on it into the hand, and a pistol docks on any free hip, or any other socket, that takes it.

### Saves, resets, and missions

The save sidecar records whether the slot's pistol and each carried pistol is single, and so does the reset's own record ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Saves, resets, and loads"](two-handed-inventory.md#saves-resets-and-loads)), so the respawn brings the slot's pistol back as one pistol beside its carried twin rather than splitting it again. A mission that takes the slots notes whether the pistol slot held one pistol, and its give-back is one pistol again. A carried single pistol that never comes back after a reset makes the slot's pistol of its kind the game's pair again, or keeps it the pair when it has not come back yet, so no pistol is lost (`single pistols: the second pistol never came back after the reset`).

### Readout

The debug UI's Status tab has `SEPARATE PISTOLS` in its `INVENTORY` section: whether the slot's pistol is one pistol, the twin on its way or why a pair stays the game's, and how many pistols are single, how many pairs were split, and how many came as one. The debug UI's `VR inventory` section has the same, with the last thing that happened, and marks each single pistol among the entries.

## Grenades as objects

The grenade taken from the pouch is the game's own grenade from the moment it is in the hand to the moment it detonates ([controllers.md, "Grenades, aim assist, and reloads"](controllers.md#grenades-aim-assist-and-reloads) describes the take and the throw). Two things make it behave as an object rather than a throw animation: its pin, and catching. The code is `payload/src/controllers/weapons/grenade/` (`pin.rs` and `catch.rs`), and the settings are under `controllers.weapons.grenade`.

### The pin and cooking

With `pin.enabled` (on), a grenade comes out of the pouch with its pin in. The update that primes it stops its fuse ([`Grenade::m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##field-m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer), the switch the game's beacon uses to keep its own timer still) and fills it again, after checking that the primed object is a grenade ([`Grenade::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##func-TYPE_ID)); the log line is `grenade: taken with its pin in, the fuse held`. The pin is pulled by either:

- the other hand, with a grip press at the grenade (`GrabVolume::GrenadePin`, a sphere of `pin.radius_m`, 0.08 m, at the held grenade, placed while the pin is in and that hand holds no gun);
- the holding hand's trigger, with `pin.trigger` (on), for a throw with one hand. While a grenade is in the off-hand, a press of that hand's trigger that pulls the pin is the pin's for as long as it is held, and does not reach the tether retract the trigger otherwise drives; with the pin out, with `pin.trigger` off, or with a caught grenade, the trigger has nothing to pull, so it retracts the tethers as an empty hand's does.

The next weapon update lets the fuse run, and from then the grenade cooks in the hand: the fuse warning's ring and pulses start, and held past its fuse it explodes there. Both hands play `Event::GrenadePin` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)), and the log line is `grenade: pulled the pin` with the puller. A grenade thrown with its pin still in has its fuse started at the throw, so it lands with its whole fuse rather than lying live in the world forever. Writes to the grenade happen only inside the grenade weapon's update, where the weapon's weak reference proves the grenade alive.

### Catching a live grenade

With `catch.enabled` (on), a free hand catches a live grenade near the player and throws it on. Every grenade launched, by the player or anyone else, passes through the grenade's launch ([`Grenade::PropelToward`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##func-PropelToward)), already detoured for the throw. The detour notes each one with its physics body ([`Explosive::m_PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_PfxInstance)) and the body's owner as the launch finds them. On the input tick each noted grenade's body is read through the hand bodies' ownership check (the body still exists, is dynamic, and is owned by the same object), which proves the grenade has not detonated or been removed; one that fails is forgotten.

Each live grenade within `catch.range_m` (4 m) of the player is a grab volume (`GrabVolume::LiveGrenade`, radius `catch.radius_m`, 0.18 m, larger than a pouch's since a flying grenade moves between ticks) for each hand that holds no gun, grenade, or charge, while no grenade is in the hands. The player's own grenade waits `catch.own_grace_s` (0.4 s) after its launch, so it is not taken back as it leaves. A grip press there catches the grenade nearest the hand (`grenade: caught a live grenade`, with `Event::GrenadeCatch`). From then each input tick gives its body the velocity that carries it to the hand's grip over the tick, capped at `catch.max_carry_mps`, so it stays in the hand with its own physics and its fuse runs on; the fuse warning shows on that hand as for a grenade from the pouch. Opening the grip throws it: its body gets the hand's release velocity, estimated as for a throw from the pouch, plus the character's own velocity (`grenade: threw the caught grenade`). A grenade that goes off in the hand logs `grenade: the caught grenade is gone from the hand`. While a caught grenade is held the pouch gives no other grenade.

The readout shows the pin and the pulls, the live grenades noted and in reach, and the catches; the debug UI's Status tab has an `EXPLOSIVES AND MAGAZINES` section with `GRENADE PIN` and `LIVE GRENADES`, and the scenario signals `grenade.pins_pulled`, `grenade.thrown`, `grenade.caught`, and `grenade.thrown_on` count them (`testing/scenarios/grenade_pin_and_catch.toml`).

## C4 pressed onto a surface

With `c4.stick.enabled` (on), a charge from the C4 pouch ([controllers.md, "Grenades, aim assist, and reloads"](controllers.md#grenades-aim-assist-and-reloads)) can be pressed onto a surface instead of thrown (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/c4/stick.rs`). While a charge is held, and once it has been out of the pouch for `c4.stick.after_prime_s` (0.3 s, so the chest the pouch is on is never taken for a surface), each aim update casts six short rays from the held charge along the axes of the hand's grip frame, out to `c4.stick.contact_m` (0.04 m, about half the charge's thickness), with the player's own character excluded. The nearest surface they meet is touching the charge, and the charge is pressed onto it: the log line is `c4: pressed the charge onto a surface` with whether it is on an object.

The press is the throw's release with a different start and velocity. The next C4 weapon update makes the charge live as a throw does ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::Fire_Projectile`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-Fire_Projectile)), moves it to `c4.stick.standoff_m` (0.03 m) off the surface along its normal, turned as the hand holds it, drops it into the simulation, and pushes it onto the surface at `c4.stick.push_mps` (1.5 m/s) plus the character's own velocity. The charge's own collision handler then sticks it where it touches, oriented by the surface and attached to the object it touched, exactly as a thrown charge sticks where it lands, so a charge pressed onto a car rides the car. The log line is `c4: stuck the charge where it was pressed`, with `Event::C4Stick` on the hand. Opening the fingers before the charge touches anything throws it as before.

The same rays reach on to `c4.stick.preview_m` (0.3 m), and the nearest surface they meet within it is marked where the charge would stick: a red ring lying on the surface (`GrabVolume::ChargeSurface`, a marked target no squeeze takes; [grip.md](grip.md)). The ring is drawn whenever a charge is held and a surface is near, so the player sees where to press before touching.

The debug UI's Status tab shows `C4 BY HAND` with the charges thrown and pressed on, and the scenario signals `c4.thrown` and `c4.stuck` count them (`testing/scenarios/c4_by_hand.toml`).

## Aiming down the sights

The gun aim shoots along each gun's own sight line ([controllers.md, "Aiming"](controllers.md#aiming)), so a gun is aimed by its sights already. With `ads.enabled` (on; under "Scopes"), a long gun raised to the eye is lined up with it so the player can look down those sights (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/ads/`). It acts on the two-handed and special slots' guns, whose sight line in the holding wrist's frame is measured on the input tick with the foregrip's geometry: the gun's sight line (with the weapon's sight correction) relative to the wrist target its last placement followed. Each hand's long gun is measured, the wielded weapon or one armed beside it, and since there is one aiming eye, the gun lined up with it is the one raised to it: the gun whose sights (or scope's weld) take the most hold of the eye, then whose stock takes the most hold of the shoulder, then whose sight line passes nearest the eye. A gun whose pose takes no hold is not raised, and the other hand's gun stays where its hand holds it ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)). The pose is applied to the cockpit-frame hands every hand subsystem starts from, after the two-handed pose and the weight and before the recoil, so the drawn gun, the IK targets, the late update, the sight line the gun aim casts, and the scope's lens all see the gun where the eye has it. The gun moves as one rigid body; the holding hand rides it, and the hand on the foregrip with it. Nothing turns the gun toward a target.

Three steps, each fading in as its distance closes from its engage distance to its lock distance, eased, so the gun settles onto the eye rather than snapping:

1. The stock (`ads.shoulder`, on). The butt is taken `ads.butt_behind_grip_m` (0.22 m) behind the holding grip along the sight line; the shoulder pocket is `ads.pocket_offset_m` from the holding side's shoulder joint in the torso's frame (inward, down, and forward of it; mirrored for the left), the shoulder being the one the arms are remapped about, or the player's estimated one. Within `ads.shoulder_engage_m` (0.12 m) of the pocket, the gun turns about its front hand (the hand on the foregrip, or the holding hand without one) so the butt lies on the line to the pocket, fully within `ads.shoulder_lock_m` (0.04 m): the front hand sets where the gun points and the shoulder holds its back. The log line is `ads: the stock is in the shoulder`.
2. The sights (iron sights). The aiming eye is the eyes' midpoint moved `ads.half_ipd_m` (0.032 m) to the side `ads.eye` names (the gun hand's side by default, or left, or right). With the eye behind the holding grip by no more than `ads.max_eye_behind_m` (0.4 m), the head looking within `ads.max_view_angle_deg` (30 degrees) of the sight line, and the eye within `ads.engage_m` (0.06 m) of the line, the gun turns about a point `ads.pivot_ahead_m` (0.35 m) ahead of the grip on the line, about where the front sight is, so the line passes through the eye, fully within `ads.lock_m` (0.02 m). The front sight stays where the hands hold it and the eye, the rear sight, and the front sight line up, so the shot lands where the player sees the sights point. The log line is `ads: the sights are on the eye`.
3. A scope's cheek weld (`ads.cheek_weld`, on), for a scoped weapon (the sniper class, as the scopes have it) in place of the second step. A scope is lined up by moving the gun across its axis, keeping its direction, so the eye sits on the axis and the lens's view centres, within `ads.weld_engage_m` (0.05 m), fully within `ads.weld_lock_m` (0.015 m). The log line is `ads: the scope is on the eye`.

The debug UI's Status tab has an `AIMING` section with `DOWN THE SIGHTS` (iron sights or scoped, the hand whose gun is raised, the times each step took hold, and how far the eye is off the line), and the scenario signals `ads.sighted`, `ads.shouldered`, and `ads.welded` count them (`testing/scenarios/aim_down_sights.toml`).

## Guns against walls

With the hand bodies and `physical_hands.bodies.guns.enabled` on, a held gun is a body joined to the hands, which stops at walls along its whole length, and the probe below does not move a hand on it ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md)). Otherwise, a gun in the hands has no body in the physics world: the weapon's bodies are out of the world in the wielding state ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md)), so nothing stops a held rifle at a wall and it passes through. With `collide.enabled` (on; under "Scopes"), a held long gun is stopped (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/collide/`). It acts on the guns the sights act on, the two-handed and special slots', whose muzzle (the first gun's current fire point) and barrel in the holding wrist's frame are measured with the foregrip's geometry, in either hand: each hand's long gun, wielded or armed beside the wielded weapon, has its own probe, its own wall, and its own push ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)).

The gun's collision proxy is a ray rather than a body: from the holding grip along the barrel to `collide.tip_margin_m` (0.03 m) past the muzzle, cast each aim update (the simulation phase outside the physics step) with the player aim's own filter, which passes through the player, the player's weapons, foliage, fences, and glass, as the gun aim's rays do. It is cast along the gun's pose before any push, which the hand poses note each time, so the push never moves where the wall is looked for. The wall it meets is a plane (the hit point and its normal, turned to face the grip), kept for `collide.keep_s` (0.15 s) past the probe's last hit so that a probe grazing an edge does not let the gun bounce in and out.

Each hand pose then pushes the gun out of the plane, last of the cockpit-frame hand poses (after the two-handed pose, the weight, the sights, and the recoil), so the drawn gun, its sight line, the IK targets, and the late update all see the gun where the wall stops it:

1. it turns about the holding grip, away from the wall, by the least angle that puts the tip on the plane, up to `collide.max_turn_deg` (70 degrees), so a gun pushed into a wall at a slant slides along it;
2. what is left (a gun pushed into the wall head-on, or past the turn's limit) is pulled back along the gun into the hands, up to `collide.max_retract_m` (0.35 m).

The holding hand rides the gun, and the hand on its foregrip with it, so both hands stay on the gun where it is stopped, and the player's hands, which go on into the wall, are left behind for as long as the gun is against it. A gun whose grip is itself through the wall is not pushed. The first contact after a second without one logs `collide: the held gun met a wall`. Because the proxy is a ray, the gun is stopped by what it points into, not by what its sides brush, and it does not push loose objects: it is stopped by them, as by a wall.

The debug UI's Status tab has `GUN AGAINST WALLS` in the `AIMING` section (against a wall or clear, the contacts, and the turn and pull-back of a gun against one), the Controllers tab shows each hand's gun, and the scenario signals `collide.contacts` and `collide.touching` read it (`testing/scenarios/gun_against_walls.toml`).

## Settings and readout

The settings are `controllers.weapons.hold`, `controllers.weapons.holsters` for the sockets (`enabled`, `follow_docks`, `long_guns_dominant`, `stick_click_cycles`, and the hips', the shoulders', and the chest's reaches), and `controllers.weapons.separate_pistols`. `either_hand`, `handover`, `handle_radius_m`, and `direct_pickup` are on by default: each call they make is the game's own, read in the IDB (the attach-bone table writes and the interpolation skip, and `PickupItem` on a dropped weapon, the swimming pickup's path), and none writes the inventory directly. `snap_s`, `grip_to_hold`, `max_throw_mps`, `drop_replaced`, `second_weapon`, `right_grip`, `magazine_pips`, `steal`, `racks`, `take_radius_m`, `take_slide`, and `per_hand_tick` are on by default too, as every new feature is, and each can be switched off, as can `separate_pistols`. Their calls are read in the IDB as well, but they are the least proven in the headset: they equip and unequip without the animation graph's acts, which the game never does on land, a drop takes a weapon out of the inventory, which only a heavy weapon's drop does in the game, a weapon beside the wielded one is updated outside the inventory's own fan-out in a state the game never fires from, a take from a character's hands drops a living character's gun, which the game does only through an animation state's drop task, and a take off a rack completes a pickup with no animation on land.

The debug UI's Controllers tab shows the holding hand, whether the bones are owned, a hand expecting a weapon and its cause, the handovers, the table writes and snaps, the direct pickups, the fallbacks, and the last refusal; for the moves at the sockets each hand's slide under way and whether its grip holds its gun, the draws, docks, and drops, the last throw's speed, and the last refusal; the weapons armed beside the wielded one, each with its slot and magazine; and for the takes the guns in characters' hands and the weapons on racks within reach, the takes, and the copies racks gave. The debug UI's Status tab has a `WEAPONS IN HAND` section: each hand's weapon and how it came there (`DOCKED`, `PICKED UP`, `STOLEN`, `RACK`, `GAME'S DRAW`, or `BESIDE THE WIELDED`), the guns in characters' hands and the weapons on racks within reach with the takes and the copies, the weapons armed beside the wielded one with their magazines (`ARMED BESIDE`), and the last refusal of anything the hands tried with a weapon. The scenario signals `weapons.armed_in_left_hand`, `weapons.armed_in_right_hand`, `weapons.armed_carried`, `weapons.draws`, `weapons.docks`, `weapons.drops`, and `weapons.promotions` read them.

## In the headset

1. Draw a rifle from the shoulder with the left hand: `hold: the weapon is in the left hand` with `cause=draw`. It sits in the left hand, the left trigger fires it from its own muzzle along its own sight line, and the right trigger fires nothing.
2. Grab its grip with the right hand: `hold: handed the weapon to the right hand`. It moves to the right hand in one frame without streaking.
3. Brace it on the foregrip with each hand in turn.
4. Kill a soldier and grab the dropped weapon with the left hand: `hold: picked the weapon up into the left hand`. It appears in the left hand at once with no pickup animation, and the HUD shows its magazine as it was.
5. Watch `grip_watch` for a long gun turning over in the left hand, and the upper body for a left-handed animation state. Read `RIGHT HAND GRIP` on the debug UI's Status tab and the log's mirrored-grip line: with a large angle, the automatic choice takes the mirrored grip. Switch `weapons.hold.right_grip` between the right hand's own and the left hand's mirrored with a pistol and then a rifle in the right hand and note which sits in the hand the right way round, and whether its sight line still lines up. ## In the headset: separate pistols

With `weapons.separate_pistols`, `weapons.inventory.enabled`, `weapons.inventory.carry`, `weapons.hold.second_weapon`, and `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD GUNS` on, and the debug UI's Status tab open at `SEPARATE PISTOLS`:

1. Start a new game or load a save from before separate pistols: `single pistols: asked for the second pistol of the pair`, then `split the pair; the second pistol is on the left hip`. One pistol sits on each hip, and `SEPARATE PISTOLS` reads `THE SLOT'S PISTOL IS SINGLE, 2 SINGLE, 1 PAIRS SPLIT`. Watch the feet for a pistol seen for a frame before it goes onto the hip, and note whether the HUD shows a pickup.
2. Draw the right pistol from the right hip: only one pistol comes out, the left hip keeps its pistol, and the HUD shows half the pair's magazine. Fire it: one muzzle, one flash, one slide moving. Nothing appears in the left hand.
3. Grab the left hip with the left hand: `hold: drew the weapon off the left hip into the left hand`, and `reconcile: armed the weapon in the left hand`. Each trigger fires its own pistol; the pips over the left hand count the left one's rounds.
4. Open the left grip away from the body: `hold: let go of the weapon in the left hand`, and one pistol falls, as one body. Open the right grip away from the body: the right pistol falls too, and the HUD's pistol slot is empty.
5. Pick the first pistol up with the right hand: it goes into the slot. Pick the second up with the left hand: `picked the pistol up into the left hand beside the wielded weapon`. Look for a second gun anywhere on either pistol: there should be none, in the hand, on the hip, or on the ground.
6. With both pistols drawn, empty the left one, then flick and belt-load it, and check it takes half the tuned magazine. Empty the right one and let the game's reload run with the true reload off: the magazine reads the half after a tick, and the reserve grows by the rounds over it.
7. Dock the right pistol on the left hip and the left one on the right hip: each sits in its hip's holster. Draw them again with either hand.
8. With both hands empty, grab the left hip with the left hand: the left pistol is wielded in the left hand, and the right pistol stays on the right hip.
9. Save at a safehouse, quit to the menu, and continue: `the sidecar matches the save`, the slot's pistol comes back as one pistol (`a pistol is one pistol` with the respawn's reason), the carried one is restored on its hip, and no third pistol is spawned. Drop one pistol, save, and load: one pistol comes back, not two.
10. Take a pistol off a rack, and from a soldier's hands, with the slot's pistol drawn in the other hand: each comes in as one pistol beside it. Take a pistol off a rack with unlimited stock with both hands empty: the copy arrives as one pistol (`a rack's copy`), and the slot's old pistol stays on its hip.
11. Start a mission that takes the player's weapons, with one pistol in the slot and none carried: when the weapons come back, the slot's pistol is one pistol, and no second is asked for.
12. Switch `weapons.separate_pistols` off: the pistols already single stay as they are; a pair the game hands over afterwards (a supply drop) stays a pair, drawn into one hand with its second gun in the other, and fires from either trigger.
