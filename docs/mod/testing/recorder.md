# Tracking recorder

The tracking recorder writes what the OpenXR runtime gives the mod each frame to a file, with the mod's own decisions beside it as events, so that a headset session can be studied afterwards without the game: printed, evaluated against the body rig offline, or scrubbed in Rerun beside the take's video. Recordings are evidence, not something to play back into the game; the game is not deterministic, so the same input would not reproduce what followed.

The code is `payload/src/recorder/tracking/` (the recorder, and the conversion between the payload's types and the file's), `crates/tracking-format/` (the file format's data model and codec, a crate of its own so that host tools build it on Linux and Windows), `tools/tracking-synth/` (the offline tools and the synthetic recordings), and the debug UI's Tracking capture feature in `payload/src/ui/testing.rs`.

## Starting and stopping

Nothing records until it is started, and `testing.enabled` (on by default) switches every way of starting a tracking recording off. There are four ways:

- F1 starts a session recording, the video and a tracking recording together ([capture.md](../capture.md#session-recording)).
- The director's key bindings ([trailer-tooling.md](../trailer-tooling.md)): Numpad/ starts or stops a session recording. A keymap saved by an earlier build keeps its own list; an action it does not bind takes its default chord unless the saved list binds that chord to something else, and a binding of an action that no longer exists is dropped on load.
- The debug UI's Tools > Tracking capture, on the desktop or in the headset through the floating panel, which also saves the lookback (below).
- The request file, for a script. Every second (every frame in a capture harness session, whose environment carries `JC3VR_HARNESS_TOKEN`) the game looks for `test_request.toml` in the configuration directory ([settings.md](../settings.md)), reads it, deletes it, and acts on it. `testing.request_file` turns the watch off. Request files are limited to 8 KiB before TOML parsing, and the reader consumes at most the limit plus one sentinel byte, including when a file grows during the read.

```toml
command = "record"  # record or stop_recording
```

The capture harness's commands share the file ([fast-comparison.md](fast-comparison.md), [benchmark.md](benchmark.md)). Under Proton the configuration directory is inside the game's prefix, so a script writes the file there.

## Recording

A recording needs a player character to mark its start at. It starts at the top of the frame after the request, which is where the player's transform can be read, and samples once per VR frame at the end of the frame loop's begin, after the views are located and the controllers sampled; without a running session it writes no frames until one starts. Each frame records what the mod published, not the runtime's raw answers:

- Both eyes' poses in the cockpit frame (re-based against the recenter baseline) and their fields of view, when the runtime located the views.
- Both controllers' published frames: the grip and aim poses with their validity and tracking flags and velocities, every raw input (triggers, grips, sticks, trackpads, buttons, and touches), the derived presses, the interaction profiles, the focus, the mode set, and, with `testing.record_hand_joints` (on), the tracked hand joints. Each joint retains its pose, radius, and independent position/orientation validity and tracking flags.
- The semantic actions' per-hand states for `global` and the active mode set.
- The session state, whether the runtime asked for the frame to be rendered, whether the view pose was valid, and the STAGE floor height and play area.
- The recenters that took effect before the frame, with their kind, cause, and turn. From version 7 these include the follow bank, which folds a frozen turn of the body under the view into the cockpit frame ([`vr/recenter.rs`](../../../payload/src/vr/recenter.rs)); it is recorded as a yaw-and-height re-base with the cause `FollowBank` and the bank's turn.

Beside the frames, the recording carries the mod's own decisions as events, each stamped on the frames' clock and written before the frame captured after it:

- `controller_state`, once per game frame, read after the engine's render update so the late hand correction is evaluated at the sub-frame fraction that frame rendered with: the scheme, the mode and its traversal and hold refinements, the controller publication's sequence and age, the input subsystem's error, each hand's IK result, the hand tiers and their blend, the late correction's reason and wrist positions, the aim rays with their hits, the grapple aim, the weapon sight, the body rig's posture, root, step drive, push-back, seat, probe, and fade, from version 6, the root's latest on-foot tick (`root`, [rig.md](../body/rig.md#who-moves-the-root)), and, from version 7, the player character's measured motion (`character`): its measured world velocity ([`Character::m_MeasuredWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_MeasuredWorldVelocity)) and its height over the ground as its ground information last wrote it to the body rule system ([`AnimationRuleSystem::m_HeightOverGround`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::rule_system::AnimationRuleSystem##field-m_HeightOverGround), the nearer of the ground and the water, and `m_HeightOverGroundExcludingWater`), zero while the proxy stands on the ground. These are what the animation graph's speed and height conditions read, among them the wingsuit landing's ([wingsuit-landing.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-landing.md)). It leaves out what the frames hold (poses, validity and tracking flags, focus, action set, and the STAGE information). The fields are independently read subsystem snapshots, not one atomic instant.
- `grip_acquisition`, at each stage of a grip acquisition: a grip press or a queued pickup request, with the chosen candidate and every candidate the resolver offered (its distance, tier, grab volume, the provider's reach, and why it lost to the winner), the reach provider's scan, and the pickup consumer's branch; the simulation consumer's result for a request; and the first drive of what a request took. Identities are the controller publication sequence, the per-hand input-read ordinal, the grip episode, and the request ordinal ([physical-hands.md](../input/physical-hands.md#interaction-evidence)).
- `take_marker`, at each take marker ([capture.md](../capture.md#session-recording)): the marker's session ordinal, local stamp, Unix milliseconds, game-run time, and label, the same fields as its copy in the video sidecar or `markers.jsonl`.
- `gun_release`, at each attempt to let an inventory gun fall: the route, the refusal, which release calls returned, the controller publication the release estimate was checked against, and the native drop's cached gun motion and the capped velocity requested of it.

Version 7 adds events noted as moments, when they happen rather than every frame:

- `body_state`, when the body's animation state changes: the current state of the body's rule system as its state-name hash, with the name when the mod knows it (`payload/src/controllers/diagnostics/state_names.rs` lists the names the mod's code and the engine documents cite; another state shows as its hash), and the procedural body's hand-over case ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)). An unchanged state is noted again, marked as a repeat, once five seconds have passed since the last note, so a lookback save or a recording started mid-session opens with the state it started in.
- `throw`, when an item leaves a hand with a requested motion: a gun let go (beside its `gun_release`), a grenade, a caught grenade, a charge, or a carried object thrown. It holds the hand, the item, the requested linear and angular velocity in world units (per game second), the game's time scale, so that the same motion per real second is the world's times the scale, where the hand's part of the estimate came from, and, for a grenade, whether it left armed (its pin out, or caught live).
- `grip_release`, when a hand lets go of what its grip held through the physical hands: what it held (a prop, an articulated object, a door, a ragdoll, a latched door, or a character), the object and body, why (the grip opened, the hold was lost, or the hold ended), and whether the release threw it. The press and the take are `grip_acquisition`'s.
- `wingsuit_gesture`, when the wingsuit gesture recognizer's phase or the reason it is unavailable changes, the same change the log's `wingsuit gestures:` line reports ([wingsuit-gestures.md](../input/wingsuit-gestures.md)).
- `snatch`, when the grapple snatch's phase changes: the hand, the phase it left and entered, how it ended (taken, failed, cancelled, or stood down) and why, the offered target's kind and distance on arming and firing, and the yank's distance and speed when it fired ([grapple-snatch.md](../input/grapple-snatch.md)).

The character's motion adds 22 bytes to each controller state before compression (a presence byte, the velocity, and the two heights behind their own presence byte). A body state note is about 50 bytes and repeats at most every five seconds when nothing changes; the other moments are rarer and of a similar size.

Version 8 records what an offline rig test and the playtests' recurring questions (body and camera clipping, stutter, grabs that miss, bindings that confuse, refusals with no feedback) need, each behind a presence byte or as a new kind, so a version 7 file still reads. The code that samples it is `payload/src/controllers/diagnostics/`. The controller state gains four groups:

- `view`: where the cockpit sits in the world, as the head pose the cockpit composes through (the head's orientation at the eye midpoint the camera renders from; recordings from before the hands composed from the eyes hold the published head's position there), the head's cockpit position, the view frame's rotation, and the world scale (a cockpit point is the world point `head_world + view * ((p - head_cockpit) * world_scale)`, as the hands compose), and the active camera's two tick poses, the render fraction the engine blended them by, and the pose it rendered. Camera stutter and clipping show here, and a viewer places the cockpit from it instead of fitting it.
- `grapple`, while a wire is out: the hook's state, the wire's state and longest run, both wire ends' world points and states, the object the hook targets, the snatch's held line (length, tension and its ceiling, the target's mass, the brace, and the line's anchor and the target's centre), and the grapple's lock while it holds the reel's line (the brake, the held length and the stretch past it, the tension and its ceiling, the target's mass, and the hook's point and Rico's position; [grapple-lock.md](../input/grapple-lock.md)).
- `vehicle`, while in one: the class and seat the bindings resolved, the vehicle's transform at the end of the step and as rendered, how far the step moved it, its applied steering, the control each hand holds, and the mounted gun's handles and aim point.
- `frame`: the engine's render frame index, the counter of the mirror's latest present, which the frame stamp draws into the mirror's video (the frame's own present follows it), the last game frame's interval and sim ticks, and the haptics each hand last played.

A grip acquisition gains its chosen target's world point, and a throw the world point the item left the hand at (a grenade's or a charge's release point; a gun or a carried object leaves it absent, and its body's samples place it). The new kinds:

- `pose`, twice per pose-finalization pass: Rico's 23 body bones (`tracking_format::event::BODY_BONES`, hips to toes) in the character's model space, with the character's model-to-world transform, so his heading too. The `pre_ik` sample is the animated pose at the pass's entry, just before the solves, with the HumanIK `MAIN`-pass targets live there (the game's and the mod's; a wrist target the controller hands defer to the solve gate is not among them); the `final` sample is the pose after both HumanIK passes and the mod's joint writes, at the end of the pass. Positions keep a tenth of a millimetre and rotations a hundredth of a degree. Together they let a rig be replayed against the game's own animation and its result compared with what rendered.
- `rig`, each grounded-rig frame: the rig's inputs exactly as the payload built them (the room-frame eyes, head, and hands, the floor point under the pelvis, the stick, the height sampling, and the step) and its outputs (the pelvis solve, the step phase and gap, the body's, chest's, and target headings, the turn, the standing neck height, and the steps), the floor's cockpit height, and a recenter's snap. `rig_config` holds the calibration and the rig's tuning as JSON, noted when either changes and every five seconds.
- `bodies`, after each simulation step: the dynamic bodies within 8 metres of Rico's root or 3 metres of either hand body, at most 32, held ones first and then the nearest, each with its offset from Rico's root (a millimetre within 32 metres), orientation, and velocities (a centimetre or a hundredth of a radian per second), and whether a hand holds it. A body at rest is written once, and again when it moves or is introduced again. Each step walks an eighth of the world's body slots to find bodies coming near and reads the kept ones, so a body is found within eight steps, a held one at once; the step records the slots it walked, the world's peak slot, and its own cost in microseconds. `body_intro` names a body when it joins the set and every five seconds while it stays: its owner, its kind (the collider view's: a prop, a weapon, a vehicle part, a character's proxy, a ragdoll, or one of the mod's bodies), its mass, its shape's kind, and its box's half extents.
- `held`, after each simulation step, for each hand holding something through the physical hands: the object, the body, the kind of hold, the body's pose and velocities, and the hold's report (the centre of mass, the grab point and where the hand wants it, the lag, and the shares of the hand's pull and twist used).
- `force`, each time the mod sets a body's velocities or applies an impulse to one through the physical hands' writers (the carried bodies' drive, the snatch's line and catch, the shove and the knock, the door's swing, and the released guns, magazines, grenades, and charges): the source file and line that asked, the body, its owner and mass, its velocities before the write, and the velocities set or the impulse and where it was applied. With the bodies' velocities each step, a viewer can tell the mod's share of a body's motion from the rest of the world's.
- `injected`, each input tick the bindings land action writes: each write's action map, the game's action, the value, the phase and how long it was held, and what became of it.
- `diagnostic`, a refusal or another moment a playtest needs explained, with its subsystem, hand, and evidence: a trigger press that fired nothing and why, a door tear refused and why, the swim gate opening and closing with the signals that read swimming, and the magazine pouch going unplaced because the skeleton was not read. Each comes from one call, `controllers::diagnostics::report`, which also writes the same text to the session log as an info line under `controllers`, `<subsystem>: <what>: <evidence>` with the hand as a field, so the log and the recording never disagree. Other refusals are already events: a snatch's failure and its reason (`snatch`), the wingsuit and parachute gestures' blocks (`wingsuit_gesture`, the recognizer serves both), and a lost hold or a door hold's end and why (`grip_release`).
- `haptic`, each one-off cue the mixer takes: the hand, the source event, the primitive, the amplitude, the length, and the frequency.
- `settings`, every setting as TOML (the settings file holds only the changed ones, [settings.md](../settings.md#the-files-format)) when a recording starts, at the head of a lookback save, and whenever a change is saved, so a take says what it ran with.
- `frame_timing`, each second: the frame-time breakdown the log's `perf: frame breakdown` line and the debug UI's Performance tab show, as each slot's own time per frame, longest call, and calls. The recorder's own sampling outside the capture is its slot, `recorder`.

The additions cost about 3.3 megabytes a minute before compression in an ordinary take at the 2 November session's rate (44 rendered frames and 30 steps a second): the poses about 1.4, the controller state's groups 0.5, the rig 0.5, a handful of moving bodies 0.5, and the injected actions and the timing the rest. A dense scene with 32 moving bodies and both hands busy adds about 2 more. Against the 8.4 megabytes a minute a version 7 take wrote, an hour grows from about 520 to between 700 and 820 megabytes. On the game thread the reads and the encoding cost tens of microseconds a frame; the body walk's slice is the largest part and records its own cost.

Version 8 still needs a headset session to check:

- In a dense place (a town, Hope Lake), the `bodies` samples' cost and the world's peak slot, and the `recorder` slot of `frame_timing`, which should stay within a few hundredths of a millisecond a frame.
- That the `pre_ik` pose holds all 23 bones on every path the local player's pose takes (on foot, swimming, the wingsuit, a vehicle seat), and that the `final` pose matches the rendered body in the video.
- That `export-rerun` places the cockpit from `view` with the drawn wrists on the registered grips.
- That a refused trigger press, a door that will not tear, and an unplaced pouch each show in the export's log.

The payload's own enums are recorded as their labels and nested runtime values as their Rust `Debug` text, read against the recording's build stamp, so the file does not change when the payload's types do. The events are evidence for the offline tools.

The header holds the start marker (the player character's world matrix, and the name of a director bookmark within two metres, if any), the build stamp, the stamp of the recording's start, the semantic action schema's version, the dominant hand, the world scale, and the director's time scale.

The file goes to `recordings/<stamp>/tracking.jc3trk` in the session folder ([sessions.md](../sessions.md)). A writer thread appends input records and their frame as one queue batch, so queue pressure cannot accept only one part of that batch. A rejected batch increments the frame-drop counter and retains an explicit input-loss boundary for the next accepted batch or the final writer drain. Oversized batches, encoding errors, and I/O errors are failures rather than queue drops. Tools > Tracking capture shows frames, drops, accepted encoded bytes, duration, and the matching writer's completion error. Accepted stored bytes are not a durability guarantee. The complete logical-byte count includes only fully accepted original batches and the preamble. Full hand joints dominate the snapshot size; input records add source and observation data. A recording stores the same full samples in independent lossless version 5 chunks without changing sample rate or hand-joint capture; the reader still accepts uncompressed records. Real-capture performance has not been benchmarked.

### Lookback

While motion controllers are enabled and the OpenXR runtime is up, the recorder keeps the last ten seconds of capture batches (each frame with its input records and events, encoded once exactly as a recording writes them) whether or not a recording runs, bounded also to 64 MiB. Tools > Tracking capture shows how much it holds, and "Save the last 10 s" (or F2 while no recording runs) writes it to `recordings/<stamp>/lookback.jc3trk` in the session folder on a writer thread of its own. The start marker is the player's world matrix on the oldest kept frame. The times in a lookback file start where the window started, not at zero; a reader measures from its first frame. When earlier batches were evicted, the file opens with an input discontinuity counting the input records they took with them. Events noted while neither a recording nor the lookback collects are not kept, and a bounded queue of pending events drops (and counts) any beyond its size when no frame drains it.

Stopped writers retain their owners until termination is confirmed. Completed writers are reaped without waiting for disk on the game thread. Starting another recording is refused when the bounded writer-owner capacity is full. Shutdown reports unsafe unmapping while any retained writer still runs; a stopped writer's I/O error and its thread liveness are separate outcomes.

### File format

The format is a real wire format with a version, and its decoder is covered by round-trip tests. All numbers are little-endian. A file is the magic `JC3VRTRK`, a `u16` format version, a `u32` header length and the header, and then records until the end of the file, each a tag byte, a `u32` length, and the payload. Version 1 contains snapshot frames. Version 2 retains the same header and frame layout and adds independently versioned input-observation records. Version 3 stores each atomic batch in an independent checksummed chunk, using LZ4 when smaller or raw storage otherwise. Versions 1 through 3 retain their joint layout and decode aggregate validity into both position/orientation validity flags and aggregate tracking into both tracking flags. Their upper joint bits remain ignored. Version 4 stores all four component flags in the existing byte, with their conjunctions in the two low bits; its reader rejects nonzero reserved bits and inconsistent conjunctions. It accepts direct frame/input records or the existing independently checksummed chunks. Version 5 keeps the version 4 layouts and adds event records (tag 4), direct or inside a chunk, each an event's time, a kind byte, and the kind's fields; a version 4 file with an event record is an error. Version 6 keeps the version 5 layouts and appends the root's tick to the controller state event, behind a presence byte; a version 5 controller state reads without it. Version 7 keeps the version 6 layouts, appends the character's motion to the controller state event behind a presence byte (a version 6 controller state reads without it), adds the event kinds 5 through 9 (`body_state`, `throw`, `grip_release`, `wingsuit_gesture`, and `snatch`), which a version 5 or 6 container rejects as unknown kinds, and adds the recenter cause 2, the follow bank. Version 8 keeps the version 7 layouts, appends the view, grapple, vehicle, and frame groups to the controller state, the target point to the grip acquisition, and the release point to the throw, each behind a presence byte (a version 7 event reads without them), and adds the event kinds 10 through 21 (`pose`, `rig`, `rig_config`, `bodies`, `body_intro`, `held`, `force`, `injected`, `diagnostic`, `haptic`, `settings`, and `frame_timing`), which a version 7 or earlier container rejects as unknown kinds. Its dense samples are quantised: a rotation as its smallest three components in six bytes, and a position or velocity as three fixed-point `i16`s. A pose names its bone list by a set number, so the bones it holds can grow without a version. New recorder and synthetic output uses version 8; synthetic recordings remain snapshot-only and do not establish input order. Explicit legacy frame encoders reduce each component pair to its conjunction, which is lossy. Compression runs in the writer thread. Decoding validates stored and expanded sizes, record counts, aggregate budgets, exact decompression length, and checksum before parsing inner records. A truncated final outer chunk preserves earlier complete chunks; a complete corrupt chunk is an error. Chunk budgets are resource limits, not a guarantee that the full decoded recording and conversion fit available memory. Legacy readers retain their unknown-tag behavior; versions 3 through 5 skip unknown optional tags and rejects unknown required tags, and a file whose last record was cut short (the game stopped mid-write) reads as the records before it. Any other malformed field is an error that names the field and its byte offset. The per-field layout is in `crates/tracking-format/src/codec.rs`, and the events' in `crates/tracking-format/src/event_codec.rs` and its submodules. The payload records in its own types and converts them to the crate's field by field, so a field added to a recorded type does not build until the format accounts for it.

### Input observations

The sampler captures protected raw controls and the active semantic actions in one unordered sync group. Runtime change times are retained as metadata in their own clock; neither sampling-loop order nor different runtime change times establishes an observed button order. Each record retains logical time, clock and source generations, exact value bits, focus/context, source-query outcome, recording-relative capture time, and an actual controller-publication identity when one exists. Failed, absent, empty, and limited source queries remain distinct. Cached possible bindings do not identify individual contributors to a merged semantic value.

Bounded history loss and invalid sampling produce explicit discontinuities rather than merged taps. Recorded boundaries retain their original position and loss count. Legacy or incompatible input history is unavailable, not reconstructed from frame snapshots. These records do not yet include policy settings, accepted chord decisions, or output requests, and the new chord arbiter does not consume them in gameplay yet.

## Offline tools

`tools/tracking-synth` reads any recording, recorded or synthetic. Run it on the host:

```sh
cargo run -p tracking-synth --target x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu -- <command> ...   # on Linux
cargo run -p tracking-synth -- <command> ...                                     # on Windows
```

The `--target` is needed on Linux because the workspace builds for Windows by default. `scripts/rerun_take.sh` runs `export-rerun` and `scan-view` on a take's folder after each take.

`tracking-synth dump <file>` prints any recording, recorded or synthetic, to stdout: a header, then each frame and event in time order, one line each. `--json` prints JSON lines instead, a `header` object followed by one `frame` or `event` object per line, for scripts. Input records are counted in the header, not printed. Each event prints a one-line summary (a version 8 controller state prints whole, a pose its heading and bone count, a bodies sample its first bodies); the JSON lines hold every field, the settings' text among them. `--from <time>` and `--to <time>` limit the frames and events printed to an inclusive stretch, either end open. A `<time>` is seconds on the recording's clock (`--from 754.2`), the clock of the frames' and events' times, or an OpenXR predicted display time in nanoseconds (`--from xr:123456789000`), the clock a video recording's sidecar stamps its frames with. A display-time `--from` starts at the first frame at or after it and a display-time `--to` ends at the last frame at or before it, with the events between them. There is no wall-clock form: the file holds no wall-clock anchor except inside `take_marker` events, whose times can be used as seconds. The text form prints the version 7 moments on one line each (a body state by name, or by hash when it has none, and a throw's speed per game second and per real second), a follow bank as the turn of the cockpit frame on the frame it took effect in, and the other events as their fields; the JSON form carries every field.

`tracking-synth rig-eval [--height <m>] [--eye-height <m>] [--tuning <json>] <file> <out.csv>` runs the grounded body rig's solver (`body-rig`) over a recording with a simulated pelvis that follows the step drive, and writes one CSV row per grounded frame: the time, the video's time when the take's sidecar (`frames.jsonl` and `stream.json`) sits beside the recording, the step phase and the steps committed, the step demand, the neck's offset over the pelvis and the lean's reach, the bend, the crouch, the neck's and the standing height, the head's, the body's, and the chest's yaw, the simulated pelvis, the stick, and the head's pitch, and it logs a summary with the steps, the body turns and their median and ninetieth-percentile sizes, the head's mean angle from the body and from the chest, and the held leans (idle stretches longer than eight seconds with the neck more than 8 cm out). It runs only while the recorded hand-over case is `on foot`, keeps its state through a spell off the ground shorter than a second, turns the room with every recenter and follow bank, and snaps the pelvis under the neck at a manual or seat recenter, as the rig's recenter does. `--tuning` takes the rig's tuning as JSON (`{"pelvis":{"lean_radius_m":0.25}}`), with any field left out at its default. `tools/analysis/plot_rig_eval.py <out.csv> <dir> [<from>-<to>...]` plots it against the video's time ([body/rig.md](../body/rig.md#offline-evaluation)). On a version 8 recording, `--pelvis recorded` feeds it the payload's own rig inputs and `--pelvis pre-ik` the game's animated hips, and each row is diffed against the rig the game ran ([rig.md](../body/rig.md#offline-evaluation)). With `--weights`, `--ik` also replays the IK body's FK pelvis and foot contacts over the recomputed estimate and logs a summary for each `--contact <json>` tuning given (the contact model's tuning, any field left out at its default), or once with the defaults: the steps per standing minute and their rules, the reach the planted feet would need, the twist's distribution, and the steps per glance ([ik-body.md](../body/ik-body.md#the-offline-replay)).

`tracking-synth export-rerun [--from <time>] [--to <time>] [--video <video>] <file> -o <out.rrd>` writes a recording, or a stretch of it, as a Rerun recording to scrub in the Rerun viewer: the head, the controllers, Rico, the offline rig, and the throws and snatches in 3D, the scalars as plots, the events as a text log and state lanes, and the take's video cut to the stretch ([rerun.md](rerun.md)).

`tracking-synth estimate-body --weights <safetensors> [--from <time>] [--to <time>] [--lead <s>] <file> -o <out.csv>` runs the learned body estimator over a stretch from the recording's raw head and grip poses and writes its estimate at 60 Hz as a CSV, starting `--lead` seconds (10 by default) before the stretch so the estimator's window is full at its start ([body/body-estimator.md](../body/body-estimator.md)).

`tracking-synth scan-view [--video <video>] [--out <report.md>] <file>` scans a take's video for flashes and checks each against the camera's place relative to Rico's head ([view-scan.md](view-scan.md)).

## Synthetic recordings

A synthetic recording is written from a script instead of recorded in a headset, so the body rig and the offline tools can be exercised without anyone wearing one. `tracking-synth` writes them:

```sh
cargo run -p tracking-synth --target x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu   # on Linux
cargo run -p tracking-synth                                     # on Windows
```

With no script named, the tool writes every built-in script to `target/tracking-synth/<script>.jc3trk` in the repository it was built from. Naming scripts writes only those, `--out <dir>` writes elsewhere, `--rate <hz>` samples at another frame rate than 90 Hz, and `--list` prints the scripts with a line on each. The files are regenerated rather than committed: each runs to between 400 KB and a megabyte. Regenerate them after a change to the tool, the file format, or the semantic action schema. The tool's own tests generate them in memory as fixtures for `rig-eval` and `export-rerun`.

The scripts are:

- `stand_still`: standing for twelve seconds with a relaxed stance's sway.
- `walk_room_forward`: walking 1.5 m forward, pausing, and stepping back to the start.
- `walk_room_square`: walking a 1.5 m square clockwise, turning the body right at each corner.
- `lean_forward`: leaning from the hips over a table, the head 35 cm forward and 20 cm down, and back.
- `crouch`: squatting 45 cm at the hips and standing up.
- `turn_in_place`: turning the whole body 90° left on the spot, the head leading, and back.
- `stick_walk`: standing still with the left stick pushed forward for 3 s at full, 3 s at half, then released.
- `stick_strafe_and_turn`: strafing right and left with the left stick, flicking the right stick right twice, and holding it left.
- `look_around`: turning the head 70° left and right and looking down at the floor, the torso still.

A script (`tools/tracking-synth/src/scripts.rs`) describes the player as a body rather than as three tracked devices. Its tracks are keyed over time and eased between keys with the quintic smootherstep, so every key is a moment of rest, as a body's moves start and stop. The body's tracks are where it stands on the floor, its heading, how far the hips drop, how far the torso leans forward about the hips, the head's yaw and pitch on the neck, and the amount of standing sway; each hand's tracks are an offset of the grip from where the arm hangs, the thumbstick, the trigger, the squeeze value and force, and the buttons held.
