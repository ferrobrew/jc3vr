# Physical weapons

A weapon behaves as one physical object: it is held in whichever hand took it, it moves between hands, it is picked up by the hand that touches it, and it goes onto the body and comes off it as the same object. The loadout table says where every weapon is ([two-handed-inventory.md](two-handed-inventory.md)), and the game's slots follow it; the game keeps owning the weapon objects, the reserves, and the HUD of the selected weapon, and the mod owns where each gun is and which hand fires it. This page covers the hand-facing mechanics; the sockets on the body are in [weapon-sockets.md](weapon-sockets.md), the gun as a body joined to the hand bodies in [physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md), grenades, C4, and the detonator in [grenades.md](grenades.md), and aiming down the sights and guns against walls in [long-gun-poses.md](long-gun-poses.md). The engine side is in [weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md) and [item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md), and the attach bones in [weapon-attachment-and-arm-ik.md](../../engine/character/weapon-attachment-and-arm-ik.md).

Everything here acts only while the bindings inject, under the controller scheme with a controller active. The code is `crates/jc3vr-weapons/src/controllers/weapons/hold/`, and its numbers are the hold tuning (`crates/jc3vr-weapons/tuning/hold.toml`, [tuning.md](../tuning.md)).

## Either hand

A weapon rides the hand the loadout table holds it in ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The live table"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-live-table)). The table's hands change at once by the hands' own moves: a draw off a socket, a dock onto one, a pickup or a take, a handover, a let-go. A weapon the game wields on its own lands in a hand of the table's once per input tick:

- A pickup or a take leaves the grabbing hand expecting that one weapon, so another weapon the game wields first does not take it. The expectation lapses after the inventory tuning's `loadout.expect_s`.
- A weapon the game wields on its own (the weapon wheel, a switch, an automatic draw) lands in the acting hand (`scheme::acting_hand()`) when the table has it empty, otherwise in the other hand. While both hands hold something, it is put away again.
- A weapon the game puts away on its own (its holster act, a vehicle's entry) leaves its hand in the table, docked on the socket the game docks its slot on, or let go of when it left the slots.

While neither hand holds a weapon, either hand draws a long gun off the back (`volumes::reserve_long_guns`): a right-handed player reaches across to the left shoulder for the special weapon, or takes either shoulder with the left hand. The layout of the pouches follows the gun hand, so a long gun drawn by the off-hand moves it with it.

A single pistol ([below](#separate-pistols)) is a weapon of one gun and moves as a rifle does. The game's pair, while one exists, is one weapon of the table's, held in one hand with its second gun in the other.

## The owned attach bones

A gun is placed at the world matrix of the bone its attach-bone table ([`WeaponData::m_lBoneAttachementPerWeaponState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_lBoneAttachementPerWeaponState)) names for the weapon's state. The game rewrites the tables at the end of every pose-finalization pass ([`WeaponBase::UpdatePostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdatePostFinalizePose)), and its state tasks move guns between bones within the pass, so the mod writes the holding hand's bones at three points: before each placement ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)), after the end-of-pass rewrite ([`Inventory::UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose)), and at the weapon's own per-tick update, before it fires. The detours live in `hold/mod.rs`; they run on the simulation's worker threads for every character, so they compare against the table's published hands before taking any lock.

A single gun on the other hand's attach bone is moved to its counterpart on the holding hand, whatever the slot. A weapon of two guns (the game's pair) keeps its first gun in the holding hand and its second in the other, so held in the left hand its layout is mirrored (`hold::layout::placed_bone`). The write is idempotent, so the three write points agree. The game's own moves within a hand, and a gun it puts on a holster or back bone, are kept. Both the wielding and the dual-wielding entries are written, and the weapon's state is never changed. A gun whose hand changed since its last placement skips its render interpolation for that placement ([`WeaponBase::SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame)), so a handover moves the gun in one frame rather than streaking across.

Everything that derives a gun's hand from its attach bone follows without change: the rigid grip, the gun aim's sight line, the shot's hand (recoil, heft, and fire haptics), the reload's free hand, the magazine pouch's side (`volumes::gun_side`), and the render-time late update.

## The right hand's grip

The rigid grip pins a gun to its hand's wrist at the hand's canonical grip, the hand's attach bone's pose on its wrist in the skeleton's bind pose ([hands-and-aiming.md, "The rigid grip"](hands-and-aiming.md#the-rigid-grip)). The skeleton relates its left and right bones with every local axis flipped rather than as mirror images, so one gun model hung off both attach bones sits in one hand turned half round from the other hand's mirror: the left hand's grip holds the game's pistols the right way round, and the right hand's own grip holds a gun backwards.

The left hand always takes its own grip. The right hand takes its own grip unless it is turned more than the hands tuning's `grip.auto_turn_deg` from the left hand's grip mirrored onto the right wrist, which it then takes (`hands::grip::pick_right_grip`, in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/hands/grip.rs`). The mirror is across the body's lateral axis (the axis the two wrists' bind positions differ most along) and flips the gun's own lateral axis, so the gun stays right-handed. The Status page's `Right hand grip` row shows which grip the right hand took and the angle between the two.

## Firing

Only the holding hand's trigger fires a gun, and in a vehicle the holding hand's vehicle weapon action, which the gun takes over from the vehicle's weapon; in the driver's seat the triggers are the pedals and that action alone fires the gun ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Land vehicles"](bindings.md#vehicles)). With a gun in the off-hand, that hand's trigger presses the off-hand fire action (`fire_off_hand_pistol`), and the tether retract moves to the off-hand's stick click. Both triggers drive the one fire action, so while the wielded gun is in one hand and only the other hand's trigger is pulled, the weapon handling holds the fire inputs back (`weapons::finish::fire::primary_trigger_idle`).

Every gun in a hand fires from that hand's own trigger: the wielded weapon, whose update the inventory's fan-out runs with its hand's fire input in place of the game's, and each gun armed beside it, which the mod updates ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The per-hand tick"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-per-hand-tick)). A gun fires while its trigger is pulled and nothing holds it back: the hand carries it by a part other than its grip, its dock's slide runs, a reload act runs, the true reload holds it ([reload.md](reload.md)), or the shooting permission is withheld. The game's pair fires from the game's own fire input, which alternates its guns.

### The shooting permission

Every held-gun fire path (the input layer's fire readiness, the per-hand update, and the wielded weapon's substitute input) takes the game's shooting permission through one gate, `weapons::permission::shooting_allowed`: [`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsShooting`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsShooting), or a waiver. The game withholds the permission in free fall, fast fall, the wingsuit's rolls, evades, flinches, and exits, and the parachute's opening ([weapon-firing.md, "The permission by state"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-firing.md#the-permission-by-state)). Those states pose the game's own arms and mostly put its weapon away, but a gun in a hand is posed by the controller and updated by the mod, so the gate waives the permission while the character flies the wingsuit, is under a deployed or vehicle-attached parachute, or free- or fast-falls.

The waiver never applies during a grapple wind-up, a reel-in, or while the hook has a live wire, which keeps the game's shoot-while-reeling upgrade and its live-wire rule, except on a line of the mod's own: while the snatch's line is out or the grapple lock holds a swing, the gate waives the permission on foot and in the air, since the other hand holds that line. It never applies on the ground, swimming, or in a vehicle otherwise, so vehicle entries and exits, ragdolls, hit reactions, grenade throws, and melee still withhold fire.

Where the permission is withheld and not waived, the gun keeps its trigger reserved and a press does nothing. Releasing the reservation would hand the trigger to the game's own action for it mid-press (accelerate in a car, for one) whenever a short transition withholds the permission.

## Held guns during traversal

The mode table (`weapons::capabilities::Capabilities`) lets the held guns act (fire, release, handover, support, docking, and reload) in every mode but UI mode: on the ground, swimming, falling, under the parachute, in the wingsuit, and in any seat. The explosive pouches are left out swimming and in the wingsuit, and the weapons in the world without a body and the weapon wheel are also left out in a seat. Controller disable, lost tracking, and input-owner stand-down still prevent fire.

A hand on a firing grip can fire its own gun. A hand carrying the gun by another part or supporting the other hand's gun reserves its trigger without firing. In a driver's seat no gun reserves a trigger, so the pedals stay the pedals. `TickStart::held` publishes these reservations for the bindings layer, which suppresses only the semantic actions sourced from a reserved trigger; on the tick ownership changes, it suppresses a whole engine target a changed trigger contributes to, so another source of that target can lose that tick too.

## Handing a gun over

A grab volume (`GrabVolume::Handle`) sits on the pistol grip of the gun held in one hand while the other is free, within `hold.handle_radius_m` (player-scale). Its place is the grip the last native placement put relative to the holding wrist, carried onto the wrist as it is now (`hold::world::grip_on_current_wrist`). A new placement, a hand change, or the hold's teardown invalidates the record, and without a valid one the volume sits at the holding wrist.

A grip press there hands the gun to the free hand: the table moves it at once, the attach-bone tables follow on the next placement, and the grab pulses the grab cue. The transfer target needs a free receiving hand and no engaged foregrip; a touch on another gun region keeps that region's action, and the reload and action targets keep their own arbitration. A long gun's handover volume sits on its grip wherever the gun is, and a hand can also carry a long gun by another part, unable to fire it ([physical-guns.md, "Grabbing a gun anywhere"](physical-guns.md#grabbing-a-gun-anywhere)).

## Picking a weapon up into the hand

A grab of a weapon lying in the world ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md)) takes that weapon straight into the grabbing hand instead of clicking the use-item action. On the input tick it checks that the reached body still belongs to the weapon (a weapon's body leaves the world when it is picked up or despawned, so this proves the object is alive), that the weapon is still dropped, that it is not a hand grenade and does not give a copy ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)), and that the animation allows a pickup ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsPickup)).

The take goes through the loadout table (`hold::pickup::take_by_table`), and the game's equip never runs, so the other hand keeps what it holds. The weapon becomes the player's without the game's pickup: its pickup virtual ([`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup)) sets the player as its holder and takes its bodies out of the world, and its stored rounds go to the reserve as the game's pickup gives them ([`Inventory::AddAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-AddAmmo)). A heavy weapon stays dropped after the pickup virtual, since the game only ever wields one straight from the ground, so the carry holsters it with [`WeaponBase::SetState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SetState). The reconcile then makes the game follow: the weapon goes into its slot and is wielded when the hand is the primary, and is armed in the hand otherwise ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The slots as a projection"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-slots-as-a-projection)). The gun then slides into the hand over `hold.snap_s` from its pose in the world before the take, so it is seen to leave the ground rather than appear in the hand.

The game's own pickup takes the weapon instead for the embedded power core, whose pickup spawns and equips another weapon. It leaves the hand expecting that weapon and calls [`WeaponBase::PickupItem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupItem) with the character's game-object subobject, the call a swimming character's pickup makes: the weapon goes into the inventory and is equipped there and then, with no pickup animation. The weapon already in the slot leaves the slot first and stays where the table has it on the body ([inventory-world-and-carry.md, "The weapons beyond the slots"](inventory-world-and-carry.md#the-weapons-beyond-the-slots)).

Under the controller scheme the hands are the only way to pick a weapon up (`hold::button_pickup`). The game's weapon pickup panel is hidden: in place of the player's pickup readout ([`Player::UpdateWeaponHUD`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-UpdateWeaponHUD)) the mod clears the panel and its remembered offer, so the game shows it again once the gamepad scheme returns. The use-item weapon input reads zero for the length of the weapon handling's call that queues the pickup act ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponHandling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponHandling)) and is put back afterwards, so the use action still enters vehicles and runs the other interactions.

A refused pickup falls back to the use-item click, which passes the held-back input for `hold.use_item_grant_ms`, so the game picks the weapon up its own way. The refusals that do not fall back are a hand that holds something already, a take the table did not complete (the weapon is put back into the world where it is), and a game's pickup that ran but left the weapon out of the hands.

### The use-item pickup

The game's own pickup (the use-item action's pickup animation, whose track message completes it, and a swimming character's pickup) is routed into the same take (`hold::use_item`). For the local player, the detour of [`WeaponBase::PickupItem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupItem) does not run it for a weapon of the slots the table tracks: it keeps the weapon alive and queues it, and the next input tick takes it through the table into a free hand, the dominant first, or puts it back into the world with both hands full. A weapon with unlimited stock, whose copy comes from the pickup's start ([`WeaponBase::PickupStart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupStart)), goes into no slot, so its completion is skipped.

## Taking a weapon from a source

Every take of a weapon into a hand goes through one path (`hold::source`), whatever the weapon's source: lying in the world, held out by a placement rule (on a rack, or set down in a locker), one gun of a rack's pistol pair ([paired-rack-weapons.md](paired-rack-weapons.md)), or a gun in a living character's hands or holstered on its body. The source decides how the weapon is given up; the delivery decides how it reaches the hand, and is the only difference between a hand's grab and the [grapple snatch](grapple-snatch.md):

- a hand that closes on the weapon within reach takes it at once (`source::take`): the source gives it up, and it slides into the hand through the pickup above;
- the snatch takes it from range (`source::free`): the source gives it up into the world with its bodies, the line pulls it in, and the catching hand takes it through the same pickup (`source::deliver`). A source that makes the weapon it gives (a rack's copy, a pair's staged pistol) gives it a few frames later, placed where it hung.

Every source makes the same check before it gives its weapon up (`pickup::preflight`), so a take the hand could not finish leaves the weapon where it was: the weapon is one a hand takes (not a hand grenade, and not a weapon that hands out copies unless the take is of the copy), the table or the game's pickup can take it into the hand, and on the game's route the animation allows a pickup. The table records the source and the delivery apart (`TakeSource`, `Delivery`), so a snatched weapon keeps its provenance.

### Offers on weapons without a body

A gun in a character's hands and a weapon on a rack have no body in the physics world, so the hands' reach never finds them. `hold::world` offers them instead: each is a segment along the gun from the grip of the hand that holds it to the other grip ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition), or the gun's origin without one), carried into the cockpit frame, and each hand without a gun is offered the point of each segment nearest it as a grip target (`GrabVolume::WorldWeapon`), marked where the hand would take it. A grab takes the target whose segment is nearest the hand, and the same distance bounds the offer and the completion. Racks and lockers are reached within `hold.take_radius_m`, and guns on characters within the wider `hold.steal_radius_m`, since the character moves and turns while the hand closes (both player-scale). Source, hand, and occupancy checks still run at completion. These offers do not cover dropped weapon bodies or the snatch's remote targets, which have their own probes.

## Taking a gun from a character

A free hand takes a gun out of a living character's hands, or off its holster, by grabbing it (`hold::steal`). The inventory's end-of-pass detour, which runs for every character, notes each other character within `hold.steal.notice_range_m` of the player (out to the snatch's range while the [grapple snatch](grapple-snatch.md) is on) that wields a gun of the pistol, two-handed, special, or heavy slot (`engine::takeable_gun_slot`, which also says which rack and locker weapons a hand or the snatch takes), and the guns of those slots holstered and visible on its body: each gun's segment, its first gun's pose, and a weak reference to the character from its own ([`Character::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WeakThis)), which says whether it still lives without keeping it alive.

Within reach, the gun wins over the character holding it however much nearer the character's capsule is ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md#reaching-a-character)), and the hand bodies do not strike a character whose gun the hand is after ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md#hits)). A grip there:

1. locks the character alive for the whole take ([`WeakPtrGameObject::Lock`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/types::shared_ptr::WeakPtrGameObject##func-Lock)) and checks that it still holds the gun in the slot noted (in its hands, or still holstered), that the player's animation allows a pickup, and that the table can take the gun, so a refused take leaves the gun where it was;
2. drops the gun at once while the character's inventory still holds it ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop)), which unequips it from the character;
3. lets the slot go ([`Inventory::DropWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DropWeapon)), which defers no second drop since the gun is already dropped: with the selection on for a gun in its hands, as its death does, so it selects another weapon, and with it off for a holstered gun, so it keeps what it wields;
4. staggers the character toward the player ([`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction)) with an impulse between its stumble and knockdown thresholds (`hold.steal.over_stumble`, capped at `hold.steal.under_ragdoll`), so it reacts to the yank without falling;
5. takes the gun into the grabbing hand through the table, sliding there from where it was on the character.

What the character does next is the game's handling of a character with nothing wielded ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "A character that loses its gun"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#a-character-that-loses-its-gun)): it draws another weapon it carries, and may go looking for one lying in the world.

## Taking a weapon off a rack

A free hand takes a weapon off a rack, or out of a locker, by grabbing it (`hold::rack`). The input tick walks the player's valid interaction entries as a swimming character's pickup does ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "Racks"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#racks)), takes each weapon an entry offers in the rack state or lying in the world (held alive while it is read), and offers it as above. A locker's weapon is offered the same way out of the rack state. One a character holds, one already being picked up, and a mounted gun are left out. A grip there finds the weapon again among the entries, holding it alive through the take, and:

- a weapon the rack hands over goes into the grabbing hand through the table, after the call that starts the game's own pickup of it ([`WeaponBase::PickupStart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupStart)), which takes it out of the rack state and tells the rack, and slides there from the rack. An entry also offers a weapon lying in the world, such as a gun the player just let go of, which the take names as taken off the ground (`hold::rack::offered_source`); [`WeaponBase::m_SpawnedFromRule`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_SpawnedFromRule) stays on a weapon after it leaves the rule's spot, so it alone does not tell a locker's weapon from one let go of.
- a rack or locker with unlimited stock ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)) gives a copy instead (`hold::rack::copy`). The take starts the game's pickup once, which tells the rack's placement rule and asks the inventory for a copy of the same spawn tag with a full magazine. The request goes through the rack-member give fence (`inventory::gives`), so the copy spawns a few frames later without the give's equip, extra ammunition, or replacement drop, and is staged rather than adopted: it leaves the inventory and is placed where the rack's weapon hangs, and the rack's own weapon is hidden ([`WeaponBase::SetVisible`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetVisible)), held alive. The staged copy then goes into the grabbing hand as the rack's own weapon would, and the rack's weapon fades back in after `hold.rack.restock_after_ms`. A copy that has not come within `hold.rack.copy_wait_ms` lets the rack's weapon show again. One copy is on its way at a time, and after an unresolved member request was cancelled, copies stay closed until the game restarts. The rack's own weapon keeps its pickup, so the game's use-item action still works there.

A weapon of a kind the player already carries in its slot is not among the valid entries as the game has it: the pickup graph's not-carried condition ([`InteractionGraphParentWeaponNotInInventory::Evaluate`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/interaction::InteractionGraphParentWeaponNotInInventory##func-Evaluate)) fails while the slot holds a weapon with the same [`m_WeaponId`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeaponId). Each hand holds its own weapon of the dual-wield slot, so for the local player a detour on the condition lets it pass for a weapon of that slot (`hold::rack::offer`), and a second submachine gun off a rack is offered and taken as a weapon of another kind would be. The two-handed and special slots keep the game's rule.

Out of reach, the snatch takes from the same sources the same way ([grapple-snatch-targets.md, "Racks and lockers"](grapple-snatch-targets.md#racks-and-lockers)).

## The sockets

Every weapon on the body sits on a socket of the loadout table's: each hip and each shoulder. An empty hand draws the weapon on a socket, and a hand holding a weapon docks it on a free socket that fits it, both without the game's acts; a release near a socket draws the weapon onto it, and a release on a taken socket swaps. [weapon-sockets.md](weapon-sockets.md) has the volumes, the draw and the dock, the attraction, and the swap.

## Letting go

With `controllers.weapons.hold.grip_to_hold` (on), a gun is held only while its grip is. Each hand watches the weapon the table has in it (`hold::dock::ReleaseWatch`). A gun taken by a grab (a draw, a pickup, a take, or a handover) is watched from the tick it is in the hand with the grip held. A gun that came into the hand some other way (the weapon wheel, a draw the game made) is watched from the tick the grip closes on it: a press, not a grip already held when the gun arrived, so a hand that held a vehicle's control while the game drew the gun does not drop it. Opening a watched grip does, as the table decides (`loadout::grab::release`):

- over a free socket that takes the gun, it docks the gun there;
- outside every socket's reach but within a socket's attraction, and not thrown, the gun travels onto that socket ([weapon-sockets.md, "Attraction"](weapon-sockets.md#attraction));
- anywhere else, it lets the gun fall, unless the other hand braces a long gun, which then stays in that hand, carried where it holds it ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md));
- for the game's pair, which cannot fall, it docks the pair on the first free socket the body prefers for it, or keeps it in the hand with none free.

With `grip_to_hold` off, a gun stays in the hand until a grab of a socket docks it.

### Stand-downs

A stand-down (a tracking or focus loss, a load) stops the watch but leaves the gun in the hand (`hold::act::clear`), and so does a game menu, the map, or the debug panel, which pause the weapon handling rather than standing it down (`weapons::stand_down` with a `StandDownScope::Pause` reason): the guns' holds, the armed weapons, and a grenade or a charge in a hand all stay as they were.

On the first tick back the watch reads the controller's grip as it is (`intent::grip_holds_now`), since the returning hand is masked until its controls rest; an untracked hand counts as closed. A gun under a grip held through the stand-down stays until that grip opens. A gun whose grip opened during the stand-down goes back on a socket that takes it, the one it was last drawn off first, and falls only when no socket takes it; when a socket would take it but cannot now (an animation that forbids holstering, as on a motorcycle), it stays in the hand until the grip closes on it again. Entering a vehicle does not release the gun.

### The release motion

A hand release copies the first gun's world point before disarming it, and asks the body's one release estimate for that point's motion (`physics::release::at_with`, [physics-substrate.md, "Release velocities"](../body/physics-substrate.md#release-velocities)), the same estimate grenades, C4, and the physical hands' props take. The estimate, its transport of the player's own movement, the speed cap (`release.max_hand_mps`), and the rig's jumps are the substrate's; the weapon-specific facts are these. The hand's history starts when that weapon enters the hand, so a newly acquired gun does not take the previous item's throw. Cached gun velocity is not added. In slow motion the hand's part of the motion is requested per game second, so the gun leaves the hand as fast as the hand moved ([slow-motion.md](slow-motion.md#throws)). The sampled point is the gun's model origin rather than a native centre of mass.

The release runs [`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop), then requests the motion on the native gun body ([`WeaponData::m_PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_PfxInstance)); the setter checks that the body is dynamic and still belongs to the weapon. Both hands, ordinary slots, and supported heavy weapons take this path. A strong reference keeps the weapon alive through the drop and the setter. The completed drop clears [`WeaponBase::m_DeferredDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_DeferredDrop), so a queued drop cannot replace the motion on the next pre-simulation update. The table then takes the let-go, and the weapon lies in the world, pinned while near ([inventory-world-and-carry.md, "The weapons in the world"](inventory-world-and-carry.md#the-weapons-in-the-world)).

`hold::act::status().releases` keeps a copy of the latest let-go attempt, refused or not (`hold::release_diagnostics`), with the route and stages it reached and the requested velocity beside the game's cached measured velocities, for diagnosing a throw that left with the wrong motion. Nothing reads the native velocity back after the setter, whose access contract at that site is unproven.

## Separate pistols

The game's pistols are one weapon with two guns ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "The dual-wield weapon"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#the-dual-wield-weapon)), so neither pistol could be dropped or held apart from the other. Each pistol is a weapon object of its own, a single pistol (`weapons::dual_wield::single`), and an item of the loadout table's like any other weapon: two pistols are a pistol in each hand, each on its own hip. New single pistols are made while the loadout table runs and the hands have not stood down; pistols already single stay single.

A single pistol is the game's dual-wield object kept to its first gun, as the game keeps a pistol that a character wields rather than dual-wields:

- its second gun and that gun's model parts are hidden after every show the game makes (the detour of [`WeaponBase::SetVisibleBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SetVisibleBase)), and each input tick for the pistols the player owns;
- its next gun ([`WeaponBase::m_NextWeaponToFire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_NextWeaponToFire)) is held on the first at each of its updates and after each shot, so it fires only the gun that is shown;
- everything of the mod's that walks a weapon's guns sees one gun (`aim::gun::guns`);
- its magazine holds half the tuned size, rounded up, and the reserve is shared; a magazine over the half (after the game's own reload, or a pistol taken full from the world) is brought down at the next input tick, the rounds over it going back to the reserve;
- dropped, it is one pistol, and it stays one when picked up again.

The slot's pistol stays in the dual-wielding state when equipped, the state the game's draw gives the player's pistols; only its first gun is in a hand.

A pair the game hands over (a new game's loadout, a supply drop, a mission's give-back, or a respawn whose sidecar knows nothing of separate pistols) is split. The slot keeps it as a single pistol, and a second pistol of its kind, the twin, is asked of the spawn system at the player ([`SpawnSystem::SpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-SpawnTagIndex), the call the inventory's respawn makes). When it comes, the carry set keeps it, the table restores it onto the hip the slot's pistol does not take (or the socket the table prefers for it), and the pair's magazine is dealt between the two. A pair split while it is in a hand keeps a pistol in each hand: with the other hand empty, the twin comes into it at once.

Until the twin comes, the pair is the game's. It stays the game's when no socket would take the twin, while the carried weapons are stashed for a mission, or when the twin has not come within `dual_wield.twin_wait_s`. A twin no longer wanted when it comes is hidden in the player's keeping until the next reset, never left in the world as a pistol too many. A pistol taken from the world (by any source above) is one pistol, an item of its own in the grabbing hand; pistols never merge back into a pair. A weapon of the pistol slot with one gun is never split.

The game docks the pistol slot's first gun on the right hip; on the left hip the mod places a pistol, as it does any weapon on a socket other than its slot's own. The game places a gun on a holster bone with the gun's own attach offset ([`WeaponData::m_AttachOffset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_AttachOffset)), and a single pistol shows only its first gun, whose offset is the pair's right pistol's. On the left hip the mod therefore places a single pistol with its second gun's offset, exchanging the two for the placement and back (`inventory::carry::borrows_second_offset`), keeping each gun's previous matrix so its render interpolation is unchanged.

The save sidecar and the reset's record note whether the slot's pistol and each carried pistol is single ([inventory-persistence.md, "Saves, resets, and loads"](inventory-persistence.md#saves-resets-and-loads)), so the respawn brings the slot's pistol back as one pistol beside its carried twin. A carried single pistol that never comes back after a reset makes the slot's pistol of its kind the game's pair again, so no pistol is lost. The Status page's `Separate pistols` row shows the slot's pistol, the twin, and the counts.

## Settings and readout

The settings are `controllers.weapons.hold.grip_to_hold` and `controllers.weapons.holsters.stick_click_cycles` ([settings.md](../settings.md)). The handover, the separate pistols, the pickup, the weapon in each hand, the takes, the sockets, the sights, and the wall push ([long-gun-poses.md](long-gun-poses.md)) have no switches of their own.

Each call the hold makes is the game's own, and none writes the inventory directly, but some go where the game never does: they equip and unequip without the animation graph's acts, a let-go takes a weapon out of the inventory (which only a heavy weapon's drop does in the game), a weapon beside the wielded one is updated in the holstered state, a take from a character drops a living character's gun outside an animation state's drop task, and a take off a rack completes a pickup with no animation on land.

The debug UI's Weapons > Hold, transfer, and grab regions live status and the Status page's `Weapons in hand` section show each hand's weapon, how it came there, and the takes in reach.
