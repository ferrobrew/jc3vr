# Swinging on the snatch's line

The grapple snatch ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md)) can hold its target on a line instead of pulling it into the hand. The code is `snatch::sling`, `snatch::brace`, and `snatch::sling_hooks` in `jc3vr-grapple`, and the tuning is the `swing` section of the snatch's tuning file (`crates/jc3vr-grapple/tuning/snatch.toml`, [tuning.md](../tuning.md)).

The grapple hand swings the held target: drag it, whirl it around, or lift it into an arc, then open the grip to let it go with its current velocity. The game's own physics and damage do the rest, so an explosive barrel slung into a wall or a soldier explodes on impact as it would from any hit. Three things swing:

- A loose object too heavy for one hand to carry (`physical_hands::remote::carry_max_kg`, the pickup's one-hand strength against gravity) and no heavier than `swing.ceiling_kg` is offered as a heavy object and always swung (`rules::prop_handling`); once the hook bites, the line holds it. Above the ceiling nothing is offered. At the shipped tuning the one-hand strength is above the ceiling, so no loose object falls in this range: every one a hand can carry is on the pull path, and a heavy one widens its catch and slows its line. Vehicles are never offered, however light, nor a standing character, nor a part still on its vehicle (a turret, a door on its hinge; a closed door is offered as a door), since the hook's attachment selects the vehicle's body for it and refuses the snatch.
- A character that ragdolls, knocked down, thrown, or dead ([engine: ragdoll.md](../../engine/character/ragdoll.md#ragdolling)), is offered as a body when the whole ragdoll is no heavier than the ceiling (`physical_hands::remote::ragdoll_target`, `snatch::target::ragdoll_offer`). The hook latches the character's bone nearest the body the aim hit, or nearest the hit when the aim met the character's capsule or hit proxy ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md#reaching-a-character)). The line's strength and pull are those of the whole ragdoll's mass. Each tick's pull is an impulse: the physical hands' tuning's `remote.ragdoll_lead_share` of it goes to the hooked body at the hook, so the body comes limb first, and the rest is spread over every body of the ragdoll by its mass (`remote::pull_ragdoll`, `remote::spread_pull`), since the ragdoll's constraints cannot pass a whole body's momentum along the chain of bones within one physics step. A character whose ragdoll cannot be read is pulled by the hooked body alone. A body's line reels in at `ragdoll.mps` to `ragdoll.length_m` from the grapple hand, slowing as it nears that length to `ragdoll.ease_per_s` times the line left per second (never below the swing's reel), so the body arrives at a walk at arm's reach, where the free hand can take hold of it ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md)).
- A weapon or a light object swings while B (the grapple semantic button) is held: grip the target, hold B, and yank, or yank and then hold B while the hook flies or the line pulls, which takes the line at the target's distance. Releasing B while the grip stays closed resumes the ordinary pull to the hand with a fresh stall watch.

The line (`snatch::sling`) is a stiff, tension-only spring-damper from the grapple hand to the target's centre of mass at a held length: the rope the grapple lock also uses (`grapple::rope`, [grapple-lock.md](grapple-lock.md#the-shared-rope)), with the hand as its kinematic end. Each tick it asks the target's velocity along the line, relative to the moving hand, for the stiffness times the stretch past the held length plus the damping times the target's speed away from the hand, and gives no more tension than the line's strength. Velocity across the line and angular velocity are untouched, a slack line never pushes, and gravity and collisions stay the engine's. The change is written through the body-velocity path every hand's hold uses, so the target is never placed or keyframed.

The line's strength scales with the target's mass (`sling::line_strength_n`): `strength_n` at `reference_mass_kg`, times the mass over the reference to the power `mass_scaling`. Below one, the line pulls a heavy target harder than a light one but speeds it up less, so a fuel barrel takes a real swing to move and a rifle swings briskly; at one every mass swings alike, and at zero the line pulls every mass equally hard. A heavy target takes a bigger swing, and the ceiling, not a weak line, keeps wrecks out.

The held length starts as the distance at the bite and reels in at `reel_mps` toward `swing_length_m`, so a target hooked far off is drawn in and swung around the player rather than hanging off a long line that a hand's motion turns by only a few degrees. The reel takes in line no faster than the target comes, keeping it at most `reel_lead_m` inside the target (`sling::reel_in`), so a heavy target that lags is pulled at the line's strength. It never pays line out. A rope cannot push, so a target reeled in keeps its speed once the reel stops and coasts on toward the player until friction, gravity, or a swing takes it. A reel speed of zero keeps the length the hook bit at.

The swung target collides with everything, the player's own body included. The line's anchor is the tracked wrist frame with the aim tuning's `ray.grapple_origin_m`, and the native device proxy's visual getters receive that frame during the owned wire update, so the game's line runs from the tracked forearm. A stretch past `max_stretch_m` ("the target is beyond the line"), an anchor faster than `max_anchor_speed_mps` (a tracking jump), an invalid sample, tracking loss, a lost line, changed target ownership, an occupied hand, a hand-role change, leaving the supported mode, or opening a menu ends the line. A stationary swing does not use the pull's stall timeout ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md#the-pull-and-the-catch)).

The owned wire's native constraint creation and tension callbacks are excluded while swinging (`sling_hooks`), so the target does not receive a second rope force and Rico is not pulled toward a heavy one. The exclusion starts at the fire, before the hook bites, and ends before an ordinary pull resumes. The input tick owns target references and body access outside physics work; the hooks copy the published anchor and compare wire and proxy identities, and never resolve target references or drive target bodies on a worker.

`GrappleButton` owns a fresh B press only after the grip has armed a remote target, from the arming until the catch or the miss; on a heavy object the owned press does nothing, rather than firing the game's grapple from under the armed snatch. It consumes that press and release through cancellation and stand-down until a neutral observation on the original hand and on-foot route. B before the grip remains an ordinary grapple. The native custom-wire state ignores ordinary fire and reel input, and the input owner prevents delayed zip, tether, or release gestures from the consumed episode.

## The two-handed brace

While the line swings, the other hand can brace the grapple hand: squeeze its grip with its wrist within `brace_take_m` of the grapple hand's wrist (`snatch::brace`). The other hand must be free and empty, its grip closed on nothing; that grip is already held for the snatch while the line is out, so the brace is taken by holding the grip near the wrist, not only by a fresh squeeze there, and the squeeze takes nothing in reach. Braced, the line's strength is `brace_factor` times one hand's, and the line is anchored at the average of the two hands, keeping the grapple hand's offset to the line's origin; the anchor's move between the hand and the pair is not read as a motion of the hands. The brace holds until the grip opens, the hand takes something, a wrist loses tracking, or the wrists drift more than `brace_break_m` apart.

The other hand can instead take hold of the line ahead of the wrist, which anchors the line at that hand ([grapple-line-grab.md](grapple-line-grab.md)).

## Feedback

The grapple hand feels the line's tension by its share of the line's strength, and so does the bracing hand while it braces. In-world, the latch draws the line while it swings: from the grapple hand to the target's centre, its colour running from the latch's cyan when slack to red at the line's strength and its width growing with the tension, a ring on it where the held length ends (a target beyond the ring is stretching the line), and a dot at the anchor between the hands while braced (the snatch tuning's `latch`).

The `Snatch` status row shows the swing ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md#readouts)), and the log has `snatch: swinging on the held line` with the length, mass, and strength.
