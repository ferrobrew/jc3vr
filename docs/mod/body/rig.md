# The body rig

The rig decides who moves Rico's root on each simulation tick, walks the root after the player's head in the standing posture, leaves a lean over the feet alone, holds the view at a wall the root cannot pass, estimates the player's torso and turns Rico to face it, and places his pelvis and his feet. The aim is the Boneworks topology from [head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md#boneworks--bonelab-alignment): the player's body is ground truth, inferred from the head and hands, and Rico's body is posed to match it as far as his skeleton and the game allow. The camera stays on the player's real head throughout: the root follows the head, never the other way round. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/rig/`, and the settings are `controllers.body.rig`. The procedural body writes the pose ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md)).

## Checked observation contract

`payload/src/controllers/body/rig/observation.rs` defines a pure contract for a raw tracking publication and a corresponding previous/current simulation pair. It is compiled only for tests and has no production consumer. The current body, root-follow, and camera paths below remain unchanged.

The tracking publication identifies its sequence, predicted display time, host publication time, and origin, calibration, scale, and authority versions. Head and grip observations must share that identity. Each simulation side retains its own completed step, root transform, world anchor, cockpit basis, completed compensation, and movement owner. Construction rejects crossed versions, inconsistent pair sides, and invalid finite pose data. Consumer freshness uses the host clock and rejects a future publication before calculating age.

Raw inputs remain unchanged. Derived composition normalizes accepted near-unit rotations and applies the selected anchor, scale, basis, and completed compensation once. The root supplies model conversion rather than another transport term. Unit tests cover distinct pair sides, completed versus blocked movement, coordinate rebases, independent hand validity, stale observations, and noncommuting rotations. These tests do not establish live sampling provenance, moving-support identity, or a roomscale fix.

`payload/src/controllers/body/rig/live_observation.rs` publishes bounded read-only evidence in production. The frame loop retains the head located before replay substitution and the exact cockpit-from-LOCAL transform used during acquisition. The input sampler returns its own controller frame with the sequence and host time assigned by `controllers::frame::publish`. The publisher accepts that frame only with the same predicted display time as the located head. It preserves independent tracking flags, inactive grips, focus, and missing head velocity. Replay, failed sampling, a frame-begin failure, or an absent live source clears the raw packet rather than pairing separate latest-value globals.

The pre-IK hook separately publishes the root matrices, animated head anchors, and current model-space head, neck, and eye landmarks that it already reads. The previous anchor is the current animated head joint transformed by the previous root; it is not a previous native skeleton snapshot. The native capture and raw packet have independent host times and are never presented as one checked simulation pair. Each source retains only its latest owned publication. The diagnostic accessor rejects future times, reports source age, and rejects changes in the existing teleport and recenter counters. Those counters do not establish a complete origin epoch.

The local pose-finalization completion retains one owned diagnostic record with a monotonic local completion sequence, the root matrices already read by the pre-IK capture, and the ledger pair and counts after `Requests::complete`. A publishable completion returns an opaque token that the same native pre-IK capture retains. The consumed request retains the owner captured at its matching submission, even if a later owner selection changes the simulation readout. A completion without a pending request has no request owner. Clearing the ledger invalidates the record and discards pending credit without resetting the sequence. Teleport detection suppresses the record until the simulation handles that transition. The root frame copies the completion and completed movement together under the simulation lock. It carries this owned source, the exact final previous/current cockpit shift, and the current basis used to reconstruct both shift sides through `ComposedCockpit` to the rendered VR pose publication. This current basis does not establish a historical composition-basis pair. `ObservationStatus::frame_match` matches only a present exact completion token shared by the frame source and native capture. A replaced or unavailable completion, missing native token, missing frame, stale publication, or changed diagnostic origin counters rejects the match without reusing an older result. Repeated rendered frames can use the same completion. Equal host times never establish identity. `ObservationStatus::completion` remains a separate latest-source readout. A successful frame match removes only the completed-ledger/shift prerequisite; composition bases, per-side owners, complete epochs, the previous native pose, and support identity remain missing.

The Body observation evidence section in the body-IK debug controls reads source availability, ages, tracking validity, and missing prerequisites without logging or changing the stored status. The existing body-IK status accessor remains separate from observation collection. The completed-ledger/shift prerequisite remains missing until an exact frame/native token match is available. Composition bases, completion-time ownership for both sides, complete origin, calibration, scale, and authority epochs, the previous native pose, and support identity remain explicit missing evidence even after a match. Calibration or scale changes therefore cannot produce an eligible checked observation. No live body estimate, root request, transport correction, support identity, or gait change follows from these publications. Pure publisher tests do not establish E1 shipment evidence.

The live tracking acquisition also retains a source-owned origin token and its exact controller publication receipt through frame composition. Detected recenter, follow-bank, runtime, and teleport changes advance a non-reused generation while their mutation guard remains active. Matching-session LOCAL reference-space notifications defer acquisition until their effective time; event loss, unknown timing, or bounded pending-storage exhaustion makes provenance unavailable until runtime reset. A change spanning acquisition or composition rejects the held token rather than relabeling an older sample. This token covers live tracking acquisition through composition only. The complete origin epoch of independently captured root/native historical sides remains missing, as do calibration, scale, and authority epochs. No checked body observation or new movement authority follows from this tracking-only evidence.

## Shadow constrained estimate

`payload/src/controllers/body/rig/estimate/` is a test-only numerical model for the unified body design. It does not own the live pose, camera, or locomotion. One persistent estimate contains intended stance, pelvis compensation, torso articulation, inferred foot contacts, rates, and observation provenance. Root requests derive from stance rather than pelvis hinge displacement. Completed support-relative root travel supplies gait credit. Candidate shoulder reach constrains torso heading without copying hand directions into body yaw.

The bounded fit distributes motion across the hips, knees, ankles, and spine. Continuity and a carried heading prior choose among poses with similar tracking fit; low head height alone does not establish knee or pelvis support. Contact transitions precede final realization, and published feet match the realized ankles. Swing geometry remains support-local. Planar search directions, rate limits, and costs share the completed root frame. Failed continuing searches can hold a revalidated previous pose with explicit constraint recovery, zeroed correction rates, current residuals, and reduced authority. This is not a claim that the tracked pose is geometrically impossible.

The shadow model currently accepts an injected finite horizontal support patch, not live terrain or a verified moving-platform identity. Its numerical tests cover ambiguous observations, frame transport, contact transitions, bounded recovery, and rate variation. The existing production behavior described below remains unchanged.

Each update caches repeated numerical contact fits in a fixed-size store and prepares temporal constraint intervals once per context. Reuse preserves the selected pose, full contact history, error ordering, and committed rates. Test-only reference execution compares these values bit for bit against the uncached path. `Estimator::last_work` reports executed and logical work separately, including failed searches.

One paired release-mode Wine profile used 250 synthetic updates per scenario and implementation. The optimized p95 update time was 0.429 ms for neutral observations, 1.790 ms for retargeting, and 0.490 ms for recovery, compared with 1.020 ms, 4.266 ms, and 1.249 ms for the reference path. These results describe one synthetic run, not a live gameplay budget. Representative live tail measurements and solve-gate integration remain prerequisites to activation.

## Who moves the root

Several systems want the root: the game's own states, the hand climbing, the animation's authored motions, and the player's head. Exactly one owns it on each tick (`rig::root::owner`), chosen once in the root hook, a post-call detour on the on-foot locomotion task ([`NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask##func-Update)). The task writes the proxy's wanted velocity every on-foot tick, idle included, and velocity written there goes through the proxy's character context, which handles stairs, slopes, and support. The owners, in precedence order:

| Owner | When | The chase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game | The task did not write the velocity (the task-defined motion state in [`Character::m_CurrentMotionState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_CurrentMotionState) is not set, or Rico is attached to a vehicle), or a seat transition is under way. Every other state that moves the proxy (the grapple, jumps, stunts, ragdoll, swimming, the parachute, the wingsuit) runs a different task. | None |
| Climb | A hand grips the world. The climb replaces the proxy's motion in the pre-simulation callback. | None, and the ledger records nothing |
| Authored | The animation carries the body: an authored one-off, a hard landing, cover, or a hit reaction's hold ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)). The view rides its root motion. | None |
| Seated | The seated posture. | None |
| Player | Otherwise: on foot, standing, out of a seat. | Added |

On every tick the task wrote the velocity, the hook first replaces the clip's velocity with the stick's ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md)) and adds the palms' push off what they press into; together these are the task's movement. While the player owns the root, the chase velocity is added to it through the character's world-space accessors ([`Character::GetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetWantedWorldVelocity) and [`Character::SetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetWantedWorldVelocity)), because the proxy's own wanted velocity is character-local. Nothing scales or caps the sum but a sanity bound of 12 m/s, applied to both parts alike. The stick and the chase are independent: the stick moves the root and the camera together, so it never changes how far the root is from the head, and the chase does not yield to it. A player walking in the room while pushing the stick moves at the sum of the two.

The same hook steps the head-through-geometry probe (below) and requests or releases the crouch capsule ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)).

## The chase

### The target

The root walks toward the ground point under the player's upright eyes (`rig::root::chase::target`): the neck pivot, which sits below and behind the eyes in the head's frame, less the arm from the pivot to the eyes turned by the torso's heading. Nodding and turning the head swing the eyes about the pivot, so they move neither the pivot nor the target. The steady view stands the eyes of a neutral head directly over the root ([locomotion.md, "The steady view"](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)), so in the cockpit frame the root sits at the cockpit's horizontal origin and the target's cockpit position less the cockpit shift is the gap from the root.

### The gap and the velocity

The frame half runs once per rendered frame from the pose composition, after the simulation ticks. It folds the newly credited chase into the cockpit shift, measures the gap to the target, takes the lean out of it (below), and publishes the remaining gap in world metres together with the running total of credited chase the shift had folded in, and the head's velocity.

Each tick the player owns the root, the chase asks for

```
v_chase = clamp_len(feed_forward · v_head + gain · g_eff / dt, max_speed)
```

where `g_eff` is the published gap less the chase movement credited since the frame measured it. Two ticks that fall in one frame (the game below 30 fps) therefore never chase the same gap twice, and nothing accumulates between frames: every tick starts from the head as the latest frame saw it. `gain` (`chase.gain`, 0.4 by default) is the share of the gap closed per tick, a time constant of about 65 ms at the game's 30 Hz.

`v_head` is the target's own velocity in the cockpit frame, carried into the world, smoothed over 50 ms, with the first 5 cm/s taken off so tracking noise never drives a root at rest. It cancels the lag a pure proportional chase leaves behind a walking player (about 12 cm at 1.4 m/s). It is zero while the gap lies inside the lean, because a head moving within a lean moves nothing below it. `feed_forward` (`chase.feed_forward`) weights it, 1 by default, and `max_speed_mps` (`chase.max_speed_mps`) bounds the chase at 4 m/s by default. The gain and the feed-forward are debug UI tunables, because how the proxy's character context answers a wanted velocity has not been measured.

## The lean

A player leaning over a table or reaching for the floor moves the head out over feet that stay put. Without an allowance the chase would walk the capsule into the table, and the capsule's push-back would turn the lean into the world shoving the player. A lean from the hips lowers the neck pivot as it moves it out, so the pivot's drop below the player's standing height `d` bounds how far it may be out without a step (`rig::lean`):

```
A(d) = clamp(gain · (d − deadband), 0, max)
```

with `lean.gain` 4 m per metre of drop, `lean.deadband_m` 4 cm for a standing head's bob, and `lean.max_m` 0.35 m by default. The allowance is widest along the torso's forward: an ellipse `A` forward, `lean.side_share` (half) of it to either side, and `lean.back_share` (a quarter) of it behind. The part of the gap inside the ellipse is the lean, and the chase closes what lies outside it; a gap outside is scaled back along itself onto the ellipse.

The standing height is the neck pivot's height that nine in ten of the recent samples lie below, sampled while the player is on foot, standing, and looking about level, the same measure the procedural body uses ([procedural-body.md, "The player's head"](procedural-body.md#the-players-head)), and measured afresh after a floor jump. Nothing else is held between frames, so standing back up removes the allowance and the gap together, with no root motion and nothing to forget. A crouch drops the pivot straight down and leaves no gap for the allowance to absorb. Walking in the room crouched leaves at most `A` of trail, which the spine shows as a lean and which drains the moment the head rises.

The lean also tells the procedural body how far the head is out over the feet it steps (`rig::lean_allowance`). With `lean.enabled` off the allowance is zero.

## The bookkeeping

The camera is the anchor carried by the root, plus the cockpit position less the cockpit shift, composed with the view's rotation and the world scale. When the chase walks the root toward the head, the anchor moves with the root while the cockpit position stays, so without a correction the camera would run ahead by the distance the root covered, and the gap would never close. The shift (`rig::root::shift`) takes the chase's part of the root's displacement back out of the cockpit.

The ledger (`rig::root::ledger`) keeps the split. Each tick the player owns the root, the hook submits the task's and the chase's displacements as separate vectors. The local character's pose-finalization entry completes the request against the step's solved root displacement, from [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT0) to [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1). When the achieved displacement matches the requested sum, the whole chase is credited (exact); the match tolerates the `f32` rounding of the two absolute positions, which grows with the distance from the world origin. When something stopped or deflected the root, only the chase's forward projection on the achieved travel is credited, bounded by both the achieved and the requested distances (projected); a blocked chase perpendicular to a wall slide contributes nothing. The ledger keeps the running total of credited chase at the last two completed steps, and the frame half folds in everything completed since it last looked, so several steps between rendered frames all count and rendering never consumes a request. The shift is a step-spaced pair like the anchor it compensates: each side of the head pose pair composes with its own shift, so the engine's interpolation moves the anchor and the shift together and the camera holds still between steps.

Attribution errors cannot separate the body from the head. An under-credit leaves the camera moved by the uncredited part, and an over-credit leaves it behind by the excess; either way the next tick measures the gap from the head as it now is, so the root still converges on it. An error is a small mismatch between the camera and the real head, never a body left behind. A tick some other owner moved the root on records nothing, so a climb's motion is never credited as chase.

## Walls

The view stays with the real head, so a player who walks or leans into a wall would see through it while the root stands at the wall. Two things answer it: the push-back holds the view at the wall, and the fades darken a view that goes past it.

The push-back (`rig::root::pushback`) runs in the frame half after the gap is measured. The ledger keeps the requested and credited chase distances of its last two steps; when less than half of what they asked for was credited, and the gap outside the lean is over `pushback.min_gap_m` (5 cm), the player's chase is obstructed. A wall the root slides along at a shallow angle leaves most of the chase credited, so it is no obstruction. The view may then go only a threshold past the root:

| Owner | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Player, obstructed | `pushback.obstructed_m`, 0.10 m |
| Player, unobstructed | none: the chase owns the gap |
| Authored | `pushback.authored_m`, 0.30 m, which only catches a runaway gap while the chase is suspended |
| Climb and Seated | `pushback.reach_m`, 0.45 m, about an arm's-length lean |
| Game | none |

The part of the gap beyond the threshold is taken out of the cockpit by sliding the cockpit shift back along the gap, on both sides of the pose pair so the view does not interpolate across it, and the view holds rigidly at the obstruction. It is a translation of the room against the world, never a rotation. The slide is capped per episode at `pushback.debt_cap_m` (0.30 m). With the cap spent, the push-back fade rises over `pushback.fade_span_m` past the threshold, black 0.15 m past it. The episode ends once the gap has stayed under half the threshold for `pushback.reset_s` (0.2 s), measured against the tight threshold while none is in force, so a slide that flickers in and out of obstruction does not start a fresh episode each time. The slide an episode applied stays: the room has slid against the world, and the camera stays that far from the real head until a recenter. A teleport or a recenter ends the episode. With `pushback.enabled` off, nothing slides and the fade answers the gap past the threshold at once.

The obstruction needs two steps of the ledger to show, so a head walking into a wall at a metre a second goes up to about seven centimetres past the threshold before the view is pulled back to it in one frame.

The head-through-geometry probe (`rig::collision::probe`) catches what the push-back cannot: a head pushed into geometry the root is nowhere near, such as a low beam or a wall the player leans into from a stand. On each tick the player's on-foot task runs, it casts from the last position the head held in the open to where it is now, and from the animated head, which the capsule keeps in the open, so a head the body has carried round the obstruction counts as clear again. A single ray through the eye centre lets the side of the head through a corner or a door frame, so the pair is cast three times, through the eye centre and 8 cm to either side across the view, and the deepest hit counts. The fan stands in for a swept sphere until the game's sphere cast is reverse-engineered. Its depth drives the head fade ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)).

## Teleports and seat exits

A teleport (a camera reset, or a single-tick jump of the root further than the body's speed explains, `chase.warp_min_m` at rest; [controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)) clears the ledger and restarts the head's velocity, and in the standing posture re-bases the shift so the head sits over the root, with no interpolation from the previous shift.

On the first frame out of a vehicle seat after an exit, a head within a metre of the root is left to the chase: the body walks to the head and the view does not move. A head further off is put back over the root, a jump the seat fade hides where the seat transition has one.

## Posture

The posture is `Auto`, `Seated`, or `Standing` (`controllers.roomscale.posture`). `Auto` is seated or standing by the head's height above the floor ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)). A saved `Roomscale` loads as `Standing`.

- `Standing`: the true floor height, physical crouching, the crouch capsule, and the player owning the root.
- `Seated`: the seated eye reference and the crouch toggle ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)); the root moves only by the stick, the lean still places the feet, and the push-back holds a lean at an arm's length.

## Recentering

On foot in the standing posture with the chase on, the manual recenters (F7, the debug UI's and the pause menu's buttons, the stick click chord, and the recenter on resuming gameplay) re-base the yaw and the height only: the chase keeps the position, so a position re-base is never needed, and one could put the body through a wall the chase could not cross. The yaw re-base turns the shift with the cockpit frame, so the root stays where it is in the room. Seated, in a seat, with the chase off (nothing else brings the body back under the head), or without a recent frame, they re-base fully, which makes the head the cockpit origin and clears the shift. Any recenter clears the ledger, restarts the head's velocity, and ends a push-back episode.

## The torso

Nothing tracks the torso, so its heading and joints are inferred once per rendered VR frame from the cockpit-frame head pose and the raw controller grips (`rig::torso`), in the player's own metres, after Parger et al. The heading is the head's heading, weighted by `torso.head_weight` (0.3), plus the horizontal direction from the neck pivot to each tracked hand. Each hand's direction is normalized before it is flattened, and its flat part is scaled by the square of its own length, so a hand held out in front counts nearly in full and a hand hanging at the side nearly not at all. The hands therefore hold the chest when the player glances aside with them held out, and the head leads when they hang. The head's heading is its forward axis on the ground, blended with its up axis as it pitches, so that looking straight down at the belt still has a heading. The result is held within `torso.neck_limit_deg` (65°) of the head, at once whatever the filter, and follows through a critically damped filter with the time constant `torso.follow_s` (80 ms).

The joints hang from a neck pivot set below and behind the eyes in the head's frame, so nodding moves the eyes and not the shoulders. The shoulder joints sit below the pivot by the body dimensions, along the torso's heading, and match the estimate the hand remap uses for an upright head ([controllers.md, "Body dimensions"](../input/controllers.md#body-dimensions)); the hand remap estimates the shoulders from the torso's heading, so looking over a shoulder does not swing the arms' pivots. The pelvis sits a spine's length below the chest, and the hip joints straddle it at a generic fraction of the shoulder width. A recenter turns the estimate with the cockpit frame, so the torso stays where it is in the room.

## The body turning under the view

The view composes the head onto Rico's body frame, so turning Rico would turn the view ([head-and-body.md, "HMD pose to camera"](head-and-body.md#hmd-pose-to-camera)), and the game's own turn toward the aim is suppressed in VR. With `torso.follow` on (`BODY FACES YOUR TORSO` on the `VR: BODY` page, on by default), the view frame is the body frame turned back by the body's heading relative to the view, so the body can turn without the view following it (`headpose::follow`).

While the body follows (on foot, in gameplay, under the headset), the view's heading is the turn accumulator's, which only the stick, the mouse, or a snap step moves, kept as a pair spaced one input tick apart. The offset between the body and the view is read on each side of the pair from the same tick-spaced body transforms ([`Character::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT0) and [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1)) that the view interpolates between, so the composed view heading is exactly the accumulator's whatever the body does: the game's own rotations, the orientation executor's lag, or the turn toward the torso. The body is steered, through the same face-dir target the turn input uses ([`NStateTask_LocoUtil::EvaluateCharacterOrientation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-EvaluateCharacterOrientation)), to the view's heading plus the torso's heading in the cockpit frame, held within `torso.follow_deadband_deg` (10°) of it so tracking jitter does not churn the orientation executor. The body therefore turns only because the player's torso did, and the view turns only because the player pushed the turn input, which is the rule that the player is never reoriented without a movement action.

While the body follows, the view does not ride the orientation executor: a snap turn snaps the view at once rather than sweeping it at the executor's rate, and a smooth turn turns the view at the turn input's rate rather than the slower of that and the executor's step. The lead clamp that keeps the accumulator near a lagging body is skipped while following, because a lagging body does not drag the view.

When the body stops following (a jump, a vehicle, the parachute, swimming, or the setting turned off), the offset freezes and the view rides the body again, turned by a constant, with the body steered to the accumulator plus the frozen offset so nothing moves. At the top of the next frame the frozen offset is folded into the cockpit baseline: the baseline's yaw turns by it, the offset goes to zero, and the cockpit shift and the torso estimate turn with the frame, all before the frame locates the views, so the composed view is unchanged. Off foot the cockpit frame's forward is then the body's again, which the vehicles' physical controls, the driving view, and the flight steering all assume. The fold waits while the grapple filter holds the view or the swim levelling drops the body's tilt, since both compose with the turned frame. A recenter clears the offset outright, because it re-bases the cockpit to the head and turns the view anyway.

Everything that composes the cockpit into the world already goes through the view frame, so the hands, the aim, and the head pose follow without change. The consumers that relate the cockpit to Rico's own body take the offset back out: the head's body-relative rotation (the head-bone override, the neck twist, and the body IK's head rotation), and the camera's tracked eye arm, which is held at rest in the view frame rather than the body frame, because the headset's translation already carries the player's eyes round with a turn of the torso.

## The chest

The chest covers what the follow's deadband leaves. The body IK's torso rotation ([head-and-body.md, "Body IK via HumanIK"](head-and-body.md#body-ik-via-humanik)) turns the chest toward the player's torso while the rig has an estimate, and by `yaw_share` of the head's turn without one: the turn is the torso's heading carried into Rico's model space, relative to his facing, bounded by `torso.chest_limit_deg` (45°) (`rig::chest_turn`). The procedural body turns its chest and its hips toward the same heading ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-solve)).

## The pelvis

The procedural body's hips sit where the rig's pelvis puts them (`rig::pelvis`), in Rico's model space. The rest place is the middle of the stance the root carries, drawn a little toward the one foot on the ground while the other swings and sent back over the heels in a squat. From there the pelvis moves back by `pelvis.hinge_share` (0.3) of the lean the chase leaves alone, so leaning over a table hinges at the hips rather than folding the spine, and back along the body's motion by the lean into motion. A critically damped spring with the time constant `pelvis.follow_s` (60 ms) follows the target, so the pelvis is held under the neck pivot while a quick lean still bends the spine first.

The head is tracked, so leaning the body into its motion is done from below: the pelvis moves back along the motion by the spine's length times the sine of a pitch, and the spine solve leans the chest forward to the fixed neck. The pitch is `pelvis.speed_lean` (0.035 rad) per metre per second of the root's measured speed, at most `pelvis.max_speed_lean` (0.14 rad), plus `pelvis.accel_lean` (0.02 rad) per metre per second squared of forward acceleration, at most `pelvis.max_accel_lean` (0.1 rad) either way: a start leans in and a stop leans back. A sideways acceleration rolls the body into a turn, at most `pelvis.max_roll` (0.08 rad). The velocity and the acceleration are measured from the root's displacement between ticks and smoothed over `pelvis.motion_smoothing_s` (0.1 s); the stick's commanded velocity never feeds them.

## The legs

The procedural body's feet come from the rig's legs (`rig::legs`), in world space, around rest places the neutral stance carried by the root gives ([procedural-body.md, "The feet"](procedural-body.md#the-feet)). Each foot holds its world pose while it is planted. Two regimes take turns: the walk while the body moves, and the standing steps while it does not. The regime switches on the root's measured speed with hysteresis: the feet start walking above `legs.moving_mps` (0.35 m/s) and stand again below `legs.standing_mps` (0.15 m/s). A continuous weight between the two speeds blends a standing step's duration and height toward the walk's, and feet are never blended in position, so nothing slides. With `legs.gait` off (`WALKING LEGS` on the `VR: BODY` page, on by default), there is no walk, and the standing steps chase a moving body.

Moving (`rig::legs::gait`), a phase advances by the root's measured distance over the stride, and each foot lifts when its half of the cycle comes round, the one furthest from its rest first. The stride grows with the speed at a human scale, 0.9 m plus 0.35 m per metre per second, between 0.7 m and 2 m per cycle; the share of the cycle a foot spends on the ground falls from 0.62 at a walk (1.4 m/s) to 0.32 at a run (5 m/s), and the lift rises from 8 cm to 14 cm. A walking step's swing time is fixed when the foot lifts, so a change of speed mid-swing does not jerk it. A foot in the air heads for where its hip will be when it lands, plus half a stance along the measured velocity, so it passes under the hip at mid-stance, plus `legs.capture_gain` (0.5) of the velocity's change from its third-of-a-second mean times the capture point's time constant `√(h/g)`, so a start or a stop puts the foot where it catches the body. The lead from the hip is bounded by `legs.reach_share` (0.42) of the leg's length, so even a run lands its feet under the body rather than ahead of it. The stick's commanded velocity never places a foot. A strafe keeps the swinging foot at least ten centimetres out on its own side of the other foot's, so the feet never cross. Each walking foot rolls onto its toes over the last third of its stance and levels over the first half of its swing; a stop lets the heels down within a few hundredths of a second.

Standing (`rig::legs::stepping`), the support is the hull of the two footprints widened by `legs.support_margin_m` (4 cm), and the capture point is where the pelvis would come to rest at its current velocity. A foot steps when the capture point leaves the support, when the foot is further than `legs.rest_error_m` (0.22 m) from its rest place, or when it is turned more than `legs.turn_error_deg` (35°) from it. The stepping foot is the one on the side the capture point went, otherwise the one further from its rest; it lands at its rest place plus the capture offset over `legs.step_s` (0.25 s), lifted `legs.step_height_m` (6 cm). One foot steps at a time. A sway inside the support steps nothing. On a stop, a foot in the air finishes its swing toward its standing place and plants. A foot further than a few metres from its rest place (a teleport, a respawn) is planted there afresh.

The unit tests hold that a run at 5 m/s lands every foot within 0.42 of a leg's length of its hip and 0.35 m on average, that a walk never moves a planted foot, that a 5 cm standing sway steps nothing, that a 0.3 m step across the room steps each foot at most once, that a quarter turn in place steps two or three times, and that a stop never slides a planted foot and leaves both down.

## The body's volumes on Rico's skeleton

The sockets and pouches ride Rico's skeleton ([controllers.md, "Sockets, pouches, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier"](../input/controllers.md#sockets-pouches-the-foregrip-and-the-action-runs-tier)), so what the player sees on his body is where they reach, and the hand as it is drawn reaches them. Where his body and the player's disagree, the rig is what closes the gap; the volumes do not follow an estimate of the player's body instead. After the pose-finalization pass's solves ([`Character::UpdatePropEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropEffects)), the bones HumanIK drives for [`Effector::HIPS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-HIPS), the hip joints ([`Effector::LEFT_HIP`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-LEFT_HIP) and its right twin), and the shoulder joints ([`Effector::LEFT_SHOULDER`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-LEFT_SHOULDER) and its right twin), found through [`HumanIK::m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##field-m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs), are read with the head bone and published with the character's world transform (`rig::skeleton`). The weapon handling carries them into the cockpit frame through the head pose and the view frame on its next input tick (`rig::frames`).

The hip and belt volumes ride the pelvis (the line through the hip joints) and the shoulder, chest, grenade, and C4 volumes ride the chest (the line through the shoulder joints), each girdle turned up along the spine (from the middle of the hip joints to the middle of the shoulder joints) rather than along the world's up, so the configured offsets turn with the girdle they belong to and the volumes stay on the body through a crouch, a bend, a fall, a skydive, or the parachute (`weapons::volumes::BodyFrame`). Lengths that scale with the body are fractions of the skeleton's own torso.

The skeleton is used while it is younger than a quarter of a second and plausible in whatever orientation: the head a plausible distance up the spine from the shoulders and near its line, the shoulders a plausible distance from the hips, and both girdles a plausible width. Without one (garbage bone data, or no character), no body volume is offered.

## Settings

The pause menu's `VR: BODY` page has `BODY FACES YOUR TORSO` (`torso.follow`, on) and `WALKING LEGS` (`legs.gait`, on). Its `VR: COMFORT` page has `POSTURE` (`AUTO`, `SEATED`, or `STANDING`), and `BODY FOLLOWS YOU` (`chase.enabled`, on). ## Readouts and signals

The Controllers tab's "Body rig" section shows the root's owner and whether the on-foot tick runs; the gap to the target, the part outside the lean, and the target in the cockpit frame; the effective gap, the chase, the task, and the head's velocities; the lean, its allowance, the pivot's drop and the standing height, and the torso heading; the feet and the cockpit shift; the push-back's threshold, whether the chase is obstructed, the frame's push, the episode's slide, the episode count, and the fade; and the ledger's exact and projected counts, with the re-bases at teleports and seat exits.

The "Torso" section shows the estimate (the torso's heading, the head's, the neck's twist, the hands' heading and weight, and the chest and pelvis in the cockpit frame), whether the body follows and its heading relative to the view and its target, the chest turn the body IK took, the motion lean, whether the body's volumes had a skeleton to ride, and the divergence after the solves. The divergence is the player's landmarks carried into Rico's model space less his own: the torso's turn from his facing, from his chest's line, and from his pelvis's line, and the offsets of the chest, the pelvis, both shoulder joints, and both hip joints. Its checkbox turns the `body_divergence` tracing target up to `DEBUG`, which logs the same picture once a second. The offsets mix two things that are expected to differ: the proportions (Rico is taller than most players, and the hands are remapped onto him), and the placement the head pose gives the player's body. A steady vertical offset of a few centimetres is the first; a horizontal offset that grows with a lean or a turn is the second, and is the one to watch.

The scenario signals ([recorder.md](../testing/recorder.md)) are `rig.gap_m` (the gap before the lean), `rig.residual_m` (the part outside it), `rig.ledger_projected` (steps credited by projection), `rig.pushback_m` (the episode's slide), `rig.obstructed`, `rig.pushbacks` (episodes), `legs.moving`, and `legs.touchdown_lead_m` (how far ahead of its hip the last walking foot landed). On a flat floor, a replayed walk should keep `rig.residual_m` below 5 cm and `rig.ledger_projected` flat; a walk into a wall should count at least one push-back.

The pure parts run in a kinematic world in the unit tests (`rig::harness`): a root stepped at 30 Hz that integrates the composed velocity, optional wall planes, a frame half at 90 Hz, and the pose pair interpolated by the time since the last tick. They hold that a minimum-jerk walk of a metre peaking at 1.4 m/s keeps the gap under 3 cm and settles under 5 mm 0.3 s after stopping; that a hundred metres of random walking keeps the camera within a millimetre of the real head with nothing credited by projection; that the stick and the room add; that two or three ticks to a frame never overshoot; that a 0.3 m lean with a 0.12 m pivot drop, drop first, moves the root under 2 cm and standing up closes the gap; that a climb clears the ledger; that walking into a wall holds the view at the threshold until the cap is spent and fades only past it; that backing away clears the fade, ends the episode, and keeps the slide; and that sliding along a wall pushes nothing more.

## Unverified

None of this has been run in the headset.

- The proxy's character context may filter or limit a wanted velocity. A free proxy has to move by exactly its wanted velocity times the step for the exact attribution to apply at all; an acceleration limit would lag the chase, oscillate it with the feed-forward, and send every step to the projection, which leaves sideways and opposing chase-plus-stick movement partly in the view. The projected count and `rig.residual_m` show it.
- The locomotion task's update for a step has to run before the physics solve whose result the pose-finalization entry sees for the same step, so each request is completed against its own displacement, and the shift's step-spaced pair has to line up with the anchor's, so the camera holds still between steps while the root catches up.
- A lean that swings the pivot on an arc about the hips moves it out before it drops: at 20° from upright a pivot 0.7 m above the hips is 24 cm out and 4 cm down, still inside the deadband. The chase follows such a lean's first quarter metre, then holds, and walks back as the player straightens. The harness's lean drops first. Nothing in a single frame tells the first ten degrees of such a lean, already 12 cm out, from a step: its drop is under a walking bob, the gaze is no lower than a walker's, and its rate of descent falls in the same band as the bob's. An allowance that follows the geometric arc while the gaze is down cuts the excursion only to about 21 cm with the gaze moving with the torso, so the allowance is linear. Whether the allowance's gain, deadband, and cap feel right needs the headset.
- Root motion of millimetres at rest may wake the animation graph's idle and move selection or the AI's perception.
- The root turns through the orientation executor while the chase moves it; the procedural body's planted feet have to absorb both.
- A gap of more than a metre at a seat exit under the `Ride` transition jumps the view with no fade.
- Stairs, door frames, slopes, and small props may read as obstructed and push the view back. The obstruction's half-credited rule and the 10 cm threshold need tuning on stairs.
- After many push-back episodes the room may have slid far from where the player stands; whether a recenter should be suggested is open.
- Turning Rico in place through the orientation executor, with the game's turn acts suppressed, has to read acceptably, with the planted feet absorbing it.
- The camera must not visibly translate as Rico turns under it: his animated head sits a few centimetres off his root's vertical axis.
- The turn accumulator's input-tick pair and the character's world transforms have to swap on the same tick, which the exact cancellation of the body's turn in the view relies on, and folding the frozen offset into the baseline must not jump the hands or the grab volumes.
- A walk's half stance at 1.4 m/s is longer than the reach bound, so a walking foot lands at the bound and leaves a little behind its hip; the run's duty factor of 0.32 keeps its touchdowns under the body. Both need the headset to judge.
- The torso's defaults (the head's weight against the hands, the neck limit, and the time constant) and the pelvis's lean gains are starting points. Hands held out to one side while the head looks the other way pull the chest toward the hands; whether that reads right needs the headset.
- The HumanIK hip and shoulder nodes have to be Rico's anatomical left and right, and his post-solve joints a better place for the holsters than the estimated body.
- The gap measure assumes the steady view: with `controllers.locomotion.view.enabled` off, the anchor is the animated head, which sits a little off the root, and the chase holds the root that far from under the eyes.
