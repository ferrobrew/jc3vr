# The steering wheel and handlebars

The physical wheel and handlebars ([bindings.md, "Vehicles"](bindings.md#vehicles)) sit where the driven vehicle's own are, the held wheel steers as a wheel does, and the rendered wheel turns with the steering. The code is `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/driving/`: `wheel.rs` (geometry and override tables), `fit.rs`, `steering.rs`, `physical/` (grabs), `view.rs` (the driving view), and `wheel_render.rs`. Tuning is the `physical.wheel`, `physical.handlebars`, `steering`, `wheel_fit`, and `driving_view` sections of the vehicles' [tuning file](../tuning.md); the engine side is [vehicle-parts.md](../../engine/gameplay/vehicle-parts.md).

## Placing the wheel

A fixed offset from the eyes misses the wheel in most cars: the hub moves by more than the grab radius from car to car, the tilt ranges from upright to nearly flat, and the head sways with the driving animation while the wheel does not. So each input tick the fit finds the steering wheel part by name in the vehicle's physics body ([`PfxBreakable`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PfxBreakable)) and reads the hub, radius, and tilt from its model-space box, carried through the part's transform and the vehicle's end-of-step transform ([`m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_WorldMatrixT1)), the side of the step the head pose is composed for. Meshes whose box misleads have an entry in `WHEEL_OVERRIDES`, keyed by part name because part names are one per model while [`m_VehicleTypeNameHash`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_VehicleTypeNameHash) is one per variant. A vehicle with no usable wheel part (the bus, the tanks, some boats) uses the `physical.wheel` spec, carried into the vehicle's space once the driver settles.

The fit and the physical controls both reset from one `VehicleEntryIdentity` (the vehicle's address and owner, read through [`Character::GetVehicle`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetVehicle)) and the seat mode before any grip is declared, so a different vehicle or a missed observation releases the old controls. A hand holding a loadout item does not take the wheel or bars ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)).

## Turning the wheel

A grip press near the rim takes the wheel and only releasing the grip lets go. Each holding hand turns it by its change since the last tick, chosen by the `turned_by` setting: `Position` (the default) uses the hand's travel around the hub, with its twist standing in near the hub where that angle is unstable; `Twist` uses the rotation about the wheel's axis alone, which tolerates a misfitted wheel but barely turns for still wrists. Changes accumulate, so the wheel goes past half a turn and hand-over-hand works. Each hand is measured against the wheel as it was when it took hold, because the live wheel sways in the cockpit frame with the animation and a still hand would otherwise steer. With two hands, the one that turned further leads and the other only counts when it turns the opposite way, so a relaxed hand on the rim neither halves nor stops the turn.

The wheel turns one to one with the hands, so the drawn wheel stays under them, and stops at `lock_deg`, which defaults to what hands at nine and three reach without letting go: the game's steering is arcade and ramps itself, and a VR wheel has no rim to push against, so a road car's range would make full lock a chore. Let go, the wheel eases back to centre while still steering.

## Handlebars

A vehicle whose parts include a steering fork (behaviour `SteeringFork`, or a part named `<model>_upper_steering`) offers handlebars instead: motorcycles, and the ATV, the jetski, and the duck, which share classes with cars and boats. The fork's box at rest places the grips. A grip that opens keeps the bars for `controls.bars_grip_grace_s`, because an Index controller's grip often reads open for an instant. The bars' turn is read in the horizontal plane from the line between the grips (or one grip mirrored through the middle), latched when the holding hands change so a grab off the end never jumps them, and written to both `TURN_LEFT`/`TURN_RIGHT` and `BIKE_LEAN_LEFT`/`BIKE_LEAN_RIGHT`, because the gamepad's one stick writes both and the game's bikes are tuned for them together. The triggers are the throttle and brake ([cockpit-controls.md, "Cockpit buttons"](cockpit-controls.md#cockpit-buttons)).

## The driving view

Where the hub sits from the eyes is a property of the seat, so the driving view (`FIT VIEW TO THE WHEEL` on the pause menu's `VR: COMFORT` page) moves the view per vehicle to put the eyes at one standard offset from the hub, `driving_view.hub_from_eyes_m`. The view moves rather than the wheel because the rendered wheel is the vehicle's own part, and a grab volume moved off it would close on air.

The fit measures the hub from the animated head with the camera's eye arm (`jc3vr_view::hooks::camera::head_to_eye_point`), so the player's posture and any applied correction do not enter it, and only a fitted wheel is measured. Once the driver settles, the measurement is averaged and kept for the entry, so the animation's sway does not move the view. `SEAT_OVERRIDES` skips a seat or sets its correction. Only cars are corrected, since some boats are driven standing. A gunner seat is corrected from the gun's handles (`vehicles::begin::gunner_view_target`).

The correction is published through `jc3vr_body::controllers::facts::driving`, and `jc3vr_body::controllers::roomscale::compose_cockpit` eases it in after the posture, height remap, crouch, and body rig are resolved, so it neither feeds nor is undone by them. The hands and grab volumes are composed from the same head pose, so they still meet the wheel where it is drawn ([render-time-anchoring.md](../rendering/render-time-anchoring.md)). The `driving position` log line carries what a new `SEAT_OVERRIDES` entry is tuned from.

## Turning the rendered wheel

The game never turns a car's steering wheel. A detour on [`Vehicle::UpdateRender_ModelMatrices`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-UpdateRender_ModelMatrices), for the driven vehicle only, turns the wheel part's [`m_RootSpaceTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::parts::PartInstance##field-m_RootSpaceTransform) about the hub, calls the original, and restores it, so collision and deformation never see the turn. Without a held wheel the angle inverts the steering map from [`m_SteeringTurn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_SteeringTurn), so stick steering turns it too. The render update runs on worker threads, so the detour reads only an atomic holding the driven vehicle's address and a small mutex with the part, hub, and angle.

## Settings and diagnostics

The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has `WHEEL TURNED BY` and `WHEEL FULL LOCK` (`controllers.vehicles.physical.wheel.turned_by` and `lock_deg`). The debug UI's Vehicles feature reads out the wheel, the bars, and the driving view.
