# Physical weapons

The aim is that a weapon behaves as one physical object: it is held in whichever hand took it, it moves between hands, it is picked up off the ground by the hand that touches it, and it goes onto the body and comes off it as the same object. The game keeps owning which weapons the player has (one per inventory slot), the reserve, the saves and loadouts, and the HUD of the selected weapon; the mod owns where each gun is and which hand fires it. The engine side is in [weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md) and [item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md), and the attach bones in [hands-locomotion-and-state.md](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md).

None of this has been run in the headset. Everything acts only while the bindings inject, under the controller scheme with a controller active. The code is `payload/src/controllers/weapons/hold/`.

## Either hand

With `weapons.hold.either_hand` (on; the pause menu's `HOLD WEAPONS IN EITHER HAND`), the wielded weapon rides the hand that holds it rather than always the right hand. The holding hand is decided by a small state machine (`hold::holder`), fed once per input tick with the address of the local player's wielded weapon:

- A holster grab's draw leaves the grabbing hand pending, and the next weapon the game wields takes it. A draw plays after the current weapon's holster or switch, so the pending hand waits up to three seconds before it lapses.
- A direct pickup (below) leaves the grabbing hand pending for that one weapon, so another weapon the game wields first does not take it.
- A weapon the game wields on its own (the weapon wheel, a switch, an automatic draw) goes to the acting hand (`controllers.acting_hand()`).
- A handover moves the wielded weapon to the other hand at once.
- A weapon that leaves the hands forgets its hand.

The log line `hold: the weapon is in the left hand` (or right) names the hand each time a weapon comes into the hands, with the cause (`draw`, `pickup`, or `the game's draw`).

The dual-wield pair is not moved this way: it keeps its own layout, one pistol per hand ([controllers.md, "Recoil, haptics, dual-wield, and the grapple hand"](controllers.md#recoil-haptics-dual-wield-and-the-grapple-hand)). A pair taken off the ground with independent pistol draws on brings out only the grabbing hand's pistol, as a hip grab's draw does.

## The owned attach bones

A gun is placed at the world matrix of the bone its attach-bone table ([`WeaponData::m_lBoneAttachementPerWeaponState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_lBoneAttachementPerWeaponState)) names for the weapon's state. The game rewrites the tables at the end of every pose-finalization pass ([`WeaponBase::UpdatePostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdatePostFinalizePose)) and its state tasks move guns between bones within the pass, so the mod writes the holding hand's bones at the same three points the dual-wield layout does: before each placement ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)), after the end-of-pass rewrite ([`Inventory::UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose)), and at the weapon's own per-tick update, before it fires. Both detours live in `hold/mod.rs` and serve both layouts.

A single gun on the other hand's attach bone is moved to its counterpart on the holding hand (the right hand's primary or secondary attach bone to the left hand's primary or secondary, and back), whatever the slot: a long gun, or a pistol. A weapon of two guns the hold owns (the dual-wield pair while its own layout is off) keeps a gun in each hand, its first in the holding hand and its second in the other, so held in the left hand its layout is mirrored rather than gathered into one hand (`hold::layout::placed_bone`). Either way the write is the same however many times it is made, so the three write points agree. The game's own moves within a hand, and a gun it puts on a holster or back bone, are kept. Both the wielding and the dual-wielding entries are written, and the weapon's state is never changed. A gun whose hand changed since its last placement skips its render interpolation for that placement ([`WeaponBase::SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-SkipRenderInterpolationForOneFrame)), so a handover moves the gun in one frame rather than streaking across.

Everything that derives a gun's hand from its attach bone follows without change: the rigid grip pins the gun to the holding wrist, the gun aim casts the holding hand's sight line, the shot's hand drives the recoil, the heft, and the fire haptics, the reload works with the free hand, and the render-time late update corrects the gun by the holding hand. The magazine pouch is laid out on the side away from the holding hand, since it follows the gun's hand (`volumes::gun_side`). The character-level aim point is the dominant hand's while it holds a gun, and the other hand's otherwise.

## The left hand's grip

The rigid grip pins a gun to its hand's wrist at the hand's canonical grip, the hand's attach bone's pose on its wrist in the skeleton's bind pose ([controllers.md](controllers.md)). The left hand's is the one the game's left-hand pistol of the pair uses, which is right for a pistol and unproven for a long gun. With `weapons.hold.left_grip` (`LEFT HAND GUN GRIP`), a gun the hold puts in the left hand (any weapon but the dual-wield pair under its own layout), and the second weapon in the left hand, takes one of:

- `AUTOMATIC` (the default): the left hand's own grip, unless it is turned more than 45 degrees from the right hand's grip mirrored onto the left wrist, which it then takes;
- `LEFT HAND'S OWN`: the left hand's own grip always;
- `RIGHT HAND'S MIRRORED`: the right hand's grip (its attach bone's bind pose) mirrored across the body onto the left wrist's bind pose always. The body's lateral axis is the axis the two wrists' bind positions differ most along, and the mirror keeps the gun right-handed by flipping its own lateral axis, which is the mirror image of a gun symmetric across its own lateral axis.

The mirrored grip is read once per skeleton and logged with the angle between the two (`rigid grip: the right hand's grip mirrored onto the left wrist`), and `VR: STATUS` shows which grip the left hand took and that angle (`LEFT HAND GRIP`), so the two can be compared in the headset; a small angle means the skeleton's left attach bone already mirrors the right.

## Firing

Only the holding hand's trigger fires a gun. With a gun in the off-hand, that hand's trigger presses the off-hand fire action (`fire_off_hand_pistol`), and the tether retract moves to the off-hand's stick click, exactly as while the off-hand holds a pistol of the pair (`Controls::dual_wield` is set for any gun in the off-hand, in a mode whose triggers fire the hand weapons). Both triggers drive the one fire action, so while the gun is in the off-hand and only the dominant trigger is pulled, the weapon handling holds the fire inputs back (`weapons::dominant_trigger_unarmed`). The dual-wield split's per-gun pin is unchanged.

## Handing a gun over

With `weapons.hold.handover` (on; `HAND GUNS OVER AT THE GRIP`), a grab volume (`GrabVolume::Handle`) sits on the held gun's grip, at the holding hand's grip point in its weighted pose (the foregrip and the heft applied), with radius `weapons.hold.handle_radius_m` (0.07 m), for the free hand only. A grip press there hands the gun to the free hand: the holder changes at once, the tables follow on the next placement, and a foregrip hold ends. The grab pulses the grab cue. The volume is placed only while the hold owns the weapon's bones (not for the dual-wield pair, whose hands each hold their own pistol), and the deepest volume wins where it overlaps the weapon's action or the foregrip, which sit further along the gun. The log line is `hold: handed the weapon to the left hand` (or right).

With `GRAB GUNS ANYWHERE`, a long gun's handover volume sits on its grip wherever the gun is, and the foregrip is wherever the free hand closes on the gun rather than only the gun's own foregrip point; a hand can also carry a long gun by another part, unable to fire it ([physical-guns.md, "Grabbing a gun anywhere"](physical-guns.md#grabbing-a-gun-anywhere)).

## Picking a weapon up into the hand

With `weapons.hold.direct_pickup` (on; `PICK WEAPONS UP INTO THE HAND`), a grab of a weapon lying in the world ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md)) takes that weapon straight into the grabbing hand instead of clicking the use-item action. On the input tick, with the hand's reach from the last simulation tick, it checks that the reached body still belongs to the weapon (a weapon's body leaves the world when it is picked up or despawned, so this proves the object is alive), that the weapon is still dropped, that it is not a hand grenade and does not give a copy ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)), and that the animation allows a pickup ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsPickup)). It then leaves the hand pending for that weapon and calls [`WeaponBase::PickupItem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupItem) with the character's game-object subobject, the call a swimming character's pickup makes: the weapon goes into the inventory and is equipped there and then, its magazine as it was, with no pickup animation. A weapon already in its slot is dropped or despawned by the game's own inventory add, as with the game's pickup; with a free socket on the body it is carried there instead ([vr-inventory.md](vr-inventory.md#carrying-more-than-the-slots)); otherwise, with `weapons.hold.drop_replaced` (on; `PICKUPS DROP THE OLD WEAPON`), it is let fall first, as a drop below does it, so it lands rather than vanishes (`hold: dropped the weapon the pickup replaces`). The log line is `hold: picked the weapon up into the left hand` (or right), with the cause. A weapon picked up this way is followed into its hand at once, so the next placement already puts it there. With `weapons.hold.take_slide` (on; `SLIDE TAKEN GUNS INTO THE HAND`), the gun then slides from where it lay into the hand over `weapons.hold.snap_s`, the same slide a draw without the game's act uses, starting from the first gun's pose in the world as it was before the pickup, so it is seen to leave the ground rather than appear in the hand.

A refused pickup falls back to the use-item click, so the game picks a weapon up its own way, and the readout counts it with the refusal. The one refusal that does not fall back is a pickup that ran but left the weapon out of the hands, since the call already happened.

## Taking a gun from a character's hands

With `weapons.hold.steal` (on; `TAKE GUNS FROM HANDS`), a free hand takes a gun out of a living character's hands by grabbing it (`hold::steal`). A gun in a character's hands has no body in the physics world, so the hands' reach never finds it. Instead the inventory's end-of-pass detour, which runs for every character, notes each other character within a few metres of the player that wields a gun of the pistol, two-handed, or special slot: the gun's segment from its holding grip to its other grip ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition)), its first gun's pose, and a weak reference to the character taken from its own ([`Character::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WeakThis)), which says whether it still lives without keeping it alive. The input tick carries the segments into the cockpit frame (through the head's pose in the world and the cockpit, and the view frame) and places grab volumes along each (`GrabVolume::WorldWeapon`, radius `weapons.hold.take_radius_m`, 0.1 m) for each hand without a gun (`hold::take`). A grip press there takes the gun whose segment is nearest the hand:

1. the character is locked alive for the whole take ([`WeakPtrGameObject::Lock`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/types::shared_ptr::WeakPtrGameObject##func-Lock)), still wields the gun in the slot noted, and the player's animation allows a pickup;
2. the gun is dropped at once while the character's inventory still holds it ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop)), which unequips it from the character;
3. the character's inventory lets the slot go with its selection on ([`Inventory::DropWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DropWeapon)), as its death does, so it selects another weapon it carries; the gun is already dropped, so no second drop is deferred;
4. the character staggers toward the player ([`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction)), with a melee impulse just over its stumble threshold and under its knockdown threshold, so it reacts to the yank without falling;
5. the gun goes into the grabbing hand as a weapon off the ground does (the direct pickup above, with its checks), and slides there from where it was in the character's hands.

What the character does next is the game's handling of a character with nothing wielded ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "A character that loses its gun"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#a-character-that-loses-its-gun)): it draws another weapon it carries, and may go looking for one lying in the world. The log line is `hold: took the gun out of a character's hands into the left hand` (or right), and a refused take logs `hold: no weapon taken` with the reason. The direct pickup must be on, since the take ends in it.

## Taking a weapon off a rack

With `weapons.hold.racks` (on; `TAKE WEAPONS FROM RACKS`), a free hand takes a weapon off a rack by grabbing it (`hold::rack`). A rack's weapon has no body in the physics world either, so the input tick walks the player's valid interaction entries as a swimming character's pickup does ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "Racks"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#racks)), takes each weapon an entry offers that is in the rack state (held alive while it is read), and places grab volumes along it as along a gun in a character's hands. A grip press there finds the weapon again among the entries, holding it alive through the take, and:

- a weapon the rack hands over goes into the grabbing hand as a weapon off the ground does, after the call that starts the game's own pickup of it ([`WeaponBase::PickupStart`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupStart)), which takes it out of the rack's state and tells the rack, and slides there from the rack. The log line is `hold: took the weapon off a rack into the left hand` (or right);
- a rack with unlimited stock ([`WeaponBase::m_InfinitePickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_InfinitePickup)) gives a copy instead, which the game spawns a few frames later and equips. The take leaves the hand pending for the next weapon wielded, starts the game's pickup, which asks for the copy, and hides the rack's own weapon ([`WeaponBase::SetVisible`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetVisible)), holding it alive. When the copy comes into the hand, it slides there from where the rack's weapon was, so the weapon is seen to leave the rack, and the rack's weapon fades back in a moment later, restocked. A copy that has not come within three seconds lets the rack's weapon show again (`hold: the rack's copy never came`). The log lines are `hold: asked a rack for a copy for the left hand` and `hold: the rack's copy is in the left hand`.

The copy is a new object, which the brief's illusion cannot avoid with the game's racks; the hiding and the slide are what keep it from showing. The rack's own weapon keeps its pickup, so the game's use-item action still works there.

## Drawing and holstering as one object

With `weapons.hold.instant_dock` (off; `DRAW LONG GUNS WITHOUT ANIMATION`), a holster grab draws and holsters the long guns (the two-handed and special slots, on the shoulders) without the game's draw and holster animations. The gun is the same object throughout, and slides between its place on the body and the hand over `weapons.hold.snap_s` (0.12 s), eased in and out, rather than appearing in the hand when an animation reaches its keyframe.

A draw checks that the animation allows one ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsDrawing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsDrawing)) and that the slot's weapon is holstered, unequips the wielded weapon ([`WeaponBase::OnUnequip`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnUnequip)), measures how the holstered gun sits on its dock bone (its placement relative to the dock bone's current pose in the skeleton), and equips the weapon ([`WeaponBase::OnEquip`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnEquip)) for the grabbing hand. The weapon is wielding at once, and its gun rides the hand's attach bone; for the snap's duration the rigid grip places that bone part of the way from where the gun sat on the dock (the dock bone's current pose composed with the measured offset, so the start follows the body) toward the hand's grip (`hands::grip::docked_grip`). The log line is `hold: drew the weapon into the left hand without the game's draw`.

A holster checks [`GetAnimationAllowsHolstering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsHolstering), then slides the gun from the hand to where it sat on its dock at its last draw, holding the fire inputs back meanwhile, and unequips it when the slide ends, so the bone table switches to the dock bone where the two poses meet and the gun skips its render interpolation. A weapon not drawn this way since the payload loaded has no measured dock pose, and holsters at once without the slide. The log line is `hold: holstered the weapon without the game's holster`.

A refused draw or holster (the animation forbids it, the slot is empty, or the weapon is not on the body) falls back to the game's own, the slot-select click or `Character::HolsterWeapon`. The planted explosive keeps the game's.

With `weapons.hold.pistol_dock` (on; `DRAW PISTOLS WITHOUT ANIMATION`), the dual-wield pistols dock the same way, each hand its own pistol at its own hip; it needs each hand to draw its own pistol (`controllers.weapons.dual_wield.split` and `independent_draw`), and falls back to the game's draws without them:

- a hip grab with the pistols holstered asks the pistols' layout for the grabbing hand's pistol only, equips the pair at once, and slides that hand's pistol from where it sat on its holstered bone into the hand; the other pistol stays on its hip, as the layout shows an undrawn one;
- a hip grab by the other hand while the pair is wielded slides its pistol off its hip into the hand (`hold::act::draw_pistol`), measured on the hip bone the layout keeps it on, and logs `hold: drew the left pistol off its hip` (or right);
- a hip grab by a hand holding its pistol while the other holds its own slides it back onto its hip from where it was drawn and then hands it back to the layout's hip (`hold::act::holster_pistol`, `hold: holstered the left pistol on its hip`), without holding the other pistol's fire back; the last pistol is holstered as a long gun is, sliding onto its holstered bone and unequipping the pair.

With `grip_to_hold`, opening the grip on one pistol of a pair held in both hands at its own hip puts that pistol back alone rather than holstering the pair. A pistol whose pose on its hip is not known yet (never drawn this way since the payload loaded, or the dominant hand's pistol going back to the other side's bone) goes back at once without the slide.

The rigid grip shows the slide only while the hand's controller weight is above zero; a hand the controllers do not drive shows the switch at once. The holstered scale of a long gun on the back, when it is not one, still pops at the switch.

## Holsters on the docked guns

With `weapons.holsters.follow_docks` (on; `HOLSTERS FOLLOW THE GUNS`), a holster with a gun docked in it moves to the gun (`weapons::docks`). The holsters are placed from the body's landmarks and the configured offsets, but a docked gun sits where its dock bone puts it on the character's skeleton, so the volume is moved by the gun's displacement from the same holster placed on the character's skeleton. On the character's skeleton (`HolsterAnchor::Rico`) that puts the volume on the gun itself; on the player's own body it moves the volume as far, and the same way, as the gun is from its usual place. The docked guns are each shoulder's long gun in the holstered state (its grip, [`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition), or its gun's origin without one), not the second weapon, which is in a hand, and each pistol on a hip holster bone, holstered or waiting there while the other is drawn. A holster with no gun docked in it (its weapon in the hands, or none) stays where it is placed, since a held gun is holstered there, and so does every holster while the character's skeleton is not fresh and upright. A move longer than 0.6 m is taken for a bad read and not made. `VR: STATUS` shows how many holsters sit on their guns (`HOLSTERS`).

## Letting go

With `weapons.hold.grip_to_hold` (off; `HOLD GRIP TO HOLD GUNS`), a gun taken by a grab (a draw at a holster, a direct pickup, or a handover) is held only while its grip is: the hand's grip is watched from the tick the gun is in the hand with the grip held (`hold::dock::ReleaseWatch`), and opening it does one of three things (`hold::dock::on_release`):

- at the gun's own holster (the hand in the volume of the holster that carries the gun's slot), it holsters the gun, without the game's act with `instant_dock` and through the game's holster otherwise;
- anywhere else, it lets the gun fall;
- for the dual-wield pistols, which cannot leave the pair, nothing.

A gun the game put in the hand (the weapon wheel, a game draw) is sticky as before, since no grab took it.

A drop reads the first gun's measured velocity and angular velocity, which the rigid grip makes the hand's, runs the weapon's drop at once while the inventory still holds it ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop)), the call a swimming character's pickup makes on a slot's occupant, then sets the gun's body ([`WeaponData::m_PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_PfxInstance)) to the hand's motion, the linear part capped at `weapons.hold.max_throw_mps` (15 m/s), through the hand bodies' owned-body velocity write (which checks that the body is dynamic and still the weapon's), and finally clears the slot ([`Inventory::DropWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-DropWeapon), whose own deferred drop does nothing to a weapon already dropped). The game's drop alone would give the weapon the character's motion rather than the hand's, since it stops using the weapon first and so drops it as if from the holster ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md, "Dropping"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#dropping)). A heavy weapon is dropped through the slot alone, which already gives it the hand's motion. The log line is `hold: dropped the weapon from the left hand` with the speed and whether the mod set it.

A weapon let fall leaves the inventory: the slot is empty until another weapon is picked up into it, and a load restores the loadout's weapons as usual.

## A second weapon

With `weapons.hold.second_weapon` (off; `SECOND GUN IN THE FREE HAND`), the free hand can hold a second weapon while the other hand holds the wielded one, and its trigger fires it. The game wields one weapon, the selected slot's, so the second is driven by the mod (`hold::second`):

- A grab at a shoulder holster by the hand without a gun, while the other hand holds the wielded weapon, takes that slot's long gun (the two-handed or special slot, not the wielded one's) as the second weapon instead of being refused. A grab of a weapon the VR inventory carries on a socket, any kind of weapon it carries, does the same ([vr-inventory.md](vr-inventory.md#a-carried-weapon-in-either-hand)). From the holsters, the pistols and the planted explosive are never taken as second weapons. A grab by the second weapon's hand at its own holster puts it back, and at any other holster does nothing.
- The second weapon stays in the holstered state, so the inventory, the selection, the HUD, and the saves see it as holstered, and its bodies stay out of the world. Its guns' holstered entries in the attach-bone table name the hand's attach bone, written at the same three points as the held weapon's, and its holstered scale ([`WeaponData::m_HolsteredScaleValue`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_HolsteredScaleValue)) is set to one while it is in the hand; both are restored when it goes back.
- It gets the character as its user ([`WeaponBase::StartUsing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-StartUsing)), so its fire code runs as the player's, and loses it when it goes back ([`WeaponBase::StopUsing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-StopUsing), which leaves a holstered weapon holstered).
- After the inventory's own weapon updates ([`Inventory::UpdateWeapons_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeapons_Parallel) and [`UpdateWeapons_Serial`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeapons_Serial)), detoured for the local player's inventory, the mod updates it the same way through its [`UpdateFromUserPostSim_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateFromUserPostSim_Parallel) and `_Serial` virtuals, on the same thread and in the same phase. The fire input is the hand's trigger, while the animation allows shooting ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsShooting`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsShooting)). The aim point is the gun's own sight line's, cast in the aim update like the wielded weapon's; without a current one the weapon is not updated. The base update has no state check and runs the guns' attached effects, so the muzzle effects are its own.
- The rigid grip pins it to the hand, the per-shot seam gives its shots its hand (so the recoil, the heft's kick, and the fire haptics follow), and the render-time late update corrects it by its hand, as for a wielded weapon.
- Its hand's trigger does not reach the game: the fire inputs are held back unless a hand holding the wielded weapon pulls its trigger (`weapons::primary_trigger_idle`). In the off-hand, its trigger presses the off-hand fire action and the retract moves to the stick click, as for any gun in the off-hand.
- While it is held, a weapon the game wields on its own goes to the other hand, the handover volume and the foregrip are not placed for its hand, and its hand draws nothing else.
- With `grip_to_hold`, opening its hand's grip at its own holster puts it back, at a free socket that fits it docks it there, and anywhere else lets it fall with the hand's motion (`hold: dropped the second weapon from the left hand`); a slot's weapon then leaves its slot, and either lies in the world, where the VR inventory keeps it while near.
- With `weapons.hold.promote_second` (on; `LONE SECOND GUN BECOMES THE MAIN`), once nothing else has been wielded for `promote_after_s` (0.5 s), for instance after the wielded weapon was holstered or dropped, it becomes the wielded weapon in the same hand (`hold: the second weapon in the left hand is now the wielded weapon`), so the HUD and the game's reload take it over: a slot's weapon is equipped where it is, and a carried one goes into its slot, the slot's weapon taking its socket.
- It is let go of, left as the game has it, when the game wields it, takes it from its slot, or no longer has it holstered with the player as its user (`hold: the game took the second weapon`, `hold: the second weapon left its slot`), and put back when the weapon handling stands down or leaves gameplay.

The log lines are `hold: the second weapon is in the left hand` (or right) and `hold: the second weapon is back on the body`.

Mixing a pistol of the pair with a rifle is the dual-wield pair wielded with one pistol drawn (each hand draws its own pistol), and the rifle in the other hand as the second weapon. Two long guns are the two-handed and special slots, one wielded and one second, or one wielded and a carried one as the second; two weapons of one slot are held the same way, the second a carried one ([vr-inventory.md](vr-inventory.md)).

Its magazine is its own, and its reserve is the character's. The game's reload and the HUD's ammunition readout stay the selected weapon's, so:

- the true reload ([reload.md](reload.md)) turns to the second weapon when it runs lower than the wielded one (`reload::focus`): when its magazine is out or empty while the wielded weapon's is not, or when it is more than a quarter of its magazine lower. It turns back the same way, and never while a round is in a hand or a well is pushed into the belt pouch. With a gun in each hand there is no free hand for the pouch, so a second weapon is reloaded with a flick of its hand to eject and the belt push to load, and its chamber and the stop on the game's own reload follow it while the reload is on it. The log line is `reload: turned to the second weapon` (or `the wielded weapon`);
- with `weapons.hold.magazine_pips` (on; `SECOND GUN'S AMMO OVER ITS HAND`), a row of pips floats above the hand holding it (`hold::pips`), anchored to the hand's grip on the overlay: a filled pip for each round, or each share of a magazine larger than the row, a ring for each spent, and the whole row red when the magazine is empty or out.

With its magazine empty, or while the true reload holds it from firing, its trigger fires nothing, since the weapon's ammunition check would otherwise ask the character to reload, which reloads the wielded weapon, or to switch weapons.

## Grenades as objects

The grenade taken from the pouch is the game's own grenade from the moment it is in the hand to the moment it detonates ([controllers.md, "Grenades, aim assist, and reloads"](controllers.md#grenades-aim-assist-and-reloads) describes the take and the throw). Two things make it behave as an object rather than a throw animation: its pin, and catching. The code is `payload/src/controllers/weapons/grenade/` (`pin.rs` and `catch.rs`), and the settings are under `controllers.weapons.grenade`.

### The pin and cooking

With `pin.enabled` (on; the pause menu's `PULL THE GRENADE PIN`), a grenade comes out of the pouch with its pin in. The update that primes it stops its fuse ([`Grenade::m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##field-m_ShouldTickDetonationTimer), the switch the game's beacon uses to keep its own timer still) and fills it again, after checking that the primed object is a grenade ([`Grenade::TYPE_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##func-TYPE_ID)); the log line is `grenade: taken with its pin in, the fuse held`. The pin is pulled by either:

- the other hand, with a grip press at the grenade (`GrabVolume::GrenadePin`, a sphere of `pin.radius_m`, 0.08 m, at the held grenade, placed while the pin is in and that hand holds no gun);
- the holding hand's trigger, with `pin.trigger` (on), for a throw with one hand. While a grenade is in the off-hand, that hand's trigger does not reach the tether retract it otherwise drives.

The next weapon update lets the fuse run, and from then the grenade cooks in the hand: the fuse warning's ring and pulses start, and held past its fuse it explodes there. Both hands play `Event::GrenadePin` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)), and the log line is `grenade: pulled the pin` with the puller. A grenade thrown with its pin still in has its fuse started at the throw, so it lands with its whole fuse rather than lying live in the world forever. Writes to the grenade happen only inside the grenade weapon's update, where the weapon's weak reference proves the grenade alive.

### Catching a live grenade

With `catch.enabled` (on; `CATCH LIVE GRENADES`), a free hand catches a live grenade near the player and throws it on. Every grenade launched, by the player or anyone else, passes through the grenade's launch ([`Grenade::PropelToward`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Grenade##func-PropelToward)), already detoured for the throw. The detour notes each one with its physics body ([`Explosive::m_PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_PfxInstance)) and the body's owner as the launch finds them. On the input tick each noted grenade's body is read through the hand bodies' ownership check (the body still exists, is dynamic, and is owned by the same object), which proves the grenade has not detonated or been removed; one that fails is forgotten.

Each live grenade within `catch.range_m` (4 m) of the player is a grab volume (`GrabVolume::LiveGrenade`, radius `catch.radius_m`, 0.18 m, larger than a pouch's since a flying grenade moves between ticks) for each hand that holds no gun, grenade, or charge, while no grenade is in the hands. The player's own grenade waits `catch.own_grace_s` (0.4 s) after its launch, so it is not taken back as it leaves. A grip press there catches the grenade nearest the hand (`grenade: caught a live grenade`, with `Event::GrenadeCatch`). From then each input tick gives its body the velocity that carries it to the hand's grip over the tick, capped at `catch.max_carry_mps`, so it stays in the hand with its own physics and its fuse runs on; the fuse warning shows on that hand as for a grenade from the pouch. Opening the grip throws it: its body gets the hand's release velocity, estimated as for a throw from the pouch, plus the character's own velocity (`grenade: threw the caught grenade`). A grenade that goes off in the hand logs `grenade: the caught grenade is gone from the hand`. While a caught grenade is held the pouch gives no other grenade.

The readout shows the pin and the pulls, the live grenades noted and in reach, and the catches; `VR: STATUS` has an `EXPLOSIVES AND MAGAZINES` section with `GRENADE PIN` and `LIVE GRENADES`, and the scenario signals `grenade.pins_pulled`, `grenade.thrown`, `grenade.caught`, and `grenade.thrown_on` count them (`testing/scenarios/grenade_pin_and_catch.toml`).

## C4 pressed onto a surface

With `c4.stick.enabled` (on; the pause menu's `PRESS C4 ONTO SURFACES`), a charge from the C4 pouch ([controllers.md, "Grenades, aim assist, and reloads"](controllers.md#grenades-aim-assist-and-reloads)) can be pressed onto a surface instead of thrown (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/c4/stick.rs`). While a charge is held, and once it has been out of the pouch for `c4.stick.after_prime_s` (0.3 s, so the chest the pouch is on is never taken for a surface), each aim update casts six short rays from the held charge along the axes of the hand's grip frame, out to `c4.stick.contact_m` (0.04 m, about half the charge's thickness), with the player's own character excluded. The nearest surface they meet is touching the charge, and the charge is pressed onto it: the log line is `c4: pressed the charge onto a surface` with whether it is on an object.

The press is the throw's release with a different start and velocity. The next C4 weapon update makes the charge live as a throw does ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::Fire_Projectile`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-Fire_Projectile)), moves it to `c4.stick.standoff_m` (0.03 m) off the surface along its normal, turned as the hand holds it, drops it into the simulation, and pushes it onto the surface at `c4.stick.push_mps` (1.5 m/s) plus the character's own velocity. The charge's own collision handler then sticks it where it touches, oriented by the surface and attached to the object it touched, exactly as a thrown charge sticks where it lands, so a charge pressed onto a car rides the car. The log line is `c4: stuck the charge where it was pressed`, with `Event::C4Stick` on the hand. Opening the grip before the charge touches anything throws it as before.

`VR: STATUS` shows `C4 BY HAND` with the charges thrown and pressed on, and the scenario signals `c4.thrown` and `c4.stuck` count them (`testing/scenarios/c4_by_hand.toml`).

## Aiming down the sights

The gun aim shoots along each gun's own sight line ([controllers.md, "Aiming"](controllers.md#aiming)), so a gun is aimed by its sights already. With `ads.enabled` (on; the pause menu's `SIGHTS TO THE EYE`), a long gun raised to the eye is lined up with it so the player can look down those sights (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/ads/`). It acts on the two-handed and special slots' guns, whose sight line in the holding wrist's frame is measured on the input tick with the foregrip's geometry: the gun's sight line (with the weapon's sight correction) relative to the wrist target its last placement followed. The pose is applied to the cockpit-frame hands every hand subsystem starts from, after the two-handed pose and the weight and before the recoil, so the drawn gun, the IK targets, the late update, the sight line the gun aim casts, and the scope's lens all see the gun where the eye has it. The gun moves as one rigid body; the holding hand rides it, and the hand on the foregrip with it. Nothing turns the gun toward a target.

Three steps, each fading in as its distance closes from its engage distance to its lock distance, eased, so the gun settles onto the eye rather than snapping:

1. The stock (`ads.shoulder`, on; `STOCK TO THE SHOULDER`). The butt is taken `ads.butt_behind_grip_m` (0.22 m) behind the holding grip along the sight line; the shoulder pocket is `ads.pocket_offset_m` from the holding side's shoulder joint in the torso's frame (inward, down, and forward of it; mirrored for the left), the shoulder being the one the arms are remapped about, or the player's estimated one. Within `ads.shoulder_engage_m` (0.12 m) of the pocket, the gun turns about its front hand (the hand on the foregrip, or the holding hand without one) so the butt lies on the line to the pocket, fully within `ads.shoulder_lock_m` (0.04 m): the front hand sets where the gun points and the shoulder holds its back. The log line is `ads: the stock is in the shoulder`.
2. The sights (iron sights). The aiming eye is the eyes' midpoint moved `ads.half_ipd_m` (0.032 m) to the side `ads.eye` names (`AIMING EYE`: the gun hand's side by default, or left, or right). With the eye behind the holding grip by no more than `ads.max_eye_behind_m` (0.4 m), the head looking within `ads.max_view_angle_deg` (30 degrees) of the sight line, and the eye within `ads.engage_m` (0.06 m) of the line, the gun turns about a point `ads.pivot_ahead_m` (0.35 m) ahead of the grip on the line, about where the front sight is, so the line passes through the eye, fully within `ads.lock_m` (0.02 m). The front sight stays where the hands hold it and the eye, the rear sight, and the front sight line up, so the shot lands where the player sees the sights point. The log line is `ads: the sights are on the eye`.
3. A scope's cheek weld (`ads.cheek_weld`, on; `SCOPE CHEEK WELD`), for a scoped weapon (the sniper class, as the scopes have it) in place of the second step. A scope is lined up by moving the gun across its axis, keeping its direction, so the eye sits on the axis and the lens's view centres, within `ads.weld_engage_m` (0.05 m), fully within `ads.weld_lock_m` (0.015 m). The log line is `ads: the scope is on the eye`.

`VR: STATUS` has an `AIMING` section with `DOWN THE SIGHTS` (iron sights or scoped, the times each step took hold, and how far the eye is off the line), and the scenario signals `ads.sighted`, `ads.shouldered`, and `ads.welded` count them (`testing/scenarios/aim_down_sights.toml`).

## Guns against walls

With the hand bodies and `PHYSICAL GUNS` on, a held gun is a body joined to the hands, which stops at walls along its whole length, and the probe below does not move a hand on it ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md)). Otherwise, a gun in the hands has no body in the physics world: the weapon's bodies are out of the world in the wielding state ([weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md)), so nothing stops a held rifle at a wall and it passes through. With `collide.enabled` (on; the pause menu's `GUNS STOP AT WALLS`), a held long gun is stopped (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/collide/`). It acts on the guns the sights act on, the two-handed and special slots', whose muzzle (the first gun's current fire point) and barrel in the holding wrist's frame are measured with the foregrip's geometry.

The gun's collision proxy is a ray rather than a body: from the holding grip along the barrel to `collide.tip_margin_m` (0.03 m) past the muzzle, cast each aim update (the simulation phase outside the physics step) with the player aim's own filter, which passes through the player, the player's weapons, foliage, fences, and glass, as the gun aim's rays do. It is cast along the gun's pose before any push, which the hand poses note each time, so the push never moves where the wall is looked for. The wall it meets is a plane (the hit point and its normal, turned to face the grip), kept for `collide.keep_s` (0.15 s) past the probe's last hit so that a probe grazing an edge does not let the gun bounce in and out.

Each hand pose then pushes the gun out of the plane, last of the cockpit-frame hand poses (after the two-handed pose, the weight, the sights, and the recoil), so the drawn gun, its sight line, the IK targets, and the late update all see the gun where the wall stops it:

1. it turns about the holding grip, away from the wall, by the least angle that puts the tip on the plane, up to `collide.max_turn_deg` (70 degrees), so a gun pushed into a wall at a slant slides along it;
2. what is left (a gun pushed into the wall head-on, or past the turn's limit) is pulled back along the gun into the hands, up to `collide.max_retract_m` (0.35 m).

The holding hand rides the gun, and the hand on its foregrip with it, so both hands stay on the gun where it is stopped, and the player's hands, which go on into the wall, are left behind for as long as the gun is against it. A gun whose grip is itself through the wall is not pushed. The first contact after a second without one logs `collide: the held gun met a wall`. Because the proxy is a ray, the gun is stopped by what it points into, not by what its sides brush, and it does not push loose objects: it is stopped by them, as by a wall.

`VR: STATUS` has `GUN AGAINST WALLS` in the `AIMING` section (against a wall or clear, the contacts, and the turn and pull-back), and the scenario signals `collide.contacts` and `collide.touching` read it (`testing/scenarios/gun_against_walls.toml`).

## More weapons than slots

The VR inventory ([vr-inventory.md](vr-inventory.md)) carries weapons beyond the inventory's slots. The game's model stays as it is: the inventory holds one weapon per slot ([`Inventory::m_Weapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_Weapons)), and the save, the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the missions read only the slots. What makes the extras clean is what keeps a weapon alive ([weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md)): any strong reference, not the slot as such, so a carry set of the mod's references keeps an extra weapon in the game and out of the spawn system's despawn ranking, and a weapon moves into and out of a slot without being dropped.

How the hold's paths meet it:

- A weapon picked up, taken from a character's hands, or taken off a rack into a taken slot moves the slot's weapon onto a free socket of the body; only with no socket free does the old weapon fall (`PICKUPS DROP THE OLD WEAPON`), or vanish as the game has it with that off.
- A carried weapon grabbed off its socket comes into the hand: into its slot, the slot's weapon taking the socket, or, beside the wielded weapon, as the second weapon, which the mod fires from the holstered state. Two weapons of one slot are held this way.
- A held weapon put to a free socket leaves its slot into the carry set, and a second weapon let go of anywhere but a socket or its holster falls, out of its slot or the carry set.
- A second weapon held alone a moment becomes the wielded weapon, so the HUD and the game's reload take it over.
- A weapon let fall lies in the world and is kept from the despawn while it is near.

The inventory also keeps a rigid object per hand as a secondary attached object, carried keyframed at the hand's attach bone without collision and despawned when the inventory clears it (unverified); those are carried props, not weapons.

## Settings and readout

The settings are `controllers.weapons.hold`, and `controllers.weapons.holsters.follow_docks` for the holsters on the docked guns, which is on by default. `either_hand`, `handover`, `handle_radius_m`, and `direct_pickup` are on by default: each call they make is the game's own, read in the IDB (the attach-bone table writes and the interpolation skip are the dual-wield layout's, and `PickupItem` on a dropped weapon is the swimming pickup's path), and none writes the inventory directly. `instant_dock`, `pistol_dock`, `snap_s`, `grip_to_hold`, `max_throw_mps`, `drop_replaced`, `second_weapon`, `left_grip`, `magazine_pips`, `steal`, `racks`, `take_radius_m`, `take_slide`, `promote_second`, and `promote_after_s` are on by default too, as every new feature is, and each can be switched off. Their calls are read in the IDB as well, but they are the least proven in the headset: they equip and unequip without the animation graph's acts, which the game never does on land, a drop takes a weapon out of the inventory, which only a heavy weapon's drop does in the game, a second weapon is updated outside the inventory's own fan-out in a state the game never fires from, and a take from a character's hands drops a living character's gun, which the game does only through an animation state's drop task, and a take off a rack completes a pickup with no animation on land. The debug UI's Controllers tab shows the holding hand, whether the bones are owned, a pending hand and its cause, the handovers, the table writes and snaps, the direct pickups, the fallbacks, and the last refusal, and for the docking the slide under way, the hand whose grip holds the gun, the draws, holsters, and drops, the last throw's speed, and the last refusal, and for the second weapon its hand and slot, whether it is carried and from which socket, its trigger, the mod's updates of it, the drops and promotions, and the last refusal, and for the takes the guns in characters' hands and the weapons on racks within reach, the takes, and the copies racks gave. The pause menu's `VR: STATUS` page has a `WEAPONS IN HAND` section: each hand's weapon and how it came there (`DOCKED`, `PICKED UP`, `STOLEN`, `RACK`, `GAME'S DRAW`, or the second weapon), the guns in characters' hands and the weapons on racks within reach with the takes and the copies, the second weapon with its hand, its magazine, and which weapon the reload is on, and the last refusal of anything the hands tried with a weapon.

## Unverified
