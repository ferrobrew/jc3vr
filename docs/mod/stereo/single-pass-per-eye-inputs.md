# Single-pass per-eye inputs

Under single-pass stereo ([single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)) an instance-doubled draw covers both halves of the double-wide target in one submission, so every input it takes from the engine's per-pass staging is the centred render camera's: the camera position, the light positions, the screen position, the previous-frame matrices, and the field of view of cameras sized from the target. Each section names one such input and how each eye gets its own. [single-pass-collapse-defects.md](single-pass-collapse-defects.md) covers the passes that run once over the whole target.

## The eye-camera buffer

A pixel shader rewritten at creation finds its eye from the half its pixel lies in and reads that eye's rows from a mod-owned pixel constant buffer. Slot 11, which no game pixel shader declares, carries the eye-camera buffer (`jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::eye_camera`); slot 13 carries per-block rows while a block runs. `eye_camera` rewrites the buffer at every [`RenderEngine::SetAllGlobalShaderProgramConstants`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-SetAllGlobalShaderProgramConstants), from the rows the engine has just staged. Inside the routed range under the collapse each eye's block holds the staged rows moved to its eye, and a boundary row holds the first pixel of the right eye's half of whatever target is bound, so it follows reduced-resolution targets. Elsewhere both blocks hold the staged rows and the boundary lies past every pixel, so a rewritten shader reads what the engine's would; the range guard restores that when the range ends. The per-eye re-issues and the masked runs of a split pass land in one half, and their block holds what `eye_globals` stages into `cb0` for the run, so nothing is applied twice.

The rewrites below compose in the fragment hook in a fixed order (eye camera, screen door, velocity), the later ones reading the buffer the first declared. An unbound buffer reads as zeros, which every rewrite treats as "no correction".

## The camera position

The engine stages the camera position into the fragment `GlobalConstants` ([`RenderEngine::m_FPGlobalConstData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##field-m_FPGlobalConstData)) once per pass, and nearly half the game's pixel shaders read it, for view vectors, camera distances, and the forward-lit families' sun-shadow lookup (engine [shaders.md](../../engine/rendering/shaders.md#pixel-shaders-that-read-the-camera-position)). The `character` material, for one, folds a camera-measured sheen into the albedo it writes, so the player's own clothing differed visibly from an isolated render. `single_pass.pixel_camera_per_eye` has `dxbc_stereo::localize_eye_camera` rewrite every shader that reads those rows to read its eye's block: the two camera position rows and the cascade translation row, which is baked relative to the camera and has to move with it.

Every camera read moves, including the ones that only feed a fade, because that is what an isolated eye computes; the choices kept on the centre (LOD and tessellation selection) are not made in pixel shaders. On the vertex side, the four camera-only reprojected families (`generaljc3`, `landmark`, `layered`, and `layeredblend`) keep `cb0[4]` centred because their position path adds it back, and in them it feeds only a distance fade. The rewrite declines a shader with no `SV_Position` input, one that already declares slot 11, and one that indexes `cb0` dynamically from a base that could reach a camera row. Turning the switch on needs a shader reload; turning it off applies at once.

## The local lights

[`LightManager::SetupLightingData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::light_manager::LightManager##func-SetupLightingData) subtracts the render camera position from every light position and bakes it into every spot projection (engine [lighting-shadow-pipeline.md](../../engine/rendering/lighting-shadow-pipeline.md#43-the-lighting-frame-constants-are-camera-relative)), so under the collapse each light vector is off by the eye's offset, which visibly moves the glow of a light the player carries. `single_pass.local_lights_per_eye` adds the eye's offset from the centre to each eye's block, and the lights rewrite (`crates/dxbc-stereo/src/rewrite/lights.rs`) corrects the light reads of every shader that binds `LightingFrameConsts`: a position read becomes the position minus the offset, and a projection's translation row gains the projection applied to the offset. A read is corrected only when its pixel position is eye-relative (a temp last written as a position minus a camera row, or the resolve's depth times its view ray). The vegetation blend permutations measure from a position already relative to their own camera and are left alone, and the light-propagation-volume transforms stay centred, since the volume is built once around the camera.

## The screen-door pattern

The alpha-tested and fading materials discard against `frac(dot(SV_Position.xy, k))` for a fixed vector (engine [shaders.md](../../engine/rendering/shaders.md#the-screen-door-hash)), and the volumetric fog dithers by the same hash. The vector is not a whole number of cycles per pixel, so in the right half a different set of pixels survives each fade than in an isolated eye. `single_pass.screen_door_per_eye` rewrites every such `dp2` (`dxbc_stereo::localize_screen_door`) to read `x` minus the eye boundary. The sun-shadow filter's rotation, the other hash of the pixel position, has its vector zeroed by the shadow PCF patch ([shader-patching.md](../rendering/shader-patching.md)), and a zero vector is left alone.

The tree impostors build the same hash not from `SV_Position` but from their clip position over twice the inverse target size, which under the collapse is the centred camera's over both eyes' width. `dxbc_stereo::localize_projected_screen_door` rewrites their pixel shaders to compute the offset an eye-sized render would from the pixel position. A detour on `TreeImpostorRBType::Setup` (`impostor_pixel_size`) also halves the display width for the call, since the impostors' footprint, and so their alpha-tested silhouette, is derived from it ([single-pass-render-blocks.md](single-pass-render-blocks.md)).

## The motion vectors

The velocity pass encodes the current screen UV less the previous frame's, from clip positions its vertex shader hands down (engine [shaders.md](../../engine/rendering/shaders.md#the-velocity-passs-motion-vectors)). Under the collapse the previous position comes through the centred camera's double-wide previous view-projection, from every source (the global rows, the buildings' camera-relative ones, and the matrices blocks bake), and the current one is in the eye's half only where the remap computed it. Every eye's motion would carry a ramp of about half a pixel per pixel from its centre plus the eye-to-centre parallax.

`dxbc_stereo::localize_velocity` rebuilds both positions after the encoding's divide. The current NDC comes from `SV_Position` over the eye's half, which serves every family whatever its vertex shader did. The previous NDC comes from the interpolated previous clip position through `S P_prev E⁻¹ P_prev⁻¹`: the previous projection, the doubling of horizontal clip, and the eye's offset `E` recovered from `M_eye` as `P_eye⁻¹ M_eye P`. The eyes are rigid with respect to the camera, so the previous eye cameras relate to the previous render camera as the current ones do. The map carries the eye's offset but not its cant (`eye_camera::eye_offset_view`), because an isolated eye's previous view-projection is the centred camera's orientation too. The rows follow the boundary in the eye-camera buffer, blended in by a weight row that is zero outside an eye-split pass; the previous position is blended before its divide, so zero rows cannot produce a NaN.

## The ambient-occlusion volumes

An occlusion volume ([`RenderBlockAOBox`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockAOBox)) projects its corners through the full view-projection, hands its pixel shader a projective screen UV, and maps the scene depth at that UV through `ScreenToLocal`, which [`RenderBlockAOBox::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockAOBox##func-Draw) builds from the camera. Re-issued per eye unpatched, each eye would sample the depth stretched across it. `single_pass.ao_volumes_per_eye` draws them once: `aobox` reads the eye's full view-projection rows and is instance-doubled, and `dxbc_stereo::localize_ambient_occlusion_volume` rewrites the pixel shaders to work from `SV_Position`, multiplying the matrix by a per-eye `P_eye` (`single_pass::ao_volumes`, bound at pixel slot 13) so the centred matrix applied to it equals the eye's own matrix applied to the eye's own UV.

## The road surfaces' clip origin, per eye

The road surfaces (`layeredroad`) and the `generalmkiii*roadbias` permutations subtract `cb0[9]`, the render camera position staged by [`RenderEngine::SetGlobalShaderConstants`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-SetGlobalShaderConstants), rather than `cb0[4]` before the view-projection. Left centred, each eye's road texturing slides by half the eye distance, a dense speckle on its high-frequency layers. `single_pass.clip_origin_per_eye` has the remap (`ClipOrigin::PerEye`) read each eye's copy of the row from `cb13` in the shaders that read the view-projection rows without `cb0[4]`; in the others `cb0[9]` is a fade and is left alone.

## The planar reflection camera

The WaveWorks reflection is rendered in `PreDraw` from [`WaterPatchManager::m_ReflectionCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/water_patch_manager::WaterPatchManager##field-m_ReflectionCamera), which [`WaterPatchManager::UpdateReflectionCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/water_patch_manager::WaterPatchManager##func-UpdateReflectionCamera) sizes from the active camera and aspect ratio, so under the collapse it would hold twice an eye's field. The collapse replaces its width and aspect with one eye's right after the engine sets them, before the projection is rebuilt. Replacing rather than correcting afterwards matters because the draw thread can render the previous frame's reflection from the live camera meanwhile; this job is also why the dispatch's pinned flags must end with it ("The routed range" in [single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)).

## Non-indexed draws drawn once for both eyes

A non-indexed draw in the routed range outside the geometry passes is treated as a fullscreen pass and drawn once at the full viewport. Several are scene geometry, and `draw_detour` promotes those, when a patched vertex shader is bound and the pass is on an allowlist, to one two-instance `DrawInstanced` with the viewport split. The allowlist is needed because promoting every such draw also catches `lpvinit` and the moon's model in passes where that moves the lit colour.

- The static light sprites ([`RenderBlockTypeLights`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeLights), in `RP_DEFERRED_LIGHTS` and `RP_LENSFLARE`). Stretched, each light would land at twice its distance from the centre and be depth-tested against a pixel it does not represent. `lightsource` reads the eye's full view-projection rows, and a detour on [`RenderBlockTypeLights::Setup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeLights##func-Setup) sets the sprites' inverse screen width to one eye half's (`static_lights`).
- The moon (`RP_MOON`, the `open` shader) and the stars (`starsshader`), reprojected. `M_eye`'s depth row would mix the other clip components into a depth pinned to the far plane, so both `cb13` slots carry `M_eye` with its depth row replaced by the identity for these draws, as the sun's per-eye re-drive does (`single_pass::sun`). Needs `single_pass.reproject`.
- The water reflection planes' proxy ([`RenderBlockWaterReflectionPlane::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockWaterReflectionPlane##func-Draw)), whose depth the reflections trace water against. `watermask` is on the reprojection allowlist, and the pixel shader's load then reads each eye's own depth.

The light-propagation-volume initialisation (`lpvinit`) is correct drawn once: it writes a volume texture, its geometry shader drops the viewport index, and the `cb0[4]` reference that got it claimed reads only a component `cb13` carries unchanged. The other non-indexed draws left at `once` are the fullscreen passes, which take their eye inside the draw.

## Asymmetric and canted eyes

A real headset's eyes have mirrored asymmetric frusta, and some cant each display outward, so an eye differs from the centred camera in projection and orientation as well as offset. The capture harness's headset rigs exercise these paths ([single-pass-validation.md](single-pass-validation.md)).

- The clouds take clip from the full view-projection, invisible as an error for symmetric parallel eyes at cloud distance but wrong under asymmetry and cant. `single_pass.clouds_per_eye` puts the four cloud vertex shaders on the reprojection, by exact name since the cloud name space also holds the cloud-shadow pass.
- A split pass's reconstruction basis, the decals' and clustered assignment's views, the SSAO and reflection rows, the fog volume, the water, and FSR's per-eye matrices all carry the eye's resolved pose. The motion vectors' previous view carries only the offset (above).
- The cull and shadow-fit frusta bound the two eyes in the head frame over each eye's turned frustum corners (`jc3vr_view::vr::frame::head_frame_tangents`), in every mode.
- Multipass's FSR stereo correction keeps a per-eye history (`jc3vr_view::stereo::VpHistory`); one snapshot that each dispatch overwrote would correct eye 0 through eye 1's projection.

### The terrain hull cull

Both terrain block types bake an offset view-projection into their hull constants once per frame per slot ([`RenderBlockTypeTerrain::SetupConstantBuffers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeTerrain##func-SetupConstantBuffers), [`RenderBlockTypeTerrainPatch::SetupConstantBuffers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeTerrainPatch##func-SetupConstantBuffers)), and the hull discards patches outside it. The patch system's hulls test it unconditionally, so clearing the type's cull flag ([culling.md](culling.md)) does not reach them. Under the collapse the bake is the centred camera's, so a canted left eye loses distant terrain at its outer edge as a hard, patch-shaped cut. `single_pass.terrain_cull_both_eyes` substitutes, inside the routed range, a matrix whose `x`, `y`, and `w` columns come from the projection bounding both eyes (`jc3vr_view::vr::frame::eyes_union_projection_standard`) and whose depth column is the engine's. The per-slot cache is stamped stale whenever the substitution changes, so a reflection pass still bakes the engine's matrix. The cull only drops what the clipper would, so no pixel changes.
