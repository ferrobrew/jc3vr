# Grapple snatch

The grapple hand pulls things to itself. The grapple button keeps doing what it does in the game (a tap fires, a hold tethers to and between targets), and the grip pulls: with the empty grapple hand pointed at something out of reach, a squeeze and a yank back away from it fire the game's hook at it and snatch it from wherever it is (the ground, a rack or a locker, a character's hands, a holster on a character's body) into the hand, in the held position. From there it is an ordinary held thing. Weapons are the core of the feature; loose objects, grenades, and placed charges come to the hand the same way, car doors are pulled open on the line, and an object too heavy for a hand or a ragdolling body is swung on the line ([grapple-snatch-swing.md](grapple-snatch-swing.md)). Each kind of target, and how it is freed and finished, is in [grapple-snatch-targets.md](grapple-snatch-targets.md).

The code is `controllers::snatch` in `jc3vr-grapple`, with the aim patch in `jc3vr_body::controllers::aim::grapple_weapons`; the engine side, including why the game's own grapple never hooks a weapon, is "Weapons and the grapple" in [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md#weapons-and-the-grapple). The snatch ends in the [two-handed inventory](two-handed-inventory.md)'s takes: its arrival lands the item in the loadout table and the catching hand, never through the game's equip, while the other hand holds whatever it holds.

The game has no pull of a light object to the player and cannot reach into a character's hands. The snatch keeps the game's own grapple wherever it can, so it reads as the same device doing one thing more: the game's aim, the game's hook, line, and flight from Rico's left forearm, and the game's own pickup and steal rules at the end. The mod adds the trigger, the pull, and the arrival. The snatch has no switch.

## Targeting

The snatch aims with the grapple's own aim, the off-hand's ray the mod feeds to the game's grapple ([hands-and-aiming.md](hands-and-aiming.md#grapple-aim)). The game's aim ray passes through weapons, so `aim::grapple_weapons` patches the grapple's pass of the direct-aim raycast alone, never the physics system's layer table, which every query on the layer shares (the gun's aim among them, which would then stop on guns on the ground). Other characters' grapples, the camera's pass, and every other query are untouched.

Inside the grapple's pass, a detour of [`PlayerAimControl::CheckDirectTargets`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-CheckDirectTargets) first casts again along the same ray on [`VEHICLE_RAYCAST`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-VEHICLE_RAYCAST), which meets weapons, out to the ray's closest hit, with the aim's own filter ignoring the player and its vehicle. It steps past what that layer meets and the aim's does not (a character's capsule, a projectile, a vehicle's collision body) a few times. A weapon lying in the world that it finds is written into the ray's closest hit ([`CastRayInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::CastRayInfo)), and a holey hit beyond it is dropped from the [collector](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimRaycastCollector##field-m_HasHoleyHit), so the game does everything it does with a hit with the weapon: the slot's game object, the grapplable flag, the last-valid grapple cache, and the latch of a fire. The weapon found is published for the snatch, or, when there is none, the object the aim's own ray hit. Because the line is the aim's own cast, nothing behind a wall is found.

The snatch's candidates (`snatch::target`), with the snatch's tuning (`crates/jc3vr-grapple/tuning/snatch.toml`, `SnatchTuning` in `controllers::snatch::config`, [tuning.md](../tuning.md)):

- The guns in characters' hands and the guns holstered and visible on their bodies, which the steal notes out to the snatch's range ([physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-gun-from-a-character)): the gun whose holding hand's grip, or whose holstered origin, is nearest the ray within `cone_deg` and `range_m`, with a line-of-sight cast to it on the aim's layer that ignores the player and the character.
- Weapons, loose objects, grenades, and placed charges lying in the world, off the ray as well as on it: a gun lying flat subtends a fraction of a degree at range, more than a tracked hand can hold on it. The snatch finds the dynamic bodies whose boxes lie near the ray (`physical_hands::remote::dynamic_near`, reading only the broad phase's boxes) within the same cone widened in proportion to the distance, up to `floor_cone_widen_deg` more at the range (`snatch::rules::floor_cone`), measured to the body's centre, with the same line-of-sight cast.
- The weapons a rack or a locker holds out, which have no body in the world ([grapple-snatch-targets.md, "Racks and lockers"](grapple-snatch-targets.md#racks-and-lockers)).
- What the aim hits itself: a weapon on the ground, a loose object, or a car door's handle. At the ray, it wins over a candidate off it, since the choice takes the smallest angle.

The chosen target shows one marker, a ring in its kind's colour (a weapon's, an object's, or a vehicle's), which stands in for the game's highlight: the game's reticle is hidden under hand aim. A gun still on its mount is never offered (`engine::on_mount`). A loose object too heavy for one hand, up to the swing's ceiling, and a ragdolling body are offered to be swung on the line ([grapple-snatch-swing.md](grapple-snatch-swing.md)).

In the grip's one targeting rule ([grip.md](grip.md)) the snatch is the lowest tier, `Tier::Remote`, taken only when nothing at all is in reach (not even a surface to climb or a reach margin), so anything within real reach wins. It is offered only to the grapple hand, empty (no gun, foregrip, grenade, charge, or prop), on foot with no traversal, with the hook free (no active or attachment wire, no fire pending, the hook inactive) and the grapple button up. A fist never takes it: only a press aimed at it arms the snatch.

The grapple hand is the off-hand of the acting hand. The game's grapple device is on Rico's left forearm, so a snatch in a left-handed setup still leaves the opposite arm.

## The interaction

1. Aim: point the empty grapple hand at the target. The marker shows it.
2. Grip: squeezing arms the snatch on the target under the aim. Nothing flies yet, and the target is held while the grip is. The target's ring gives way to the latch: a cyan dot ringed on the target and a line from the gripping hand to it, drawn through the scene for as long as the snatch is armed (`snatch::latch`, the overlay's `SnatchLatch` source). The latch goes when the yank fires or the snatch is cancelled.
3. Yank: a pull of the hand back along its aim, faster than a threshold, while the grip is held, fires it. The pull is measured along the grapple hand's aim at the grip edge, in the cockpit frame (`controllers::yank::reach_along`), not as the hand nearing the body: seated, a hand already close to the body pulls back and out past its side, which nears neither the chest nor the head. The speed is measured from whichever sample in the detector's window gives the pull its length, so a hand held still at the grip before it pulls is not slowed by the pause. The yank has its own detector (`snatch_yank` in `controllers::snatch::config`, the snatch tuning's `yank`), gentler than the zip's ([physical-grapple.md](physical-grapple.md#the-yank)), since the grip already arms it and the yank only confirms. Opening the grip before the yank disarms it, and a punch or a swing that closes the hand does not snatch, since the grip comes first.
4. Flight: the hook flies from the left forearm to the target and bites, and the line yanks the target toward the hand, a real body flying with real velocity and spin that collides with whatever is in the way. Over the final approach it slows a little and comes straight at the hand, and a gun turns in the air to arrive in the pose the catching hand holds it in ([The aligned landing](#the-aligned-landing)).
5. Catch: the line steers the target to whichever free, empty hand is nearest its incoming path, whether or not that hand's grip is squeezed, so the player can pull with the grapple hand and hold the other hand out to receive it. A couple of ticks out the line goes slack and the target flies on by its own momentum, and a hand catches it when its grip closes on it within the catch zone, or a moment after it passed. If no hand closes on it, it flies past or hits the player, and drops.

Hold to hold: opening the grip at any point cancels. Mid-flight, the line comes away and the target keeps its momentum and falls. Leaving the feet (a vehicle, a fall, a ragdoll, a reel) cancels it too.

While the line is out, the other hand's grip, when that hand is free and empty, is held for the catch (`snatch::reserved_hands`): a press of it closes the hand on nothing, so it takes nothing in reach (a holster, a pouch, a ledge, a prop) and drives no game action, and a hand already closed on nothing stays so. A hand whose grip holds something else never catches. When the other hand catches, its grip holds the target from then on as the grapple hand's would have.

Haptics: `Event::SnatchArm` when the grip arms it, `Event::SnatchFire` when the yank fires it, with the grapple's fire cue on the snatching hand, `Event::SnatchHit` when the hook bites, the line's tension (`Sustain::GrappleTension`) while it yanks, and `Event::SnatchCatch` on the catching hand; a refused or failed snatch, a missed catch among them, plays `Event::SnatchRefused` on the grapple hand ([haptics.md](haptics.md)).

## The pull and the catch

1. A weapon without a body is first freed into the world at its source. A gun in a character's hands goes through the steal's drop and stagger (`steal::release`, through the hands' take from a source, `source::free`, [physical-weapons.md, "Taking a weapon from a source"](physical-weapons.md#taking-a-weapon-from-a-source)), which leaves it a dropped weapon with bodies; the check that the hand can take it comes first, so a refused take leaves the gun in the character's hands. A freed gun is fired at on the next tick its body can be read, within `free_timeout_s`.
2. The game's hook is fired at the target through [`GrapplingHook::FireHookCustom`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-FireHookCustom), the entry point the game's own vehicle grapple and stunt-position grapple use, with a target info built on the target's game object ([`HookTargetInfo::SetupTargetFromGameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::HookTargetInfo##func-SetupTargetFromGameObject), latched at the object's origin) and the ghost-wire flag on. A ghost wire flies to the target and, when its last casts miss, as they do on a weapon, attaches to the target object itself. Before a weapon on the ground is fired at, the pickup's rules are checked (not a grenade or an unlimited-stock copy, the animation allows a pickup, and the table can take it into the hand), so a snatch the hand could not finish never flies.
3. When the wire's hook end is attached ([`WireEnd::m_WireEndState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##field-m_WireEndState) and its proxy), the hook has bitten (`pull::bite`): the mod notes where the hook's end sits in the target body's frame, takes the native rope off the line (its constraint removed, and its re-creation and tension skipped while the line pulls, through the same exclusion the swing uses, `sling_hooks::exclude`), and plays the hook's impact. Then the line yanks (`pull::pull`): each tick the physical hands' hold solve, the physics every hand's hold on a body goes through, pulls the body toward the receiver's wrist with a strength of the target's mass times `line_accel_mps2`, no twist of its own, and gravity in the solve. Beyond `approach_m` it pulls at the hook's point at `line_speed_mps`, aiming `arc` of the distance still to go above the wrist so a far target clears the ground, and the pull off the centre spins the target, up to `max_spin_rps`. Within `approach_m` the line's speed falls in proportion to the distance to `arrival_speed_mps` at the wrist (`rules::line_speed`), the arc fades (`rules::pull_point`), and the line pulls at the body's centre, so the target keeps its spin but its centre flies at the wrist. A target heavier than `heavy_mass_kg` cannot bring its centre as near the palm, so its catch zone widens and the line asks less speed of it (`rules::HeavyCatch`).
4. The line steers to its receiver (`catch::receiver`): of the free, empty hands, the one whose wrist is nearest the segment from the target's centre to the eye point, whatever its grip is doing, kept until the other is nearer by `landing.hysteresis_m`. It is chosen afresh each tick while the line pulls and kept once the line is slack. When the target is as near that wrist as it flies in `coast_s`, kept between `slack_min_m` and `slack_max_m` (`rules::slack_radius`), the line goes slack (the phase reads inbound): it stops pulling and the target flies on by its momentum, still on the hook.
5. The catch is swept (`catch::CatchWatch`): the target is within a hand's zone when its centre passed within `catch_zone_m` of the hand's wrist anywhere along its path since the last tick (for a gun the landing steers, the gun's grip point against where that hand will hold the grip), and the hand catches it when its grip is closed, on nothing or on the snatch, while the target is within the zone or up to `catch_grace_s` after. The hands that may catch are the receiver and the free, empty hand other than the grapple hand; the grapple hand, whose grip holds the line throughout, catches only as the receiver. Of two that catch on one tick, the one the path passed nearer wins. The wire comes away, and a weapon is taken into the catching hand through the direct pickup (`pickup::take`, with the source it was freed from, so the table keeps its provenance, and `Delivery::Remote`) by its grip, with a slide into the held pose as long as the landing left work for it (`landing::arrival_slide`). Every rule of the pickup applies again. Nothing draws the target into a hand once the line is slack.

A slack target that recedes from every catching hand or stops, while no hand with its grip open still has it within its zone or grace, or that flies longer than `max_slack_s`, has been missed: the wire comes away and the snatch fails as "the hand missed the catch". A target the hand catches but cannot take (the table refuses the weapon, or the loadout the grenade) is brought to rest at the hand, so it drops there as one let go of does, rather than flying on past the player; the table's own reason is logged.

The snatch fails, cancelling the wire and leaving the target where it is, on a stall while the line pulls (the body staying within `stall_distance_m` over `stall_window_s`, snagged on the world), a target farther from the hand than the wire's [`m_MaxLength`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHookWire##field-m_MaxLength), a hook that flies longer than `attach_timeout_s`, or a wire the game ends. Ordinary targets are held by a strong reference from the fire to the end, so the despawn never takes one mid-flight; a pooled C4 charge uses a weak handle and its allocation identity instead ([grapple-snatch-targets.md, "Placed charges"](grapple-snatch-targets.md#placed-charges)).

The hook's state machine serialises snatches: `FireHookCustom` refuses while a line is out or the hook is busy, and the mod refuses before it while a normal fire is pending, which `FireHookCustom` does not test. The custom fire is accepted while dual tethers are attached, since they are not the active wire, and the hook ignores the grapple input in its custom state. While the snatch's line is out the bindings hold the grapple button back from the game, and the snatch ends its custom wire itself on every exit and every stand-down of the bindings, checking first that the wire is still the hook's active one.

### The aligned landing

A gun lands in the hand ready to shoot (`snatch::landing` for the math, `snatch::arrival` for the engine side). At the bite, while the gun lies still, the landing reads its body's frame (the centre of mass and the body's rotation) in the gun's own frame, whose origin is the grip's attach point. Each tick of the flight then knows where the grip is and, for each catching hand, where the hand will hold the gun: the hand's IK wrist target, extrapolated by its motion to the catch (at most `landing.max_extrapolate_s`), composed with the rigid grip's canonical grip on that wrist frame (`grip::canonical_on_wrist_frame`). The hand it steers for is the line's receiver. The drive starts at the final approach, or at the bite inside it, and comes to full strength over `landing.ramp_m` or `landing.ramp_ticks`.

The rotation steers the flying body itself, through the velocity setter the line uses (`jc3vr_body::controllers::physics::set_body_velocities`): each tick the body's angular velocity moves toward the hand's own turn rate plus the turn still to make over the time left until the gun reaches the catch radius (`landing::turn_drive`, `landing::time_to_go`), held to `landing.max_spin_rps` and `landing.max_angular_accel`. Its gain grows as the catch nears, so the turn is closed by the catch, and it keeps running after the line goes slack, since it cannot change whether the catch happens. Most snatches start a metre or two out and fly a few ticks, so the turn has to be fast, which is why the landing has its own caps above the line's spin. The position is corrected only while the line pulls: the line's aim moves off the palm to where the hand will hold the gun's centre, by the part across the line only, held to `landing.lateral_window_m` (`landing::aim_shift`). Once the line is slack nothing moves the gun toward a hand, so a hand that moves away still misses.

At the catch the log says how far the gun arrived from where the hand holds it (`snatch: the gun arrived … degrees and … m from where the … hand holds it`), and the take's slide is the longer of the snap's length in proportion to the angle left over `landing.full_slide_deg` and the time the gun takes to fly the distance left, with none under `landing.settled_deg` and `landing.settled_m`.

For `watch_ms` after a catch the snatch watches the gun in the hand (`arrival::ArrivalWatch`): each tick it logs `snatch: the caught weapon in the hand` with the weapon's state, whether its first gun's model instance is drawn ([`ModelInstanceFlags::VISIBLE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::model_instance::ModelInstanceFlags##flag-VISIBLE)), whether the rigid grip pinned it, and the slide's progress, and it shows a model found hidden while the gun is in the catching hand ([`WeaponBase::SetVisible`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetVisible)). A catch can show a moment with no gun drawn, between the take (which takes the weapon out of the world and holsters it) and the first placement on a settled hand body; the watch's lines say whether the model was hidden or drawn somewhere unseen.

## Swinging on the line

A heavy loose object or a ragdolling body is never pulled into the hand: once the hook bites, a held line swings it from the grapple hand, and holding B swings a weapon or a light object the same way. The line, its brace by the other hand, and its button ownership are in [grapple-snatch-swing.md](grapple-snatch-swing.md).

## The snatch's sounds

The snatch plays the grapple's own sounds and effects, from the hook's effect container (`snatch::juice`; the engine side is "The hook's sounds and effects" in [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md#the-hooks-sounds-and-effects)). The custom fire plays no fire animation, and the hook's state stays custom, so of the game's own only the wire's flight loop plays by itself.

- The fire: the device's fire sound and muzzle effect, the container's `GrappleFire` usage ([`GRAPPLE_FIRE_USAGE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##const-GRAPPLE_FIRE_USAGE)), played as the game's track message plays it. The container's `GrapplePreFire` usage, a sound only, is not played: which point of the game's fire it belongs to is unverified, and the snatch's yank fires at once.
- The bite: the hook's material impact, [`WireEnd::PlayImpactEffect`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-PlayImpactEffect), which wants a cast hit's material, fed by a cast from the hand through the hook's end on the layer that meets weapons (overshooting by `impact_overshoot_m`), kept only when it hits the target.
- The yank: the reel's loop ([`m_ReelingInEffectUsage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##field-m_ReelingInEffectUsage)), held on while the line pulls by a detour of [`GrapplingHook::UpdateLoopingEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-UpdateLoopingEffects), which otherwise plays it only while the hook reels in. When the line goes slack or the snatch ends, the game's own update stops it softly.
- The line coming away (the catch, a miss, a cancel, a failure): the retract's one-off ([`m_RetractingActiveWireEffectUsage`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##field-m_RetractingActiveWireEffectUsage)).

## The grapple button at a gun

With the aim seeing weapons, the grapple button acts on a gun as the game acts on any light object, with one difference (`snatch::hooks`). Both act only while the bindings inject, since the aim sees weapons only then.

- A hook fired at a weapon flies as a ghost wire: a detour of [`WireEnd::SetExtend`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-SetExtend) sets the ghost flag when the latched object is a weapon. The hook's own casts never hit a weapon's body, so a plain hook would attach to whatever lies under or behind the gun and zip Rico there; a ghost hook attaches to the gun itself ([`WireEnd::ForceAttachToTarget`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::wire_end::WireEnd##func-ForceAttachToTarget)). It covers the first end of a tap or a hold and the second end of a tether, so a hold tethers a gun to a car, or two guns together, through the game's own tethers.
- A tap at a gun tosses the gun to the player, not Rico to the gun. A weapon's grappling mode is "reel in, no attach", and the game has no pull of a light object, so where the game would start its reel on an attached line to a weapon (in [`GrapplingHook::OnPreSimUpdate`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-OnPreSimUpdate), the local player's hook inactive, the button up, no tether pending), a detour ends the wire ([`GrapplingHook::CancelActiveWire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-CancelActiveWire)) and the next input tick tosses the gun on a ballistic arc to land a little in front of the player (the snatch tuning's `toss`). A stand-down of the bindings before that tick drops the toss, and narrows the characters' gun notice back to reach until the snatch offers targets again. The tap fetches a gun for a hand that is not free, or for the gamepad's habits; the snatch is the hand's.

## Readouts

The Status page's `Grapple` section has a `Snatch` row, which also heads the Grapple feature's readout: the snatch's state (idle, armed, freeing, hook out, pulling, inbound once the line is slack, swinging), the target offered with an object's mass and its distance, or why none is offered (not on foot, the hand not empty, the button held, a line out, the hook busy); while swinging, the target's mass, the held length, the tension of the line's strength, and whether it is braced; and the counts of failed snatches and tosses, the mass of a loose object under the aim whatever the snatch would do with it, and the last refusal.

The log, at info level under `controllers`, follows a snatch from `snatch: armed; a yank fires` and `snatch: yanked; firing` through the freeing and the bite to the catch (`snatch: the weapon came into the … hand`, `snatch: the object came into the … hand's hold`, `snatch: the grenade came to the … hand`), or `snatch: failed` with the reason and `snatch: cancelled` with why. The swing logs `snatch: swinging on the held line` with the length, mass, and strength, and the brace and the line grab their holds and releases. Each grip press's log notes the remote target offered.

## Design notes

- A tap of the grapple button at a gun tosses the gun to the player's feet: the game's "reel in, no attach" read as the object reeled in, not the player, so nothing zips and the gun comes within a step.
- The hook latches the weapon at its origin rather than at its grip: a dropped weapon's grip has no cheap object-space point, and the pull and the catch work on the body's centre of mass either way.
- The line's yank is the hold solve with the line's own speed and strength and no twist: the target keeps its momentum, the pull off its centre spins it, and nothing places it. A pull whose speed fell to nothing with the distance read as placed rather than thrown, so the line slows only to `arrival_speed_mps` and goes slack before the hand rather than braking: the target arrives hard, as a yanked thing does.
- The catch is the hand's: a swept test along the target's path, so a fast target is not stepped over, with a radius of the hand's own reach and no pull toward the hand. The final approach exists because a line that fixes the target's path a metre out turns wherever the hand goes during the slow flight that follows, the arc's rise, and the drop under gravity into miss distance, and because the pull at the hook spins the target, so a slack target's centre carries the spin sideways. No bend of the slack target toward the palm is added: within the slack's reach, a cone small enough to keep the catch physical covers less than the catch radius already does. The catch and landing tuning come from replays of recorded sessions' snatches against a model of the line.
- Either hand catches, and the line steers to the hand held out to receive whatever its grip does: a squeeze to catch never takes what the hand happens to be near, the grapple hand's own held grip does not take every target that reaches it, and a squeeze up to `catch_grace_s` late still catches.
- A heavy object swings on the line rather than coming to the hand, and the line is a rope rather than a rod: it pulls but never pushes, so it reads as a line and needs no rule for a target pushed away.

## Headset checklist

1. Point the empty grapple hand at a gun on the ground 10 m away: the marker shows on it. Move the hand onto a crate within reach: the crate's marker wins. Point at a gun behind a wall: no marker.
2. Squeeze without yanking: a bump, and the cyan latch on the gun until the hand opens. Open the hand: the latch goes and nothing flies.
3. Squeeze, then yank: the device fires with the game's fire sound and muzzle flash, the hook bites the gun with its impact sound and a thud on the hand, the reel's whine plays as the line yanks the gun toward the hand, and with the hand held still it smacks into the hand, the line comes away with the retract, and the gun sits in the held pose. Pull the hand away as the gun arrives: it flies past and the snatch fails as a missed catch. Open the hand mid-flight: the gun falls.
4. Snatch a rifle from 1.5 m, 5 m, and 15 m, holding the hand as if to shoot: it arrives grip first and pointing where the hand points, the log's `the gun arrived … degrees` reads under 10, and the trigger fires at once. The rifle is drawn throughout the catch; if a frame shows the hand empty, the `the caught weapon in the hand` lines say why.
5. Snatch with the grapple hand, then hold the right hand out between the gun and your face, its grip open: the line steers to the right hand, which catches on a squeeze, even a moment late. Keep the right grip open: the gun flies past, and the left hand does not take it. Squeeze the right grip on a holster or a pouch while the line is out: it takes nothing.
6. Snatch a gun from a soldier's hands at 10 m, and the pistol on a soldier's hip while it holds its rifle: the soldier staggers, and the gun flies in.
7. Tap the grapple button at a gun on the ground: Rico stays put, and the gun lands at your feet. Hold the grapple button on a gun, aim at a car, and let go: the gun is tethered to the car. With tethers attached, try a snatch: it works with the tethers untouched.
8. Snatch a rifle off a finite rack and a locker's long gun: the gun drops off and flies in, and the rack's slot is empty after. Snatch from a rack with unlimited stock: the phase reads freeing briefly, a copy drops from where the rifle hung, and the rack restocks. Snatch one pistol of a finite pair: only that pistol leaves, and the magazines and the reserve add up to what the pair held.
9.
