# Single-pass collapse defects

Under single-pass stereo ([single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)) one walk of the render-pass list renders both eyes side by side into a double-wide target, from a render camera centred between the eyes that carries eye 0's projection and no cant. Anything that takes a per-eye quantity from that camera, or pairs a per-eye quantity with a full-width one, is wrong in at least one eye. The defects below come from passes that run once over the whole double-wide target and from that pairing; [single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md](single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md) covers the per-eye inputs of the draws that render both eyes in one submission. The switches named here are rows of the switch table under "Configuration" in [single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md); each correction is measured against the isolated eyes as described in [single-pass-validation.md](single-pass-validation.md).

Several corrections rewrite a pixel shader at creation to find its eye from the half of the target its pixel lies in, and to read that eye's rows from a mod-owned pixel constant buffer; "The eye-camera buffer" in [single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md](single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md) describes the buffer most of them share.

## Four ways content goes wrong under the collapse

The first defects found under the collapse made content slide across the screen as the camera moved: sun shadows, terrain patches, foliage, water, decals, clouds, and smoke. They look like one bug and are four, sharing only a shape: a per-eye quantity paired with a full-width one. A fixed 2x horizontal scale error is a 2x horizontal motion gain, so content rendered or sampled at the double-wide scale sweeps past at twice the camera's rate, which reads as sliding over the world rather than as a static mis-scale.

The engine's screen-size constant is not the mechanism. The engine has one screen-size constant, `cb0[8].zw` (staged in [`RenderEngine::m_FPGlobalConstData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##field-m_FPGlobalConstData)), derived from the device display size, which the mod's `ResizeBuffers` substitute sets to the double-wide width. The buffers it addresses are double-wide too, so it is self-consistent, as are the `ftoi SV_Position` texel fetches (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.3).

### 1. Draws that never get an eye viewport bound

The collapse moves the eye split from pass level to draw level, so a draw arriving on an entry point nothing detours inherits whatever the two viewport slots hold, which is mostly the full viewport. Two families arrive that way:

- GPU-indirect draws (`DrawIndexedInstancedIndirect` and `DrawInstancedIndirect`; engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.2). The near tessellating terrain patches and the dominant foliage path submit here. `single_pass.indirect_per_eye` re-issues them once per eye with both viewport slots pinned. The instance counts live in a GPU buffer and cannot be doubled on the CPU, so the re-issue puts the geometry in both eyes at the right size, without parallax unless the block or shader supplies a per-eye transform.
- Non-indexed world geometry (`Draw`). This entry point is mostly the fullscreen passes', so the collapse resets to the full viewport for it, which would stretch the decal, road, and skidmark blocks that also submit there across both halves. `single_pass.slot13_per_eye` routes an allowlist of passes (`GEOMETRY_SLOT13_PASSES`) through the per-eye re-issue, and the collapse promotes a patched non-indexed draw to one two-instance draw (see [single-pass-draw-calls.md](single-pass-draw-calls.md)). It is an allowlist rather than a heuristic because a fullscreen triangle and a decal box are identical at the draw call: misclassifying a fullscreen pass as geometry is a visibly wrong frame, while a pass missing from the allowlist only falls back to the stretched behaviour, so a pass is added when evidenced, never on suspicion.

### 2. One eye-0 reconstruction basis over the whole double-wide target

A fullscreen pass is one draw with one constant buffer, so a pass that rebuilds world position from depth gets one basis while its quad spans both halves: the left half gets eye 0's frustum compressed 2x horizontally and the right half a basis unrelated to it. The error is a function of the view matrix, so it turns with the camera, and the passes that sample the sun cascade over the reconstructed positions make the shadows slide.

The `PerspectiveFovInverse` consumer set is closed at seven (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.1). `single_pass.reconstruct_per_eye` splits the deferred resolve, and the atmospheric scattering is always split with it, since splitting only the resolve leaves the scattering painting the error back over it. `single_pass.ssao_per_eye` and `single_pass.ssr_per_eye` handle their blocks in one run (below), and the subsurface skin and the depth of field are always split. `DrawPassThrough` needs nothing, since it is reached only under wireframe.

The shared machinery is `reconstruction::split_fullscreen_pass` in `jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::screen`. Each run is masked with a scissor rather than a half viewport, so the quad keeps its one-to-one NDC-to-pixel mapping and its G-buffer sampling stays correct; the per-eye basis comes from folding the full-target-NDC to eye-NDC remap into the substituted inverse (`reconstruction::eye_half_inverse`), which also carries the eye's turn from the centred camera for canted eyes. A run that never masked has already drawn the whole target, so the split stops after one run rather than double-exposing an accumulating pass. The split reports which of the three outcomes happened (`Split`, `Demoted`, or `NotTaken`), because a caller cannot infer it: only `NotTaken` means the pass still needs issuing, and the froxel split needs to distinguish a demotion. With `single_pass.fullscreen_camera_per_eye`, each masked run also gets its eye's camera position and the matching sun-shadow cascade translation in the fragment `GlobalConstants` (`jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::eye_globals`), put back after the run.

Compute work cannot be masked: a dispatch ignores the scissor and the viewport, its reach is fixed by its thread-group counts, and the blocks address their textures straight off `SV_DispatchThreadID` with no origin term (nor could a UAV supply one, since a D3D11 texture UAV selects a mip and an array slice, never a sub-rectangle). The depth-of-field prepass is split, so its compute work is scheduled instead: `reconstruction::DispatchPhase` issues it whole-target on the first run of the split, since the masked draws consume what it produces. That is the same single whole-target pass the unsplit block makes, so the eye-seam bleed of its separable blur is inherent to the collapse.

The screen-space decals have the same defect without being a `PerspectiveFovInverse` consumer: [`RenderBlockTypeSSDecal::Setup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeSSDecal##func-Setup) builds a basis inline into fragment `cb1[0..3]`, and the pixel shader derives the depth-fetch UV projectively from its own clip position. `single_pass.ssdecal_per_eye` fixes both together: the block is re-issued per eye restaging that eye's basis (from the eye's view, `ss_decal::eye_view`), and the `ssdecal` pixel shaders get one spliced instruction biasing the depth UV into that eye's half (`dxbc_stereo::bias_ssdecal_depth_uv`). The same UV feeds the reconstruction matrix, which wants the per-eye value, and the depth fetch, which wants the double-wide one, so they cannot be corrected as one.

The volumetric fog generation has the defect too. [`RenderBlockFogVolume::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockFogVolume##func-Draw), in `RP_FOG_VOLUME_GENERATE`, binds the block type's half-resolution fog target and builds a texture-UV-to-world matrix by inverting the render context's [`m_ViewProjectionF`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::graphics_engine::RenderContext##field-m_ViewProjectionF) itself. Each pixel samples depth at its UV, reconstructs through that matrix, and ray-marches the sun cascade and cloud shadow from the camera towards the result, so one centred basis is wrong in both halves, most visibly around the sun. The collapse runs the block once per eye with the at-entry mask, which follows the block onto its reduced-resolution target. For each masked run it gives the render context that eye's view-projection pre-multiplied by the inverse of the full-target-NDC to eye-NDC remap, so the inverse the block takes is the eye's with the remap folded in. The depth sample keeps the double-wide UV, the march starts from the eye position the shared camera staging puts in the fragment globals, and the render context is put back after the call. The upsample that follows only compares depths at matching UVs, and the composite maps the whole fog texture over the whole target, so neither needs a split.

#### SSAO, per eye in one run

[`RenderBlockSSAO::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockSSAO##func-Draw) builds the generation pass's basis and normal rotation from the render context's own projection and view (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.1; its `PerspectiveFovInverse` call feeds only the temporal resolve), so under the collapse one centred basis serves both halves. The generation pass is also wrong in three ways no basis can fix: its taps are isotropic offsets in screen UV, which span twice the view horizontally on a double-wide target; taps near the seam read the other eye's depth; and its spiral rotation and quad parity come from `SV_Position`, which in the right half is offset by the eye's width. The bilateral blur's outer taps and the composite's bilinear upsample also reach across the seam.

`single_pass.ssao_per_eye` fixes it without a split. `dxbc_stereo::localize_ambient_occlusion` rewrites the generation shader to take its eye from the half its screen UV falls in, read that eye's basis and rotation rows from a mod-owned pixel constant buffer, place each tap in the eye's own UV, clamp every depth and normal sample to the eye's half, and make the pixel position eye-local. `dxbc_stereo::localize_ambient_occlusion_filter` clamps the blur's and the composite's occlusion samples the same way. `jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::ambient_occlusion` builds each eye's rows as the block derives them from an eye's render context, with the horizontal basis terms remapped onto the eye's half, and binds them for the length of the block. Everything else in the block is per pixel or steps whole texels, and the temporal resolve is seeded fresh on every stereo frame (the `m_FirstPass` force in `jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::screen::ssao`), so the block keeps its one run and the draw count of a single-eye render. Where the depth and normals agree with the isolated eye's, the occlusion agrees; see "The ambient occlusion's sampling, the same in every mode" for the sampling that makes this exact.

#### Screen-space reflections, per eye in one run

[`RenderBlockScreenSpaceReflection::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockScreenSpaceReflection##func-Draw) captures the scene colour and G-buffer gloss into its half-resolution targets, traces the reflections with one fullscreen draw (`screenspaceraytracereflection`), and blurs the result with two separable draws. The ray march rebuilds each pixel's world position along a view ray its vertex shader interpolates from the `PerspectiveFovInverse` basis, projects the reflected ray's two ends through the render context's view and projection, steps between them in the target's pixels (whose size it is handed), samples the linear depth at each step, turns a hit back into the world through the camera transform, and reads the reflected colour from the previous frame's scene through the previous view-projection (masking the reflective water planes through the current one). None of those matrices is the basis a per-eye split substitutes, so splitting the block leaves both runs projecting through one eye-sized projection onto the double-wide depth, and the history lookup through the centred camera's double-wide previous view-projection lands both eyes on the middle of the previous frame.

`single_pass.ssr_per_eye` traces both eyes in the block's single run. `dxbc_stereo::localize_screen_space_reflection` rewrites the ray march to take its eye from the half its screen UV falls in, rebuild the view ray from three per-eye coefficients of that UV, read the eye's view, projection, camera transform, and the two view-projections from a mod-owned pixel constant buffer, march against a target size halved horizontally (so the march runs in the eye's own pixels and stops at the eye's edge, as it does at the edge of an eye-sized target), move every depth sample into the eye's half, and make the pixel position its start is jittered by eye-local. `jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::screen_space_reflection` builds the rows from each eye's projection and pose as an eye-sized render stages them. The view-projection for the water-plane mask is remapped onto the eye's half, and the previous view-projection is the engine's staged one moved onto the eye's half, which is the lookup an isolated eye makes: the engine's previous view-projection is the centred camera's in an isolated render too, so matching it, not correcting it, keeps the eyes equal to their isolated renders. `dxbc_stereo::localize_reflection_blur` clamps the blur's taps to the eye's half, which is what the clamping sampler does at the edge of an eye-sized target. The scene-colour capture is per pixel, and the ray march's starting camera position is per pixel through the eye-camera rewrite. The simpler `screenspacereflection` permutation, which marches through the current view-projection over the target's own UV, gets the per-eye view ray and view-projections and keeps its double-wide march. The block draws four times under the collapse, as an eye-sized render does.

The block's compute blur, selected by [`m_ComputeBlur`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockScreenSpaceReflection##field-m_ComputeBlur), is not rewritten (a known gap in [single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)).

### 3. A projective screen UV in per-eye space against a double-wide buffer

The legacy `Water*` and `WaterBox*` family takes its reflection, refraction, and depth UV from a CPU-staged matrix, projectively, normalized over the viewport while the buffers it indexes are the whole target (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §4.3). The collapse composes one more bias per eye, `u' = (u + eye) · 0.5`, onto the rows the block type staged. Because the type stages them once per pass, ahead of the `Draw` being re-issued, the fix restages the rows per eye and puts the type's own rows back afterwards. It is not reprojected by `M_eye`: the geometry still rasterizes from the collapsed centre view, so the UV has to describe where that geometry landed. The re-issue lives in `jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::scene::water`. The affected family may not be on screen at the water-quality setting in use, which makes a headset comparison the only way to tell the fix from a no-op.

#### The WaveWorks water

`NvWater*` builds its screen UV from `SV_Position × cb0[8].zw`, which is consistent under double-wide, so the projective-UV mechanism above is the legacy family's only. `NvWater*` has a different defect: its vertex shader writes clip from a baked model-view-projection in its own constant buffer, so both eyes would see the centre view, and flat water at the wrong depth is invisible in a screenshot and wrong only in a headset. `single_pass.nvwater_per_eye` substitutes the eye's view and reverse-Z projection into the render context, re-runs [`NvWaterHighEndRenderBlock::Setup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::NvWaterHighEndRenderBlock##func-Setup) so the block rebakes its matrices and constant buffers from them, and re-issues `Draw` into that eye's half, skipping the block's auxiliary foam and wake passes. The second eye's [`WaveWorksSimulationStep`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::WaveWorksSimulationStep) is suppressed, since it is the one non-idempotent call in the block. `single_pass.ssdecal_geometry_per_eye` applies the same shape to the decal box.

One `NvWater*` screen-space read is not consistent under double-wide: the planar reflection. Every `nvwater*` pixel shader that binds `g_ReflectionTexture` forms its horizontal coordinate as the screen UV divided by `g_UVScale.x`, so each eye would read half of the reflection stretched over its own half. The collapse gives that division the eye-local numerator `2u - eye`; the rewrite finds the division by the resource and constant names, covers the permutations that bind the reflection, and is bound per eye by the re-issue (`jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::water_pixel_variants`) together with the lake permutation's halved refraction displacement. The refraction, depth, ambient-occlusion, and dynamic local reflection lookups keep the full-target UV, because those buffers are double-wide. The reflection texture itself is rendered at one eye's field (see "The planar reflection camera, at one eye's size" in [single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md](single-pass-per-eye-inputs.md)).

Two more per-eye inputs ride on the same re-issue. The water pixel shaders measure the view vector of their Fresnel, reflection, and specular terms from the fragment `GlobalConstants` camera position, which the re-issue moves to each eye for that eye's draw, with the staging split fullscreen passes use. And the second eye's draw can find pixel state the first eye's draw left unbound, so `water_fragment_state` captures it during the first eye and fills any slot that comes back empty: the shadow maps and their samplers, the WaveWorks constants and samplers, and the sky-lighting textures and `LightingFrameConsts`. Without the sky lighting, the second eye's water comes out uniformly about 2% darker than an isolated render of that eye.

#### The water box surface, and the legacy family's clip path

The water-box surface grid (`NWater::DrawWaterBoxSurface`, run over every registered box from inside `CNvWaterHighEndRenderBlock::Draw`) has no per-eye treatment. Its clip path is the legacy family's. The block stages a box transform (a scale by the half-extents plus a translation of `centre − camera position`), and `waterboxsurface` does:

    world_rel = box_transform(cb1[0..3]) · position
    clip      = cb0[0..3] · (world_rel + cb0[4])

`cb0[0..3]` is the full, translation-bearing view-projection, not the translation-free `m_OffsetViewProjection` at `cb0[29..32]`, so the projection is out of the remap's reach. This is the whole legacy family's idiom: `waterbox`, `waterboxbelow`, `waterboxsurface`, `watershader_lod0`, and `watershader_lod1` read `cb0[0..3]` plus `cb0[4]`, and `waterboxclear`, `watergodraysshader`, and `waves` read `cb0[0..3]` alone. (`nvwaterbox` and `nvwaterbox_tess` read `cb0[4]` alone, with clip from the block's baked matrix, the shape `baked_cb_block_owns_vs` handles.)

The `cb0[4]` reference alone lets the remap claim these shaders, the camera-only misclassification in its most damaging form: the eye offset is added to the shader's reconstructed world position and the result is viewed from the centre, so the geometry is displaced in the opposite direction to the parallax it should have. A per-eye re-issue draws one instance, so `SV_InstanceID & 1` resolves to eye 0 in both halves and both eyes get eye 0's displacement, a rigid world-space offset of the water by half the IPD.

Reprojection is the transform these want: it replaces the clip position with `M_eye · clip_center` regardless of the source matrix, and fixes the parallax, the wrong-direction displacement, and the double-wide squash together. It is unreachable because `should_reproject_camera_only` is gated on the reprojection allowlist and the `water*` family is excluded. It cannot simply be un-gated. Reprojecting the geometry moves where it rasterizes, while the same shaders emit a projective screen UV on `TEXCOORD1` from rows the type builds out of the centre view-projection, which mechanism 3 corrects without `M_eye` because the geometry rasterizes from the centre view. Reproject the geometry and the staged rows would have to be rebuilt from the eye's full view-projection instead. The two changes are one change, and doing either alone trades a known defect for a subtler one. The surface grid is also drawn from `NWater::DrawWaterBoxSurface` rather than `WaterBoxRenderBlock::Draw`, so the per-eye re-issue in the water hook does not reach it.

### 4. A reduced-resolution target handed the scene's viewport

A target-blind viewport split re-derives the eye halves from the recorded scene viewport before every draw in the range, regardless of what the engine has bound. Several passes draw into a shared quarter-resolution off-screen target (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §9), and a draw into a `W × H/2` target handed a `2W × H` viewport is magnified 2x about the target origin and cropped to a quadrant; for the right eye, whose half starts at `x = W`, it is clipped away entirely. `single_pass.collapse_viewport_follows_target` keeps a second record that is always the live bind, so the split follows the bound target. The scene record cannot simply follow the engine, because a half-resolution post target would then mis-split the scene itself.

The composites need no matching change and must not get one: they map the whole low-resolution texture over the whole target with a baked UV, so a half-and-half texture composites correctly on its own, and both blend rather than overwrite, so re-issuing either per eye would double-composite.

## The glare chain at the eye seam

The glare chain (engine [rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/rendering.md) §3.3) builds the bloom in about two dozen fullscreen draws over targets from a half down to a sixteenth of the screen. Under the collapse every one is double-wide with the eyes side by side, and every draw reads its source by the screen UV, so each pixel reads its own eye except at the seam. There the Gaussian and saw blurs' taps, the box downsample's far corner, the bilinear upsample that accumulates each level into the next, and the opening's three-by-three neighbourhood all read the other eye, where an eye-sized target would clamp to its own edge. At the coarsest level one blur spans a large share of an eye, and the levels accumulate, so a sun near one eye's inner edge brightens the other eye's whole inner half.

`dxbc_stereo::localize_post_filter` rewrites a filter so the eye is the half its screen UV falls in, every filtered sample's horizontal coordinate is clamped to the texel centres of that eye's half of the sampled texture (measured with `resinfo`, so no size constant is involved), every gather is clamped a whole texel in, and every texel load outside the half returns zero, which is what the clamping sampler and an out-of-range load give at the edges of an eye-sized texture. The chain draws with the generic copy program and the box downsample, which other passes share, so the rewrite cannot replace them at creation: `jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::post_filters` builds each qualifying program's rewrite as a variant when the engine creates it (found by the names their resource tables bind their sources under), and the `Draw` detour binds the variant only for the draws issued inside [`GlareEffect::Apply`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::GlareEffect##func-Apply). The composite's `BloomTexture` and `SecondaryBloomTexture` lookups cross the seam through their bilinear footprint too, and since the composite always draws over the whole target, `dxbc_stereo::localize_composite_bloom` rewrites them at creation, on top of the overlay rewrite (`dxbc_stereo::localize_screen_overlays`).

The chain keeps the draw count of a single-eye render, one run per frame, against two under multipass; the treatment adds a shader lookup and swap per draw on the CPU and a clamp per sample on the GPU. The level sizes are the screen size shifted right, so the eye boundary falls on a texel boundary at every level only while the eye's width is a multiple of sixteen; otherwise the half the clamp keeps to straddles one texel at the coarser levels. A stripe a few pixels wide on each eye's inner edge, which first appears when the volumetric fog is composited, predates the post chain.

## The ambient occlusion's sampling, the same in every mode

The SSAO block draws six times: the full-resolution linear depth, the half-resolution generation, the bilateral blur's vertical and horizontal passes, a pass that leaves the horizontal result unchanged, and the full-resolution composite into the scene target's alpha. In three places the occlusion samples on or next to a texel boundary, so which texel, or which weight, the sampler takes rests on the coordinate's last bits:

- The generation's centre depth and normal samples sit at the centre of a half-resolution pixel, which is exactly the boundary between two full-resolution texels.
- The blur steps its taps by about half a texel times a depth weight close to one, so its odd taps land within a hair of a boundary.
- The composite's bilinear upsample quantizes its weights.

The interpolated UV is not the same function of the pixel in an eye-wide target and a target twice as wide, and the sampler's conversion of a normalized coordinate carries one bit less of the position in a texture twice as wide, so single-pass and an isolated eye disagree at columns where the UV's rounding puts a coordinate on the wrong side, in stripes with a period set by the eye width.

`single_pass.ssao_exact_sampling` makes every mode do the same arithmetic. Under the collapse `dxbc_stereo::localize_ambient_occlusion` computes the eye's UV from the pixel position over sizes read from the depth texture, places the taps in it, and takes only the samples onto the eye's half; outside the collapse `dxbc_stereo::snap_ambient_occlusion_uv` rewrites the same passes to compute the UV the same way. In both, the generation's centre samples move half a full-resolution texel off the boundary, the blur's samples read through loads of the texel `floor(coordinate × size)` names, and the composite blends its four texels in the shader. In the left eye every product is the eye-sized target's halved or doubled exactly, so the two agree to the bit wherever their inputs do. The switch changes the isolated eyes' and multipass's occlusion too, by which of two adjacent texels a sample reads and by a weight's last bits.

## The clustered froxel grid, per eye

Without a correction the grid is built with one eye's projection paired with the double-wide tile count, so it is twice as wide as the frustum it describes and every local light lands in the wrong tiles (each [`LightManager::LIGHT_GRID_TILE_SIZE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::light_manager::LightManager::LIGHT_GRID_TILE_SIZE) pixels wide), for both eyes, and for the roughly twenty forward-lit render-block types that sample it as well as the deferred resolve. `foliage` reads it a frame early, inside the G-buffer range. Engine [lighting-shadow-pipeline.md](../../engine/rendering/lighting-shadow-pipeline.md) §4.1 and §4.2 describe how the grid is built and who consumes it.

`single_pass.clustered_per_eye` rides the per-eye resolve split: each run narrows the light-assignment viewport to its own half of the tile grid, rebuilds the geometry transform from that eye's projection, makes the tile bounds affine in the absolute tile index, and suppresses the second run's `Graphics::Clear`. The two halves then compose, on the three properties §4.1 records (the clear is the only whole-target step, the assignment blend is commutative over disjoint tiles, and the fill is per-tile-local), so the grid ends the frame valid in both halves, which is what the forward consumers need this frame and the next. Nothing is written into engine memory; every substitution is a constant-buffer upload or viewport and clear state the mod already intercepts.

The collapse also assigns each eye's lights from that eye's position, by writing the eye's world offset into the translation row of the uploaded `cb2` view matrix (`clustered_lighting::split::eye_assignment_view`, which also turns it to a canted eye). That is algebraically identical to biasing the light positions, and it needs no engine write because that row is where the engine's own zeroed translation goes.

The split declines itself, leaving the unsplit behaviour and logging once, when the eye seam would not fall on a whole tile column (a double-wide width that is not a multiple of twice `LIGHT_GRID_TILE_SIZE`), when the bound assignment target is not the tile grid it sized for, or when the dispatch has no lights.

One decline involves two preconditions that disagree. The resolve mask refuses unless the bound viewport is the collapse's double-wide target (a pass binding anything else is not the one being split), whereas the assignment split engages as soon as the bound target is the tile grid it sized for, which an off-scene dispatch can satisfy. When that happens the assignment fills eye 0's half and the resolve then proves unmaskable, so no second run fills the rest, and because the grid is shared the missing half shows on the main scene rather than on the dispatch responsible. Rebuilding it would mean re-issuing the whole block, resolve included, which is the double exposure the demotion rule prevents. So the split stands down for that graphics context instead: maskability is a property of the pass rather than of the run, so one observation settles it, and the scene's own context, which does mask, is unaffected.

## The shipped settings under single-pass

The capture profile (`apply_profile` in `crates/jc3vr-capture/src/testing/visual/mod.rs`) pins or turns off several things a player runs with, so the comparisons say nothing about them unless they are put back ("Running the shipped settings" in [single-pass-validation.md](single-pass-validation.md)).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fsr.antialiasing` | FSR | the engine's SMAA, or the mod's TAA ([taa.md](../rendering/taa.md)) | FSR in place of the engine's anti-aliasing, post-tonemap | once per eye over its half with `single_pass.fsr_per_eye`; once over the double-wide target without |
| `fsr.stereo_mv_correction` | on | on (inert while FSR is off) | the parallax term FSR's velocity decode adds | per eye from each eye's own matrices with `single_pass.fsr_per_eye` |
| `foveation.enabled` | on | off | the peripheral stencil drop before the shading passes and the fill-in after | one draw each over the double-wide target, per-pixel eye with `single_pass.foveation_per_eye` |
| `exposure.force`, `post_fx.skip_histogram` | off, off | pinned, skipped | the engine's histogram metering and exposure adaptation | metered once per frame over both eyes; one exposure for both |
| `post_fx.skip_fade` | off | skipped | the screen fade's colour multiply | once over the double-wide target |
| `post_fx.skip_player_damage` | off | skipped | the player-damage pass | once over the double-wide target |
| `post_fx.skip_motion_blur`, `skip_motion_blur_recon`, `skip_dof` | off | motion blur skipped | the motion blur and the depth of field | never run in VR, whatever the switches say (below) |
| `vr.native_resolution`, `vr.resolution_scale` | on, 1.0 | on, left as configured | the per-eye render size | the double-wide target requires native resolution |
| `director.clean_view.scope` | the desktop overlay only | every overlay | the mod's own overlays | composited outside the eye targets the comparison reads |
| `director.paused`, `vr.freeze_mode` | running, off | held, full camera | the clock and the camera | what makes the four modes comparable at all |

Foveation. Run as for one eye, the mask and fill-in would put one fovea on the seam, sized for the double-wide target, so each eye would drop pixels at its own centre of view; see "Under single-pass stereo" in [foveation.md](foveation.md) for `single_pass.foveation_per_eye`.

FSR. Run once over the double-wide target, FSR would scale the motion vectors, which the velocity pass writes per eye, to the double-wide width, reproject one history across the seam, and give both eyes the correction of a centred camera whose eye and centre matrices agree, adding no parallax where an eye rendered on its own adds its eye's. `single_pass.fsr_per_eye` resolves each half through its own eye's context; see "Under single-pass stereo" in [fsr.md](../rendering/fsr.md). FSR is not deterministic from capture to capture, so its noise floor is its own self-repeat.

Exposure. Under the collapse the histogram is metered once per frame over both eyes, so both eyes get one exposure, as both eyes of multipass get eye 0's. An isolated eye of an asymmetric rig sees different content from the pair, so its exposure differs, and comparisons under the rigs with the metering on differ for that reason alone. The first capture after a large change of scene has not converged in thirty warm-up frames, which is one reason the first schedule after a teleport is discarded.

The screen fade and the player damage both run once over the double-wide target. The `playerdamageeffect` pixel shader reads the scene at the pixel's own screen UV and has no other screen-space input, so it is the same per pixel as in an eye-sized target, and the fade is a colour multiply. Neither draws in a harness scene, so neither is measured; whether `PlayerDamageEffect::Apply` draws anything other than that pass is unverified.

### The motion blur and the depth of field

Neither runs while a VR session runs, whatever the `post_fx` switches say: both smear the view with the camera's motion and focus, which in the headset are the player's own head and gaze. The payload zeroes the camera contexts' motion and radial blur factors on the head-tracked path (`clear_blurs` in `jc3vr_view::hooks::camera`), and the post chain's hooks (`jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::post::post_effects`) skip [`MotionBlurEffect::Apply`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::MotionBlurEffect##func-Apply) and answer [`PostEffectsManager::IsBokehActive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::PostEffectsManager##func-IsBokehActive) with false, which drops the depth of field's own passes (the downscale-and-focus prepass, the bokeh blur, and the depth-of-field composite family) and leaves the plain blur, which the glare reads, and the plain composite. [`DepthOfFieldEffect::Apply`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::post_effects::DepthOfFieldEffect##func-Apply) itself always runs in VR, since its composite is also the HDR-to-LDR tonemap; `post_fx.skip_dof`, which skips it whole, is ignored in VR. The motion blur's `Apply` also carries the heat-haze distortion and, with the engine's SMAA T2x mode, a temporal composite; neither runs in VR either (FSR replaces the engine's anti-aliasing, and multipass drops T2x to SMAA 1x). The debug UI locks the three switches while VR renders; outside VR they keep their bisection roles. Forcing them off saves 0.10 to 0.17 ms of GPU time a frame, all of it the motion blur's pass.
