# The two-handed inventory

The local player's two hands are independent. Each holds its own weapon or object, items pass between the hands, weapons dock on the body's sockets and are drawn from them, and the player carries what the body holds rather than what the game's slots allow. Boneworks and Half-Life: Alyx are the guiding light: the player does everything with their own body, even where that adds friction, and nothing docks, draws, or switches by itself. The [grapple snatch](grapple-snatch.md) is built on top of it.

This is the plan of record and the description of what is built. Phases 1 to 3 are built ([Phases](#phases)): the loadout table is the only writer of where the player's items are ([The live table](#the-live-table)), the game's slots follow it ([The slots as a projection](#the-slots-as-a-projection)), every grab at the body is decided against it ([The grabs against the table](#the-grabs-against-the-table)), every gun in a hand fires from its own hand ([The per-hand tick](#the-per-hand-tick)) and is weighed, sighted, braced, and stopped at walls as its own ([The gun in either hand](#the-gun-in-either-hand)), two pistols are a pistol in each hand ([The pistols as plain items](#the-pistols-as-plain-items)), and the hands' props and explosives are the table's too ([Props and explosives in the table](#props-and-explosives-in-the-table)). Phase 4 is built too: every take and give lands in the table and a hand without the game's equip ([The takes](#the-takes)). So is phase 5: traversal leaves the hands as they are ([Traversal](#traversal)), the heavy weapons dock on the back ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)), the table snapshots and restores itself at every cache and reset ([The caches and the resets](#the-caches-and-the-resets)), a lost weapon comes back ([Letting go](#letting-go)), a swimming pickup keeps the slot's weapon ([The takes](#the-takes)), and a scheme switch keeps the weapons beyond the slots ([The architecture](#the-architecture)). Phase 6 is built as far as the snatch's own first phase: weapons on the ground and in characters' hands snatched into the grabbing hand through the table ([grapple-snatch.md](grapple-snatch.md)). Phase 7 is not built yet: the per-hand finger poses. In the design sections below, each part that waits on them says so. None of it has been run in the headset. It acts while the bindings inject, under the controller scheme with a controller active.

## Why the game's inventory is replaced

The game wields exactly one weapon by construction ([engine: weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md), [engine: item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md)). Its eight slots are a fixed count, `Character::GetWieldingWeapon` returns the selected slot's weapon, and nearly every consumer reads that one weapon: the fire tick, the reload paths, the track messages, the animation graph, aim, the HUD, the AI's threat rating, the camera, the vehicles' and traversal's holstering, the saves, and the missions. Workarounds that keep that model (a second weapon fired beside the wielded one, a carry set beside the slots, pistols split from their pair, takes that reshuffle the slots after the game's own pickup) each meet every other: a take with the free hand goes through the game's pickup, whose equip takes the other hand's weapon away, and a weapon beside the wielded one lacks what the wielded one has.

So the mod owns weapon handling for the local player. The game keeps three jobs: owning the weapon objects (their spawn, despawn, and references), simulating the weapons themselves (projectiles, effects, damage), and holding the ammunition reserves. Everything else that reads the wielded weapon is replaced by the mod, patched to work per weapon, or fed with the primary hand's weapon.

## The model

### The loadout table

One table holds every item the player has: each is in exactly one place, the left hand, the right hand, a body socket, or the world (kept near the player). Items are weapons, the props a hand picks up, and a grenade or a C4 charge while a hand holds one. Both hands are the same type: a gun in either hand has its own hand's trigger, aim, recoil, reload, and pips, and a heavy weapon held in one hand is simply very heavy and lifts only so far. The weight, the recoil, the sights, the scope, the wall collision, the regions, the foregrip, and the handover act on each hand's gun alike ([The gun in either hand](#the-gun-in-either-hand)).

The primary hand is the dominant hand when it holds a weapon, otherwise the other hand. The primary follows from the hands and is never set on its own.

The grapple hand is a hand like the other: it holds anything the other can. The grapple is not an item: it stays a device on the grapple hand's wrist, worked by its button whatever the hand holds. A hand that holds something is not pinned to the line while hanging from it, and the grapple hand's trigger retracts the tethers only while its held item, if any, does not use the trigger. The anchored tier frees a grapple hand that holds anything the table has in it, or a gun of the wielded weapon (`hands::tiers`); a gun in the grapple hand moves the retract to its stick click, and a grenade there holds its trigger only for a press that pulls its pin ([physical-weapons.md, "Grenades as objects"](physical-weapons.md#grenades-as-objects)). A prop or a charge leaves the trigger to the tethers.

### The game's slots are a projection

The game's slots are made to match the table, never the other way round:

- the primary hand's weapon is in its slot, selected, and wielded, for the consumers the mod feeds rather than patches;
- before the game stores its save, and before a mission, a checkpoint, or a challenge caches the player's weapons, the slots are rewritten to a sensible loadout of what the table holds, and the mod's sidecar records the rest ([Saves, resets, and loads](#saves-resets-and-loads)). The save stores only the slots and the ammunition reserves, so the projection covers the game's half;
- a heavy weapon is never projected into the heavy slot: a weapon in that slot replaces the body's idle and strafe with the minigun's, the base layer drops it, holstered or not, on grapple fire, grenade throws, falls, the parachute's and the wingsuit's opening, vehicle entry, and swimming, and its unequip drops it. Built ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)).

The table takes a snapshot of its own at each mission checkpoint, since the mission manager caches the slots at every checkpoint and a death, failure, or checkpoint restart restores that cache: a slot of another weapon type is replaced (the old object despawned), a slot the cache had empty is emptied, and the occupied slots' ammunition reserves are zeroed first. Weapons outside the slots are kept by a restore, never removed or duplicated, but share reserves with the slots. Built ([The caches and the resets](#the-caches-and-the-resets)).

Every spawn reset (death and checkpoint restarts, fast travel, a challenge's start, retry, or abort, a mission's start, loading a save, the frontend) replaces every slot weapon with a new object of the same type, keeping the magazines and re-equipping the selected slot. The table re-keys its slot weapons across a reset, and the weapons outside the slots come back on their sockets ([Saves, resets, and loads](#saves-resets-and-loads)).

The game's word on ownership is final. A mission or challenge that removes the player's weapons removes them from the table, the hands' included; a weapon the game gives goes to an empty hand if one is free, otherwise to a socket that fits it. On death and respawn the game's rules decide what the player owns, and the hands come back empty with everything else on its socket. Built for the gives outside a reset and a mission's hold ([The takes](#the-takes)); a give the reset owns is bound by the reset's completion, or by the checkpoint's restore that follows it ([The caches and the resets](#the-caches-and-the-resets)).

### No automatic moves

Nothing moves an item but the player. Not built yet, unless marked built:

- Every take (from the ground, a rack or locker, a character's hands, the game's use-item pickup, a mission's give, the grapple snatch) lands in the table and then in the grabbing hand. The game's equip never runs, so a take never touches the other hand. Built ([The takes](#the-takes)); the snatch for weapons on the ground and in characters' hands.
- Entering a vehicle, gliding, opening the parachute, falling, and swimming leave the hands as they are. The base layer's state tasks that holster or hide the wielded weapon (on vehicle entry, the wingsuit, free fall, diving, the ragdoll, and death), stop its use, or drop a heavy weapon are patched out for the local player, and the neutral state the game forces on every teleport, revive, and cutscene setup, which unequips the wielded weapon, is undone by the reconcile. A hand holding a gun cannot take the steering wheel or a control, so the player drives one-handed or docks first; a gun in a hand in a seat fires, since the mod ticks it rather than the game's wielded tick, which skips non-motorcycle seats. Built ([Traversal](#traversal)). A weapon the game still puts away on its own (its holster act, the weapon wheel, a mounted gun's use) leaves its hand in the table, docked or let go of.
- The game's weapon cycling, the slot selects, the weapon wheel, and the automatic switch away from an empty weapon become requests on the table or go away. Built: the sockets never select a slot, and the automatic switch is off by default; the wheel and the cycling are still the game's, and the weapon they draw lands in a free hand.
- Ammunition pickups credit the reserve for any weapon in the table: the auto-pickup's weapon check (`Inventory::HasWeaponWithAmmoType`) is patched to see the table, and its only caller credits the ammunition itself. Built ([The takes](#the-takes)).
- The game's own automatic moves are routed through the table or refused: the combat mental state's draw, the relaxed state's holster, the drop of an empty power-core weapon or a spent single-use launcher, and the store's reselection on close. Built: the mental state's draw and holster are cut by the aim-state pin's mental-state cuts.

A heavy weapon does not restrict anything yet: the player can grapple, throw, glide, and swim while holding one. Making the player-initiated actions refuse while a heavy weapon is held, as its weight would suggest, is left for later.

### Letting go

A weapon a hand lets go of stays where it falls, protected from the despawn while the player is near ([The weapons in the world](#the-weapons-in-the-world)). It returns to a free socket that fits it only when it is lost rather than dropped:

- out of the world: it fell through the terrain or below the world's floor, or sank out of reach in water;
- left behind at speed: it left the hand while the player travelled fast (the wingsuit, a vehicle, the parachute, or a fall), so the player never chose to leave it there.

Swapping a weapon on the ground for another, or walking away from one, never brings it back. Retrieving a weapon dropped at an ordinary pace is the grapple snatch's job.

As built (`weapons::inventory::lost`, `weapons.inventory.return_lost`, on): each input tick measures the player's speed from the change of their position, smoothed over a quarter second, and the player travels fast from `lost_speed_mps` (10 m/s, above a run and below any vehicle, glide, or fall worth the name); a hand's let-go while they do is recorded at speed (`live::set_fast`). A weapon lying in the world is out of the world once it is more than `lost_depth_m` (10 m) below the sea's level, which only sinking out of reach or falling through the terrain takes it to, and a weapon let go of at speed is left behind once it is farther than `left_behind_m` (30 m) from the player, a distance rather than a time, since the speed that left it makes the distance come quickly and a weapon dropped from a slow glide stays within reach longer. Each lost weapon is told to the table (`loadout: … moved from the world (let go of at speed) to the outer back: left behind at speed`), which returns it to the first free socket that fits it, and the carry set takes it there; with none free the table has it at the player's feet, and the carry set holds it on the body for one tick and lets it fall from there at the next (`inventory: brought a lost weapon back to …`). The judgement runs before the pins, so a weapon is returned before it is far enough to be forgotten. The thresholds are in the debug UI's Controllers tab, under "VR inventory", beside the readout of the speed and the returns. Off, a lost weapon stays where it is, pinned while near, and is forgotten once far; a let-go is then always at an ordinary pace.

Decisions taken here:

- The world's floor is the sea's level less a margin rather than a probe of the terrain under the weapon: no terrain query is bound, and a weapon that falls through the terrain keeps falling until it passes any margin.
- A weapon returned to the feet is not lost again for being left behind: its departure is the return's, not the let-go at speed.

## Sockets

The sockets are on Rico's skeleton ([body/rig.md](../body/rig.md#the-bodys-volumes-on-ricos-skeleton)): the two hips, the two shoulders, a third place across the back, and the chest ([The places and their invariants](#the-places-and-their-invariants)). The pouches are beside them. Each socket takes its size class or smaller, mirrored for handedness. A held weapon shows before the hand opens whether the socket under it fits, by a red marker on each socket that does not, and a dock, by a grab or by opening the grip over the socket, confirms with the grab cue. Props have no socket and no backpack: letting go of one drops it. Grenades and C4 stay consumables drawn from their pouches. A heavy weapon docks on the back's three places like a long gun ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)).

## Ammunition and reload

One reserve per ammunition type, shared by both hands: two pistols of one calibre draw on one stock. The universal belt pouch hands out what the gun in the other hand takes ([reload.md](reload.md)). Every reload is the mod's true reload, per weapon: each gun's magazine, chamber, and action are its own. Each gun's own ammunition check asks the character to reload the wielded weapon whenever its magazine runs low, and a gun that does not reload by magazine (the pump shotgun, the timed reloads) cancels the character's reload every tick; both requests are refused for the local player, so one hand's gun never reloads or interrupts the other's ([The per-hand tick](#the-per-hand-tick)). Walking over a weapon lying in the world credits its stored rounds for any weapon the table has on the body, not only the slots' ([The takes](#the-takes)). Not built yet: the game's own reload paths stay open for the weapons the true reload leaves to the game.

## The HUD

Each hand shows its own weapon's state; the per-hand ammunition pips do this for a gun beside the wielded one, and a fuller per-hand HUD is the goal. Until then, the game's ammunition counter shows the primary's.

## Firing

Each hand's gun has the player as its user and its holder, and the mod ticks its update (both the parallel and the serial halves) with the hand's own inputs and aim point ([The per-hand tick](#the-per-hand-tick)). The fire path keys everything on the weapon's user rather than its state or the selected slot: the heat, the gunshot event the AI hears, the bullets' owner and the player's bullet flag, the muzzle flash and sound, the recoil, the spread, the rumble, and the camera shake all follow. A gun with no user does not update at all, and the holder gives the bullets the bodies they ignore, the player's own and the vehicle's. The shooting permissions gate only the inputs the game's own fire tick passes down, so the mod checks them for its own inputs.

Each gun keeps its own aim mode, which the mod drives per hand: the game's aim-mode setter reaches only the selected weapon. Starting to use a gun drops the player out of precision aim when that gun has none, and the camera hints are global and keyed by type, so two guns of one type would remove each other's hint when one stops being used: the mod owns the hints.

## Animation

The arms are the mod's IK. Moving the primary takes no act: the old weapon is unequipped and the new one equipped (`WeaponBase::OnUnequip`, then `OnEquip`), which is what the draw clip's own equip message does; a draw, holster, or switch clip still playing is aborted first, since its messages equip and unequip by slot when they fire. The mod's draws and docks play no act. Not built yet: the game's own acts (a reload of a weapon the true reload leaves to the game, the wheel's draw and switch) still play.

The body never takes the combat stance. With a weapon wielded, the animation graph's base layer switches the locomotion, the walk starts, the turns, and the stunt turns to their combat families, whose default clips are the rifle's whatever the weapon and with no modifier set. The weapon-wielded condition reads false for the local player's base layer, so the body keeps the relaxed families whatever the hands hold, and the procedural body and the rig own the stance ([body/procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md), [body/rig.md](../body/rig.md)). The weapon's class reaches the graph only through the character modifiers, which the mod already strips. No weapon condition guards a vault, a climb, the parachute, the wingsuit, swimming, or vehicle entry.

The game has no per-hand hold layer: its hand poses are per weapon class, one clip covering both hands' fingers, with right and left versions for rifles and one for pistols. A hand's fingers can take its weapon's class pose by sampling that clip once with the engine's own loader and blender, caching it, and copying that hand's finger bones at the pose seam each tick. Not built yet: that is phase 7's polish, to judge in the headset, beside the grip poses that cover the fingers today ([grip-poses.md](grip-poses.md)).

## The architecture

The table is the one source of truth for the player's items, owned by one module, and nothing else holds any of that state.

- The core is pure: the table, its places, and its transitions, with its invariants in the types, tested without the game ([The core as built](#the-core-as-built)).
- The hooks are thin adapters. A detour decides nothing: it turns what the game wanted (a pickup, a give, a removal, a save about to be stored, a mission cache) into an event for the table, or reads the table's published snapshot. One reconcile step per tick makes the game match the table: the slot projection, each weapon's state and user, its placement and visibility, and its tick.
- The readouts are one: a Status row and a debug UI section show the whole table, and the log records every transition with its cause, so a bug is either the table being wrong or the reconcile not applying it.

What each hand does to the object it holds (the armed weapon's bones and user, the slide between a socket and the hand, the grip watch, the pickup's joint, the throw's stage) is per-hand runtime state beside the table, not an item's place.

Switching the control scheme away from the controllers (or turning the mod's weapon handling off) projects the table back into the game's slots, so nothing is lost; switching back rebuilds the table from the slots and the sidecar. Gamepad play is expected to be rough meanwhile. Built: the stop (`inventory::stop`) runs when the scheme becomes the gamepad's, when the bindings or the controllers are switched off, and when `weapons.inventory.enabled` or `project_slots` is switched off; a pause of the controllers (the debug panel up, a frame without controllers, lost tracking) keeps the table. The table is put back into the slots as a store would write them, the hands' armed weapons are disarmed, and the weapons the slots cannot hold stay in the carry set, stashed: hidden, with no grab volume, and recorded by the sidecar at a save (`inventory: the table stopped; the weapons beyond the slots are kept stashed`). When the table runs again it starts from the slots, as a load does, and each kept weapon comes back onto its socket, with any a reset recorded meanwhile respawned (`inventory: the table runs again; the kept weapons are back on the body`). The hands come back empty, since the game's one weapon was wielded in between. With `weapons.inventory.carry` off the stop lets them fall instead.

## The core as built

The code is `payload/src/controllers/loadout/`. It is pure, apart from the object address an item is bound to. The debug UI's Controllers tab has a `Loadout` section whose `Sandbox` header drives a table of its own with made-up objects, showing the items, the primary, the slots in play, the last store's sidecar, and the latest transitions and refusals.

### Items and their identity

An item is a weapon (its spawn tag's hash, its slot, and its size class), a prop, a grenade, or a C4 charge. Its identity is the table's own, assigned when it enters the table and never reused in a session. Beside it the table keeps the object the item is now: a live address, or nothing while it waits for one, either for its slot's new object across a spawn reset or for a spawn of its type after a restore. The address is the one place the core meets the game; the hooks and the reconcile turn it into an object.

### The places and their invariants

There are six sockets: each hip, each shoulder (where the game docks its two-handed slot's weapon, on the right, and its special slot's, on the left, whatever the dominant hand), a third place across the back, and the chest. The size classes are, smallest first, the pistol, the submachine gun, the long gun, and the heavy weapon. The hips and the chest take submachine guns and smaller, and the back's three places take everything, the heavy weapons included. The core can take a body with fewer places on the back and the hips, or no chest socket; the live table has them all. A socket a smaller layout lacks keeps the weapon already on it until the player draws it.

The table's layout carries the invariants. An item is a value that cannot be copied, owned by exactly one container: a hand, a socket, or the world. Each hand and socket holds at most one. A socket's item can only be made through a check that it fits, and only a weapon can lie in the world, so a prop, a grenade, or a charge let go of leaves the table. No two live items share an object, and the primary is never stored: it is the dominant hand when that holds a weapon, otherwise the other hand when it does, otherwise none.

### The transitions

Each transition applies whole and returns its changes (an item entered, moved, left, was bound to a new object, or lost its object), each with its cause for the log, or is refused with a typed error and leaves the table as it was:

- a take into an empty hand, from the ground, a rack, a rack's copy, a locker, a character's hands, a mission's give, the grapple snatch, a pouch, or out of the air; each source brings only its kinds (a pouch a grenade or a charge, a catch a grenade, the ground a weapon or a prop, the rest weapons), and a weapon lying in the world taken again keeps its identity;
- a dock of a hand's weapon on a socket the body has, which is free and fits it; a draw from a socket into an empty hand; a hand-over to an empty other hand;
- a let-go, recorded as at an ordinary pace or at speed;
- a loss of a weapon lying in the world: out of the world whatever its departure, or left behind only when it was let go of at speed. It returns to the first free socket that fits it, or, with none free, to the player's feet, so that the body being full never loses a weapon;
- a game's give, to an empty hand (the dominant first), else a free socket that fits it, else the player's feet;
- a game's removal of one object or one item from wherever it is, or of every weapon on the body, the hands included; the weapons lying in the world stay, since the game takes what the player holds;
- a game's drop of an object the player has, wherever it is: a weapon lies in the world as one the player dropped, and anything else leaves;
- a weapon the mod spawned to stand for one the player has (one of the sidecar's after a load, or the second pistol of a pair split in two), onto its recorded socket when that is free and fits it, otherwise where a weapon goes when the player does not choose;
- a restored item's object spawned.

Where a weapon goes when the player does not choose (a give with the hands full, a loss, a respawn, a restore) is the socket the game docks its slot on, then the snuggest socket that fits it, the dominant side first.

### The spawn reset and the caches

At the reset's start every weapon the play projection puts in a slot waits for its slot's new object; a slot already waited for stays as it is. At its end the weapons in the world leave the table, and each new slot object re-binds the item waiting for that slot with the same type, so it keeps its identity and its place. A new object nothing waits for goes onto the body, and an item whose slot the game emptied or gave another type leaves. A respawn also empties the hands: their weapons go onto the body and anything else leaves. A travel keeps the hands, and a load starts the table again from the slots.

At each of the game's caches (a checkpoint, a mission taking the player's weapons, a challenge) the table snapshots the weapons on the body with their places and the slots the store projection gave them. A checkpoint's restore returns the table to the snapshot: what was gained since leaves, and every item comes back to its socket, all taken out before any goes back so that two that swapped places swap back. A mission's or a challenge's restore brings the snapshot's weapons back beside what the player holds since. Either way the hands come back empty, the restored slots' objects re-bind the snapshot's items by slot and type, and a snapshot's weapon whose object is gone waits to be spawned again.

### The projection

Each weapon slot holds one weapon of that slot from the body: the primary hand's, then the other hand's, then the one on the socket the game docks the slot on, then the other sockets' in order, the oldest first among equals. Only a weapon bound to a live object goes into a slot, and one in the world never does. The primary hand's weapon is selected. The heavy slot stays empty, in play and for a store or a cache alike ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)), and for a store or a cache the sidecar records every weapon on the body with its hand or socket and its slot, or none for one beyond the slots. The consumables' slots stay the game's.

### The events

A hook or the hands send one event per thing that happened: a take, a dock, a draw, a hand-over, a let-go, a loss, a give, a spawn, a removal, a game's drop, a weapon restored onto the body, a store about to happen, a cache, a restore, a reset beginning, and a reset completing with the slots the game now holds. The fold applies each whole or refuses it, and returns the transitions and, for a store or a cache, the slots and the sidecar the game should be given before it goes on.

### The grab at a socket

What a hand does at a socket is decided against the table's snapshot (`loadout::grab`): an empty hand's grab of a socket with an item draws it, unless both hands holding a weapon is off and the other hand holds one; a hand holding a weapon docks it on a free socket the body has that fits it; and a release over such a socket docks there, anywhere else lets go, and for a weapon that cannot lie in the world (the game's pistol pair) docks on the first free socket the body prefers for it, or keeps it with none free. Each refusal is typed: nothing there and nothing to put there, the socket taken, the weapon not fitting, the other hand's weapon.

## The live table

The live table is the one `Loadout` of the local player's items (`loadout::live`), behind one lock, and published with its snapshot at the end of each input tick. It is the only writer of where the items are: the grabs, the takes, the holding subsystems, and the game's own draws and holsters send it events, and the reconcile makes the game's slots, its wielded weapon, and the weapons armed in the hands follow it ([The slots as a projection](#the-slots-as-a-projection)). The table runs while the VR inventory is on, takes the weapons of the slots the inventory tracks (the pistols, the two-handed, the special, and the heavy weapons), and has every socket.

The table says which hand holds what, and the placement follows it ([physical-weapons.md, "Either hand"](physical-weapons.md#either-hand)). It publishes each hand's weapon as one object address per hand, which the placement detours compare against before any lock, what each hand holds of any kind, and the place of every item on the body, each behind a leaf lock of its own (`loadout::live::published`), after every fold that can move an item: at the end of the input tick, and at once for the moves the hands make between ticks. Every gun in a hand rides its hand's attach bones, and the carry set places each weapon beyond the slots where the table has it: on its socket's bone and offset, or in a hand. A weapon the game wields lands in a hand of the table's (`loadout::live::expect`): the hand a take left expecting it (a pickup, a take from a character or a rack, for up to three seconds), or for the game's own draw the dominant hand when it is empty, otherwise the other. The expectation is per-hand runtime state beside the table.

What feeds it:

- On its first input tick the table starts from the slots, as a load does.
- The hand moves. The calls that move an item send the move at once (a draw off a socket, a dock onto one, a pickup or take the mod makes, a handover, a let-go), and the reconcile follows. The input tick sees the game's own moves: a weapon the table has in a hand that the game no longer wields and that is neither armed there nor kept by the carry set (the game holstered or dropped it) docks on the socket the game docks its slot on, or is let go of when it left the slots; then the wielded weapon lands in a hand when the table has it in none: drawn off its socket, taken off the ground, or, for a weapon the table does not know, taken from where the expecting hand's take says it came from (a pickup, a character, a rack, or a give).
- The props, grenades, and charges the holding subsystems have in the hands ([Props and explosives in the table](#props-and-explosives-in-the-table)).
- The weapons the game no longer keeps: each tick, every weapon of the table's that is in no slot, the carry set, or a hand of the game's is asked what became of it ([The weapons in the world](#the-weapons-in-the-world)). One lying in the world that the table still has on the body is a game's drop, and one another character holds or whose object is gone is a removal. A weapon the pins left to the game is removed as forgotten.
- The spawn reset: its pre-reset event begins the reset and removes the carry set's weapons, which the inventory releases and the reset then removes; the first input tick after its post-reset event completes it with the slots it left, once no tracked slot's spawn is pending (or ten seconds have passed). The reset is a respawn when the game respawned the player before it, a load when the game loaded the player's save data or the frontend reset since, and a travel otherwise ([The caches and the resets](#the-caches-and-the-resets)). A load of the save data also completes a load on its own, so a load that does not reset first still starts the table from its slots.
- The store of the save data is a store; the table's projection is kept for the readouts.
- The missions' and the challenges' caches, their removals, and their give-backs, and the mission manager's checkpoint snapshot and its replay ([The caches and the resets](#the-caches-and-the-resets)). The inventory stashes the carried weapons while a mission or a challenge holds them and keeps their objects, so each item a give-back leaves waiting for a spawn is bound to a kept weapon of its type.
- A sidecar weapon the restore respawns onto a socket, and the second pistol of a pair split in two, are restored onto their sockets.

The readouts are a Status row (`LOADOUT TABLE`: where the items are, and how many events were refused) and the debug UI's `Loadout` section (the table, the last store, and the latest transitions and refusals). The table's transitions are the log: each change at info level, with the item's number, its kind and spawn tag hash, and the cause, such as `loadout: #3 (a long gun of the two-handed slot (0x…)) moved from the right hand to the world (dropped): let go of`.

Decisions taken while wiring it:

- The hand moves are sent from the call that makes them where the call knows the move, and otherwise from the input tick's view of the hands. The table leads the hands: a weapon the game wields lands in a hand of the table's rather than the table following a hand chosen elsewhere, so the placement has one source. A move the table already shows sends nothing, and a hand the table disagrees about is left as the table has it.
- A weapon the game wields lands only in a hand the table has empty; with both hands full it is put away again.
- The hold needs the table: with the VR inventory off, the game's own layout stands, as with `weapons.hold.either_hand` off.
- A heavy weapon is the table's like any other, kept beyond the slots ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)).
- The game's drop is a core event of its own: the game drops weapons the player has without any hand letting go (the carry set switched off, the game's own drops), and its word on ownership is final, so the weapon lies in the world, where the pins keep it.
- A removal of every weapon takes the weapons on the body and leaves those lying in the world: a mission takes what the player holds, not what lies near them.
- A weapon the game gives outside a reset and a mission's hold is placed by the table at the give's seam ([The takes](#the-takes)). One the reset owns, or one given while a mission holds the player's weapons, is unknown to the table until a hand draws it or the reset's completion or the give-back binds it, and is taken then as a give; one the game then drops is not in the table's world.
- A weapon another character takes leaves the table, as the core decided, and is never the player's again until a hand takes it back ([Other characters and missions](#other-characters-and-missions)).

## The per-hand tick

Every gun in a hand fires from that hand's own trigger and aims at that hand's own point (`weapons::hold::tick`). The fire path keys on the weapon's user, so a gun the game does not wield fires as the player's from the holstered state.

- A weapon the table holds in a hand that the game does not wield is armed there (`weapons::hold::armed`): its guns' holstered attach-bone entries name the hand's bone, its holstered scale is one, and the player uses it ([`WeaponBase::StartUsing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-StartUsing)); disarming puts the entries and the scale back and stops using it. Its holder stays the player, so its bullets ignore the player's own bodies. This is per-hand runtime state beside the table, keyed by the hand: the table says what the hand holds, and the armed state what the mod did to the object to hold it there. The rigid grip, the gun aim, the per-shot seam, the sockets' volumes, the finger poses, and the render-time late update treat an armed weapon as held ([physical-weapons.md, "A weapon in each hand"](physical-weapons.md#a-weapon-in-each-hand)).
- After the inventory's own weapon updates, the mod updates each armed weapon through its parallel and serial update virtuals with its hand's fire input and aim point, the input given only while the shooting permission allows it and the true reload does not hold the gun.
- The wielded weapon is still updated by the inventory's fan-out, but with its hand's fire input in place of the game's own: the gun aim's per-weapon detour hands it over, so both hands fire the same way and the fire action reaches no gun. The game's pistol pair, kept whole while the pistols are not made separate, fires from the game's own fire input, which alternates its guns.
- Each gun's ammunition check asks the character to reload the wielded weapon when its own magazine runs low, and a gun that does not reload by magazine cancels the character's reload every tick. Inside the local player's checks both requests are refused (`weapons::empty`, on the detours of [`Character::StartReloadingWieldingWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-StartReloadingWieldingWeapon) and [`Character::CancelReload`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-CancelReload)).
- Each armed gun takes the wielded weapon's aim mode, which is the player's, since the game forwards the player's mode to the selected weapon alone. With the controller scheme refusing precision aim, that is normal aim.
- A weapon's use hint is keyed by hash in the camera's hint table, added at its start of use only when absent and removed at its stop only when present ([`GameCameraManager::HasHint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::game_camera_manager::GameCameraManager##func-HasHint)), so two guns of one type share one reference and the first to stop takes it away. A stop of a local player's weapon is noted, and the next input tick puts the hint back while a gun in a hand still in use carries it.
- The true reload acts on each hand's gun at once ([reload.md](reload.md#each-hands-gun)): its chamber, its action, its belt push, and its magazine are its own, and its record is keyed by the table's item, so a weapon keeps its chamber across the spawn reset's re-key and a handover.

The per-hand tick is behind `weapons.hold.per_hand_tick` (on), in the debug UI's Controllers tab. Off, the wielded weapon fires from the game's own fire input, an armed gun fires only while its magazine holds a round (its ammunition check would otherwise reload the wielded weapon), and nothing is refused, set, or put back; the arming and the per-hand reload stay, since they are not the tick's.

Decisions taken here:

- The game's fan-out keeps updating the wielded weapon, and only its input is the hand's: updating it a second time from the mod would advance its timers twice a frame, and the fan-out's own choice of what to update (the mounted gun, the boost wingsuit's guns, the grenade) stays the game's. A seat that skips the wielded weapon's update still skips it; that is phase 5's.
- The requests are refused only inside the ammunition check, so the reload input's own reload start still goes through to the game. With the true reload off, an empty gun no longer reloads on its own; its reload input does.
- The armed guns' aim mode follows the wielded weapon's rather than reading the player's, which the bindings do not reach from the character; the two are the same whenever a weapon is wielded, and normal aim otherwise.

## The gun in either hand

The subsystems that act on a gun in a hand key it by the hand, not by the game's wielded weapon. Each input tick reads the gun in each hand (`weapons::engine::in_hands`): the wielded weapon in the hand its first gun rides, and a weapon armed in a hand in its own. A long gun armed in a hand is a long gun by its slot, since it stays in the holstered state, and each hand's long gun is measured on its own wrist, its geometry kept per hand.

- The weight (`weapons::heft`): each hand's gun has its own weight, spring, and freeze, so a rifle armed in the off-hand beside a pistol sags, trails, and sways as it would alone, and kicks and spreads as a rifle held in one hand. A round carried to the reload freezes the gun in the other hand, and a hand closing on a foregrip freezes the gun it closes on.
- The recoil (`weapons::recoil`): each hand has its own spring, kicked by the shots of the gun in that hand, and the one-handed kick scale is that hand's gun's weight.
- The wall collision (`weapons::collide`): each hand's long gun has its own probe, cast each aim update, its own wall, and its own push, so two long guns are stopped at a wall each by what it points into.
- The sights (`weapons::ads`): each hand's long gun is solved for the eye, and the one raised to it is lined up (`ads::raised`): the gun whose sights or scope's weld take the most hold of the eye, then whose stock takes the most hold of the shoulder, then whose sight line passes nearest the eye. A gun whose pose takes no hold is not raised. The aiming eye is the raised gun's hand's side by default.
- The scope (`weapons::scope`): each hand's scoped gun is identified, its sight line anchored to its own wrist, and its lens placed, and the lens drawn is the scope raised to the eye (`scope::chosen`): a scope an eye is at, then one placed with a sight line, then the eyepiece nearest an eye.
- The regions (`weapons::regions`): each hand has its own carry and its own draw waiting for its gun, so a long gun drawn by its barrel or picked up by its stock is carried in its hand, unable to fire, whether the game wields it or it is armed beside a pistol in the other hand. Each hand's finger pose takes the part it carries its gun by, and its trigger fires nothing while it does. A carry ends when its hand holds another gun or none.
- The foregrip, a touch along the gun, and the handover (`weapons::foregrip`, `weapons::hold::hand_over`): these take the other hand, so they act on the gun held in one hand while the other is free (`weapons::engine::lone_gun`), wielded or armed: the handover on any such gun, and the foregrip, the touches, the slides, and the grip taken from a carrying hand on a long gun. With a gun in each hand neither hand is free, so there is one such gun at most, and one foregrip hold, keyed by its holding hand and its weapon.

Decisions taken here:

- The sights and the scope are single (one aiming eye, one lens), so they act on one gun at a time, picked each frame by how far it is raised to the eye rather than by which gun the game wields: the hands are equal, and the gun at the eye is the one the player is looking down. The pick keeps no state of its own: each gun's hold fades in as it comes to the eye, so the pick passes from one gun to the other as the second rises past the first.
- The foregrip and the handover pick their gun by which hand is free rather than by which gun the game wields. A gun armed in a hand while the other is free is the reconcile's passing state (the primary is the only hand holding a weapon, and the next reconcile wields its weapon), so in play the lone gun is nearly always the wielded one; keying by the free hand makes the rule hold through that state rather than depend on it.
- The per-hand state is keyed by the hand rather than by the item. A gun's weight and its recoil are of the hand holding it: a gun handed over or drawn again starts at rest in its new hand, and a hand that takes another gun drops the last one's spring.

## The slots as a projection

The game's slots are made to match the table each input tick (`weapons::inventory::reconcile`), after the table's feed, and at once after the hands move a weapon:

- Each slot the inventory tracks (the pistols, the two-handed, and the special weapons) holds the weapon the play projection puts there. A weapon that leaves its slot stays alive in the carry set ([The weapons beyond the slots](#the-weapons-beyond-the-slots)) and goes out through [`Inventory::RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RemoveWeapon), unequipped first when it is wielded; one that comes in goes in through [`Inventory::AddWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-AddWeapon) without an equip. A weapon in a slot that the table does not have on the body (a mission's give before a hand takes it) is left there.
- The primary hand's weapon is wielded: moving the primary unequips the old weapon and equips the new one, with no act, after aborting a draw, holster, or switch clip still playing ([`EventIdSymbolTable::ACT_ABORT_WEAPON_PARTIAL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable)), and only while the inventory's permissions allow a draw or a holster. With no weapon in the hands, a weapon of the table's the game wields is unequipped. A weapon the game wields that the table does not have on the body (a grenade, the planted explosive, a mounted gun) is left to the game.
- Every other weapon a hand holds is armed there, and a hand's armed weapon that left it, or that the game now wields, is disarmed. When the primary moves to a hand whose weapon is armed, that weapon is disarmed and wielded in the same hand.
- A slot's weapon on a socket other than the one the game docks its slot on is placed there by the mod: its holstered attach-bone entries name the socket's bone at the hold's write points, and the socket's offset moves it after the inventory's placement.
- Before the game stores its save, and before a mission caches the slots, the tracked slots are given the store projection's content. In play they already hold it, since the two projections are the same; this catches a move the hands made since the last pass.
- Before the game's own pickup puts a weapon into a taken slot, the slot's weapon leaves the slot and stays on its socket.

The projection is behind `weapons.inventory.project_slots` (on), in the debug UI's Controllers tab. Off, the table stops as for the scheme switch ([The architecture](#the-architecture)): it is put back into the game's slots as a store would write them, the hands' armed weapons are disarmed, the game's own one-weapon model runs, the carried weapons left over are kept stashed until the table runs again, and the hold keeps the game's layout.

Decisions taken here:

- The heavy slot is emptied of a heavy weapon the table has on the body, as the other tracked slots are of a weapon the projection does not put there ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)).
- The store and cache seams apply the store projection only to the tracked slots, for the same reason.
- A weapon the game holsters or drops on its own (its holster act, a vehicle's entry) leaves its hand in the table at the next input tick, docked or let go of; undoing those is phase 5's. A weapon armed in a hand, or one the carry set keeps off the slots, is never taken for such a departure.
- A weapon the game wields while both hands of the table are full is unequipped again, since it can land in no hand.
- Switching the projection off is the scheme switch rather than a mode where the table moves the hands and the game keeps its slots: the table's moves need the reconcile to happen in the game at all.
- The weapons beyond the slots are kept stashed across a stop rather than dropped or respawned from the sidecar: their objects stay the player's, so switching back neither duplicates them nor depends on a save.

## The grabs against the table

Every grab at the body is one decision against the table's snapshot ([The grab at a socket](#the-grab-at-a-socket)):

- An empty hand's grab of a socket that holds a weapon draws it: the table takes the draw at once, the reconcile wields the weapon when the hand is the primary and arms it there otherwise, and the gun slides off the socket into the hand. Its pose on the socket's bone is measured at the draw and kept per weapon and socket.
- A hand holding a weapon docks it on a free socket that fits it, by opening its grip over the socket (the grip model's release rule, [grip.md](grip.md), decided against the table) or by a grab there. The gun slides from the hand onto the socket when its pose there is known, and the table takes the dock when the slide ends; a wielded weapon is unequipped then, and the other hand's weapon becomes the wielded one when that hand is the primary now.
- Opening the grip anywhere else lets the weapon go into the world with the hand's motion, whichever hand holds it. A long gun the other hand braces stays in that hand, carried where it holds it.
- Each hand has its own slide and its own grip watch, so both hands draw, dock, and let go at once.

The sockets' volumes come from the snapshot (`weapons::docks`; [physical-weapons.md, "The sockets"](physical-weapons.md#the-sockets)): a socket holding a weapon has a volume on the weapon's grip, for the hands that may draw it; a free socket that takes a held weapon has one where that weapon would sit (where its grip was on the socket's bone when last seen there, or the bone moved by the socket's offset), for the hand holding it; and a socket that does not take the held weapon, being taken or too small, is marked for that hand in the refusal colour and never taken, so the hand knows before it opens. Each socket's volume has its kind's reach (the hips', the shoulders', or the chest's, the outer back taking the shoulders'). Nothing selects a slot, clicks the slot-select actions, or asks the game to holster: the table takes the move and the reconcile does the rest.

The switches: `weapons.holsters.enabled` off places no socket volumes, so nothing is drawn or docked at the body and a released gun falls; `weapons.holsters.follow_docks` off reaches the hips, the shoulders, and the chest at the body's landmarks rather than where their weapons are seen; `weapons.hold.second_weapon` off lets a hand draw only while the other holds no weapon; `weapons.hold.grip_to_hold` off keeps a gun in the hand until a grab of a socket docks it. With the table not running (the VR inventory or `weapons.inventory.project_slots` off), no socket volumes are placed and the game's own selection (the weapon wheel and the stick click's cycling) draws and puts away its one weapon.

Decisions taken here:

- The chest socket takes a submachine gun, as every socket takes its size class; there is no select of the planted explosive at the chest. The C4 pouch gives a charge in the hand.
- A heavy weapon docks on the back like a long gun, since it is never in its slot and so never unequipped ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)).
- The game's pistol pair, which stays one object when the pistols are not made separate, is drawn and docked whole; it cannot lie in the world, so opening the grip on it away from a socket puts it on the first free socket the body prefers for it, and with none free it stays in the hand.
- A grab of a socket by the hand reaching for a pouch beside it draws what is there, whatever the other hand holds: the hands are equal, and the nearest grab point decides between the socket and the pouch.
- A draw refused by the animation ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsDrawing`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsDrawing)) and a dock of the wielded weapon refused by it ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsHolstering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsHolstering)) do nothing, rather than leave the table and the game disagreeing until the next tick.
- With the table not running, a grip release is not watched: guns stay in the hand until the game puts them away, since there is no socket to dock on.
- The hand holding a weapon the game does not wield declares a gun grip for its fingers, as the wielded weapon's hand does.

## The pistols as plain items

A pistol is an item like any other weapon. A pair the game hands over is split into two single pistols (`weapons::dual_wield::single`; [physical-weapons.md, "Separate pistols"](physical-weapons.md#separate-pistols)): the slot keeps one, and the twin is spawned and restored onto the hip the slot's pistol does not take, or the socket the table prefers when that hip is taken. Two pistols are a pistol in each hand, each its own item, drawn, docked, held, fired by its own hand's per-hand tick, and reloaded on its own, each on its own hip.

The game docks the pistol slot's first gun on the right hip, so a single pistol in the slot is the game's to place on the right hip alone; on the left hip the mod places it, as it does any weapon on a socket other than its slot's own. A pistol on a hip sits in the hip's holster, with no offset, unless the slot holds the game's pair, whose two pistols take both holsters, and a carried weapon on a hip then sits beside it by the hip's offset.

The single pistols are behind `weapons.separate_pistols` (on). Off, a pair stays the game's: one item with a gun in each hand (`weapons::hold::layout`), drawn and docked whole, fired by the game's own fire input from either trigger, which alternates its guns, and reloaded as one weapon of one magazine.

Decisions taken here:

- A pair split while it is in a hand, its second gun in the other, keeps a pistol in each hand: the slot's pistol stays in the table's hand, and the twin comes into the other hand at once when that hand is empty and a weapon in each hand is on (`weapons.hold.second_weapon`), otherwise onto its hip.
- The sidecar records each carried weapon on the socket the table has it on, rather than the one it came into the carry set on.
- A tap of the reload button ejects the dominant hand's gun, or the other hand's when the dominant holds none; the pair has the one magazine.

## Props and explosives in the table

Everything a hand holds is in the table. Each input tick the table is fed what the holding subsystems have in each hand (`loadout::live::HeldOthers`): the loose object the physical hands' pickup carries, the throw's primed grenade or a caught one, and the C4 throw's primed charge. A hand that took one takes it (from the world, the pouch, or out of the air), and a hand whose prop is let go of, lost, or torn away, whose grenade or charge was thrown or pressed on, lets it go, which takes it out of the table; a grenade that went off in the hand, or one the game took back, is removed as gone.

What a hand holds is then one condition, read from the table (`loadout::live::held_kind`): a hand holding a prop, a grenade, or a charge draws nothing off a socket, takes nothing from the pouches, takes no gun out of a character's hands or off a rack, is not offered the other hand's gun, and is not pinned to the grapple line; a hand holding a weapon, a grenade, or a charge has no palm, and one carrying a prop keeps the palm it holds the prop with, which neither probes nor pushes. The physical hands, the throws, and the catch keep their own state of how they hold it (the pickup's joint or carry, the stage, the pin), as per-hand runtime state beside the table.

The props, the grenades, and the charges are behind their own switches (`physical_hands.pickup.enabled`, `weapons.grenade_throw`, `weapons.grenade.catch.enabled`, `weapons.c4_throw`); off, the hands hold none, and nothing enters the table.

Decisions taken here:

- Only a loose object a hand carries is an item. A hand's grip on something hinged, a vehicle's door, or a character is the world's, held by the physical hands alone.
- A prop both hands hold is the item of the hand that took it first; the other hand's hold is a grip on that item, not an item of its own.
- The table is fed from the holding subsystems' state at each input tick rather than at each of their changes, since the throws' stages move on the weapons' updates on the simulation's threads. A grenade primed and thrown between two input ticks never enters the table.
- A respawn takes the hands' props and explosives out of the table, as the core does; a prop the pickup still holds after it comes back in at the next tick.
- The anchored grapple tier reads the table one tick behind, as it read the wielded weapon, and the anchor's blend absorbs the tick. A hand holding anything stays free rather than holding the line with its item, since putting a gun or a prop on the line would move it without the player.
- Whether a held item uses the grapple hand's trigger is decided per press: a gun always does, and a grenade only for a press that begins while the trigger can pull its pin. The press stays the grenade's until the trigger opens, so the tethers never start retracting under a trigger the player pulled for the pin.

## The takes

Every take of a weapon from the world lands in the table and then in a hand, and the game's equip never runs, so a take never touches the other hand. Where it goes is one decision against the table's snapshot (`loadout::take`): the grabbing hand when it is free, or for the game's use-item pickup, which no hand grabs, a free hand, the dominant first; a hand that holds something takes nothing, and with both hands full the use-item pickup takes nothing either.

A take through the table (`weapons::hold::pickup::take_by_table`) makes the weapon the player's without the game's pickup ([`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup): the holder, the holstered state, the bodies out of the world, the pickup interaction off, the HUD's pickup notice), credits its stored rounds to the reserve, as the game's pickup does ([`Inventory::AddAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-AddAmmo)), and has the carry set keep it. The table takes it into the hand with its source, and the reconcile follows at once: the weapon is wielded when its hand is the primary, after the projection gives it its slot, and armed in its hand otherwise. A weapon whose slot is taken goes into the table beside the slot's weapon, which stays on its socket or in the other hand; the projection then decides which of them the slot holds, and the other is kept by the carry set. The log line names both hands: `hold: took the weapon into the left hand through the table; the right hand keeps what it holds`, with the slot, the source, and what the other hand holds.

The sources:

- the ground: the physical hands' direct pickup of a weapon they reach, and the weapon handling's take of one (`physical_hands::pickup`, `weapons::hold::pickup`), as `taken off the ground`, the same item again for a weapon the table has lying in the world;
- the racks and the lockers (`weapons::hold::rack`): a rack's weapon after the game's pickup start, which takes it off the rack and tells the rack, as `taken off a rack`, and a locker's weapon, offered lying in the world, as `taken out of a locker`;
- the characters' hands (`weapons::hold::steal`), as `taken from a character's hands`. The hand and the route are checked before the character lets go, so a refused take leaves the gun in its hands;
- a rack with unlimited stock (`weapons::hold::rack`): its copy is a give, which the hand the grab left expecting it takes, and the game's own use-item pickup of such a weapon gives a copy the give rule places;
- the game's use-item pickup (`weapons::hold::use_item`): the pickup's completion ([`WeaponBase::PickupItem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupItem)) is detoured for the local player. It may run wherever the character's track messages are handled, so the detour keeps the weapon alive and queues it, and the next input tick takes it into a free hand, or puts it back into the world with both hands full. A weapon with infinite pickups, whose copy comes from the pickup's start, only loses its stored rounds there, without the pickup's unequip.

The game's gives take one chain ([engine: weapon-gives-and-restores.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-gives-and-restores.md#the-give)): [`Inventory::GivePlayerWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GivePlayerWeapon) writes the slot's respawn-table entry, and the spawned weapon arrives through [`Inventory::WeaponSpawned`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-WeaponSpawned), which would equip it (putting the wielded weapon away) and drop or despawn the slot's occupant. An ordinary world weapon never passes through it. The give's detour (`weapons::inventory::gives`) records each give to the local player's tracked slots by slot and spawn tag while the table places gives (it runs, `either_hand` is on, no reset is under way, and no mission holds the player's weapons), marking one made inside the pickup start of a weapon with unlimited stock as a rack's copy. The arrival's detour takes over a matching weapon and queues it, and the next input tick, before the table's feed, hands it over on the main thread: the slot's occupant is first taken out of the slot onto the body where the table has it, and the respawn table's equipped slot ([`Inventory::m_WeaponToSpawnEquippedWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnEquippedWeapon)) is turned to no slot for the call, so the weapon arrives in its slot not equipped, with the entry's magazine and its overflow to the reserve as the game gives them. The table then places it: a hand a grab of a rack left expecting a copy takes it (`a rack's copy taken`), and any other give goes by the give rule, an empty hand (the dominant first), else a free socket that fits it, else the player's feet, where it is dropped. The log line is `inventory: the game's give went to the right hand` (or the socket, or the world), with the slot and the source.

A give the reset owns is left to the game: the records are forgotten at the reset's pre-reset event, so a checkpoint's restore, which gives before the reset, and the respawn after it arrive as the game makes them, and the reset's completion binds them. A give while a mission holds the player's weapons is left to the game too, since the give-back restores the table's cache.

The ammunition auto-pickup credits a weapon lying in the world's stored rounds while the player's inventory holds a weapon of its ammunition type ([engine: item-pickup.md](../../engine/gameplay/item-pickup.md#picking-up-a-weapon)), and its check reads the slots alone. The check's detour (`weapons::inventory::ammo`, on [`Inventory::HasWeaponWithAmmoType`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HasWeaponWithAmmoType)) also answers yes for the local player's inventory when a weapon the table has in a hand or on a socket is of that type, so a rifle carried beyond the slots collects its rounds as the slot's does. Its one caller credits the rounds itself, so nothing else changes. The types are published as a mask at the end of each input tick, after the reconcile, and read without a lock, since the credit runs wherever the pickup interaction activates; they are cleared when the table stops, and the check is the game's alone then.

The game's own pickup takes the weapon, equipping it and putting the wielded weapon away, only where the table cannot hold it in a hand of its own: while the table does not run, with `weapons.hold.either_hand` off, for a heavy weapon with `weapons.inventory.heavy_on_back` off, for the embedded power core, and with `weapons.hold.second_weapon` off while the other hand holds a weapon.

The switches and what each falls back to:

- `weapons.hold.direct_pickup` off: a grab of a weapon on the ground clicks the use-item action, whose pickup the table still takes into a free hand while it runs; the steal and the racks need it, so they take nothing.
- `weapons.hold.steal` off: no volumes on the guns in characters' hands. `weapons.hold.racks` off: no volumes on the racks' weapons; the game's use-item action still takes them, through the table.
- `weapons.hold.either_hand` off: every take is the game's pickup, which equips the weapon and puts the wielded weapon away, the game's layout standing, and every give is the game's, equipped as its entry says.
- `weapons.hold.second_weapon` off: a take while the other hand holds a weapon is the game's pickup, which swaps the weapons, as one weapon at a time asks.
- `weapons.inventory.enabled` or `weapons.inventory.project_slots` off: the table does not run, every take is the game's pickup, with the weapon the pickup replaces dropped first when `weapons.hold.drop_replaced` is on, and every give is the game's.

Decisions taken here:

- The use-item pickup with both hands full takes nothing and leaves the weapon where it lies, rather than docking it on a socket: nothing moves an item but the player, and the player chooses the hand by freeing one.
- The game's pickup line is not played for a take through the table.
- A swimming character's pickup drops the weapon in the picked weapon's slot before it picks up ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponHandling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponHandling)). Before the local player's weapon handling, while the player swims, presses use, and the animation allows a pickup, the first weapon the interaction entries offer is found as the game finds it, and when the table takes it, its slot's weapon is taken out of the slot first (`weapons::hold::use_item`, the same vacate a take into a taken slot makes), so the drop finds the slot empty and the weapon stays where the table has it (`hold: a swimming pickup's slot was emptied first`). The VR inventory readout counts them. It rides `weapons.hold.either_hand`; off, the game's swimming pickup drops the slot's weapon as before.
- The source of a give is not told apart beyond a rack's copy: the give's callers (the missions, the challenges, the graph script, the events, the rewards) all reach it with the same arguments, and the table's rule is the same for each.
- A give is handed over on the next input tick rather than inside the arrival, which runs wherever the spawn system calls back: the slot's occupant and the reconcile are the main thread's. The weapon lives on the queue's reference for that tick.
- A heavy weapon is taken through the table like any other, so its take leaves the other hand's weapon where it is ([Heavy weapons](#heavy-weapons)).

## Traversal

Traversal leaves the hands and the body as they are (`weapons::inventory::traversal`). The base layer's weapon tasks run on entering their states ([engine: animation-graph.md, "The base layer's weapon tasks"](../../engine/character/animation-graph.md#the-base-layers-weapon-tasks), which lists which states own each): the unequip task ([`NWeaponUnequipTask_OnEnter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon_animation::NWeaponUnequipTask_OnEnter)) on the vehicle entries, the seats, the rides, the grapple's reels into a seat, the wingsuit, the free falls, the dives, the hit reactions' flights, the ragdoll, and dying; the hide-in-vehicle task ([`NWeaponHideInVehicleTask_OnEnter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon_animation::NWeaponHideInVehicleTask_OnEnter)) in the seats; the stop-use task ([`NStopUsingWeaponStateTask_OnEnter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon_animation::NStopUsingWeaponStateTask_OnEnter)) in the ragdoll, the crashes, dying, drowning, and the get-up; and the heavy-weapon drop ([`NDropWeaponStateTask_OnEnter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon_animation::NDropWeaponStateTask_OnEnter)) on the grapple, the throws, the falls, the parachute's and the wingsuit's opening, the vehicle entries, and swimming. Each entry is refused for the local player while the table runs. The hide task's exit still runs: it puts back only a weapon its entry hid. Opening the parachute carries no unequip task at all, only the drop.

The forced neutral state ([`Character::ForceNeutralState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-ForceNeutralState), on every teleport's start, a revive, a cutscene's setup, the vehicle exit's upward teleport) unequips the wielded weapon. Its detour tells the live table which weapon it took (`live::neutral_forced`), and the next input tick keeps that weapon in its hand rather than taking the unequip for a holster; the reconcile then wields it again, or arms it in its hand while the animation refuses a draw. The note ends once the weapon is wielded or armed, or no hand holds it.

The inventory's weapon update skips the wielded weapon in every seat but the motorcycle's, and while the boost wingsuit flies ([`Character::HasBoostWingsuit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HasBoostWingsuit)), whose guns it updates instead. There the reconcile wields nothing: it unequips the wielded weapon, without waiting for the animation's holster permission, since the game's own task would have, and every gun in a hand is armed and fired by the per-hand tick. Off the seat, the primary's weapon is wielded again.

A hand holding anything of the table's (`live::held_kind`) takes no new vehicle control, cockpit button, mounted gun's handle, or parachute handle, and keeps one it already holds; the wingsuit's arms steer only while neither hand holds anything, and the stick steers meanwhile (`vehicles.free_hands_only`, on, in the Controllers tab's vehicles section; off, a holding hand takes them as an empty one does).

The traversal patches are behind `weapons.inventory.keep_through_traversal` (on). Off, the tasks and the forced neutral state are the game's: the wielded weapon is put away on entering a seat or the wingsuit and docks on its socket in the table at the next tick, the slots' weapons hide in a seat that hides them, and in a seat the wielded weapon is not ticked. The Controllers tab's `VR inventory` readout counts each task refused and the forced neutral states kept.

Decisions taken here:

- Every instance of the unequip task is refused, the mounted-gun entries included: a mounted gun's use unequips the wielded weapon through its own start of use, which the table takes for a holster as before, so the player's weapon docks while the hands man the gun.
- The hide task is refused rather than its weapons kept visible otherwise: the body's weapons stay on the body in a seat, as the hands' do, and the carry set shows and hides with the slots' weapons.
- In a seat the hands' guns are armed rather than the mod ticking the wielded weapon a second way: arming is what the per-hand tick already does for the other hand, and the fan-out's choice of what to update stays the game's.
- The wingsuit's arms do not steer with a hand that holds something, rather than steering by the free arm alone: the bank is the difference between the two arms, which one arm cannot give. The parachute's handle is the free hand's alone, the other side's handle staying neutral.

## Heavy weapons

A heavy weapon is kept beyond the slots like every other weapon the player has, and is never wielded from the heavy slot (`weapons.inventory.heavy_on_back`, on). Its unequip ([`WeaponBase::OnUnequip`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnUnequip)) drops whatever the heavy slot holds rather than holstering it, and the base layer's drop task, the forced neutral state, and the holster input's heavy drop all drop it through the slot ([engine: weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md#dropping)). With the slot empty none of them reaches it, and the drop task is refused besides ([Traversal](#traversal)).

- A take of a heavy weapon goes through the table ([The takes](#the-takes)): the carry set keeps it, the hand holds it, and the other hand keeps what it holds. The game's use-item pickup of one is routed the same way, and a heavy give goes by the give rule.
- In a hand it is armed and fired by the per-hand tick, as the second weapon is; the primary's heavy weapon is not wielded, since no slot holds it, so the game's wielded-weapon consumers follow the other hand's weapon when it has one, and none otherwise.
- It docks on the right shoulder, the left shoulder, or the outer back, which take heavy weapons, and is drawn from there.
- The reconcile treats the heavy slot as a tracked slot whose projection is always empty: a heavy weapon of the table's found in it (the game's own pickup while the table was off, a restore) is taken out into the carry set. A wielded one is left to the game, since its unequip would drop it.
- The store and the caches leave it out of the slot too: the save stores the heavy slot's type as none ([engine: weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md)), and the graph script's give-back skips the heavy slot, so a heavy weapon put there would be lost; beyond the slots, the sidecar and the reset's record carry it.

Off, a heavy weapon is the game's as before: its pickup wields it from its slot and puts the other hand's weapon away, every socket refuses it, and it is dropped when put away. The heavy weapons do not restrict anything yet: the player can grapple, throw, glide, and swim while holding one.

Decisions taken here:

- The heavy slot stays empty at the store and the caches as well as in play, against the plan's first intent of projecting it there: the game discards it at both seams, so a heavy weapon is recorded where it survives, beyond the slots.
- A spent single-use launcher, which the game drops through the heavy slot, stays where the table has it until the player lets go, since the slot is empty; that the launcher's drop goes through the slot alone is read from the symbol dump and unverified.

## One socket notion

The loadout's sockets are the mod's only notion of a place on the body. The sockets' grab volumes (`GrabVolume::Socket`), the carry set, and the sidecar all name a `loadout::Socket`. The adapters in `inventory::sockets` give what the game's side needs of one: the bone and the offset a weapon the mod places is placed by, and the name the sidecar keeps it under. The sidecar's names are `back_first` for the right shoulder, `back_second` for the left, `back_outer`, `hip_left`, `hip_right`, and `chest`, and the settings keep their offset fields' names.

The game docks a holstered weapon by its slot, never mirrored for handedness ([`WeaponBase::UpdatePostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdatePostFinalizePose)): the two-handed and heavy slots' guns on [`BACK_ATTACH`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::SafeBoneIndex##variant-BACK_ATTACH), on the right of Rico's back, and the special slot's on [`BACK_ATTACH_2`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::SafeBoneIndex##variant-BACK_ATTACH_2), on the left. So the right shoulder is the two-handed and heavy slots' own socket and the left shoulder the special slot's, whatever the dominant hand, and the core's preference and projection take them so.

## The weapons in the world

The inventory (`weapons::inventory`; the code is `payload/src/controllers/weapons/inventory/`) is the game's side of the table's weapons, with `weapons.inventory.enabled` (on; it and the inventory's other switches are in the debug UI's Controllers tab, under "VR inventory"). It covers the pistols, the two-handed weapons, the special weapons, and the heavy weapons; the grenade, the planted explosive, the beacon, and the sun gun stay the game's alone. The engine side is in [weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md).

The inventory (`inventory::world`) keeps a weak reference to each weapon it has seen in a slot, the carry set, or a hand ([`WeaponBase::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeakThis)), which tells whether it still lives without keeping it alive, and each input tick asks what became of each of the table's weapons that is in no slot, the carry set, or a hand: a weapon whose holder ([`WeaponBase::m_Holder`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Holder)) is another character is that character's and leaves the table; one whose object is gone leaves it; any other is lying in the world, with its distance from the player.

The game removes a dropped weapon by its distance from the player and the view, and never one that something else holds a strong reference to. With `weapons.inventory.pin_world` (on), a weapon the player let go of is pinned (`inventory::pins`), held by a strong reference, while it is within `pin_radius_m` (8 m), and once pinned until it is past `unpin_radius_m` (12 m), so one at the edge does not flicker. At most `max_pinned` (6) are pinned, the nearest first, since a pinned weapon keeps its resources loaded. An unpinned weapon past `forget_m` (60 m), or beyond the most the table keeps in the world, is left to the game: it leaves the table. The pins read the weapons lying in the world from the table, and each pin and its release is logged (`inventory: keeping a weapon lying in the world from the despawn`, `inventory: no longer keeping a weapon lying in the world`). Weapons the player never touched are never pinned, so the game's own clean-up of the world goes on as before.

The spawn system's reset (a load) releases its references to every object and replaces the slots' weapons. At its pre-reset event ([`Inventory::m_SpawnSystemPreReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemPreReset), seen through the local player's [`Inventory::HandleEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HandleEvent)) the inventory releases every reference it holds (`inventory: the spawn system is resetting`, `inventory: released every weapon`), so no pinned weapon outlives the reset outside the spawn system, and the reset's end takes the weapons lying in the world out of the table. Switching the inventory off does the same.

## The weapons beyond the slots

The game's inventory holds one weapon per slot ([`Inventory::m_Weapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_Weapons)), and the save, the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the missions read only the slots. What keeps a weapon alive is any strong reference, not the slot as such, so a reference of the mod's keeps a weapon beyond the slots in the game and out of the spawn system's despawn ranking, and a weapon moves into and out of a slot without being dropped. With `weapons.inventory.carry` (on), every weapon the table has on the body that no slot holds is in the carry set (`inventory::carry`): held by a strong reference of the mod's, holstered, with the player as its holder, so the HUD, the weapon wheel, and the saves do not list it. Which socket each sits on is the table's.

Each socket is a bone of the character's (the right shoulder's the back dock bone the two-handed slot docks on and the outer back's the same, the left shoulder's the one the special slot docks on, the hip holster bones, and the chest's explosive bone) with an offset in the character's frame (`offsets`, x across, y up, z backward), so a weapon beyond the slots sits beside the slot's weapon on the same bone rather than inside it; a pistol on a hip sits in the holster instead ([The pistols as plain items](#the-pistols-as-plain-items)). The bones follow the body, as the slots' weapons do.

A carried weapon is placed after each placement of the inventory's own weapons ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms), the hold's detour): its guns' holstered attach-bone entries name the socket's bone, the weapon's own placement ([`WeaponBase::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) puts them there, and the offset moves them; one in a hand rides the hand's bone. It is shown and hidden whenever the inventory's weapons are ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponVisibility`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponVisibility), detoured), so it hides and shows with them in vehicles and the wingsuit, and each pose-finalization pass is ended for it as the game ends it for the slots' weapons.

A weapon picked up into a slot that holds another weapon (a pickup into the hand, a take from a character's hands, a take off a rack, or a rack's copy) does not drop the slot's weapon: before the pickup, the slot's weapon leaves its slot ([`Inventory::RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RemoveWeapon), unequipped first if it was wielded) and stays where the table has it, on its socket. A change of the slots leaves the HUD's weapon list to be rebuilt from the slots ([`HUDUI::InitWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-InitWeapons)).

Switching `weapons.inventory.enabled` or `weapons.inventory.project_slots` off first puts the table back into the slots, as a store would write them, and keeps every carried weapon left over stashed until the table runs again ([The architecture](#the-architecture)); switching `weapons.inventory.carry` off lets them fall ([`WeaponBase::OnDrop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnDrop), `inventory: dropped the carried weapons`), so the body keeps only what the slots hold. The spawn system's reset releases them. A weapon an area clean-up takes out of the spawn system while it is carried lives on through the carry set's reference, outside the spawn system, and is recorded and respawned at the next reset as usual.

## The caches and the resets

The game takes the player's weapons away and gives them back at three caches, and replaces them at every spawn reset ([engine: weapon-gives-and-restores.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-gives-and-restores.md), [engine: weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-ownership-and-despawn.md#the-spawn-reset-and-the-respawn-table)). At each seam the slots are given the store projection first, so the game records what the player has, and the table takes its own snapshot or restores it (`inventory::missions`, `inventory::challenges`).

- The mission manager's checkpoint ([`MissionManager::CacheWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-CacheWeapons), detoured): the table snapshots itself as the checkpoint's cache, the slots are given the store projection, and the carried weapons are recorded for a restart (`inventory: a mission checkpoint snapshots the player's weapons`). Its replay ([`MissionManager::RestoreCachedWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-RestoreCachedWeapons), detoured, after the player's respawn) marks the respawn for the checkpoint: the reset that follows respawns the checkpoint's carried weapons rather than those carried at the death, and at its completion the table returns to its snapshot. What was gained since leaves, every item comes back to its socket, and the gives the replay made are bound by slot and type. The snapshot stands for every restart from the checkpoint.
- A mission's graph-script cache ([Other characters and missions](#other-characters-and-missions)).
- A challenge's: its start ([`ROMManager::StartROM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMManager##func-StartROM)) and its object's initialisation ([`ROMObject::PostInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMObject##func-PostInit)) record the loadout, the start removing every slot when its spawn point overrides the loadout and the initialisation despawning each slot the challenge does not allow. Before each the table snapshots itself as the challenge's cache, keeping the first through a retry or a restart; when either took a weapon from the slots, the table takes every weapon on the body and the carried weapons are stashed. Its abort ([`ROMManager::AbortROM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMManager##func-AbortROM)) and the restore of its record ([`ROMObject::RestoreWeaponsAndAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::ROMObject##func-RestoreWeaponsAndAmmo)) are the give-back.

A give-back is folded once no tracked slot's spawn is pending, and after the spawn reset when one is under way, since the challenges' give-backs come with one. A mission's or a challenge's snapshot is used up by its give-back. A reset during a hold (a death while a mission holds the weapons) does not respawn the carried weapons onto the body: its record is held back until the give-back, and a respawned weapon binds the snapshot's item waiting for one of its type rather than coming in as a new item.

The reset's kind decides what the hands keep, and is read from the seams before it rather than from the player's health, which the respawn has restored by the time the reset runs:

- a respawn when the game respawned the player since the last completion ([`Player::ReInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-ReInit), detoured): a death, a mission's failure or restart, the teleport to a mission's start, and the return to a challenge's start, since each restarts from a restart point, which revives the player. The hands come back empty;
- a load when the game loaded the player's save data, or the frontend reset the game's systems ([`GameStateFrontend::ResetSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateFrontend##func-ResetSystems), detoured, the quit to the menu, which also forgets the reset's record of the carried weapons, so a new game carries nothing in);
- a travel otherwise: a fast travel, a challenge's start, retry, restart, or abort, and the map's teleport. The hands keep what they hold.

The weapons beyond the slots come to the player after every teleport: the carry set places them on the body's bones whatever the teleport, and a reset, which releases them, respawns them at the player.

These seams ride the table's own switches (`weapons.inventory.project_slots`, and `weapons.inventory.persist` for the carried weapons' records). With the table off, the caches and the resets are the game's alone.

Decisions taken here:

- A challenge that takes any weapon from the slots takes all of them for the table, and stashes the carried ones: the challenge decides the loadout, and its give-back restores everything, so the table does not try to keep the classes the challenge allows apart from the ones it does not.
- A mission's give-back is the cache's restore, not the slots filling again: the first reading of the graph script missed the function, which the disassembler mislabels.
- The checkpoint's snapshot keeps the hands empty, as every restore does, since the restart is a respawn.

## Saves, resets, and loads

The game's save stores only the slots, and the spawn system's reset (a load, and possibly a death or a fast travel) replaces the slots' weapons by type and lets every other object go. With `weapons.inventory.persist` (on), the carried weapons come back too (`inventory::persist`, pure, and `inventory::restore`):

- At the reset's pre-reset event, the carried weapons are recorded (each one's spawn tag hash, magazine, socket, and whether it is a single pistol) with whether the pistol slot's weapon is a single pistol, before the carry set lets them go (`inventory: the spawn system is resetting`).
- Each time the game stores the player's save data ([`StorePlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-StorePlayerSaveData), detoured), the slots are given the store projection's content and the save sidecar is written: `inventory.toml` in the per-user configuration directory beside `settings.toml` ([settings.md](../settings.md)), on a writer thread, through a temporary file moved over the old one. It holds a format version, the carried weapons, and a fingerprint of what the store writes: each slot's weapon type (the heavy slot's always zero, as the save has it) and every reserve (`inventory: wrote the save sidecar`). While pistols are made separate it also says whether the pistol slot's weapon is a single pistol (`slot_pistol_single`), and each carried single pistol says so (`single`).
- Each time the game loads the player's save data ([`LoadPlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-LoadPlayerSaveData), detoured, after the original), the sidecar is read and its fingerprint compared with what the load put into the inventory: the respawn table's types ([`Inventory::m_WeaponToSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawn)) and the reserves. A slot whose type the game does not know now (content not installed) loads empty and still matches. With a match, the sidecar's carried weapons of known types are kept for the respawn (`inventory: the sidecar matches the save; its carried weapons come back`); a missing, unreadable, other-version, or other-save sidecar carries nothing extra, and the load is otherwise untouched (`inventory: the load carries nothing extra`, with the outcome, and a warning for an unreadable file).
- After the reset's post-reset event, the first input tick with the player respawns the carried weapons: from the load, when there was one since the reset, and otherwise from the reset's own record, when it is less than two minutes old and for the same character (a quit to the menu followed by a new game should not carry them in). Each is asked of the spawn system by its type at the player ([`SpawnSystem::SpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-SpawnTagIndex), the way the inventory respawns a slot), and when it comes back it is taken into the carry set ([`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup)) with its magazine as it was ([`WeaponBase::SetMagazineAmmo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-SetMagazineAmmo)), a single pistol made single again, and restored onto its recorded socket, or the one the table prefers when that is taken. The pistol slot's weapon the game respawns is made single when the record says it was, and is otherwise split as a pair the game hands over; a carried single pistol that never comes back makes it the game's pair again. The log lines are `inventory: respawning the carried weapons` and `inventory: restored a carried weapon on the right shoulder`; a spawn that has not come within ten seconds is given up.

The game keeps one save per user, and the mod cannot name which save a load reads, so the sidecar is not keyed to a save: the fingerprint is what ties it to the save it was written with. A load of an older save, written before the last store, does not match, and nothing extra is carried. After a load, a weapon the sidecar recorded in a hand comes back on a socket, since the hands come back empty on every load.

## Other characters and missions

A weapon the player let go of that another character then holds (its holder is that character) is that character's: it leaves the table (`loadout: #3 (…) left the table from the world (dropped): another character took it`), and the inventory watches it while that character keeps it no further than `forget_m`, for the `WITH OTHERS` count and so a single pistol stays one pistol in their hands. It is never pinned, even if the character drops it again. Taken back (a take from the character's hands, or picked up after the character drops it), it is a new item of the table's.

A mission that takes the player's weapons away caches the slots in a graph script's weapon cache, which forces the slots' weapons out ([`NodeWeaponCache::CacheAllWeaponSlots`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-CacheAllWeaponSlots), detoured). The table caches before the game does, and takes every weapon on the body, the hands' included, after it. The detour notes whether the pistol slot held a single pistol, and the next pistol to arrive in the slot is one pistol again when it did, rather than a pair to split. The carried weapons are then stashed (`inventory::missions`): every carried weapon is hidden, keeps no grab volume, and stays carried (`inventory: the player's weapons were taken; the carried weapons are stashed`). The give-back is the cache's own restore ([`NodeWeaponCache::RestoreWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-RestoreWeapons), detoured), after which the carried weapons are shown again (`inventory: the player's weapons were given back; the carried weapons are back`) and the table restores its snapshot once the slots' spawns arrive. The remove-where-empty strips of the graph script's node remove single slots through the marked removal rather than the cache's, so their weapons leave the table as gone and the carried weapons stay. The challenges are in [The caches and the resets](#the-caches-and-the-resets).

## Settings and readouts

The inventory's settings are `controllers.weapons.inventory`: `enabled`, `project_slots`, `carry`, `persist`, `keep_through_traversal`, `heavy_on_back`, the lost-weapon return's `return_lost`, `lost_speed_mps`, `left_behind_m`, and `lost_depth_m`, the pins' `pin_world`, `pin_radius_m`, `unpin_radius_m`, `max_pinned`, and `forget_m`, and each socket's `offsets`. The vehicles' `controllers.vehicles.free_hands_only` keeps a holding hand off the controls. The hands' are `controllers.weapons.hold` and the sockets' `controllers.weapons.holsters` ([physical-weapons.md, "Settings and readout"](physical-weapons.md#settings-and-readout)).

The debug UI's Controllers tab has a `VR inventory` section listing the carried weapons and the weapons lying in the world (each one's object, whether it is kept, and its distance) and the counts, and its settings, with each socket's offset; the table itself is in its `Loadout` section. The debug UI's Status tab has a `LOADOUT TABLE` row and an `INVENTORY` section: `TRACKED WEAPONS` (the weapons on the body and in the world, the weapons with other characters, and the resets), `CARRIED` (each weapon beyond the slots and its socket, and the weapons kept from pickups' drops), `SAVE SIDECAR` (the writes and the carried weapons in the last, or a failed write), `LAST LOAD` (what the last load made of the sidecar, and how many weapons the last respawn put back), `KEPT IN THE WORLD` (the weapons kept of those in the world, the pins since load, and the weapons left to the game), and `SEPARATE PISTOLS`. The scenario signals `inventory.kept`, `inventory.pins`, `inventory.resets`, `inventory.carried`, `inventory.restored`, `inventory.sidecar_writes`, `weapons.draws`, `weapons.docks`, `weapons.armed_carried`, and `weapons.promotions` read them (`testing/scenarios/inventory_keep_dropped.toml`, `inventory_two_rifles.toml`, and `inventory_rifle_each_hand.toml`).

Settings that earlier builds had and this one does not (the game's-act draws, the pistol pair's split and its layout, its half magazines, and the carry set's socket counts and radius) are skipped when an old settings file loads, and logged as unknown.

## RE answers

The phase 1 pass answered the questions it opened with; the findings are in the engine docs and bound in the pyxis defs.

1. A gun ticked outside the selected wield fires fully as the player's, in the holstered state, with the player as its user (see "Firing").
2. The graph's weapon conditions change the base layer's families and stance, not what the player may do (see "Animation"); the base layer's state tasks are what move weapons on their own (see "No automatic moves").
3. Per-hand weapon animation is per class, from one clip for both hands, and feasible at the pose seam (see "Animation").
4. The shooting permissions gate only the game's own fire tick's inputs; they are cleared in vehicle entry and exit, the ragdoll, hit reactions, death, the grapple's fire and reel, the free fall's and the wingsuit's tricks, the parachute's opening, underwater, mounted guns, grenade throws, melee, and the pickup.
5. Each gun keeps its own aim mode; the precision aim and the camera hints are the contention (see "Firing").
6. Moving the primary with `OnUnequip` and `OnEquip` and no act is stable; nothing in the player's graph equips on its own.
7. The game's deferred equip is the AI's only; the player's weapons are equipped, unequipped, selected, given, and removed through a few choke points, which the table routes: `Inventory::AddWeapon`, the give's spawn callback, the removal and despawn, `WeaponBase::OnEquip` and `OnUnequip`, the selection setter, and the auto-pickup's weapon check.
8. The three weapon caches (the mission manager's at each checkpoint, the spawn-weapons node's, and the challenges') compare per slot by type (see "The game's slots are a projection").
9. The spawn reset runs on every load-state entry and replaces the slot weapons (see "The game's slots are a projection").
10. Every seat but the motorcycle's skips the wielded weapon's and the grenade's tick; the boost wingsuit replaces the wielded tick with its own guns; nothing in code holsters for the parachute, swimming, or the grapple's hang, so what does comes from the graph.

Which of the base layer's states own its unequip and hide-in-vehicle tasks is answered from the decoded graph ([engine: animation-graph.md](../../engine/character/animation-graph.md#the-base-layers-weapon-tasks)). Still open: how the seats' poses take armed hands under the IK; the order in which a weapon's own pre-simulation update handles its deferred operations; and the bone lists of the arms', the lower body's, and the recoil's clips.
