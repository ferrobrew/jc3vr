# Single-pass per-eye inputs

Under single-pass stereo ([single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)) an instance-doubled draw covers both halves of the double-wide target in one submission, so every input it takes from the engine's per-pass staging is the centred render camera's: the camera position, the light positions, the screen position, the previous-frame matrices, and the field of view of the cameras sized from the target. Each section below names one such input and how the collapse gives each eye its own, including the draws promoted to one submission for both eyes and the inputs that differ under asymmetric and canted eyes. [single-pass-collapse-defects.md](single-pass-collapse-defects.md) covers the passes that run once over the whole target. The switches named here are rows of the switch table under "Configuration" in [single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md).

## The eye-camera buffer

A pixel shader rewritten at creation finds its eye from the half of the target its pixel lies in, and reads that eye's rows from a mod-owned pixel constant buffer. Slot 11, which no game pixel shader declares, carries the eye-camera buffer (`jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::eye_camera`), whose boundary row (the first pixel of the right eye's half of the bound target) several rewrites share. Slot 13, the other free one, carries per-block rows while a block runs. Outside an eye-split pass the boundary lies past every pixel and the rows hold the engine's staged values, so a rewritten shader reads what the engine's would, and the shadow, reflection, and other `PreDraw` passes are unchanged.

## Each eye's camera position in the pixel shaders

The engine stages the camera world position into the fragment `GlobalConstants` ([`RenderEngine::m_FPGlobalConstData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##field-m_FPGlobalConstData)) once per pass, which under the collapse means from the centred render camera, and 432 of the game's 961 pixel shaders read it: for the view vector of their specular, Fresnel, sheen, and environment terms, for camera distances, and as the origin the forward-lit families' sun-shadow lookup subtracts (engine [shaders.md](../../engine/rendering/shaders.md#pixel-shaders-that-read-the-camera-position)). A split pass and the water's per-eye draws have each eye's rows staged into `cb0` for them, but an instance-doubled draw covers both halves in one submission, so everything it drew would be shaded from the point between the eyes. The `character` material, for one, folds a cloth-sheen term measured from the camera into the albedo it writes to the G-buffer, so the player's own clothing and gear differ visibly from an isolated render.

`single_pass.pixel_camera_per_eye` fixes the whole class without adding a draw. `dxbc_stereo::localize_eye_camera` rewrites, at creation, every pixel shader that reads the camera rows: the shader compares its `SV_Position.x` with the boundary and reads the rows from its eye's block of the slot-11 buffer. Each eye's block holds the two camera position rows and the cascade translation row, which is baked relative to the camera position and has to move with it: the same three rows `eye_globals` moves for a split pass. `eye_camera` rewrites the buffer at every [`RenderEngine::SetAllGlobalShaderProgramConstants`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-SetAllGlobalShaderProgramConstants), from the rows the engine has just staged, the cadence `cb13` follows on the vertex side:

- Outside the routed range, and in any frame without the collapse, both blocks hold the staged rows unchanged and the boundary lies past every pixel. The range guard puts the buffer back to the staged rows when the range ends, for the draws before the next upload.
- Inside the routed range under the collapse, each block holds the staged rows moved to its eye, and the boundary is the first pixel of the right eye's half of whatever target is bound, derived from the viewport the collapse splits, so it follows the reduced-resolution targets. A boundary change rewrites the buffer; the per-bind check costs two relaxed loads.
- The per-eye re-issues and the masked runs of a split pass land in one half, so they read that eye's block. For a split pass and the water, that block holds the values `eye_globals` stages into `cb0` for the run, so the rows are not applied twice.

Every camera read moves to the eye, including the ones that only feed a fade or a distance threshold, because that is what a separately rendered eye computes; the choices the design keeps on the centre (the LOD and tessellation selection) are not made in a pixel shader. Two draws crossfading at one pixel both read the same eye, so their fades stay complementary. On the vertex side, the four camera-only reprojected families (`generaljc3`, `landmark`, `layered`, and `layeredblend`) keep `cb0[4]` centred on purpose, since their position path adds it back; in all four it feeds only a squared camera-distance fade, so no centred view vector reaches their pixel shaders through an interpolant.

All 432 camera readers are rewritten. The rewrite would decline a shader with no `SV_Position` input, one that already declares slot 11, and one that indexes `cb0` dynamically from a base that could reach a camera row; the shaders that index `cb0` dynamically all start past the cascade translation row. The rewrite composes after the others in the fragment hook. The water's per-eye variants are built from the engine's own bytecode, since its per-eye draws stage the rows into `cb0`. Turning the switch on needs a shader reload; turning it off takes effect at once, because the buffer then holds the staged rows everywhere.

## The local lights, from each eye

The engine builds the local point and spot lights' frame constants relative to the render camera (engine [lighting-shadow-pipeline.md](../../engine/rendering/lighting-shadow-pipeline.md#43-the-lighting-frame-constants-are-camera-relative)): [`LightManager::SetupLightingData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::light_manager::LightManager##func-SetupLightingData) subtracts `RenderContext::m_RenderCameraPosition` from every light position and bakes it into every spot projection. Under the collapse that is the centred camera, once per dispatch, while every pixel's position is measured from its own eye, so each light vector is off by the eye's offset from the centre. The lights a held device carries sit a few tens of centimetres from the camera, so a few centimetres of error visibly moves their glow on the player's body.

`single_pass.local_lights_per_eye` measures them from each eye without adding a draw or touching the engine's buffer. Each eye's block of the slot-11 buffer gains a row holding the eye's world offset from the centred camera (zero outside an eye-split pass), and the lights rewrite (`crates/dxbc-stereo/src/rewrite/lights.rs`) corrects the light reads of every shader that binds `LightingFrameConsts`. A light position read, the addend of an `add` or `mad` whose other term is the negated pixel position, becomes a read of the position minus the offset; a projection's translation row becomes the row plus the projection's linear part applied to the offset. A read is corrected only when its pixel position is relative to the pixel's eye: a temp the shader last wrote as a position minus a camera row (checked per component along the program, and not rewritten later in an enclosing loop), or the resolve's depth times its view ray. That covers the resolve permutations and the hair (lights and the projection) and the forward-lit families (lights). The two vegetation blend permutations measure from a position their vertex shader made relative to its camera, so their light data and pixel position already share one camera and are left alone. The light-propagation-volume transforms stay centred on purpose: they are placed around the camera like the cascade fit, and the volume is built once.

## The screen-door pattern, from each eye

The alpha-tested and fading materials discard against a threshold hashed from the pixel position, `frac(dot(SV_Position.xy, k))` with a fixed vector (engine [shaders.md](../../engine/rendering/shaders.md#the-screen-door-hash)): the vegetation and `general_lod*` distance and LOD crossfades, the `generalmkiii*` alpha tests, `prop`, `buildingjc3`, and the vehicle paint's stipple. The volumetric fog dithers its march by the same hash. The vector is not a whole number of cycles per pixel, so the pattern does not repeat at any horizontal offset: in the right half of the double-wide target a different set of pixels survives each fade than in an eye rendered on its own, while the left half, whose pixel positions are an eye-sized target's, matches.

`single_pass.screen_door_per_eye` rewrites every `dp2` of the `SV_Position` input's `.xy` with an immediate vector (`dxbc_stereo::localize_screen_door`) to read an eye-local position: `x` minus the eye boundary where the pixel lies at or past it, `y` unchanged. The boundary is the eye-camera buffer's, which `eye_camera` publishes in every eye-split pass, with both eyes' blocks holding the staged rows when the camera rows are not moved per eye. An unbound buffer reads as a boundary of zero and leaves the position unchanged. The rewrite composes after the eye-camera rewrite and reads the buffer that one declared. The sun-shadow filter's rotation, the other hash of the pixel position in the game's shaders, has its vector zeroed by the shadow PCF patch ([shader-patching.md](../rendering/shader-patching.md)) before this rewrite runs, and a zero vector is left alone. The ambient-occlusion generation pass makes its position eye-local in its own rewrite.

## The tree impostors' screen door, per eye

The tree impostors fade in over a distance band by a screen-door dissolve with the same hash and vector. The impostor permutations build the hash's input not from `SV_Position` but from the clip position their vertex shader passes down, divided by `w` and by twice the inverse target size in the vertex globals, which in an eye-sized render is the pixel's offset from the target's centre. Under the collapse that clip position is the centred camera's, ahead of the reprojection, and the target is both eyes wide, so the pattern comes out twice as wide and centred between the eyes, and a card in the fade band keeps a different set of pixels in each eye than in an isolated render; whole distant trees differ.

The collapse rewrites the impostor pixel shaders that build the hash that way (`dxbc_stereo::localize_projected_screen_door`) to read the offset an eye-sized render computes from the pixel position: `x` made eye-local against the eye-camera boundary, less half the eye's width, and half the target's height less `y`. Outside an eye-split pass the rewritten shader hashes the engine's own offset. The impostors' pixel size is staged at one eye's width by a detour on `TreeImpostorRBType::Setup` (`impostor_pixel_size`).

## The motion vectors, in each eye's screen space

The velocity pass encodes, per pixel, the current screen UV less the previous frame's, from a current and a previous clip position its vertex shader hands down (engine [shaders.md](../../engine/rendering/shaders.md#the-velocity-passs-motion-vectors)). In an eye-sized render the current position's NDC is the pixel's own, and the previous one comes through the render camera's previous view-projection, which for an isolated eye or a multipass dispatch is that eye's own previous camera. Under the double-wide collapse both positions leave the eye's space:

- The previous position comes through the centred camera's previous view-projection over the double-wide target, whose horizontal row is the eye-sized one's halved, from the centred camera's position rather than the eye's. That holds for all three of its sources: the vertex `GlobalConstants` rows the model families read, the camera-relative ones the hardware-instanced buildings read, and the matrices the vegetation and other blocks bake into their own constant buffers. Rescaling only the global rows therefore leaves most of the buffer wrong, since a meadow's vectors are almost all the vegetation's.
- The current position is in the eye's half only where the vertex shader computes it from the remap's per-eye rows. The reprojected families and the vegetation drawn once move `SV_Position` alone, and leave the copy the pixel shader reads at the centred camera's.

So every eye's horizontal motion would carry a ramp of about half a pixel per pixel from its centre, clamping at the buffer's limit over the outer parts of each eye, plus the parallax between the eye and the centred camera.

The collapse rewrites the velocity pixel shaders (`dxbc_stereo::localize_velocity`). Right after the encoding's divide, the current NDC is rebuilt from `SV_Position`: `x` from the left edge of the eye whose half the pixel lies in over one eye's width, `y` from the target's top edge over its height. The previous NDC is rebuilt from the interpolated previous clip position taken through a per-eye matrix, `S P_prev E⁻¹ P_prev⁻¹`: `P_prev` the render camera's previous projection (the double-wide one), `S` the doubling of the horizontal clip coordinate, and `E` the eye's offset from the camera, recovered from the eye's `M_eye = P_eye E⁻¹ P⁻¹` (the map the `cb13` reprojection reads) as `P_eye⁻¹ M_eye P`. The eyes are rigid with respect to the camera, so the previous frame's eye cameras relate to the previous render camera as this frame's do to the current one. The map carries only the eye's offset, not its cant (`eye_camera::eye_offset_view`), because an eye rendered on its own takes its previous view from the centred camera's orientation: the engine's previous view-projection carries none of the cant the camera hook applies after the fact.

The rows live after the boundary in the eye-camera buffer, and `eye_camera` writes them in the eye-split passes from the viewport the collapse splits, the `M_eye` the `cb13` upload has just computed, and the render camera's projections. The shader blends the rebuilt positions in by a weight in those rows, one in an eye-split pass, so outside one, or with the buffer unbound (all zeros), it subtracts what the engine's shader does; the previous position is blended in clip space, before its divide, so zero rows cannot produce a NaN. Building the current position from the pixel serves every family, whatever its vertex shader did to `SV_Position`, and it is what an eye-sized render's interpolated copy amounts to. The rewrite composes after the eye-camera and screen-door rewrites and reads the buffer they declared. Nothing is drawn differently.

## The ambient-occlusion volumes, per eye

An ambient-occlusion volume ([`RenderBlockAOBox`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockAOBox), a box or a sphere drawn in [`RP_AO_VOLUMES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderPassId##variant-RP_AO_VOLUMES)) darkens the scene inside it. Its `aobox` vertex shader projects the corners through the full view-projection (`cb0[0..3]`) and hands the pixel shader a projective screen UV derived from that clip position; the pixel shader (`aobox` or `aosphere`) samples the scene depth and the reflective water planes' depth at that UV and maps each through `ScreenToLocal`, a matrix [`RenderBlockAOBox::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockAOBox##func-Draw) builds from the render context's camera and the box's transform, into the box's unit space, where the occlusion falls off from the centre. The result is multiplied into the target the SSAO composite reads (`AOVolumeSrc`), so it reaches the image only through the SSAO block.

Re-issued per eye unpatched, the volume's UV would be the eye-local position of the pixel while the depth texture spans both eyes, so each eye's left half would sample the left eye's depth stretched across a whole eye and its right half the right eye's, through the centred camera's matrix: sharp vertical edges at each eye's centre column and copies of nearby geometry half an eye to the side.

`single_pass.ao_volumes_per_eye` draws the volumes once for both eyes. The `aobox` vertex shader reads the eye's full view-projection rows, so its indexed draw is instance-doubled and each eye's half rasterizes the volume from that eye. `dxbc_stereo::localize_ambient_occlusion_volume` rewrites both pixel shaders to work from `SV_Position` alone: the eye is the half the pixel lies in, both depths are sampled at the pixel's double-wide UV, and every product of a depth with the matrix's depth row becomes the product of the whole matrix with `P_eye · (x, y, depth, 1)`, divided through by its `w`. `jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::ao_volumes` builds `P_eye = T⁻¹ · M_eye⁻¹ · T · S_eye` per eye (with `T` the UV-to-NDC flip, `M_eye` the reprojection, and `S_eye` the map from the eye's pixel to the UV an eye-sized render reads there, scaled by [`m_UVScale`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::graphics_engine::RenderContext##field-m_UVScale)), so the centred matrix applied to it is the eye's own matrix applied to the eye's own UV, and binds the rows at pixel slot 13 for the pass's draw walk. The pass's draws are halved against the per-eye re-issue.

## The road surfaces' clip origin, per eye

The road surfaces (`layeredroad`) and the `generalmkiii*roadbias` model permutations subtract the camera position from the world position before the translation-free view-projection, like the rest of the remapped scene, but take that position from `cb0[9]` rather than `cb0[4]`. Row 9 is the render camera's position, staged by [`RenderEngine::SetGlobalShaderConstants`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-SetGlobalShaderConstants). With row 9 left centred, each eye would project the road from half the eye distance away from where it is: the depth of a flat surface barely moves under a shift within its own plane, but its texturing slides by that much, which on the roads' high-frequency layers is a dense speckle in the G-buffer normals, albedo, and specular across the whole road.

`single_pass.clip_origin_per_eye` has the remap (`dxbc_stereo::patch_vertex_shader_with` with `ClipOrigin::PerEye`) read `cb0[9]` from each eye's copy of the row in `cb13`, in the shaders that read the view-projection rows without `cb0[4]`; in the others `cb0[9]` is a distance fade and is left alone. The copies sit after the reprojection block, five rows apart so the remap's row base reaches each (`dxbc_stereo::CLIP_ORIGIN_ROW_BASE`). Outside the routed range they hold the staged row itself. No draw is added.

## The planar reflection camera, at one eye's size

The WaveWorks water's planar reflection is rendered in `PreDraw` from the water patch manager's reflection camera ([`WaterPatchManager::m_ReflectionCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/water_patch_manager::WaterPatchManager##field-m_ReflectionCamera)), which [`WaterPatchManager::UpdateReflectionCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/water_patch_manager::WaterPatchManager##func-UpdateReflectionCamera) sizes every frame from the active camera's dimensions and the camera manager's aspect ratio. Under the double-wide collapse both are the double-wide target's, so the reflection texture would hold twice an eye's horizontal field, and the reflection under the water's normal disturbance would land on different texels than in an isolated render: a fine speckle over the far, grazing part of a lake. The reflection camera mirrors the head camera, not an eye, so the two isolated eyes' reflections are byte-identical.

The collapse sets the reflection camera's width and aspect ratio to one eye's right after the engine sets them, under the double-wide collapse only; the engine's caller rebuilds the projection from them straight after, and sets them again on the next frame. The values are replaced rather than corrected after the call, because the draw thread can render the previous frame's reflection passes from the live camera while the game thread updates it, and a correction would leave a window in which the camera holds the double-wide parameters with its projection marked dirty. The update runs as a job on the engine's job pool, which is why the single-pass flags pinned for a dispatch must end with it (see "The range flag belongs to the draw thread" in [single-pass-stereo.md](single-pass-stereo.md)). It adds no draw.

## The static light sprites

[`RenderBlockTypeLights`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeLights), the static light sprites (the distant town and street lights), draw in [`RP_LENSFLARE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderPassId##variant-RP_LENSFLARE) with the `lightsource` program pair and one non-indexed `Draw` per block. The sprite vertex shader projects each light's world position through the full view-projection in the global vertex constants and expands the quad around it in clip space by a pixel size times the clip `w`. Under the collapse three things are wrong with that:

- A non-indexed draw outside the geometry passes is treated as a fullscreen pass and drawn with the whole double-wide viewport, so the centred camera's view is stretched over both eyes: each light lands at twice its distance from the target's centre, and eye 1 shows the right half of the centre view.
- The sprites are occluded by the hardware depth test alone (the `lightsource` pixel shader samples no depth), so each stretched sprite is tested against the depth of a pixel it does not represent, and lights the terrain hides come out in front of it.
- The inverse screen width that turns the pixel size into clip units is one over the back buffer's width, which is both eyes' width.

The collapse draws them once for both eyes. The `lightsource` and `lightsource_fakelight` vertex shaders read the eye's full view-projection rows (their clip position is a scene view-projection times a world position, and the clip-space offset moves with the sprite), and `draw_detour` promotes a non-indexed draw with a patched vertex shader in the two passes the light type draws in within the routed range ([`RP_DEFERRED_LIGHTS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderPassId##variant-RP_DEFERRED_LIGHTS), where `Setup` selects the fake-light programs, and `RP_LENSFLARE`) to one two-instance `DrawInstanced` with the viewport split. The promotion is a pass allowlist rather than every patched non-indexed draw: promoting all of them outside the geometry passes also catches the light-propagation-volume initialisation (`lpvinit`) and the moon's `open` model, and moves the lit colour away from the isolated eyes. The sprites then rasterise in each eye's half from that eye's projection and are depth-tested against that eye's depth. A detour on [`RenderBlockTypeLights::Setup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeLights##func-Setup) rewrites the sprite constants' inverse screen width to one over the width of the eye half the collapse splits the bound target into (`static_lights`), so a sprite is as wide as in an eye-sized render. The sprites' distance fade and size curve read the centred camera row, as the scene's other LOD decisions do. Outside the routed range (the planar reflection's distant-light pass, which reuses the program) the shader reads the mirrored rows and the draw is not promoted, so the reflection is unchanged. Each call stays one submission, now carrying both eyes.

## The moon, the stars, and the water reflection planes, once for both eyes

Three more non-indexed draws in the routed range, outside the geometry passes, would be treated as fullscreen triangles and drawn once with the full viewport.

The moon is one quad in [`RP_MOON`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderPassId##variant-RP_MOON), drawn by the `open` vertex shader, which takes the reprojection. With one instance and the full viewport it would take eye 0's transform over both halves: missing from eye 0 and twice as wide in eye 1. The collapse promotes it to one two-instance `DrawInstanced` with the viewport split, as the static light sprites are promoted. The `open` shader can pin a sky body's clip depth to the far plane, and `M_eye`'s depth row mixes the other clip components back into that depth, which would push the moon off the plane; for the promoted draw both `cb13` eye slots carry `M_eye` with its depth row replaced by the identity row, the correction the sun's per-eye re-drive takes (`jc3vr_render::stereo::single_pass::sun`). It needs `single_pass.reproject` for the `open` rewrite. The stars (`starsshader`) are promoted and reprojected the same way, their clip depth kept on the far plane.

The water reflection planes' proxy ([`RenderBlockWaterReflectionPlane::Draw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockWaterReflectionPlane##func-Draw), in [`RP_REFLECTIVE_WATER_PLANES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderPassId##variant-RP_REFLECTIVE_WATER_PLANES)) is a world-space quad whose depth the screen-space reflections trace water against. Its `watermask` vertex shader is a bare multiply-add chain over the offset world-view-projection the block stages at vertex `cb1`, and its `waterplane` pixel shader writes the quad's depth where it lies in front of the downsampled scene depth, loaded at the pixel's own position. Drawn once from the centred camera across the double-wide target, each eye's water depth would be half of a stretched quad, tested against the other eye's depth at the seam. The collapse puts `watermask` on the reprojection allowlist and promotes the proxy draw to two instances routed to the eye halves; the pixel shader's load then reads each eye's own depth. The water mask volumes (`CRenderBlockWaterMask`, not bound in the defs) share the vertex shader and stage the same matrix, and their indexed draws are promoted like any patched draw.

The light-propagation-volume initialisation (`lpvinit`) is the other patched shader on a non-indexed draw in the routed range, and it is correct drawn once. Its vertex shader turns each texel of the reflective shadow map into a cell of the light-propagation volume, and a geometry shader routes each point to its volume slice, so it writes a volume texture, not the scene. The remap claims it on a `cb0[4]` reference that reads only the row's `w`, which `cb13` carries unchanged in both eye slots, and the viewport index its rewritten vertex shader writes is dropped at the geometry shader. Drawn with the volume target's own viewport (`collapse_viewport_follows_target`), the one draw injects one volume placed by the reflective shadow camera, which both eyes then sample.

The other draws treated as `once` inside the routed range, outside a render block's per-eye re-issue, are the fullscreen passes (the deferred lighting and its light assignment, the sky's atmospheric scattering, the screen-space reflection trace, blur, and composite, the low-resolution cloud and particle composites, the fog volumes, the subsurface skin, the scene capture, and the depth downsample), which run once over the whole target by design and take their eye inside the draw where they need one, and two non-indexed draws with shaders the identity census cannot name, after the road passes, whose output is unexamined.

## Asymmetric and canted eyes

A real headset's eyes have asymmetric frusta, mirrored between the two, and some headsets cant each display outward, so an eye differs from the centred camera in its projection and its orientation as well as its offset. Under the collapse the render camera is centred, carries eye 0's projection, and has no cant, so anything that takes one of those from the render camera rather than from the eye is right only for symmetric, parallel eyes. The capture harness's headset-like rigs exercise these paths ([single-pass-validation.md](single-pass-validation.md)).

- The clouds. The cloud layers' vertex shaders take their clip position from the full view-projection (`cb0[0..3]`), which the remap does not make per eye, so both halves would draw the clouds through the centred camera: invisible while the eyes are symmetric and parallel, since the cloud distance makes the offset vanish, but with eye 0's projection in eye 1 under asymmetric frusta and without the cant in both eyes under canted ones. `single_pass.clouds_per_eye` puts the four cloud vertex shaders on the reprojection, by exact name since the cloud name space also holds the cloud-shadow pass. Their clip depth is never pinned to a plane, so `M_eye`'s depth row is harmless to them.
- The reconstruction basis of a split pass carries the eye's turn from the centred camera (`reconstruction::eye_half_inverse`), and the screen-space decals and the clustered light assignment use the eye's view (`ss_decal::eye_view`, `clustered_lighting::split::eye_assignment_view`). The SSAO and reflection rows, the fog volume, the water, and FSR's per-eye matrices are built from the eye's resolved pose.
- The motion vectors' previous view carries only the eye's offset (see "The motion vectors, in each eye's screen space").
- The cull and shadow-fit frusta are a centred symmetric frustum bounding the two eyes. A canted eye reaches past its own half-angles on its outer side in the head frame, so the bound is taken in the head frame, over each eye's frustum corners turned by its orientation (`jc3vr_view::vr::frame::head_frame_tangents`), in every mode.
- Multipass's FSR stereo correction keeps the centred camera's view-projection per eye (`jc3vr_view::stereo::VpHistory`); a single snapshot that each dispatch overwrote would correct eye 0 through eye 1's projection.

### The terrain hull cull

Both terrain block types bake an offset view-projection into their hull and domain constants once per frame per slot ([`RenderBlockTypeTerrain::SetupConstantBuffers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeTerrain##func-SetupConstantBuffers), [`RenderBlockTypeTerrainPatch::SetupConstantBuffers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_block::RenderBlockTypeTerrainPatch##func-SetupConstantBuffers)), and the hull discards a patch whose control points, pushed out by the patch radius, fall outside one side of that frustum. The base terrain's hulls gate the test on the baked `FrustumCull` flag; the patch system's `volumetricterrain4`, `volumetricterrainhullclip4`, and `volumetricterrainwaterclip4` hulls test unconditionally, so clearing the type's flag (which `jc3vr_render::hooks::graphics_engine::scene::terrain_cull` does for the scene cull) does not reach them. An eye rendered on its own, and each multipass dispatch, bakes its own frustum there, so the cull is exact. Under the collapse the bake is the centred camera's: eye 0's projection with no cant, its clip range stretched over the double-wide target. A canted left eye sees past its left edge, so distant terrain at the outer edge of a canted left eye goes missing as a hard, patch-shaped cut; the right eye's outer side lies inside the stretched clip range. The domain programs read each eye's own rows inside the routed range, so the bake reaches nothing but the cull.

`single_pass.terrain_cull_both_eyes` substitutes, for a bake inside the routed range under the collapse, an offset view-projection whose `x`, `y`, and `w` columns come from the projection bounding both eyes exactly (each eye's frustum corners taken in the head frame, plus the lateral margin for the eyes' offset; `jc3vr_view::vr::frame::eyes_union_projection_standard`) and whose depth column is the engine's, so the near and far tests are unchanged. The per-slot cache is stamped stale whenever the substitution changes between bakes, so a reflection pass outside the range still bakes the engine's matrix. The cull only drops what the clipper would also drop, so the substitution changes no pixel an eye's own frustum covers. It changes a constant, not a draw.
