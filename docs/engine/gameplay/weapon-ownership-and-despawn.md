# Weapon ownership, despawn, and saves

What keeps a weapon object alive and in the world, how the spawn system decides to remove one, how a weapon moves into and out of an inventory slot without being dropped, and what a load and a save do to the player's weapons. The gives, removals, and mission caches are in [weapon-gives-and-restores.md](weapon-gives-and-restores.md). The pickup, equip, and drop themselves are in [weapon-equip-and-drop.md](weapon-equip-and-drop.md), and a weapon lying in the world in [item-pickup.md](item-pickup.md).

## A weapon is kept by strong references

Every weapon is spawned by the spawn system, which keeps a strong reference to each object it spawned. An inventory slot ([`Inventory::m_Weapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_Weapons)) is another strong reference, and so is any reference something else takes by locking the weapon's weak reference to itself ([`WeaponBase::m_WeakThis`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_WeakThis)). The object is destroyed when its last strong reference goes.

The spawn system's per-frame despawn ranking ([`SpawnSystem::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-Update)) reads the strong count of each spawned object's control block. An object whose count is not one, the spawn system's own reference, is held by something else: it ranks zero and is never queued for despawn, nor chosen when its budget pool is over its instance limit. Any other object is ranked from its distance to the streaming centre and the view, whether it is dead, and its interest; past its pool's threshold it is queued for despawn, and the pool's highest-ranked object is queued when the pool is over its limit. The object's mission-critical flag protects it only while a mission or an encounter is active, and is cleared otherwise.

So a weapon in a slot is never despawned by the ranking, and a dropped weapon is despawned once its rank passes its pool's threshold or its pool is over budget: there is no despawn timer on the weapon. A weapon anything else holds a strong reference to is kept the same way as a slot keeps it, and released, it is ranked again from the next frame. Explicit despawns (area clean-ups, despawn volumes, mission clean-ups) and the formula of the rank are in the symbol dump only and unverified here.

## Into and out of a slot without a drop

[`Inventory::RemoveWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RemoveWeapon) takes a weapon out of its slot and releases the slot's reference, and nothing else: no drop, no despawn, and the weapon's state, user, and holder ([`WeaponBase::m_Holder`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Holder)) stay as they were. A weapon some other strong reference holds therefore stays holstered on its character, outside the inventory.

[`Inventory::AddWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-AddWeapon) puts a weapon into its slot. An occupant other than the weapon is dropped (or despawned when the slot's drop-old flag is clear) first, so a slot emptied with `RemoveWeapon` beforehand loses nothing. It then runs the weapon's pickup virtual ([`WeaponBase::OnPickup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-OnPickup)) with the character as the holder and, with the equip modes ([`EquipMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::EquipMode)), unequips the wielded weapon and equips the new one.

`OnPickup` on its own takes a weapon into a character's keeping without any slot: it sets the holder, puts the weapon in the holstered state (its bodies out of the world), disables its pickup interaction and hint, and for the locally controlled character sets the avatar ready for combat and shows the weapon in its slot's arm of the weapon selection ([`HUDUI::OnPickupWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-OnPickupWeapon)). [`HUDUI::InitWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-InitWeapons) rebuilds the selection from the inventory's slots.

A weapon's placement ([`WeaponBase::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##vfunc-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) places its guns on its holder's bones for its state, whoever calls it; the inventory's placement calls it for each slot's weapon. [`Inventory::UpdateWeaponVisibility`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponVisibility) shows or hides the slots' weapons with their character. Neither reaches a weapon outside the slots.

## The spawn reset and the respawn table

[`SpawnSystem::ResetSystem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-ResetSystem) sends `spawn.reset_all_spawn_rules` at once, destroys every pending spawn request, releases its references to every spawned object, and posts `spawn.post_reset_all_spawn_rules`, which its receivers handle later. An object something else still holds outlives the reset, but the spawn system no longer knows it.

### What resets

Two callers matter. The load game state's entry ([`GameStateLoad::OnEnter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateLoad##func-OnEnter)) runs its reset step ([`GameStateLoad::ResetSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateLoad##func-ResetSystems), which the disassembler mislabels as a console lock getter): it aborts an active challenge unless the no-unload flag is set, resets the dialogue, resets the spawn system, and resets the heat. The frontend's reset ([`GameStateFrontend::ResetSystems`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameStateFrontend##func-ResetSystems)) resets it on the quit to the menu.

Every entry to the load state therefore resets. The load state is entered for a load, a continue, a new game, and a world reload (unverified: read from the symbol dump), and by every player teleport that asks for it ([`GameWorld::TeleportPlayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game_world::GameWorld##vfunc-TeleportPlayer) with its load-state argument). The player's respawn ([`Player::ReInit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-ReInit)) always teleports with the load state, and so, per the symbol dump, do the fast travels, the teleports to a restart point and to a mission's start, a challenge's retry, restart, and abort, the attack-wave and destruction challenges, and the map's teleport. `ReInit` is called by the restart from a restart point ([`MissionManager::GoToRestartPoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/mission::MissionManager##func-GoToRestartPoint)) and by the player's post-physics update; `GoToRestartPoint` is called on the player's death, a mission's reset and failure, the teleport to a mission's start, and the return to a challenge's start. So a death, a mission failure or checkpoint restart, a fast travel, a challenge's start, retry, restart, or abort, a load, a mission start's teleport, and the quit to the menu all reset. A chapter change has no transition of its own, and resets only if it teleports with the load state (unverified). Teleports without the load state (a placed teleport object by default, the teleport event handler, the player spawn manager's in-game teleport) do not reset; they force the character's neutral state at the start and respawn the player's gear at the end (unverified: read from the symbol dump).

On a death the order is: `GoToRestartPoint` calls `ReInit`, which revives the character (forcing its neutral state, [`Character::ForceNeutralState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-ForceNeutralState), which unequips the wielded weapon), returns to normal aim, teleports with the load state, fills the slot weapons' reserves ([`Inventory::GiveMaxAmmoToAllWeaponInInventory`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GiveMaxAmmoToAllWeaponInInventory)), and writes the inventory's reset flags ([`Inventory::m_SpawnSystemResetFlags`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemResetFlags)). With a mission active, `GoToRestartPoint` then replays the checkpoint's weapons ([weapon-gives-and-restores.md](weapon-gives-and-restores.md#the-mission-managers-checkpoint-cache)), whose gives are written into the respawn table before the reset. The load state resets on the next frame.

### Which entry is which

The reset itself does not say what it is for, and by the time it runs the respawn has already revived the character, so the health is full again at its pre-reset event. What precedes it does say:

- `ReInit` revives the character and teleports with the load state, and nothing else calls the revive with that teleport. Its callers are `GoToRestartPoint`, twice (with the restart point's transform, and with none when the restart point is not found, which respawns at the default location), and the player's post-physics update on a request flag of the player's, also with no transform. So every `GoToRestartPoint` (a death, a mission's reset or failure, the teleport to a mission's start, the return to a challenge's start) is a respawn: the character is revived and its neutral state forced.
- The frontend's reset is the quit to the menu; what follows is a load or a new game.
- A load of the player's save data comes before the reset of a load or a continue.
- Any other entry (a fast travel, a challenge's start, retry, restart, or abort, the map's teleport) is a teleport that keeps the character as it is.

### The inventory's handlers

Each inventory subscribes both events ([`m_SpawnSystemPreReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_SpawnSystemPreReset) and `m_SpawnSystemPostReset`) and handles them in [`Inventory::HandleEvent`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-HandleEvent), telling them apart with [`EventMatches`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/event##func-EventMatches).

On the pre-reset event it refreshes the HUD's weapons and fills the respawn table ([`Inventory::m_WeaponToSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawn)) slot by slot. With [`RESPAWN_RESET_LOADOUT`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::SpawnSystemResetFlags##flag-RESPAWN_RESET_LOADOUT) set it copies the reset loadout ([`m_WeaponToSpawnReset`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnReset)) in; otherwise, for each slot with no pending entry, a weapon, and not the heavy slot, it records the occupant's type with its magazine (with [`FULL_MAGAZINES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::SpawnSystemResetFlags##flag-FULL_MAGAZINES), a full magazine and the extra-ammunition flag). It clears the pending spawns ([`m_WeaponPendingSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponPendingSpawn)), records the selected slot as the equipped slot ([`m_WeaponToSpawnEquippedWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToSpawnEquippedWeapon)), does the same for the grappling device, and clears the flags.

On the post-reset event it marks all eight slots for removal ([`m_WeaponToRemove`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_WeaponToRemove)), clears the character's next selected slot, requests the spawn of every slot's entry ([`Inventory::RequestWeaponSpawn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RequestWeaponSpawn)) at the character, and requests the grappling device ([`RequestSpawnGrapplingDevice`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RequestSpawnGrapplingDevice)). The disassembler swaps the names of these two requests.

The next pre-physics update ([`Inventory::UpdatePrePhysics`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdatePrePhysics)) carries out the marks: a mounted weapon is stopped and released, and any other occupant is despawned. Each arriving respawn ([`Inventory::WeaponSpawned`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-WeaponSpawned)) clears its slot's mark before the add replaces the occupant, dropping or despawning it by the entry's drop-old flag ([weapon-gives-and-restores.md](weapon-gives-and-restores.md#the-give)).

A weapon's type is its spawn tag index ([`GameObject::GetUniqueSpawnTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##func-GetUniqueSpawnTagIndex)), and its persistent form is the tag's hash ([`SpawnSystem::GetTagHashFromTagIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/spawn_system::SpawnSystem##func-GetTagHashFromTagIndex), back with `GetTagIndexFromTagHash`, which gives `-1` for a hash no loaded tag has).

### What survives

Every reset replaces every slot weapon with a new object of the same type, equips the selected slot's, and keeps each magazine (or fills it with [`FULL_MAGAZINES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::SpawnSystemResetFlags##flag-FULL_MAGAZINES)). No slot weapon object survives a reset, and the selection survives only as the equipped slot. A weapon held outside the slots by another strong reference survives as an object, but its spawn-system ownership is gone and it stays where it was before the teleport.

## What a save stores

The player's part of a save ([`PlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/save::PlayerSaveData)) is stored by [`StorePlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-StorePlayerSaveData): each slot's weapon type as a tag hash (zero for an empty slot, and always zero for the heavy slot), every reserve, the grappling device's type, the player's upgrade name, two bavarium upgrade flags, and the downloadable content bits. No magazine, no selected slot, and nothing else per weapon is stored. Upgrades are the profile's, not the weapon's.

[`LoadPlayerSaveData`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/save##func-LoadPlayerSaveData) reads them back, resets the respawn table, writes each known type's tag index into it with a full magazine, and sets each reserve. It spawns nothing: the post-reset event's handler spawns the table, and the player's post-physics update re-requests the table's unspawned entries once the save data has loaded ([`Inventory::RespawnPlayerGear`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-RespawnPlayerGear)). Whether the post-reset event is handled after the load in every load path is unverified.

The save reads only the slots and the reserves. Reserves are global per ammunition type, so a weapon outside the slots shares its type's reserve with the slot weapons.

## Mission weapon caches

Missions, the graph script, and the challenges take the player's weapons away and give them back through three caches: the mission manager's checkpoint snapshot, the graph script's weapon cache with its own give-back ([`NodeWeaponCache::RestoreWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graph_script::NodeWeaponCache##func-RestoreWeapons)), and the challenges' records. They are in [weapon-gives-and-restores.md](weapon-gives-and-restores.md).
