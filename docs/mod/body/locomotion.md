# Stick locomotion and the steady view

On foot the game moves Rico by his clips: the movement task's speed is the animation's root speed ([engine: the locomotion task](../../engine/character/root-motion-and-proxy.md#the-locomotion-task-runs-at-idle-and-writes-the-proxy-input-directly)), so starts wind up, stops decay, turning starts swing the body, and a run surges with every step. A view hung from the animated head carries all of that, plus the walk's bob and the idle's sway, into the headset. The stick locomotion takes the velocity and the view away from the animation. The code is `crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/locomotion/`, tuned by `crates/jc3vr-body/tuning/locomotion.toml`. Its one switch is `SMOOTH STICK MOVEMENT` on the comfort page (`controllers.locomotion.stick.enabled`); the debug UI's On foot feature shows the velocity, act filter, watchdog, steady view, and neutral head status.

## The velocity

`locomotion::after_task` runs after [`NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_MovementLocomotionTask##func-Update) on the ticks the task wrote the proxy's velocity, and replaces its horizontal part through [`Character::SetWantedWorldVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-SetWantedWorldVelocity), keeping the vertical. It is the first of the on-foot contributions to the wanted velocity ([physics-substrate.md](physics-substrate.md#the-wanted-velocity)), before the palms' push and the rig's step drive ([rig.md](rig.md#who-moves-the-root)).

The direction is the move stick turned into the world by the view's heading; Rico's facing never steers it. The target speed is the game's own, read from [`ObjectBlackboard::SPEED_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard::SPEED_ID), which [`SetupTargetSpeeds`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_InputLocoSetTargetSpeedsTask##func-SetupTargetSpeeds) sets to the walk, run, or backpedal speed and zeroes at a drop or a wall, so distances stay balanced and the ledge stop works. The velocity moves toward the target at fixed acceleration and deceleration rates (`accel::step`), so starts, stops, and turns never overshoot and the view feels only a steady push.

After a gap in the task (a jump, a fall, a vehicle exit) the integrator starts from the proxy's real velocity, so a landing carries its speed. A velocity above the target coasts down, but never faster than the proxy's own horizontal speed (`accel::coast_limited`), so what slows the body slows the coast and a fast reel's end does not leave the reel's speed for the next step. While the animation carries the body (an authored motion or a hit reaction's hold, per the procedural body's [hand-over table](procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)) the task's velocity passes through untouched, since that root motion is the motion.

## The turning animations

With the stick driving the velocity, the turning animations would only swing the body, and the view with it. The act filter (`locomotion::acts`, an owner in the [act arbiter](act-arbiter.md)) handles the local player's acts while the headset drives the view and the player is on the ground:

- Turn-in-place acts (rotate-on-spot and its warp flavors, the rotation starts, the quarter and half turns, and the heavy weapon's aim-focus rotations from [`QueueAimRelativeOnSpotActions`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueAimRelativeOnSpotActions)) are dropped, and the orientation executor yaws the root toward the rig's yaw instead. They pass while the rig is grounded and Rico stands still, so a body turn plays the game's own turn-in-place ([rig.md](rig.md#the-yaw)), unless the IK body plants the feet.
- Turning starts, stops, and flicks ([`QueueFlickStick`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueFlickStick), [`QueueAimFlickStick`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueAimFlickStick)) are replaced by their straight versions, so the state machine still passes through its start, stop, and flick states. A replacement the state machine would refuse ([`AnimatedModel::TryAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-TryAct)) queues the original.
- The stunt turns ([`QueueStuntTurns`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-QueueStuntTurns)), which turn the body toward the stick on a vehicle's roof where the view rides the body, are all replaced by `ACT_TO_IDLE`, the act the game queues in their place when the stick points straight ahead, and dropped on a refusal. The game queues them only while it applies its stunt modification ([`Character::ShouldApplyStuntModification`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-ShouldApplyStuntModification)), where the mode detection may read a vehicle, so they have their own scope that needs neither the on-foot mode nor the stick switch.

A filter that removes acts can leave the state machine waiting for one it never gets. On foot the filter acts in short bursts, so a watchdog lets every act through for a while when it has acted without a break for too long, and logs a warning. The stunt scope stays out of the watchdog: the input tasks queue a stunt turn every tick the stick points at a side with no stunt area, and its replacement is the game's own no-turn act, so nothing is left waiting.

## The steady view

`locomotion::anchor::stabilize` runs at the anchor capture at the start of pose finalization (`hooks::character::body_ik`), once per sim tick, and decides what the view hangs from: Rico's animated head, or the root-fixed anchor, which is the root ([`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1), with `T0` for the pose pair's previous tick) carrying a neutral standing head straight above it. No clip moves that anchor, so the view moves only with the root and the player's own head. The head stands straight above the root, not at its small horizontal offset, which would swing round as the body turns under the view ([rig.md](rig.md#the-body-turning-under-the-view)).

In the headset the hand-over table's case decides (`Case::view_holds_root`, in `controllers::body::procedural::authority`). The view holds the root through everything that happens to Rico on foot: walking, jumps, hand climbing, falls and landings, stumbles, authored one-offs, a ragdoll and its get-up, and death, since the player stands on their own feet through all of them. It keeps the animated head where the player rides something the game moves: vehicles and their stunt positions, the grapple's traversals, hangs, and ledge climb, swimming, the wingsuit (including the transition into it), the parachute, and scripted sequences. Outside the headset the mode decides instead: the root on foot and through a jump's grace period, the animated head everywhere else.

The neutral pose is measured, not assumed: the animated head, neck, and eyes are averaged while Rico stands still on the ground, once per session (the On foot feature can measure it again), and until then the view keeps the animated head. Switching anchors blends over a short eased window. The anchor also publishes whether Rico is off the ground on foot before the mode detection reports a fall (`AnchorStatus::airborne`), for the [wingsuit gestures](../input/flight.md#opening-and-closing).

### Following the head off the root

A ragdoll, its get-up, and a death (`Case::view_follows_head`) carry Rico far from the root for seconds, so there the view follows his animated head's position, staying upright with the neck model's eyes (`locomotion::view_follow`). The follower eases the head's offset from the root and adds the root back each tick, so the body's own motion carries the view uncapped. A landing, fall, stumble, one-off, or stopped task (`Case::view_may_escape`) escapes to the follower too when it carries the head far enough from the root horizontally or below it for long enough, as a fast fall's landing that pitches Rico forward does; a landing's dip is too short to. A handover from a head-held case (the wingsuit, a vehicle) with the head already past that distance starts escaped (`Escape::seed`), so the view rises with Rico after a crash rather than blending through his body.

### The camera's anchor and its geometry

In the headset the anchor moves only the camera: `stabilize` returns the camera's head, neck delta, and eye arm separately from the anatomy's, the head pose composes from the camera's (`headpose::anchors::Geometry::view_head`), and `headpose::anchor` reports the same head so a consumer subtracting it has the player's tracked offset. Outside the headset both use the blended anchors.

The root-held camera hangs its eyes from the neutral head by the tracked neck model (`locomotion::view::neck_model_anchors`): the head bone is the neck pivot and the eyes sit `body_rig::body::EYES_TO_NECK` from it. The rig and the body read the player's neck pivot by the same offset, so at the recenter's orientation the pivot is the neutral head and Rico's head bone, which the body aims at the pivot ([procedural-body.md](procedural-body.md#the-torso-on-the-animations-hips)), stands there unbent.

Three corrections keep the camera out of geometry and level:

- The floor: the head never stands lower than a clearance over the ground under Rico (`headpose::anchors::Geometry::floor`, from the rule system's ground height, water excluded), so a crash recovery or a death clip cannot put the camera under the road. The cases that hold the body off the ground below it (`Case::head_may_lie`: a seat, a hang, a climb, a swim) are exempt, since the rule system zeroes the reading while the proxy is supported.
- The upright frame: the root-held head stands straight up in the world, and the view's frame turns toward the root's upright heading (`Geometry::view_frame`), so a ragdoll's tilted root never tilts the view.
- The face clearance: on the animated head the eyes are the clip's eye bones, which sit inside the skull, so a clip that folds the chest toward the face or lays the head back would put the chest past the near plane. While the game poses the body out of a seat (`Case::game_poses_body`), the eyes are held a few centimetres out along the face's forward, eased in and out, returned as a model-space offset (`Stabilized::face_offset`) that the capture adds to the arm it rebuilds in the filtered head frame.

On the ground (not [`Character::IsInAir`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInAir)) the root's height is smoothed by a critically damped follower with a capped lag, so the proxy's stair pops read as a ramp; leaving the ground winds the smoothing out quickly, and a large change (a drop, a teleport) passes at once.

Because the camera's anchor contains no animation, nothing composed onto it does either: the roomscale cockpit, the head-height remap, and the body IK's head target all measure the player's head against the root-fixed head.
