# Havok bodies, the physics step, and collision filtering

How the game's physics advances, where code can change the world before a step, how the engine makes a free dynamic body of its own and tears it down, and how the collision filter decides what touches what. The engine is Havok Physics 2014.1 in its newer form (`hknpWorld`, `hknpBody`, `hknpMotion`), not the older `hkpWorld` family. A loose object's body, its motion and owner, and the velocity setters are in [item-pickup.md](../gameplay/item-pickup.md#a-body-in-the-havok-world).

## The physics step

The physics system ([`PhysicsSystem`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem)) owns one Havok world ([`PhysicsSystem::m_World`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##field-m_World)), made by its constructor when the application starts and kept for the whole session. Loading a save, dying, and streaming the map change what is in the world, not the world itself.

[`PhysicsSystem::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-Update) advances it. The game's update ([`Game::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::Game##func-Update)) runs the current game state's update (`CGame::UpdateGame`), and `CGameStateRun::Update`, like the loading and front-end states' updates, calls the physics update inline, which calls `PhysicsSystem::Update` and then dispatches the queued collisions, the vehicles' post-collision work, and the damage listeners' events. So the physics steps on the game thread, inside the game's update, between the state update's pre-simulation work and the character updates that follow it.

`Update` fires the pre-update signal, clears the collision array, and updates the bodies that do not simulate. It then returns without stepping while either of its two suspend flags is set; `CGameStateRun::Update` sets one of them from its update context's pause. Otherwise, when the world exists and the time it was given is above a small threshold, it fires the pre-simulation callbacks and steps the world exactly once by [`PhysicsSystem::STEP_SECONDS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem), whatever time it was given: the collide phase and the solve phase run as tasks on the physics thread pool, which the call joins before it returns. One update is one step; the game does not sub-step.

### Pre-simulation callbacks

A pre-simulation callback ([`PfxPreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback)) is a C++ object with a three-slot virtual table: a destructor, [`preSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback##vfunc-preSimulationCallback) with the world and the time `Update` was given, and [`onWorldShiftCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxPreSimulationCallback##vfunc-onWorldShiftCallback) with the shift of a world recentring. The ragdolls, the grappling hook's wire, and the AI world register one.

[`PhysicsSystem::AddPreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-AddPreSimulationCallback) appends to a `std::vector` of callback pointers; [`RemovePreSimulationCallback`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-RemovePreSimulationCallback) clears the matching entries without erasing them. The walk that fires the callbacks, before each step, calls each non-null entry and compacts the cleared ones away as it meets them; the world-shift walk does the same with the shift slot. So a callback may remove itself or another while they fire, but an addition then could reallocate the vector under the walk. The engine never deletes a callback; its owner removes it and frees it.

A callback runs on the game thread, outside the step, with the world otherwise idle: it is where the ragdolls and the wire change the world before the step integrates it. A velocity set there is what the step integrates; a body made, added, or removed there is in or out of that step.

### World recentring

The Havok world is simulated about a moving origin. A Havok-space position plus [`PhysicsSystem::m_AccumulatedShift`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##field-m_AccumulatedShift) is the game-space position. When the camera strays far enough from the origin, `CPhysicsSystem::RecenterWorld` adds to the shift, moves every body and the broad phase by its opposite, and then calls each callback's world-shift slot. A body's game-space pose does not change across a recentring; only code that keeps Havok-space positions of its own has to move them.

## Making a free dynamic body

The grappling hook's hang proxy (`CGrapplingHangPhysicsProxy::Init`) makes a dynamic capsule that belongs to no game object, and its sequence is the template for any such body:

1. Construct a body construction info ([`hknpBodyCinfo::Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpBodyCinfo##func-Construct)), which sets no reserved id, no motion, the default quality and material, a zero collision-filter word, no name or local frame, and the identity orientation.
2. Make the shape. [`hknpCapsuleShape::createCapsuleShape`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpCapsuleShape##func-createCapsuleShape) allocates a capsule from the calling thread's Havok heap with the segment's two end points in body space and the radius, and returns it with one reference, the caller's. Set it as [`hknpBodyCinfo::m_shape`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpBodyCinfo##field-m_shape), and the Havok-space position and orientation in the info.
3. Construct a motion construction info ([`hknpMotionCinfo::Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpMotionCinfo##func-Construct)) and fill its mass properties from the body info with [`initializeWithMass`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpMotionCinfo##func-initializeWithMass): the inverse mass, the inertia from the shape, and the centre of mass and orientation from the body's pose. The shape, position, and orientation must be set first. The hang proxy overrides the inertia with a capsule's afterwards; that is not needed. [`hknpMotionCinfo::m_enableDeactivation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hknpMotionCinfo##field-m_enableDeactivation) starts set, which lets a resting body fall asleep.
4. Create the body with [`hknpWorld::createDynamicBody`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpWorld##func-createDynamicBody), passing [`DO_NOT_ADD`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpAdditionMode##variant-DO_NOT_ADD) to leave it out of the simulation. It copies the body info, creates the motion, creates the body (taking a reference of its own to the shape), and writes the new body id through its second argument. The disassembler names the body creation it calls `hknpWorld::setBodyShape`.
5. Allocate the wrapper with the engine's [`OperatorNew`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/memory##func-OperatorNew) and construct it ([`PfxRigidBody::Construct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-Construct)) with the body id, an empty data pointer, and a clear add flag. The wrapper is an engine physics instance ([`PfxInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance)): it sets itself as the body's instance property, which every query, listener, and damage path reads to find a body's owner, and its owner ([`PfxInstance::m_GameObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxInstance##field-m_GameObject)) starts null, as the hang proxy's stays. It also sets a body flag and writes the body's collision-filter word to [`NO_COLLISION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-NO_COLLISION).
6. Give the body its collision layer and system group ([`PfxRigidBody::SetCollisionLayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetCollisionLayer), [`SetCollisionSystemGroup`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetCollisionSystemGroup)); the hang proxy takes a fresh group from the filter. Its damping, quality, and gravity factor ([`SetGravityFactor`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##func-SetGravityFactor), which copies the motion's properties into the wrapper, changes them, and gives the motion the changed properties) may be set here too.
7. Release the caller's reference to the shape. A Havok object's count is the low half of [`hkReferencedObject::m_memSizeAndRefCount`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkReferencedObject##field-m_memSizeAndRefCount); the release is inline in the game's code: an object whose allocation-size half is zero is not counted, and otherwise the count is decremented atomically and an object whose count was one is destroyed through its [`deleteThisReferencedObject`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/havok::hkReferencedObject##vfunc-deleteThisReferencedObject) slot.

Shapes and other Havok heap objects come from the Havok memory router of the calling thread (a thread-local), which the game thread and the physics workers have. The steps above run on the game thread outside the step.

### The wrapper's lifecycle

The wrapper's virtual table carries the rest of the body's life:

- [`AddToWorld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-AddToWorld) adds the body, with any body attached to it, to the simulation at once when it is not already in it, and [`IsAddedToWorld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-IsAddedToWorld) says whether it is.
- [`RemoveFromWorld`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-RemoveFromWorld) takes it out again; the body keeps existing and can be added later.
- [`GetTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-GetTransform) and [`SetTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-SetTransform) read and place the body in game space, converting with the accumulated shift. The transform's rows are the body's axes, as in the game's other matrices. Placing keeps the velocities.
- The deleting destructor ([`Destruct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody##vfunc-Destruct)) removes the body from the simulation, clears its instance property, destroys it, and, with [`DESTRUCT_FREE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxRigidBody), returns the wrapper's memory to the engine allocator. Deleting the wrapper is the whole teardown.

Several of these are mislabelled in the disassembler, which the pyxis items note: the destructor is named `CPfxRagdollProxyPhantom::Destruct`, `AddToWorld` is named after the ragdoll proxy phantom, `SetTransform` is named `CPartInstance::ClearWindowDamage`, `SetCollisionLayer` is named `boost::shared_ptr<CAnimationRuleSystem>::operator->`, and the filter's group counter (`CPhysicsSystem::GetNewSystemGroup`) is named `CAnimatedModel::GetModel`.

## Collision filtering

The world's collision filter is a constraint collision filter (which also keeps bodies joined by a constraint apart) over the engine's group filter ([`CustomGroupCollisionFilter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::CustomGroupCollisionFilter)). It reads each body's collision-filter word ([`hknpBody::m_collisionFilterInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::hknpBody##field-m_collisionFilterInfo)), which holds a layer, a subsystem id, a no-collide id, and a system group in the high half:

- Two bodies in different system groups, or both in system group zero, collide when the filter's layer table says their layers do.
- Two bodies in the same non-zero system group collide only when their layers are equal, at least one has a non-zero subsystem id, and neither's subsystem id is the other's no-collide id. A ragdoll's parts use this to collide with one another selectively.

Every character proxy gets a system group of its own when it is created, with the proxy on [`CHARACTER_COLLISION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-CHARACTER_COLLISION) and a zero subsystem id. A body put in a character proxy's group on another layer with a zero subsystem id therefore never touches that proxy, nor anything else in the group with a zero subsystem id.

The layers are [`PfxLayer`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer). The physics system's constructor fills the table pair by pair. The rows that matter for a body that should meet the world and characters but not queries:

- [`WEAPONS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-WEAPONS) collides with the static, terrain, dynamic, destruction, character collision, character ragdoll, and character-attached dynamic layers, with the three vehicle collision layers and the vehicle raycast layer, and with the listener-effects layer. It does not collide with itself, so two such bodies never touch, and it does not collide with [`RAYCAST_EVERYTHING_PHYSICAL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-RAYCAST_EVERYTHING_PHYSICAL), the layer the player's aim query uses, or with the trigger and explosion shape-query layers.
- [`CHARACTER_ATTACHED_DYNAMIC_RB`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-CHARACTER_ATTACHED_DYNAMIC_RB), the grappling hook's hang proxy's layer, collides with the static, terrain, dynamic, destruction, and character collision layers and the vehicle layers, but not with ragdolls.
- [`FULL_COLLISION`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PfxLayer##variant-FULL_COLLISION) collides with everything, queries included.

The vehicle raycast layer meeting the weapons layer means a vehicle's wheel rays can hit a weapons-layer body. Whether the explosion and trigger queries, and the AI's obstacle tracking, see such a body in other ways is unverified.

## Unverified

- Which body quality the hang proxy's quality preset is: the game sets one on that proxy, and the preset's flags are not established.
- How the engine's contact and damage listeners treat a weapons-layer body whose instance has no owner when it meets a ragdoll; the hang proxy, which also has no owner, never meets one.
- The release slot and layout of the contact-impulse event on an engine physics instance.
