# Render-time anchoring

The simulation ticks at 30 Hz and the engine draws each object between its last two ticks' cached matrices, by the frame's interpolation fraction. The headset's view, the hands drawn by the late update (`payload/src/controllers/hands/late/`), and the upper body the presentation layer re-poses ([presentation-layer.md](presentation-layer.md)) are placed from the latest tracking at render time instead. Anything the mod places from a tick's data and draws relative to the player therefore has to be anchored to a render-time pose, or it is drawn where the player was up to a tick ago. At walking pace that is a few centimetres; in a car at 114 km/h it is about a metre a tick, and a falling or wingsuiting player outruns it as fast.

## The rule

Anything drawn relative to the player is anchored at render time:

- an overlay primitive placed in the cockpit frame, or from a point that rides with the player, is anchored to the head (`Anchor::Head`, an offset in world axes from the eyes' render-time position, the render camera's) or to a hand (`Anchor::Hand`, an offset in one of the hand's poses as it is drawn at render time, `late::drawn_hands`, which stays on the hand where the hand falls short of the controller). `Anchor::World` is for points that lie in the world: a grapple target, a gun's hit point, a pickup on the ground;
- a model the mod holds in a hand is re-pushed at render time from the hand's render-time pose or correction, after the engine's own render update has pushed its interpolated matrix;
- a model the mod places on the body keeps its interpolation: both of its cached matrices are written on the tick, so the engine draws it between them with the body it rides.

The published head (`headpose::query`) follows Rico's head joint and the HMD's travel. The camera hangs its eye point off that position by the arm from the head joint to Rico's eye midpoint, ten to fifteen centimetres forward and up, and `hooks::camera::eye_points` is the one place that hangs it: the camera writes its T0/T1 pair from it, and everything placed from the cockpit takes its origin from it. `CockpitTransform::current` maps the cockpit head (the HMD's eye midpoint) to the current eye point (`hooks::camera::eye_point`), so the hands, the aim rays, the body's grab volumes, the vehicles' controls, and the cockpit-frame markers land where the player sees their own hands and body through the rendered eyes. The late update composes the render-time hands on both sides of the head pose pair from that side's eye point, and interpolates them attached to the camera's pose, so a hand rides the camera across the frame. A head anchor's offset is taken from the tick's eye point and resolves against the render camera's position. Composed from the published head instead, every hand and marker would sit that arm behind and below where the player sees it, about 0.19 m from the real hand on the 6 November take with the reach clamp, and resolved against the camera from the published head, a marker would float that arm ahead of what it marks.

While the camera is not placed from the headpose (an authored view), the rendered camera is not at the eye point, and the head anchors resolve against the camera while the hands stay composed from the eye point. The head-through-geometry probe casts from the same eye point, and the recorder records it as the view's head position, so a recording places the cockpit as the hands were composed.

## Moving-frame sampling

The late hand frame captures the character transport pair after the head pair is published. Wrist references interpolate in the character's local frame, then use the same interpolated character transform as the skeleton. Render-time controller poses interpolate relative to their head transport pair. This preserves the arc of a turn rather than interpolating world positions across it. The gun mesh uses the same character-relative interpolation before the hand correction is applied. The existing root transport supplies platform movement; these consumers add no platform velocity.

The physical-hand render offset is sampled from the current body after simulation and frozen before the render hands are composed. Its reference is the palm the last step drove the body toward: the published palm target from `physics::state::acting_palms`, including the physical gun's support and weight target, led by the palm's world velocity over the step's lead (`Target::led`). Measured against the published palm without that lead, a free body reads as a hand ahead of its controller by the palm's motion over the lead, which is the hand's swing plus the player's own travel: a few centimetres at rest, a third of a metre in a sprint, and more at a low frame rate, where the lead grows with the frame. The late update then carries the drawn hand that far off the end of the arm. Subtracting a raw controller reference would cancel or duplicate the support and weight. The residual is converted to the publication's cockpit frame before the render head pair transports it. The render path does not blend that residual one physics step behind. A failed body read uses the latest bounded pre-step residual. The sampler never drives or places a body.

Gun parts use the ownership of the held or wielded weapon, including a weapon retained in a hand while the native state is holstered in a vehicle. Each paired gun keeps its hand. A part that rides the gun interpolates its local transform relative to that gun and is composed beneath the gun's rendered parent. Slide, bolt, barrel, and magazine animation therefore retains its relative travel. A part placed explicitly in a reload hand follows that hand instead. A weapon that is actually on a body socket receives no hand correction.

The collider view discovers bodies and shape geometry at physics-step cadence, then refreshes retained body transforms and broad-phase boxes every rendered frame. It draws the current collision pose, without delayed snapshot interpolation or a hand correction. Contact rings remain at their recorded world positions. The readout reports the physics-step identity, pose-read count, render fraction, and pose sample age separately. For a held gun, it compares the submitted mesh pose with the current body identified by that hand's held-gun record. A mesh/body gap is a timing or pose observation; it is not a measurement of native hull fidelity. The collider sample runs after the engine's render update so it can compare that frame's submitted mesh rather than the preceding frame's matrix.

## Verification limits

Pure-logic regressions cover parent-relative translation and rotation, immediate physical-offset sampling, held ownership through holstered vehicle states, local action travel beneath a moving parent, and current collision display independent of the render fraction. These checks do not establish game or headset acceptance. Standing-platform translation, acceleration, braking, and turning remain separate from seated travel. Runtime checks must also cover both hands, gun transfer, reload-hand placement, action cycling, and actual contacts with the collider view enabled. Removing the extra interpolation delay can expose physics-step jitter; it must not be tuned by moving current collision geometry onto a delayed mesh.

## Where it applies

Overlay markers and readouts:

- The grip targets' markers (`grab/markers.rs`, [grip.md](../input/grip.md#markers)): a marker in the cockpit frame (the body's sockets and pouches, the vehicle's controls and buttons, the parachute's handles) is anchored to the head; a marker on a held gun (its grip, foregrip, action, and well) to the holding hand's grip; and only a marker on what lies in the world (an object, a weapon, a door, a character, a surface to climb) is a world anchor.
- The grab view (`physical_hands/grab_view/`, [grab-view.md](../input/grab-view.md#anchoring)): its palm markers and the hands' ends of its lines are anchored to the hands, a target's end where the target's grip marker is, its reach rays to the head, and its door handles, candidates, and the physical hands' refusals to the world.
- The wingsuit guide is anchored to the hands.
- The physical hands' palm boxes, their press rings and shove lines, and the line from a held object's grab point to where the hand would have it (`physical_hands/draw.rs`) are anchored to the head.
- The debug panel (`hud/egui_panel/`, [hud.md](../hud.md#where-the-debug-panel-lives)) is placed in the cockpit frame and composed into the world on eye 0 through the render-time head, and while a hand holds it, from that hand's aim pose in the controller frame sampled for the frame being drawn.
- The body mirror's figure is anchored to the head. The grenade fuse ring, the wrist detonator, the ghost hands, the gizmos, and the debug panel's pointer are anchored to the hands. The gun's laser starts at the hand and ends at its hit, a world point.

Models in a hand:

- A held gun's model and its separate parts are re-pushed by the late update with the holding hand's correction ([`WeaponBase::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdateRender) and the parts' render update, detoured).
- The grenade primed from the pouch, a live grenade a hand caught, and the C4 charge primed from the pouch are re-pushed at the grip of the hand as it is drawn (`hands/held.rs`), through post-call detours on [`Explosive::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-UpdateRender) (the grenade's model, [`m_ModelInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##field-m_ModelInstance)) and [`PlantedExplosive::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::PlantedExplosive##func-UpdateRender) (the charge's in-hand model, [`m_InactiveModelInstance`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::PlantedExplosive##field-m_InactiveModelInstance)). The throws still place each on the tick, which moves its physics and its effects; each registers what its hand holds every tick, clears the registration when it lets go or throws, and a registration not refreshed lapses after a little more than a tick. The caught grenade is carried to the hand by its physics velocity, which trails the hand further, so drawing it at the hand matters most there.

Models on the body:

- The weapons the VR inventory carries beyond the slots are placed on their sockets on the tick through the weapon's own placement, which writes both cached matrices. A weapon comes into the carry set with its render interpolation skipped, so that it does not streak from the slot's bone to its socket, and that skip lasts until [`WeaponBase::UpdatePostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdatePostFinalizePose) clears it, which only the inventory's slots receive. Left set, the placement would copy the new matrix into [`WeaponData::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_WorldMatrixT0) every tick, so a carried weapon would be drawn un-interpolated, a tick ahead of the body: the rifle pops at speed, and a pistol floats below a falling player. So the carry set ends each pass for its weapons as the game does for the slots' (`inventory::carry::end_pass`, from the hold's detour of [`Inventory::UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateInventoryPostFinalizePose)), clearing the guns' [`m_SkipRenderInterpolation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponData##field-m_SkipRenderInterpolation) and their model parts'.
- A slot's weapon on the body, the snap between a dock and the hand, and the slides of a draw and a holster are placed by the game's placement or the rigid grip's pin, so they interpolate with the body; the part of a snap in the hand rides the late update's correction.
- The pouches' models (a grenade, a C4 charge, and the held gun's magazine, [pouch-models.md](../input/pouch-models.md)) are instances the mod owns, with no cached matrices of their own. Each is placed on the tick in the character's model space, and pushed once per rendered frame as that placement between the last two ticks', carried by the character's world transform between its last two ticks', at the frame's interpolation fraction, so it interpolates with the body it rides.

The headset checklist: drive at speed and fall with a rifle and a pistol carried on the body and watch them stay on their sockets; hold a primed grenade, a caught grenade, and a C4 charge while moving the hand quickly and while driving, and check that each stays in the hand; and drive with the vehicle controls' markers, the reload well's, and the pouches' showing, and check that they stay on the wheel, the gun, and the body. With the hands composed from the rendered eyes: hold each controller in view, standing, crouched, seated in a car, swimming, and under the parachute, and check that the drawn hand covers the real hand's place (no hand floating behind and below it), including with the arm fully stretched out; look down at the pouches and sockets and check that a hand reaching for one takes it where it is drawn, and that its marker sits on it; and check that the body mirror's figure, the palm boxes, and the grab view's rays still sit on what they mark.
