# Decoupling HUD parts

The HUD renders into one texture shown on one floating panel ([hud.md](../hud.md)). Several VR features want a part of it somewhere else: the reticle at the aim point, the readouts on the hands and the wrist, the weapon selection cross at the steering hand ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)), and the world markers at their own depths. The HUD atlas does this: at each capture it moves the parts into their own cells of the HUD texture, outside the stage, and moves them back straight after, and each cell is drawn on its own quad. Markers that find no cell are cropped from the panel instead. How the engine captures and draws the UI is in [ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md).

## Why the atlas

Scaleform hands the renderer one snapshot per capture, a capture is safe only on the game thread inside `PreRender`, and the renderer consumes only the latest snapshot. Rendering the movie once per part needs a capture per part, and several captures in a row collapse to the last. A second movie instance kept in sync would have to mirror invokes, display-info writes, and tweens, and its ExternalInterface calls would land on the native handlers twice ([scaleform.md](../../engine/scaleform.md#standalone-movies-crendertotextureui)). The depth-split mechanisms tried earlier fail on capture timing or on structural edits to the render tree ([the constraints](../../issues/08-14-hud-overlays-and-depth.md#constraints-established)). Cropping one render takes whatever else is drawn in the rectangle and cuts filters that spill past it, so it is only the fallback for markers without a cell.

The atlas keeps one capture per frame at the seam the engine already uses, so every part updates at the full rate. Its only edits are node-local display-info writes, undone before the next advance, so hit tests, ActionScript, the engine's tweens, and the next `Advance` all see the original layout, and the engine's own `Render` runs unchanged. A part in its own cell cannot be overdrawn by a marker or a centre notice, and the glyph and filter caches see the same transform every frame.

## The alpha of the HUD texture

The HUD texture holds premultiplied colour with coverage alpha, which the Scaleform HAL's blend states establish ([ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md#colour-alpha-masks-and-filters)). Compositing it as straight alpha would darken antialiased text edges, glows, shadows, and fades a second time. Every composite of a HUD-style texture therefore blends premultiplied (`ONE, INV_SRC_ALPHA` on colour and alpha): the panel quad (`crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/quad/mod.rs`, shared by the debug panel, the controls summary, the readouts, and the markers) and the desktop mirror's egui overlay (`crates/jc3vr-view/src/vr/mirror/mod.rs`). The cursor dot's shader premultiplies its own output. Anything that fades a quad in its shader must scale all four channels, not alpha alone.

## World markers

The code is `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/world_markers/`; the engine side is in [ui-rendering.md](../../engine/rendering/ui-rendering.md#points-of-interest).

Classification. Inside [`HUDUI::UpdatePOIs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##func-UpdatePOIs), the `Get2DInfo` detour sees every point of interest's projection through the panel's view-projection. The panel subtends much less than the headset's view, so a marker the panel puts off screen but that lies inside the headset's field of view (both eyes' together, shrunk by `world_markers.fov_inset_deg`, or `world_markers.fallback_half_fov_deg` without a headset) is reclassified on screen and clamped into the safe area. The HUD then draws its icon rather than an edge arrow, which would point at the panel's edge for a marker plainly visible in the world.

Snapshot. At the capture seam, in the gameplay HUD mode and outside the cinematic and hidden states, the record list is walked under its lock, and each shown, live, on-screen record's id, world point, 2D offset, and clip origin is kept. The snapshot is keyed by the movie's snapshot id, and the render side picks the layout whose key matches the displayed snapshot, falling back to the newest.

Render. Stage coordinates map to the HUD texture through the show-all visible frame (`jc3vr_view::hud::stage_frame::StageFrame`). On eye 0 each marker gets a crop around its clip origin, or its atlas cell; after the UI renders, each crop is copied into its own slot texture and cut from the HUD texture, so the panel no longer shows it. Multipass repeats this on every dispatch; under the single-pass collapse it runs once on eye 0. Each slot is drawn as a billboard at its marker's world point, facing the head with the head's up, at the angular size per texel the panel shows, so it looks the size it did on the panel at any distance. Billboards draw farthest first, before the panel, without a depth test.

Depth. Each marker is drawn on the nearest visible surface along the line of sight to its point, so it never reads as farther than a wall it shows through, which would be a stereo conflict. On eye 0 a compute pass (`marker_depth_cs.hlsl`) reads the main depth buffer at each marker's pixel, keeping the farthest of a small cross of taps so a surface counts only when it covers the footprint, and the linearised depth is read back asynchronously; stale readbacks are discarded. Depth sees foliage, decals, and LODs that physics proxies miss. A marker with no recent depth falls back to a physics raycast from the head (`crates/jc3vr-body/src/controllers/aim/marker_rays.rs`) in the player aim's simulation update. The `world_markers` tuning sets the rest: a minimum surface distance so the player's own hands and gun do not catch every marker, engage and release thresholds so a marker does not hop, the pull in front of the surface, and a log-space ease of the drawn distance.

## The atlas as built

The code is `crates/jc3vr-hud/src/hud/atlas/` (the layout, the seam, and the render side), `hud/anchored/` (the hand and wrist readouts), and `hud/reticle.rs` (the aim part). It is behind `hud.atlas.enabled` (on; `HUD ON THE HANDS` on the pause menu's VR display page). The fixed extents and capture scales are in `atlas/seam.rs`, and the placements in the HUD tuning's `anchored` tables.

Room. With the atlas on, the atlas feeds [`ComputeSafeArea`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-ComputeSafeArea) the largest stage-aspect rectangle inside the texture, so the bands the show-all scaling exposes above and below the stage hold nothing the HUD lays out. Menus get the full layout back because the safe-area input switches with the HUD mode, and the seam acts only once the stage-shaped layout is applied.

Layout. `atlas/layout.rs` gives the grenade readout the right end of a band (it parks unused icons far to its right, which then fall off the texture rather than into another cell), then packs shelves of cells: the weapon, heat, and tether readouts, the selection cross (extent from [`HUDUI::m_SelectionWheelBB`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##field-m_SelectionWheelBB)), the aim part, the labelled marker's wide cell, and as many marker cells as fit. A part taller than a band is captured scaled down; markers never are. The readouts' extents are fixed from the movie's shapes, so animations that reach past them are cut at the cell. The layout is a function of the configuration and the stage, and markers keep their cells across captures (`MarkerSlots`), so a frame that pairs a snapshot with a neighbouring capture's layout still agrees.

Seam. In the [`MovieImpl::CaptureImpl`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::scaleform::MovieImpl##func-CaptureImpl) detour, in the gameplay mode and outside the cinematic and hidden [`HUDUI::m_State`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::hud_ui::HUDUI##field-m_State) states, each part's display info is read through its cached handle, converted into its parent's space, and moved by one [`SetDisplayInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::scaleform::ValueObjectInterface##func-SetDisplayInfo) write of position and scale. Markers with a cell are moved to it under the list's lock. After the original capture every moved clip is written back with the exact values read; markers are found again by their clip value, so a record removed during the capture is skipped. The selection cross moves only while the weapon wheel publishes a steering hand, and the readouts only under the controller scheme; a readout moved but not drawn (an untracked hand) is not shown.

Render. On eye 0 the capture matching the displayed snapshot is chosen, each moved part's cell is copied into its own texture after the markers' crops, and both bands are cleared, so the panel shows the stage only; multipass repeats this per dispatch. The parts draw after the panel. Redirect maintenance runs before the main [`UIManager::StartRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-StartRender) worker is queued, under the UI-deferred lock and then the graphics context lock; [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw) consumes the matching job, and only with the redirect applied calls [`UIManager::SyncRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-SyncRender) and [`UIManager::Submit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##vfunc-Submit), outside both locks, before sampling. None of this repeats the movie capture.

### The aim part

The aim part gathers the weapon reticle group, the grapple reticle's base, ghost, and attached clips, and the hit marker into one cell whose origin stands for the aim point, under either scheme. The weapon reticles and the hit marker sit at the screen centre in the movie and are carried with their offsets from it; the movie places the grapple base and ghost at the projected grapple aim point, so each is carried with its own origin; the attached clip is carried only while it sits at the base. `hud/reticle.rs` draws the cell after the panel as a billboard at the reticles' panel angular size, toward the aim point the grapple-reticle hook records ([hud.md](../hud.md#hud-depth)) at the smoothed aim depth, or along the head's forward when none was recorded for half a second. Under the controller scheme the hit marker hides with the weapon reticle, so the cell shows reticles only where the gaze aims.

### The readouts

Each gun in a hand has an ammunition counter the mod draws (`hud::anchored::hand_ammo`): its magazine and its user's reserve, red when empty. The game's weapon readout shows only the wielded weapon, and nothing in some seats, so the mod draws a counter for every gun and keeps the game's readout in its cell undrawn. A counter stands over the middle of the gun as drawn this frame (`controllers::hands::late::rendered_guns`), raised along the gun's up axis clear of its box, with its text along the gun's own axes, so it turns with the gun. It is drawn without a depth test and placed clear of the hand and gun, since depth would hide it in the gun near the face. Raised to sight along (`anchored.sight_reach_m`, `anchored.sight_cone_deg`), the gun would put the counter in the sight picture, so it fades out.

The tether readout is the game's own, with its fade, `anchored.tether.back_m` back along the off-hand's forearm from the wrist the grapple fires from, upright and facing the eye. The selection cross is drawn where the steering hand was when the wheel opened, pulled toward the head and held relative to it. The wrist readout sits on the inside of the off-hand's forearm, read with the palm up (the controls summary is on the back, so the two never face the head together), and stacks the mod's health bar, the heat readout, and the grenade readout. The health bar (`anchored/health.rs`) is the only health readout in VR, since the game's is the full-screen vignette the floating HUD suppresses: [`Character::GetHealth`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##vfunc-GetHealth) over [`m_MaxHealth`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_MaxHealth), painted on the CPU with a draining trail after a hit. The wrist shows on a glance, and also while health is low, after damage, and while the player has heat ([`HeatManager::m_Level`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/heat_manager::HeatManager##field-m_Level)).

### Hardening, cost, and limits

Pause, the comm-link map, and cutscenes put the HUD in a hidden, full-screen, or cinematic state, so nothing moves and the render side drops its frame. Every move is undone within the same capture, so nothing persists across a pause, a death, or a vehicle entry. The debug UI counts moves, restores, failures, and drift (a clip found elsewhere than its last restore put it). Per capture the atlas costs a display-info read and two writes per part; per dispatch, a few small region copies and two band clears; no capture, pass, or render target is added.

A marker leaving the list plays its hide fade in a cleared band, so it vanishes rather than fades. Occluded markers are neither depth-tested nor dimmed. The explosive reticle has no cell. Unverified: that [`ClampToScreen`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-ClampToScreen) keeps edge arrows inside the stage-shaped safe area, that no HUD ActionScript lays out against the visible frame or reads a moved part's render-side bounds, and the readouts' placements, which need the headset.
