# Motion controllers as built

The motion-controller implementation as it stands: the input foundation, scheme selection, mode detection, the bindings and their injection, the aim-state pin, controller-owned hands, per-hand aiming, the sight lines and their tuning tool, the world-space overlay layer, posture and roomscale, the weapon handling, vehicles, scopes, and the finger curls. The design and its reasons are in the plan of record, [controllers-and-roomscale.md](controllers-and-roomscale.md), whose "Implementation status" section maps each part to its settings and to its section here, lists the unverified assumptions of milestone 1, and holds the headset test checklist. The unverified assumptions of the later parts are under "Unverified" at the end of this page. Engine facts are in the engine docs linked from each section.

The code is in `payload/src/controllers/` (everything built on the controller snapshot), `payload/src/vr/input/` (the OpenXR side), and `payload/src/ui/controllers/` (the controller features of the debug UI). Every setting is in the `controllers` section of the mod's config (`ControllersConfig`), and the debug UI's features show the switches and tuning they keep, each with a live readout. The master switch `controllers.enabled` gates the per-frame input sync and the publication of the controller snapshot, so with it off every subsystem is inert. Each subsystem that changes how the character behaves also acts only under the controller scheme, so the mod behaves as it did before controllers existed whenever no controller is in use.

The defaults are the full `Controllers` preset (see "Test controls and saved tuning"), so every subsystem, including the wingsuit's arm steering and the parachute's handles, works out of the box.

## Test controls and saved tuning

Tools > Presets offers the `Controllers` and `Gamepad baseline` presets. Presets change feature switches while preserving numeric tuning, handedness, grip offsets, and sight entries. `Controllers` turns every controller feature on, as the defaults ship. The selected preset is marked modified when its owned switches differ. Optional stick-click weapon cycling, hand-aimed grenade fallback, finger source, and finger drive-all settings remain unchanged. `Gamepad baseline` disables the controller and roomscale features, which leaves the head-tracked gamepad game.

The controller settings, the shared VR turn settings, and the body dimensions are saved in the settings file with the rest of the configuration, whenever they change and without a save control ([settings.md](../settings.md)). The load validates them: a group whose values are out of range or inconsistent resets to its defaults, and a failure elsewhere in the controller settings keeps the per-weapon sight calibration.

The controller subsystems' derived state (the scheme, mode, IK, hand tiers, late correction, aim, and body rig) is recorded once per game frame as a `controller_state` event in the tracking recording and its ten-second lookback, which "Save the last 10 s" under Tools > Tracking capture writes out after the fact ([recorder.md](../testing/recorder.md#recording)).

## Input foundation

The OpenXR actions are in two layers. OpenXR gives every action exactly one owning set, so the layout is:

- `hands`, always active: the grip and aim poses of both hands, the haptic output, and the raw inputs, named for the physical inputs of the Valve Index controller (triggers, grips, sticks, trackpads, face buttons, the system button, and their touches). The published snapshot is read from these, whatever the mode, so everything built on it (the bindings, the gestures, the finger curls, the chords, input recovery, scheme detection, and the pointers) does not depend on the active set. Their localized names ask the player not to rebind them.
- `global`, always active, and one mode set at a time: the semantic actions, named for what they do in the game. The mode sets are `on_foot` (on foot, swimming, falling, and the parachute), `wingsuit`, `land_vehicle` (cars, motorcycles, boats, and vehicles of no resolved class), `helicopter`, `plane`, and `menu` (the game's menus and the native map). Falling and the parachute share `on_foot` because their tables read the same inputs, so a grapple jump does not switch sets. A vehicle seat selects its class's set, driver or passenger; the actions only one seat uses (`accelerate` and the passenger's `fire`, for example) share an input and are told apart by the seat.

Every set has priority 0: a runtime ignores a lower-priority set's binding on an input that a higher-priority active set also binds, and the raw and semantic actions bind the same inputs. Every action has both hands as subaction paths and is read per hand, so the hand a press came from stays known after the player remaps it. The runtime's binding interface (SteamVR's or xrizer's) remaps per set, and there the player sees game meanings such as "Reload (hold: holster)"; the localized names mention the overloads that the game or the mod resolves within one action.

Suggested bindings are shipped for the Index controller profile and the Oculus Touch profile. The raw inputs bind by position (the left Touch controller's X, Y, and menu button stand for A, B, and the system button). The semantic actions' defaults (`vr/input/semantic/defaults.rs`) are written per action as a side (the dominant hand, the off-hand, both hands, or a fixed hand) and a component named by its position, and lay out the tables of the plan ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Per-mode bindings"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#per-mode-bindings)). The face buttons mirror the gamepad by side: on foot the dominant A jumps and the dominant B uses and enters vehicles (the gamepad's Y), the off-hand A reloads (X) and the off-hand B releases the tethers (B); in every vehicle the dominant B and the off-hand B fire vehicle weapons 1 and 2 (or the gun a hand holds), the right controller's A takes the stunt position on a tap and exits on a hold, the left controller's A is the handbrake, the off-hand stick click grapples, and the dominant stick click is nitrous; in the wingsuit the off-hand A closes the suit and the dominant B boosts. The dominant stick click detonates. The off-hand trackpad of an Index controller selects weapon slots: a press latches the quadrant under the thumb and holds that slot's select action until the release, up for the pistols, right for the two-handed slot, left for the special slot, and down for the explosives, as on the gamepad's d-pad. The same press opens the weapon wheel, which a held press steers by pointing the hand, and the dominant stick held up (`weapon_wheel`) opens it too ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)). A few actions ship unbound: `open_commlink`, `recenter_view`, and `toggle_debug_panel` (the stick-click chords stay the built-in way) and `throw_grenade` (grenades come from their pouch). Changing the defaults changes the fingerprint below, so a payload with new defaults needs a game restart. The left-handed table is generated from the right-handed one by moving every dominant-hand and off-hand row to the other hand; rows on both hands or on a fixed hand (the menu's previous and next pages, its focus and map recenter) keep their sides. The table is chosen from `controllers.dominant_hand` when the actions are created, and suggestions are fixed once the sets are attached, so changing hands takes effect for the runtime's defaults only after a game restart. OpenXR has no per-user presets, so the mirrored table is the left-handed preset: Monado follows it, and SteamVR follows it while the player has no custom binding for the game; a player with one goes back to the default binding in SteamVR's binding interface after switching hands, so no separate SteamVR binding file ships. Changing the hand in the pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page says so beside the setting (the page's "BUTTON LAYOUT" readout reads, for example, "RIGHT-HANDED UNTIL RESTART"), and the restart notice below shows until the game restarts or the hand is changed back. The mod's hand roles follow the hand the attached bindings were suggested for until that restart (`ControllersConfig::acting_hand`, read from `vr::input::bound_hand`): the tables, the gun hand and the grapple's ray, the wrist readouts, the mirrored sticks and levers of the vehicles, and the rest, so that the roles never disagree with the buttons. With no bindings attached they follow the configured hand. The masks and the summary read the runtime's bound sources, so they place the actions where the runtime actually put them.

Each frame the per-hand state of `global`'s and the active mode set's actions is published as a `SemanticFrame` beside the snapshot, read in the same sync, and the binding tables read it (see "Bindings and injection"). Each action also carries a digital state: a boolean action's value, or a float action past the press thresholds of the input it reads by default (the trigger's, the grip's, or the trackpad's), with hysteresis, so a grip action keeps the mod's force thresholds wherever it is bound. Its press and release edges accumulate between input ticks as the raw buttons' do. The Input/bindings > Self-check live status shows it with each set's active flag and the runtime's bound sources for every action. The bound sources need the session, so the game thread queries them inside the frame's input update (see [threading.md](../threading.md)) and publishes the results, reduced for each action to the physical inputs it reads on each hand, which the bindings' masks act on, and matched to the known components, which the controls summary draws ([controls-summary.md](controls-summary.md#sources-and-eligibility)). The active sets' source paths are enumerated before every sync sample, independently of panel visibility, because rebinding within one profile raises no event. Path strings and localized names are cached by opaque OpenXR path in a bounded cache owned by the runtime's input state. Unchanged path sets reuse the published sources and tables. A profile change, a localization refresh, or the diagnostic section's periodic refresh reads every set and refreshes names. A failed path query removes accepted attribution and remains retryable. This avoids repeated name lookups and unchanged source-table rebuilds, not all allocations: active action enumeration, path snapshots, and query collections still allocate. Native-free counters measure name-call reuse; frame-time and allocation-rate effects have not been measured.

`XR_EXT_hand_tracking` is enabled when the runtime offers it, and `XR_EXT_hand_joints_motion_range` beside it when offered too; with `controllers.input.hand_tracking` set, the hand trackers are created and each hand's joints are published in the snapshot, located with the controller-conforming motion range when the instance has that extension. Whether the persisted instance was created with each of the two extensions is stashed with its handle, so a reinject re-wraps it with the same set.

Once per rendered frame, inside the VR frame loop, `vr::input` syncs the actions, locates both hands' grip and aim spaces at the frame's predicted display time, re-bases them against the recenter baseline exactly as the eye views are re-based, and publishes a `ControllerFrame`. Poses are in the cockpit frame: the OpenXR LOCAL space re-based by the recenter baseline, with OpenXR axes. A controller pose therefore composes into the world through the same transform as the head. The trigger, the grip, and the trackpad are also thresholded into digital presses with hysteresis. The trigger press reads the trigger value (`controllers.input.trigger_press`). The grip press reads whichever grip input the hand's interaction profile provides: the squeeze force on the Index (`controllers.input.squeeze_force_press`), whose capacitive squeeze value is full whenever the hand rests round the handle, so only a deliberate squeeze presses; and the squeeze value elsewhere (`controllers.input.squeeze_press`), with a press threshold high enough that a resting hand does not press. The choice is made per hand from the bound profile's suggested bindings, not from the values: a profile the runtime emulates has no force of its own and reads the value. The squeeze value still drives the finger curls, and on a force-sensing grip it also keeps a hold the press started for as long as the fingers stay round the handle ([grip.md, "The grip's press"](grip.md#the-grips-press)). The trackpad press reads the trackpad force (`controllers.input.trackpad_press`) and is split into a left-half and a right-half press, the half decided by where the thumb is when the press starts (a press on the centre line names neither) and kept until the release. The Input/bindings > Devices and thresholds live status shows each hand's squeeze value, force, which of them presses, and the trackpad force. A hand whose joints cannot be located (hand tracking is on but `xrLocateHandJointsEXT` fails) keeps its poses and inputs and goes without joints for that frame.

The snapshot stands for the controllers' state only while the frame loop keeps sampling. It is cleared when the session stops running, when a frame cannot begin, and when the controllers are disabled, and a snapshot older than half a second is treated as absent even if nothing cleared it. That bound sits above a main-thread hitch, which delays the publish and the input tick alike. With no snapshot the bindings inject nothing and the scheme falls back to the gamepad.

Render frames outpace the engine's roughly 30 Hz sim tick, so a button pressed and released between two ticks would be invisible to a consumer that compared states tick to tick. The snapshot's edge latch accumulates press and release edges across publishes until they are taken. A change of mode set does not interrupt it, since the raw inputs come from the always-active `hands` set. The input tick is the single taker. The first snapshot after a clear has nothing to compare against and latches no edges, so a button held across a clear is not pressed a second time.

The OpenXR instance and session persist across an uninject and reinject, and action sets can be attached to a session only once. On uninject the action handles are stashed in the process environment with a fingerprint of the action layout (every set and action name with its type) and of every binding it suggested (for each shipped profile, the raw inputs' bindings and the semantic suggestions for the configured dominant hand), and on reinject they are re-wrapped. The suggestions are part of the fingerprint because a runtime takes them only before the sets are attached: a payload that re-wrapped the handles under different suggestions would keep the old ones without a sign. A payload whose layout, suggestions, or dominant hand differ from the stashed one cannot re-wrap the handles and cannot attach new actions to the persisted session, so its controllers stay unavailable until the game restarts; bring-up reports this and puts the stash back for a payload with the matching layout.

Bring-up records why a restart is needed (`vr/input/restart.rs`): the stash was made for another layout, the stash differs only in the dominant hand (its fingerprint matches the other hand's), or the session refused the sets because it already has some attached. A changed `controllers.dominant_hand` while the input is attached is the fourth reason, which leaves the controllers working with the other hand's layout. While any of them holds, a head-locked notice panel (`hud/restart_notice.rs`) shows ahead of and below the view in the headset, and so on the desktop mirror, saying "Restart the game to use the motion controllers" or "Restart the game to apply the dominant hand" with the reason under it; the debug UI shows the same as a banner at the bottom of its surface. The notice reads the published input status and the config, so the draw task can ask for it without the runtime lock. If the action spaces cannot be created after the actions are attached, the handles are stashed rather than destroyed, since the session would not accept new ones, and the next reinject retries. The actions are created and attached whether or not the controllers are enabled, because attachment is possible only once per session.

Every vibration is a cue of the haptic vocabulary, queued from any thread into a per-hand mixer that the frame loop asks once per rendered frame what each hand plays, since OpenXR plays one vibration per controller at a time ([haptics.md](haptics.md)).

### Action schema

The sets, their actions, and the actions' types are the action schema. The runtime keys a player's saved bindings on the names, so a name never changes meaning: a rename is a removal and an addition. A unit test holds a snapshot of every set, action, and type. Changing it bumps the schema version, which is also the OpenXR application version, and adds an entry here. Adding actions is a minor change; removing, retyping, or moving one breaks saved bindings and needs a release note telling players to reset their bindings.

- Version 1: the raw inputs move into `hands`, the identical `onfoot`, `vehicle`, `airborne`, and `ui` sets of raw inputs are replaced by `global` and the six semantic mode sets, and `hands` gains `squeeze_click` (the grip as a boolean the runtime thresholds, read only by the self-check). This breaks every saved binding once, and a payload with this layout cannot re-wrap an older payload's stashed actions, so the game must be restarted.
- Version 2: `on_foot` gains `weapon_wheel`, a float suggested on the dominant stick's vertical axis, which opens the weapon wheel when pushed up ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)). It is an addition, the minor kind of change.
- Version 3: `on_foot`, `wingsuit`, `land_vehicle`, `helicopter`, and `plane` gain `grapple`, a boolean suggested on the off-hand's upper face button, whose gestures the bindings read ([grip.md](grip.md), `bindings/grapple_button.rs`). The grips grapple nothing: `fire_grapple` stays, a float for a player who binds the grapple to an analog input, and ships unbound, as `release_tethers` does, which the grapple's double tap replaces. The vehicles' `vehicle_mod` (nitrous) moves to the dominant stick's click, since the grapple took its button, and `fire_secondary` does not fire the grapple. The additions are minor; the moved suggestions change the layout fingerprint, so the game must be restarted for them (the restart notice says so).
- Version 4: `global` gains `skip_cutscene`, a boolean suggested on the right controller's upper face button whichever hand is dominant (B on the Index and on the Touch), which the game's skip prompt names. Held outside the menus, it holds `SKIP_CUTSCENE` on the UI action map, where the cutscene reads its skip ([input.md](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#the-write-api)). It shares the button with the mode's own action there (use, exit, the grapple for a left-handed player), as the gamepad's B does: the skip acts only in a skippable cutscene. An addition; the suggestion changes the layout fingerprint, so the game must be restarted for it.
- Version 5: the vehicle sets' layout changes, with no action added or removed. In `land_vehicle`, `helicopter`, and `plane`, `fire_primary` and `fire_secondary` move from the grips to the dominant and the off-hand upper face buttons, and on a hand holding a gun they fire that gun ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Land vehicles"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#land-vehicles-car-motorcycle-in-the-last-rows)); they become booleans, which a button carries, and the runtime thresholds them if a player binds them to a trigger. `exit_vehicle` moves onto the right controller's lower face button beside `stunt`, and the tables tell them apart by the press's length. `grapple` moves to the off-hand stick click, and `horn` ships unbound (the cockpit's horn button sounds it, and toggles a vehicle's magnet as the gamepad's button does). The moved suggestions break saved bindings for these sets: players reset their vehicle bindings, and the game must be restarted for the new suggestions.
- Layout only (no schema change): the wingsuit uses the physical left trigger for its machine gun, the physical right trigger for rockets, physical right upper face (B) for boost, and physical right lower face (A) for the contextual canopy action. These sides do not mirror with handedness. The physical left upper face fires the grapple. The brake and close actions remain available to custom layouts, but have no default button; the arm gestures provide them. The layout fingerprint changes, so the game must be restarted.
- Layout only (no schema change): `on_foot`'s `weapon_select_pad` and `weapon_select_press` lose their suggestions on the off-hand trackpad and ship unbound, since the pad lies under the thumb on its way to the off-hand stick click of the debug panel chord ([weapon-selector.md](../weapon-selector.md)).

### Input self-check

Once per session (and per reinject), at the first focused frame at which a hand has an interaction profile, `vr/input/self_check.rs` logs one block at info level under the `vr` target, starting `input: self-check`, and the "Semantic actions" section shows it under the `SELF-CHECK` status row. The Devices feature likewise opens with the `CONTROLLER ACTIONS` and `LAYOUT FINGERPRINT` rows, and names the OpenXR input's error only when there is one. It is on by default (`controllers.input.self_check`) and the section can run it again, for example after entering a mode whose set was inactive the first time. What to look for:

- The runtime line and each hand's profile path say what was tested.
- Under "one input in two active sets at priority 0", every line pairs a semantic action with the raw action bound to the same input on that hand. Both should read `active`. A semantic side that reads `INACTIVE` while the raw side is active means the runtime gave the input to one set only, and the semantic layer cannot share inputs with `hands` on that runtime.
- Under "grip as a boolean and as a float", `squeeze_click` (a boolean on the Index grip force) and `squeeze_force` should both be active on an Index controller. An inactive boolean means the runtime does not threshold the force for a boolean action, so grip actions must stay floats.
- Under "bound sources", each sampled action should list its default input with a localized name, in the inactive sets as in the active ones. `no sources` for an inactive set only means the runtime answers for active sets alone; an error means the call fails through the runtime or wineopenxr.
- After the block, pressing and releasing each paired input once logs an `input: self-check finding` line: "both report" is the expected result, with similar frame counts. For the grip, the finding gives the force at which the runtime's boolean pressed and released, beside the mod's own thresholds. The `SELF-CHECK` status row passes while every pair checked so far both report, with how many were checked, and fails only for a pair one side of which read nothing, which is logged as a warning; the grip's findings are measurements, never failures.

## Scheme selection

The controller scheme is in effect while `controllers.enabled` is on and a controller is active in the published snapshot (`scheme::current`). With the controllers switched off (the pause menu's motion controllers switch) or no controller frame, the gamepad scheme is in effect, so a player who never connects motion controllers, or who puts them down and switches them off, keeps the game's own behaviour with the head-tracked view. Every controller subsystem gates on the scheme.

## Body dimensions

The player's dimensions are the `[body]` section of the settings file ([settings.md](../settings.md)), which the calibration on the pause menu's `VR: BODY` page writes. Every field is optional and all values are in metres. A missing field takes the generic adult dimension, an unknown one is skipped and logged, and dimensions that cannot describe a body (`BodyDimensions::validate`) reset to the generic adult ones. The character's own proportions are an estimate from his stated height (`BodyDimensions::CHARACTER_ESTIMATE`), except for his arms, which the hands measure from the skeleton (see "Hands", "Arm reach").

The section has one field without a default, `seated_eye_height`: the head's height above the STAGE floor while the player sits upright in their chair, below the standing eye height. While it is set, it is where the seated eye reference starts and what the automatic posture's thresholds sit against ([Posture and roomscale](#posture-and-roomscale), "Posture" and "Head height"). Without it, the key is left out of the file.

The file can be written by hand or by the calibration on the pause menu's `VR: BODY` page ([pause-menu.md](../pause-menu.md#body-calibration)), which measures two things in the headset: the standing eye height (the head above the STAGE floor), or the seated eye height when calibrating seated, and the distance between the controllers' grip poses in a T-pose. Seated, the height is estimated from the arm span, which is about the height for most adults (the grip span over one less the fingertip share of both hands), and the player can correct it before saving; everything else is derived from the height as below. `controllers/body/calibration/derive.rs` derives the rest with generic adult proportions, the Drillis and Contini segment ratios: the height is the eye height divided by the eye-height ratio; the arm span is the grip span plus, on each side, the part of the hand beyond the grip (the grip pose is the centre of the closed hand, about three tenths of the hand's length from the wrist); the shoulder height, shoulder width, and hand scale with the height; and the upper arm and forearm split what the arm span leaves once the shoulders and hands are taken out, in the ratio of their standard lengths. `BodyDimensions::GENERIC_ADULT` follows the same height, shoulder, and hand ratios, but takes its arm segments straight from the tables' per-height lengths, which with the shoulders and hands add up to more than its arm span. The calibration's segments add up to the measured span instead, so for the generic adult's own measurements they come out about a sixth shorter than the defaults. Only the debug UI and the review step show the segment lengths; nothing else reads them yet. An eye height outside a standing adult's range is refused, since it most likely means a seated player or a wrong floor, and so is an arm span too short for the height (arms not spread) or longer than any adult's; a span well over the height is accepted, since the controllers cannot be further apart than the arms reach, and the review flags it (see [the pause menu doc](../pause-menu.md#body-calibration)). The values are rounded to the millimetre and validated before the file is written, through a temporary file so that a failed write leaves the old file whole, and the new dimensions apply at once.

The remap is a set of pure functions: the hands' offsets from the shoulders scale by the ratio of the arm spans about estimated shoulders (`controllers.body.remap_hands`, on by default), unless the measured remap replaces it (see "Hands", "Arm reach"), and the eye height and vertical head travel scale by the ratio of the heights (`controllers.body.remap_head`, on by default; it moves the view for every player). The head remap reaches the view through the posture (see "Posture and roomscale").

## Mode detection

The detector runs once per engine input tick, in the tick's game stage (see "Bindings and injection"), on the game thread before the world update. It reads a snapshot of the engine state and resolves the plan's rules over it ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Mode detection"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#mode-detection)), first match wins:

1. A menu is up: `ui`. The signals are the game clock's stop flag ([`Clock::m_Stop`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/clock::Clock##field-m_Stop)), which covers the pause menu, the commlink, and the other pause reasons; the UI blocker bit ([`InputSystemBlocker::Ui`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::InputSystemBlocker##variant-Ui)) of the player action map, which covers screens such as the map that do not pause; and the mod's own panel.
2. A deferred teleport is in flight ([`GameWorld::IsTeleporting`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game_world::GameWorld##vfunc-IsTeleporting)), or the character is not readable (no character, no control parameters, or no animation instance in the first frames after a spawn): hold the current mode.
3. Seated in a vehicle, including the entry and exit animations ([`Character::IsInVehicleAttachState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInVehicleAttachState)): `vehicle`. The class is resolved in the order helicopter, plane, motorcycle, car, and sea, and the seat from the vehicle's local-player seat, which selects the driver bindings. The class predicates walk the interaction graph, so they run only while the attach state holds.
4. Grapple traversal (the pre-reel, reeling in, the wind-up to a hang, and reeled in on an attached wire): the gameplay mode holds, and the traversal is flagged for the hand tiers.
5. to 7. The wingsuit, the parachute, and falling: `airborne`, with a variant for each.
8. and 9. Swimming and everything else: `onfoot`, with a swim variant.

The mode selects the semantic mode set: `onfoot` and the falling and parachute variants of `airborne` select `on_foot`, the wingsuit `wingsuit`, a vehicle its class's set (`helicopter`, `plane`, or `land_vehicle` for the rest), and `ui` the `menu` set.

A change between gameplay modes must persist for `mode::DEBOUNCE_TICKS` ticks (two) before it takes effect. The count is at the game's 30 Hz tick. Entering and leaving `ui` is immediate, because the UI signals do not flicker and a late switch would send a menu press to the gameplay bindings. The detector selects the OpenXR action set whenever the controllers are enabled, and changes nothing the game sees. The headpose sim keeps its own on-foot test.

## Bindings and injection

The input tick runs in two stages (`controllers::bindings::stage`), because in play the device poll runs in the engine's input job, which overlaps game-thread work that writes the physics world, spawns and deletes objects, and frees animation controls ([engine/gameplay/input.md](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#where-the-device-poll-runs)). The game stage runs the bindings' decisions and every subsystem's acts (the weapon handling, the inventory, the snatch, the physical grapple, the physical hands' pickups and stunts, the vehicles), and the active-input-device pin, on the game thread at the top of the world's pre-simulation queue: after the input job has finished, after the update-queue commit that opens the world's pre-simulation update, and before any character, weapon, or vehicle update and the physics step. It is run from the update queue's execution, as the first band the queue runs after that update begins ([`UpdateQueue::Execute`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/update_queue::UpdateQueue##func-Execute)), so whatever the acts spawn is registered after the commit and first updated in the next tick, as what the engine's own updates spawn is ([engine/gameplay/input.md](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#where-the-device-poll-runs)). A grenade spawned before the commit would crash the game in the same tick's post-simulation queue. When the update skips its queue (a paused context), the game stage runs after it instead, and the acts that spawn such objects (the safe grenade's draw and pickup) refuse there, since a paused update also skips the ammunition manager's pass that completes them; so does an inline game stage. The poll stage stays in the post-call detour on [`InputDeviceManager::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_device_manager::InputDeviceManager##func-Update) and lands the action writes the last game stage produced, right after the device poll, then reads and clears the look effectors. The writes cannot land in the game stage, since the pause menu's and the commlink's toggles are read from the player map inside the input job and a race's reset is read before the world update, and the next poll would overwrite a write made after them. So a decision reaches the game one sim tick after it is made, after a poll and before every reader. Where the input update runs inline on the game thread (the load state and the frontend's loading, where no world update follows), the game stage runs right after the poll stage and its writes land at once. The engine's input job holds the `InputJob` thread role ([threading.md](../threading.md)), which is how the poll stage tells the two cases apart; the game state cannot, since the main menu with the world loaded behind it runs the input job too. A handoff carries the tick, the pending writes, where they landed, and the body turn between the stages, and a poll stage with no game stage after it drops its tick with a warning.

On every input tick the bindings read the tick's semantic actions (the latest `SemanticFrame` and the edges since the last tick), evaluate the current mode's table over them, and inject the result. The gestures, grabs, and chords read the raw snapshot instead, reduced to logical controls (deadzoned sticks and triggers, per-hand buttons with their edges, and what each hand's grip would take, [grip.md](grip.md)). The bindings inject only while `controllers.bindings.enabled` is set, the controller scheme is in effect, a controller is active, the game's device poll runs, and the mod's panel is down, so a real gamepad under the gamepad scheme is never fought. The `turn` action (the dominant stick's horizontal axis by default) drives the body turn, which the next poll stage hands to the headpose's VR turn scheme (`headpose.vr_turn`) rather than injected, except in the helicopter and the plane, whose tables read their own yaw and rudder actions there.

Each table names the actions of its mode's set, never a hand or a button, so it acts on whatever the player bound them to. A press counts on either hand. The actions get their deadzone from the input they read by default: the trigger deadzone for a throttle, the move deadzone for a stick or a stick axis. The default bindings follow the plan's per-mode tables ([controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Per-mode bindings"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#per-mode-bindings)), whose right and left are the dominant hand and the off-hand (`controllers.dominant_hand`), so a left-handed layout mirrors every gameplay binding. The layout is mirrored, and a gun rides the hand that holds it: the dual-wield pistols one per hand (see "Recoil, haptics, and the grapple hand"), and any other weapon the hand that drew it, took it off the ground, or was handed it, which for a draw the game starts on its own is the dominant hand ([physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md)). Only the holding hand's trigger fires a gun, and in a vehicle that hand's vehicle weapon action fires it too, in place of the vehicle's weapon; in the driver's seat, whose triggers are the pedals, the vehicle weapon action alone does (`bindings/gun_fire.rs`). The move action's forward is the head's facing, or the off-hand controller's pointing direction when `controllers.locomotion.direction` is `OffHand`. Out of the driver's seat the throttle actions are not read, and the fire actions fire the hand weapon or the mounted gun.

Where one input means different things by context, each meaning is its own action with the same default binding, and the table reads only the one that fits: `retract_tethers` on the off-hand trigger, or `retract_tethers_alt` on the off-hand stick click while that trigger fires a pistol (`fire_off_hand_pistol`); `next_weapon` on the same stick click only when weapon cycling is on and not dual-wielding. The off-hand trigger fires a gun in the off-hand whatever the weapon, so a single gun the hold put there moves the retract to the stick click too ([physical-weapons.md, "Firing"](physical-weapons.md#firing)).

What the mod claims of a hand is masked from the semantic actions bound there, by the physical inputs the runtime reports each action bound to (the bound sources, matched against the inputs of the shipped profiles; until a set's sources have been read, for an action whose enumeration failed, and for one whose sources are all opaque paths under no user path, its default bindings stand in). A masked action reads as released on that hand. A grip press latched to a grab masks that hand's grip for the whole press, so a holster draw never also grapples. With a pistol in the off-hand, its trigger is masked from everything but `fire_off_hand_pistol`. A hand holding any part of a gun reserves its trigger from the actions bound there (`bindings/trigger_ownership.rs`), except in a driver's seat, where the triggers are the pedals and the gun fires from the hand's vehicle weapon action instead; in every vehicle seat that action, on a gun hand, fires the gun and not the vehicle's weapon. In a menu, the native UI pointer's trigger is masked from everything but `pointer_select`, which the page actions do not read. The debug panel chord masks the left stick click and the right B once it fires, the mark chord the left stick click and the right A, and input recovery masks a whole hand. An action bound to a stick click reads that hand's click as the stick-click chord arbitrates it, rather than its own state. A source the mod does not recognise is masked only with its whole hand. The yank gesture presses the grapple hand's `fire_grapple` for a tick, so it reaches whatever the grapple drives.

In the menus both hands navigate alike, whichever is dominant, with the `menu` set's default bindings on both hands. Either A confirms ([`GUI_OK`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-GUI_OK)) and either B goes back ([`GUI_CANCEL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-GUI_CANCEL)), the action the game's menus take as cancel. Either stick steps through the list and, pushed left or right, adjusts the selected row. Each hand's grip is that hand's modifier: with it held, the stick's left and right step the tabs ([`GUI_TAB_PREV`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-GUI_TAB_PREV) and `GUI_TAB_NEXT`), and A and B are the gamepad's X and Y (the special and filter actions, and the use action, which resets a setting to its default). The grip press is the deliberate one, the force on the Index, so a hand resting on the handle does not modify. Whether a press is modified is latched at its press edge, so squeezing or letting go of the grip mid-press never turns a confirm into an X or a value step into a tab step. A grip alone does nothing. Where a controller has a trackpad, a press on the left or right half of either hand's trackpad also steps the tabs; a touch alone does not, so the off-hand's touch stays free to show the controls summary. The triggers turn pages, the left back and the right forward, and pulled with the same hand's grip held the other way; while the native UI pointer shows, the pointer hand's trigger is the pointer's alone, so the other trigger turns both pages, and the stick clicks keep the gamepad's menu stick clicks (`GUI_FOCUS` on the left, `GUI_RECENTER` on the right, which recenters the map, not the view). The previous and next tabs and pages keep their physical sides.

The menu stick is read as one direction at a time. The game's base menu input ([`UIBase::BaseOnManageInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_base::UIBase##func-BaseOnManageInput)) reads only the left stick's menu actions, past a fixed threshold, ranking up over down over left over right, and it repeats a held direction itself: one step at once, then repeated steps that come faster the longer the direction is held. The d-pad actions take the same repeat through separate handlers, and injecting both would step twice. So both sticks drive the left stick's menu actions, and the bindings add no repeat of their own. A stick read as raw half-axes would step up when pushed mostly sideways with a little lift, so each hand's push latches the direction of its larger axis once that axis passes `controllers.bindings.menu_stick_push`, holds it at full value until the push along it falls below half of that, and reports nothing else meanwhile. The tab steps are press edges in the game (one tab per push), so a held tab push does not repeat.

Holding both stick clicks together for `controllers.bindings.recenter_hold_s` recenters the view in every mode. The second click must join before `controllers.bindings.stick_chord_window_s` expires, in either order. The hold starts when the second click joins. Completion requests the deferred manual recenter (see "Posture and roomscale") and pulses both hands. The command fires once, and both members remain consumed until a later observation finds both physically neutral. A click that has committed as a single cannot become a late chord.

Pressing the left stick click and then right B before `controllers.bindings.panel_chord_window_s` expires toggles the debug panel and pulses the right hand. The default panel join window is 0.35 s. Right B alone commits immediately while the left click is neutral. An unordered sync containing both new presses does not establish left-first order and cannot toggle the panel. The left click waits for the longer of the recenter and panel windows before committing its single action; the right click waits only for the recenter window. A released reserved click is delivered as an accepted press followed by a release, with no credit for the withheld physical hold time. The shared arbitration runs before gameplay stand-down and supplies the same accepted output to the game bindings, native UI pointer, and debug-panel pointer. Consumed members cannot act in any of those routes until their common physical neutral observation, whose release edges are also suppressed. Recenter takes priority if its stick pair forms first. The panel command uses the same mode transition as the keyboard panel shortcut; showing the panel and enabling editing remain separate operations. In a vehicle the left stick click is a right-handed player's grapple and the right B the primary fire, so by default the panel chord and the mark chord below do not run while a vehicle set (`land_vehicle`, `helicopter`, or `plane`) is active (`controllers.bindings.suppress_developer_chords_in_vehicles`, the debug UI's Movement > Vehicles). There the left click waits only for the recenter window, a right B or right A pressed after it is an ordinary press that fires or acts at once, an unordered sync containing the left click and one of them is not refused, and the wrist summary does not list the panel chord. A tap released sooner than the recenter window is delivered on its release, as everywhere. Turning the setting off restores both chords in vehicles, with the left click's longer wait and the fire within the join window of a grapple click forming the panel chord rather than firing. The arbiter reads the active set from the policy context, and the setting is part of the recorded timing, so a replay applies the setting the recording was made with. On foot, in the wingsuit, and in the menus the setting has no effect.

Pressing the left stick click and then right A within the same join window drops a take marker, the same action as F2 ([capture.md](../capture.md#session-recording)): an unlabelled marker into whatever records, and a save of the tracking lookback when no tracking recording runs. It pulses both hands. Like the panel chord it is on fixed physical hands, not mirrored for a left-handed player, it is withheld with the panel chord in a vehicle, and it follows the panel chord's rules in every respect: right A alone commits immediately while the left click is neutral, an unordered sync containing both new presses marks nothing, a right A pressed after the left click has committed as a single is refused rather than delivered, and both members stay consumed until their common neutral observation. The mark shares the panel's join window, so the left click's wait is unchanged and no single action gains latency. When the right B and the right A both join the left click in one group, the panel forms and the A is refused as a competing chord; recenter keeps priority over both. The chords run only under the controller scheme, so gamepad play is untouched.

The tables are declared once and read twice: by the injection, and by the controls summary on the off-hand wrist, which lists what each input does in the current mode ([controls-summary.md](controls-summary.md)).

The hand weapons are still in use while falling and under the parachute. There the dominant trigger fires (`FIRE_RIGHT`), the off-hand A reloads, and the dominant stick click detonates the placed charges, as on foot. In the wingsuit the dominant trigger and grip fire the weaponized suit's weapons, the dominant stick click boosts, and the dominant B, which opened the suit on foot, closes it. The wingsuit's `open_parachute` (the dominant A) opens the parachute however the stick moved before it. The game's evade (`WINGSUIT_EVADE`) is left unbound, because its barrel roll is dropped (below). The weaponized wingsuit's guns aim by the gaze, because the gun aim covers only the wielded slot; aiming them by the dominant hand is not built.

While a game menu or the map is up, the weapon and vehicle handling pause instead of standing down. A held foregrip, a primed grenade or C4 charge, a round in hand for the reload, and a held physical control are still held when the menu closes. A snapshot sampled from the previous action set is not interpreted through the new mode's bindings; injection waits for a matching sample without cancelling paused interactions.

Loss of a usable grip pose or controller activity masks that hand until its controls return to neutral. The other hand retains its accepted input and holds. Focus loss, a missing fresh input frame, a controller-scheme change, or another global injection stand-down still clears shared interaction state and disarms both hands. Each hand then rearms independently. The neutral sample and its queued edges are discarded. Valid finite inferred poses remain usable for hands, but physical gesture sampling requires tracked poses.

Neutral means that nothing the bindings read from the hand would act. No button other than a capacitive touch is down, the trigger is under both its digital release and its analog deadzone, the grip's press reading (the force on the Index, the value elsewhere) is under its release, and the stick is under the smallest deadzone of its consumers. For the off hand that is the move deadzone. For the dominant hand it is also the turn threshold: the smooth-turn deadzone, or half the snap threshold, where a snap rearms. A stick under a fixed floor always counts as neutral, even when a deadzone is set smaller, so that a worn stick's resting drift cannot keep a hand masked. A stick that close to centre only feeds a slow walk or turn, never an edge. The Input/bindings > Devices and thresholds live status shows each hand as ready, not injecting, or masked, along with the control that holds it. A hand that stays masked for more than a second is logged, again at a fixed interval while it stays masked, and its rearm is logged after that.

Float actions with press behavior use an action-owned trigger, grip, or trackpad threshold with hysteresis. Suggested bindings do not define that behavior. The optional analog grapple actions use trigger thresholds even though they ship unbound.

Where a semantic action stands for a gamepad button, the table holds every gameplay action that the game's default gamepad keymap puts on that button, and the game's own context selects the one that applies. This keeps the game's tap and hold overloads intact: the reload button is also the holster (a short hold), `USE_VEHICLE_MOD` is nitrous when held and the turbo jump when tapped, a held `ENTER_VEHICLE` enters the gunner seat, and a held `FIRE_GRAPPLE` tethers. Two overloads the mod resolves itself, both by `controllers.bindings.long_press_s`. The `pause` action (the system button of either hand by default): a press shorter than that clicks the commlink on release, and holding it that long clicks pause. And in a vehicle the stunt and the exit, which share the right controller's A: a press released sooner takes the stunt position on its release, and one held that long taps the gamepad's Y at that moment, which the game reads as the exit; the seat change, Y held, is not offered. In the car's driver's seat, on the motorcycle, and on a vehicle's mounted gun, with the vehicle moving at least `PARACHUTE_BAIL_MIN_SPEED_MPS` (`controllers::bindings::tables::declare`, the minimum of the animation graph's bail condition; [input.md, "Leaving and changing seats"](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#leaving-and-changing-seats)), the long press holds the gamepad's A's `OPEN_PARACHUTE` instead, from the moment it becomes long until it is let go (`sink::Input::LongHeld`), which the game takes as the parachute bail once held past its hold time; it never taps Y as well, since the game would queue the plain exit first. The speed is the vehicle's travel between its last two world transforms (`vehicles::seat_vehicle_speed_mps`); a passenger seat, the boats, and the aircraft keep the plain exit. Melee (`PUSH_GRAPPLE`) is left unbound, because on the button that releases tethers it would melee on every release. The sight tuning tool's capture chord is armed only while the tool is on and its marker is placed: on foot, holding both grips and pressing the dominant A captures. While it is armed, the dominant grip withholds the off-hand's grapple, so the dominant grip is squeezed first, and both grips together withhold the dominant A's jump. With the dominant grip held, the off-hand's grip press is part of the chord and not a foregrip grab, so the chord neither takes nor lets go of a foregrip. A grip press is latched at its press edge to the grip's default action or to a grab of the volume the hand is in, so a hand leaving a volume mid-press does not turn a grab into a grapple. Outside every grab volume, an off-hand grip press fires the grapple (see "Sockets, pouches, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier").

The game's readers tell a tap from a hold by the effector's state time, and [`LocalPlayerActionMap::ForceSetPressed`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::LocalPlayerActionMap##func-ForceSetPressed) zeroes that time on every call ([input.md, "The write API"](../../engine/gameplay/input.md#the-write-api)). The hold tracker therefore keeps each held action's time itself and writes the effector's whole state: the first tick of a press writes a click with no time, every held tick writes a press with the time held so far, and the tick after the press ends writes a release with the final time, which the next device poll turns idle. An analog action above a fixed value also reads as pressed, standing in for the device button threshold, which is not read.

Each write resolves its action through the same lookup the game's readers use, so it lands in the effector the next reader picks. Values are only ever raised, so a physical device's reading in the same effector is never reduced. An action no device binds resolves to the null effector, and the write is skipped and reported. The writes go to one of three targets: the player action map through its blockers; the player map ignoring the blockers, for the pause and commlink toggles, which the game reads that way while a menu is up; and the UI action map ([`ui_action_map`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::ui_action_map)), which the game's menus read. The pause and commlink clicks go to both the player map and the UI map.

A write must not outlive the bindings' intent, and the device poll cannot be relied on to end it. While [`InputDeviceManager::m_Enabled`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_device_manager::InputDeviceManager##field-m_Enabled) is clear, which is how the mod's debug panel takes the game's input, [`InputDeviceManager::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_device_manager::InputDeviceManager##func-Update) reads no devices, so every effector keeps the value and state last written to it. A click left there reads as a fresh click on every frame: the pause click alone opens and closes the pause menu in a loop. The device-manager detour reads the flag before the poll and passes it to the input tick, and while it is clear the bindings stand down like any other idle reason, logging the change in either direction.

The hold tracker therefore remembers every effector it wrote on the last tick, the bare clicks and raised values as well as the holds, and every global stand-down (the toggles going off, the gamepad scheme, an unavailable input frame, the panel, or the disabled poll) undoes them explicitly. While the poll runs, each hold is released with its final time, keeping a physical press in the same effector as before, and the poll has already overwritten the raised values. While the poll is disabled, every written effector is returned to idle with no value. Idle rather than released, because nothing moves a released effector on and it would read as a release on every frame. The physical-press guard is bypassed there, because no device is being read and every pressed state is a stale write. When the poll runs again, the tracker is already empty and the poll starts from idle effectors.

A bare click (pause, commlink, and the other one-tick taps) is also undone on the next tick when it is not clicked again. The poll moves a clicked effector to pressed or released on every run, so an effector still clicked a tick later is one the poll skipped, which it also does when the clock's real time has not advanced. The tracker records the effector each click landed on and releases it only when that same effector is still clicked, so a fresh physical click on another device is left alone. On a tick that waits for a sample from the new action set, the holds are kept for when injection resumes and the poll releases their effectors, but the clicks are undone the same way. The writes that undo earlier ones resolve the player map ignoring its blockers, so a menu that blocked the map after the write cannot hide the effector it landed on. The Input/bindings > Devices and thresholds live status lists these writes with the rest of the tick's writes.

### Shared chord arbitration contract

`payload/src/controllers/bindings/policy_a/` supplies the live shared arbiter and its ordered-history adapter. Its protected controls are the left stick click, the right stick click, the right B, and the right A. A bounded gameplay queue is independent of the recording queue. Each successful action sync publishes one coherent raw and semantic group with a host-monotonic observation stamp, controller-frame identity, action schema, binding generation, and the exact source-query outcomes. The runtime drains those groups once before dispatch. Missing, stale, invalid, or overflowed history resets the affected routes and requires fresh neutral input.

Each ordered route sample retains effective availability in its existing `active` field: the action read succeeds, the source is active, and that hand has a usable finite grip pose. An unavailable sample retains its unmasked value but cannot establish neutral. A valid-invalid-valid pose sequence between game ticks therefore cancels the affected route and chord dependencies even when the latest pose has recovered. Missing reserved raw controls do not disable unrelated semantic routes. Optional hand-joint errors remain diagnostics and do not discard valid controller or action observations. The input record layout is unchanged; the raw routes carry one sample per protected control. The recorded policy decision has its own version, 5, whose timing ends with whether the developer chords are withheld in a vehicle set. Version 4 is one entry wider per protected control than version 3 and adds the mark command bit; the mark chord shares the panel's join window, so the recorded timing has no window of its own. A version 4 decision still decodes, with the old timing layout and the suppression read as off, since its chords ran in every set; a replay of it applies the chords in vehicles as they were recorded. A recording whose decisions are version 3 or earlier does not decode. Older recordings retain only the evidence they contain; replay still compares their recorded decisions and refuses a mismatch rather than silently accepting a changed result.

The source list returned by OpenXR identifies possible bindings, not the contributor to a merged action value. The adapter preserves the sampled semantic value and never replaces it with a raw click. Failed, unenumerated, unknown, and successful-empty source results do not fall back to suggested defaults.

Some runtimes name every bound source with a path under no user path: Monado, natively and through wineopenxr, returns paths such as `/mnd/2351` (localized "Left A"), which OpenXR says are opaque. An enumeration made only of such paths (`semantic::bound::opaque`) says nothing the mod can read, so the arbiter treats it as the binding tables' masks treat a failed enumeration: the action's suggested binding on that hand, for the hand the actions were suggested for, stands in as its attribution, and a raw protected route, which the mod owns in the `hands` set and players are asked not to rebind, is taken as verified at its fixed physical location. Recognised paths keep the strict checks, so a recognised source on the wrong hand or component still fails, and an enumeration that mixes user paths with other paths is read path by path, with the others unknown. A player who rebinds an action on such a runtime is not seen by the arbiter or the masks, which keep the suggested layout. The runtime state logs once per change of the enumerated paths that opaque sources are standing in for defaults. A merged semantic route that could include a consumed chord member remains quarantined until its own sampled value returns to neutral. Direct semantic system commands claim their reported physical sources; unrelated routes and independent wrist commands remain separate.

Every protected press retains its binding generation and observed onset identity. Rejected history cannot move the accepted time watermark backward or use stale neutral to rearm controls. Focus, tracking, binding, and consumer-context changes require verified neutral without crediting a held input as a new press. Recorded policy evaluations retain the effective timing, schema, context, raw accepted transitions, physical-component claims, direct-command acceptance, and final command set. Replay matches groups by their complete observation stamp, re-evaluates the referenced group, and compares the resolved decision before dispatch. Skipped groups have explicit cancelled outcomes. Missing or invalid decisions cancel protected output, and replay resumes only at a validated real-neutral boundary. Recording begins with an unavailable protected prefix until a recorded real-neutral boundary; starting a recording does not clear live input ownership. Runtime behavior and per-sync source-refresh cost still require headset validation.

## Gamepad semantics

The game applies the rules of the input device it considers active, from the setting [`SettingType::EPCUseGamepad`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/settings_manager::SettingType##variant-EPCUseGamepad): aim assist only on the gamepad, a different melee trigger on the keyboard, and the matching button prompts. The game sets the value from the frame's device events. The controllers inject at the action level and produce no device events, so the value would stay at whatever device the player last touched. The bindings are written against the gamepad rules, so while the controller scheme is in effect, the entry is held at the gamepad's value. It is written directly rather than through the setter, which would also re-select the controller port and may persist the change. The previous value is restored when the pin ends, unless the game has written its own value from a real device event in the meantime.

## The aim-state pin and the mental-state cuts

The engine side is [aim-state.md](../../engine/gameplay/aim-state.md). Every part acts only under the controller scheme; the arm IK skip alone has a switch, `controllers.aim_state.skip_arm_ik` (on).

- The weapon pin: [`StateBitFlag::SB_NO_WEAPON_AIM`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_NO_WEAPON_AIM) is set for the duration of the player's aim-input function, the only code that reads it, and restored afterwards. - The neutral aim point: the body's aim point on the control-parameters blackboard ([`ControlParameters::m_AimTargetWeapons`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::control_parameters::ControlParameters##field-m_AimTargetWeapons)), which turns the body toward it, is held `aim_state::NEUTRAL_AIM_DISTANCE_M` ahead of the head along the body's forward. A detour on [`Character::HolsterWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HolsterWeapon) marks the holster as deliberate, because the player's holster takes effect through the same relaxed branch that the automatic holster uses.

## Hands

The engine side is [humanik.md](../../engine/character/humanik.md) and [hands-locomotion-and-state.md](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md). Everything here is inert unless the controller scheme is in effect and the hand's controller is tracked, and each part has its toggle under `controllers.hands`.

Each hand's wrist target is composed from its controller, all in pure functions:

1. the grip pose in the cockpit frame;
2. times the grip offset (`controllers.hands.grip`, a position and a rotation in the right hand's grip frame, mirrored for the left), giving the wrist frame: the origin at the wrist joint, -Z toward the fingers, and +Y out of the back of the hand. OpenXR gives both hands' grip frames the same axes (+X points into the right palm and out of the left one, so to the player's right on both hands), so the mirror flips the grip's X as it flips the wrist's;
3. the body remap: the wrist's offset from the player's shoulder, scaled by the ratio of the arm lengths and placed at the character's shoulder (see "Arm reach" below);
4. into the world through the head's transform, the same composition the head pose uses, so that the hands and the view agree.

With `ik` set, each controller-driven wrist is queued on HumanIK's `MAIN` pass at the entry of [`Character::UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePassFinalizePose_Parallel), the seam the head and body IK already use. The position target overwrites the effector's translation. The rotation target is the delta from the animated wrist frame, which is built from the animated knuckles, to the target frame, so it never depends on how the game's hand bone is oriented at rest. Both are queued at the `UPPER_BODY` step, without interpolation or blend-out because they are re-queued every tick, with full reaches (`WRIST_REACH_T` and `WRIST_REACH_R` in `controllers::hands::ik`) scaled by the hand's tier weight. The wielded weapon rides the hand's attach bone, and the engine refreshes the weapon's cached transform from that bone after the `MAIN` solve, so the gun follows the IK-driven wrist within the same tick. While the procedural torso poses the torso, the targets are queued at the HumanIK solve gate instead, on the torso it writes, kept out of the torso and with elbow hints ([procedural-body.md, "The arms"](../body/procedural-body.md#the-arms)).

With `rigid_grip` set, a gun in a controller-driven hand is held fixed to the wrist. The attach bone is animated, so the clips playing decide how the gun sits in the hand ([hands-locomotion-and-state.md, "The gun's pose in the hand is animated"](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md#the-guns-pose-in-the-hand-is-animated)). With the animated wrist this is the game's own handling, but with the wrist on a controller it reads as the gun turning over in a still hand: in play a right-hand gun pivoted about its grip by up to about 170° over a few ticks, muzzle up and over until it lay upside down along the forearm, while the hand and fingers held still, and snapped between a few fixed in-hand poses. So a pre-call step in the inventory's placement detour ([`Inventory::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms), shared with the dual-wield layout) writes the attach bone of each gun of the local player's wielded weapon, for each hand with a controller weight above zero (the hand tiers' blend, whether or not a wrist target was queued this tick), to the hand's current wrist joint composed with the hand's canonical grip, blended from the animated pose by that weight so the gun settles into the hand as it blends onto its controller and back out as it yields: its primary attach bone relative to its wrist in the skeleton's bind pose ([`AnimationController::GetBindPoseBoneMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-GetBindPoseBoneMatrix)), read once per animation controller. The placement runs after the state tasks, after the `MAIN` solve, and after the `SECONDARY` solve, and each run is pinned, so the last one places the gun on the solved wrist. Everything that reads the gun's cached transform follows it: the muzzle effects, the gun's model parts, its physics proxy, the shot origin, the sight line, and the late update, which carries the gun and the hand by the same correction. The shot's direction is the aim point and does not depend on it. Both of a hand's attach points take the same canonical grip, so a state task moving the gun between them does not turn it, and with dual-wield each pistol is pinned to the hand whose attach bone it rides. The foregrip and the one-handed weight move the wrist target, and the gun stays rigid on the wrist they produce. A gun on a holster or back bone, a weapon in any other state, and a hand the controllers do not drive are left to the game, so drawing and holstering play as animated until the gun is in a driven hand, and a reload's flourish that keys the attach bone is not shown (the magazine, carried on the off-hand's bone during a reload, is unaffected). The game's own visual recoil on the attach bone, if any, is replaced by the mod's recoil on the hand. The code is `controllers::hands::grip`. The right hand's own canonical grip holds a gun backwards, so a gun in the right hand can take the left hand's grip mirrored onto the right wrist instead ([physical-weapons.md, "The right hand's grip"](physical-weapons.md#the-right-hands-grip)).

The gun-in-hand watch (`controllers::weapons::grip_watch`) checks the result. Each placement records, per hand, how the gun sits on the solved wrist bone (and forgets the record of a hand it put no gun in, so a gun that just went to the other hand is not still read as the first hand's, for instance by the gun bodies), how far the animation had put the attach bone from the canonical grip before the pin, and the solved wrist's distance and rotation from its target. On every input tick the watch compares the gun's rotation on the solved wrist, and relative to the wrist's target (the controller's pose for it, so a gun turned by a wrist the solve left behind counts too), taking the larger change, with the previous tick's (a change past 90° is a snap) and with the rotation it had when the hand took the gun (a departure past 60° held for 100 ms is a sweep, which a turn blended over several ticks makes). That baseline is taken once the gun's slide into the grip has ended (a draw, a take, or a snatch sliding it from where it was, or a slide through the hand from a brace), since the slide turns the gun on the wrist by design. It starts afresh when the gun moves to another attach bone or the pin takes hold or lets go, across which the rotation is expected to change, and it is forgotten when no gun is held, so a gun thrown and caught again starts a new one. A move to another attach bone is logged. A warning comes with the bone, the weapon state, whether the bone was pinned, the animated deviation, the solved wrist's miss and drift since the grab, the IK weight, the hand tier, and the character's animation state id, and warnings are limited to one a second per hand. With the grip pinned the watch should stay quiet; the Weapons > Hold, transfer, and grab regions live status shows each hand's readings and counts.

The game's secondary hand IK ([`Character::UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateSecondaryHandIKPass)), which runs after the `MAIN` solve and can move the wrists again, is switched off for each controller-driven hand. The pass is driven by blackboard keys that animation state tasks write: each wrist's weight key, which the grapple fire arm IK and the parachute handle IK write (the parachute's persist between ticks), and the weapon off-hand grip's weight key. Some of those tasks run inside the pose-finalization pass after its entry ([animation-layers.md](../../engine/character/animation-layers.md#state-tasks-inside-the-pose-finalization-pass)), so the switch acts at the `SECONDARY` pass's own entry: zeroing a present, positive key there switches that part off for the frame, and a key the game did not write is never created. At the same entry the switch zeroes the driven wrist's current translation reach. A `SECONDARY` target queued before the hand was driven, such as the parachute handle's on the tick the hand tier blends in from the wingsuit's yielded tier, would otherwise outlive its key, because the pass removes an old entry only once its reach is zero and the `MAIN` target keeps the wrist's reach up every frame ([humanik.md](../../engine/character/humanik.md#target-entry-lifetime-and-the-is_valid-flag)); it would pull the wrist back to the handle whenever the pass solves for another target. The `MAIN` solve has used the reach by then, and the next `MAIN` pass drives it back up. A wrist with no reach is not held by the `SECONDARY` solve either, so while the weapon off-hand grip still runs for the other hand (its weight key positive after the switch), the driven wrist gets a weak `SECONDARY` target of its own at its `MAIN` target's position, through the wrist's world-space position key ([`SECONDARY_HAND_POSITION_WS_LEFT_ID`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/blackboard::ObjectBlackboard##field-SECONDARY_HAND_POSITION_WS_LEFT_ID) and its right twin) and its weight key at half reach, written for the frame; otherwise the grip's solve pulls the body and the released wrist rides along off the controller. The reach is unverified in the headset. The weapon-agnostic body also switches the off-hand grip off for a hand the controllers do not drive ([unarmed-body.md](../body/unarmed-body.md)).

The hand tiers classify each hand once per input tick from the mode detector's snapshot and a few state-machine predicates. The table holds one tier per state and is fixed at the plan's table (`HandTierTable::PLAN`, [controllers-and-roomscale.md, "Hand tiers"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#hand-tiers)), with the swimming row freed by `with_swim_arms`. The free, action-runs, and yielded tiers are built, and the anchored tier is built for the grapple reel and hang states and the mounted guns. A yielded hand blends from its controller to the animation over 0.25 s and back over 0.35 s (`YIELD_BLEND_S` and `RETURN_BLEND_S` in `controllers::hands::tiers`). A state assigned the anchored tier where that tier is not built yields. The action-runs tier is described under "Sockets, pouches, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier", and the anchored tier under "Recoil, haptics, and the grapple hand" and "Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight".

With `late_update` set, the rendered hands and weapon are corrected at render time. Once per rendered frame, after the VR pose is published, the late update snapshots the latest controller poses composed with the render-time head pose pair and the targets the last two ticks' IK was asked to reach. For the engine's interpolation fraction, each hand's correction is the latest controller pose interpolated between the head pose pair's two sides, times the inverse of the two ticks' targets interpolated the same way; the grip offset is on both sides and cancels. The correction limit checks physical wrist displacement and the shortest relative rotation, rather than the translation component of a world-space correction matrix. Partial weights interpolate the reference wrist toward the latest wrist before deriving the correction, so rotating a stationary wrist does not move its pivot with distance from the world origin. The post-call detour on [`WeaponBase::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdateRender) re-pushes the model matrix of a gun the local player holds in a hand attach bone as that correction times the engine's interpolated matrix. The character render-block palette hooks pre-multiply the hand and finger bones' palette entries by the same correction, and the forearm bones' entries by a swing about the elbow that takes the rendered wrist to the corrected wrist, so the forearm stays attached at both ends. The gun and the hand therefore never separate. A gun's separate model parts (a magazine, a pistol's slide, a minigun's spinning barrel) are weapon components with their own cached matrices and their own render update ([`ModelAttachementWeaponComponent::UpdateRender`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ModelAttachementWeaponComponent##func-UpdateRender)), so the gun's re-push left them a tick or two behind it. A post-call detour on that update re-pushes each part of the local player's wielded weapon as the correction of the hand it rides times its own interpolated matrix. A part rides its gun's hand unless an animation task put it on one of the holder's bones for the placement (a magazine carried in the off-hand during a reload); that bone is reset before the render update runs, so a post-call detour on the part's placement ([`ModelAttachementWeaponComponent::UpdateAttachmentTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ModelAttachementWeaponComponent##func-UpdateAttachmentTransforms)) records it for the local player's weapons. The code is `controllers::hands::parts`, and the Body/tracking > Hand presentation live status counts the re-pushed parts. The muzzle flash, the attached effects, the shot origin, and the grapple wire's near end stay on the sim-rate transform. With `hands.presentation` on (the default), the presentation layer takes over the palette edit: the hands keep this correction, and the arms are solved again from the shoulders to them and the torso bent after the headset every rendered frame, instead of the forearm's swing about the tick's elbow ([rendering/presentation-layer.md](../rendering/presentation-layer.md)).

## Finger curls

The code is in `payload/src/controllers/fingers/`, and the settings are `controllers.fingers`. Each finger of a hand the controllers drive gets a curl from 0 (straight) to 1 (a fist), and each finger but the thumb a splay, its sideways turn at the knuckle.

Each hand joint retains position validity, orientation validity, position tracking, and orientation tracking independently. Validity determines whether a component can be consumed; tracking describes whether the runtime actively tracks it. The C4 wrist button uses a valid, finite index-tip position without requiring orientation validity or either tracking flag. The diagnostic tracked-joint count requires both tracking flags.

The curls come from one of two sources, chosen by `source`: `Auto` uses joint curls when the required orientations are valid and the input estimate otherwise. `Joints` and `Estimate` take one source only; with `Joints`, unavailable required orientations leave the animated fingers unchanged. Joint positions and tracking flags do not gate orientation-based curls.

- From the joints. Every `XR_EXT_hand_tracking` joint has -Z along its bone and +Y out of the back of the hand, on both hands, so a joint's flexion is its rotation relative to its parent joint about the parent's -X, and a knuckle's splay is the rotation about +Y. A finger's curl is the sum of its knuckle, middle, and last flexions over their sum at a full fist; the thumb's is its two outer flexions over theirs. An unavailable orientation needed by the calculation rejects joint curls; `Auto` then uses the input estimate.
- The estimate, from the inputs the fingers rest on. The thumb bends onto the face when it touches the thumbstick, the trackpad, or a face button, a little further for the buttons, which sit nearer the palm, and further again while it presses; off the face it is straight. The index finger points off the trigger, rests bent on it when it touches it, and closes with the pull. The middle, ring, and little fingers share the grip: on the Index controller they follow the grip value and its force carries them to a fist, and on a Touch controller they rest on the handle and the grip's pull closes them. The estimate has no splay.

The curls follow their source with a time constant (`FINGERS.smoothing_s`, the curls' fixed tuning in `fingers::tuning::FINGERS`), which turns the estimate's jumps when a touch sensor changes into a quick motion.

The pose is absolute: each joint's bend is measured from a straight finger and replaces the animated bend rather than adding to it. The game's free-hand animation already holds the fingers curled, so a bend added on top of it left an open hand curled and pushed a full curl into the palm. The straight finger is built from the animated palm, which stays as animated: a finger's open direction is the one from the wrist to its knuckle, and the open thumb lies in the palm's plane, turned `FINGERS.thumb_spread_deg` from the fingers' direction toward the thumb side. The skeleton's bind pose is not used: the defs bind no reference pose for it, and whether its hands are open is unknown. A joint's bend at a curl is its finger's range (`FINGERS.curl_ranges`, the bend at the open and closed ends as a share of the full joint bends `FINGERS.finger_bend_deg` and `FINGERS.thumb_bend_deg`) plus that joint's `FINGERS.rest_offset_deg`, clamped to between 30 degrees back past straight and 110 degrees toward the palm. A finger bends toward the palm about the axis square to its open direction and to the palm's normal, and turns sideways by its splay (`FINGERS.splay_scale`, clamped to `FINGERS.max_splay_deg`) about the axis square to its open direction and to the thumb side; the thumb bends across the palm toward the little finger's side. Because the bones' own frames are unknown, each bone's axis along its length is read from the animated pose (the distal bone, which has no joint beyond it, is taken to share the middle bone's), and each bone turns by the shortest arc from its animated direction to its target, so its animated roll is kept. The hand's tier weight is the share of that arc taken, so a yielded hand blends back to the animated fingers. The palm's frame is built from the animated wrist and the index, middle, and little knuckles, as the wrist IK's is. Every axis comes from the hand's geometry, so the left hand is the right one's mirror image with no per-side sign. The rest offsets and the thumb spread are where to correct the straight reference if the wrist-to-knuckle direction or the distal bone's axis is off for Rico's rig.

The fingers are written once per sim tick at the end of the pose-finalization pass, in the post-call detour on [`Character::UpdatePropEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropEffects), with [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) over the finalized model-space pose, as the head override is ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md)). The bones are Rico's `{Side}HandThumb1-3`, `{Side}HandIndex1-3`, `{Side}HandMiddle1-3`, `{Side}HandRing1-3`, and `{Side}HandPinky1-3`, found by name; the in-hand ring and little metacarpals stay with the palm. Each finger's joints are read before any is written and written from the root outward, so the result does not depend on how a write carries the bones beyond it. Writing at the sim tick rather than in the skinning palette is enough because the late update already moves the finger bones rigidly with the corrected hand, so the curled fingers follow the controller at frame rate.

A hand is curled only while the controllers drive it, that is while its wrist target was queued this tick, so the curls need `hands.ik`. A hand that holds something takes that thing's grip pose, blended from the measured curls by how firmly it grips; the sources declare what each hand holds, and the poses, the blend, and the contact fit that closes the fingers onto a held body are in [grip-poses.md](grip-poses.md).

The pose is a shape rather than bare curls: besides each finger's curl and splay, a hook that moves the bend from the knuckle to the outer joints or back, and the thumb's opposition, its turn out of the palm's plane toward the palm side. Measured curls have no hook and no opposition.

The debug UI's Grip and fingers feature shows whether the joints are located with the controller-conforming motion range and, per hand, the source in use, the tier weight, what the hand holds and how its fingers take it, and each finger's curl, splay, hook, and whether it is driven.

## Aiming

The engine side is [aim-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md) and [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md). Both parts act only under the controller scheme, in gameplay, and for a tracked hand, each behind its toggle under `controllers.aim`. The hands are composed and their rays cast in every such update, even with both toggles off, because the grenade throw and the melee target follow the hands as well.

Everything runs from the player aim control's post-simulation update ([`PlayerAimControl::UpdatePostSim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-UpdatePostSim)), the simulation-phase update in which the game casts its own aim ray. Before the update, the hands are composed into the world from the latest controller snapshot and the grapple ray is handed to the grapple's hooks. After it, the hand rays are cast, the guns' aim points are published for the per-weapon update, and the tick's results are published for the overlay. For the player, the aim update and the weapon updates run one after the other on the same thread, aim first, so the weapon update always sees the current tick's points.

The raycasts use the engine's closest-hit query ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay)) with the player aim's own filter ([`PlayerAimRaycastFilter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimRaycastFilter)), built the way the game's aim ray builds it. The filter rejects every physics instance of the ignored objects (the character's capsule, ragdoll bodies, hit proxies, and held weapons), which an instance-only filter would not.

With `gun` set, each gun of the wielded weapon aims along its own sight line: the gun's transform carried to the controller's current pose by the hand's late-update correction, composed with the gun's current fire point, whose -Z axis is the barrel, and with the weapon's sight correction (see "Sight lines"). The line is cast once per aim update up to `RAY_LENGTH_M` (`controllers::aim`). Its hit, or a far point along it, becomes that hand's aim point, never nearer than two metres, because the game fires along the barrel for an aim point near the muzzle. The per-weapon base update ([`WeaponBase::UpdateFromUserPostSim_ParallelBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdateFromUserPostSim_ParallelBase)) is handed that point in place of the character's shared one, for the gun that can fire in that call ([`WeaponBase::m_NextWeaponToFire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_NextWeaponToFire)), and the weapon stores it as its own aim target before it builds the shot. The character-level aim point follows the dominant hand's point, so the game's own setter still marks the point as set for the frame. A long gun raised to the eye is lined up with it, its stock seated in the shoulder and a scope welded to the eye, so that the sight line it shoots along is the one the eye looks down ([physical-weapons.md, "Aiming down the sights"](physical-weapons.md#aiming-down-the-sights)).

With `grapple` set, the grapple slot follows the off-hand. The ray starts at `GRAPPLE_ORIGIN_M` (`controllers::aim`) in the off-hand's wrist frame. Every part of the aim control that fills the grapple slot takes its ray from one of two getters: [`PlayerAimControl::GetAdjustedCameraMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-GetAdjustedCameraMatrix) and [`GameCameraManager::GetCameraMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::game_camera_manager::GameCameraManager##func-GetCameraMatrix). While a thread-local override is set, both return a camera matrix built from the grapple ray, so the engine fills the slot with its own logic:

- The direct-aim update ([`PlayerAimControl::UpdateDirectAim`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-UpdateDirectAim)) runs twice. The first pass, under the override, fills every slot from the grapple ray with the engine's own filter, range, holey-geometry handling, and target resolution, and the grapple slot is taken out of it. The second pass, without the override, refills every other slot from the camera as the game does, and the grapple slot is put back.
- The grapple target scoring ([`PlayerAimControl::SetTargetGrappleFitness`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-SetTargetGrappleFitness)) runs under the override, so entity attachment and tethering select their target the way the game does, around the hand's ray. The grapple's visibility check ([`PlayerAimControl::IsTargetVisibleCast`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-IsTargetVisibleCast)) does not go through the engine: the engine keeps one scheduled visibility raycast per target and answers every target type from its latest result, so a grapple request from the hand would leak into the camera-based types and theirs into the grapple. The grapple's check is instead a synchronous cast from the hand ray's origin to the target's body position, clear if it hits nothing or hits within 0.75 m of the end, ignoring the player and the player's vehicle.
- The last-valid grapple cache is re-validated under the override ([`PlayerAimControl::IsLastValidGrappleAimPosStillValid`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-IsLastValidGrappleAimPosStillValid)).

The grapple's aim assist (`aim.grapple_assist_strength`, 1 by default, and `aim.grapple_assist_cone_deg`, 5 by default, both on the debug UI's aim settings) turns the grapple ray before the aim update, so the game's own targeting above runs along the turned ray (`controllers::aim::assist::grapple_assist`). It acts only when the hand's own ray does not already hit an object, which keeps the game's own target, a weapon the snatch would take included. It walks the target system's characters, vehicles, and other objects, keeps those whose body position is within the cone of the hand's ray and within 150 m, asks the game's grapple scoring (`SetTargetGrappleFitness`, under the hand ray's view) whether each is a target the grapple attaches to, in range, and not the object the wire holds, and takes the one nearest the ray in angle with a clear line from the gauntlet. The ray turns toward its body position by the strength: at one it aims at it, and zero turns the assist off. The fitness the scoring writes on each candidate is reset by the game's own target pass in the update that follows. The readout's grapple line shows the turn and the target, and the overlay's grapple highlight adds a thin outer ring while the target was reached by the assist. A grapplable surface near the ray is not searched: the game's last-valid grapple cache already holds a surface point while the ray grazes off it. Under the gamepad scheme the aim does not act at all, so head-tracked gamepad play is unaffected.

The readout of the grapple's aim (`controllers::aim::GrappleAim`) is the grapple slot after the game's targeting only while the game takes a grapple fire ([`GrapplingHook::GetFireInputEnabled`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-GetFireInputEnabled); [grapple-pipeline.md](../../engine/gameplay/grapple-pipeline.md#the-fire-gate)). While it does not (a vehicle seat, a fire under way, a landing), nothing in the slot is reported as hit or valid and the point is marked as not current, since the slot is not refreshed: in a seat it would hold the boarding point for the whole ride. In a vehicle seat with the seat grapple on, the aim casts the off-hand's ray itself to the grapple's range and reports that instead ([seat-grapple.md](seat-grapple.md)).

The per-slot game objects are weak pointers. Moving one out and back is a raw copy with the source zeroed, which leaves its weak count unchanged; clearing one goes through the engine's own assignment, which releases the reference.

While the controller scheme is in effect, the gamepad-semantics pin holds the active-input-device setting at the gamepad, so melee, aim assist, and the prompts behave as on a gamepad. The game's own aim assist stays on the gaze; per-hand aim assist is described under "Grenades, melee, aim assist, and reloads".

## Sight lines

A weapon's sight correction moves the line's origin from the fire point and turns it, in the fire point's frame, and is looked up by the weapon's name hash in order: the per-weapon entry in the config (`aim.sights`, which the sight tuning tool writes), then the embedded sight table, then none, which leaves the line on the fire point. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/aim/sights/`.

The embedded table is `tools/sights/sights.json`, derived offline from the game's weapon models by the sight tool ([`tools/sights/README.md`](../../../tools/sights/README.md)) and compiled into the payload. Only its `scoped`, `pistol`, and `tuned` entries are used; the `iron_uncertain` and `failed` entries are not trusted and leave the weapon on its fire point. The config's table holds up to 32 entries, room for every weapon the embedded table does not trust. The Weapons > Aim, sights, and scopes live status shows where the held weapon's correction comes from.

The sight tuning tool (`aim.sight_tuning.enabled`, off) is in the debug UI's Weapons > Aim, sights, and scopes, and is the only editor of the config's table. While a weapon is held, it shows the weapon's offset and angles and their source, including the grade of an embedded entry and whether a config entry overrides one. "Line up" places a marker in the world ten metres straight ahead of the head, which the overlay draws over the scene with a dot where the dominant hand's sight line passes closest to it. The player lines the gun's sights up on the marker and captures, with the tab's "Capture" button or the capture chord (see "Bindings and injection"). A capture is serviced on the next aim update: it solves the offset that makes the dominant hand's sight line, at the weapon's current angles, pass through the marker, taking the point on that line directly above or below the muzzle, and stores it as the weapon's config entry. A capture is refused when no weapon is held, the dominant hand holds no gun, the marker is not ahead of the muzzle, or the solved origin is more than half a metre from the muzzle. Drag values adjust the offset and angles finely, writing the config entry; "Reset to the table" removes the config entry and "Reset to the fire point" stores an empty one. Tuned entries are saved in the settings file (`controllers.aim.sights`, a list of the entries held) like any other setting ([settings.md](../settings.md)). "Copy as JSON" puts the weapon's entry on the clipboard in the table's line format, graded `tuned`, for pasting into `tools/sights/sights.json`, whose extractor keeps tuned entries when it regenerates the table.

## The world-space overlay

The overlay draws coloured, alpha-blended sprites and thick lines into the scene per eye, for readouts that need their own depths. It draws at the end of [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw), onto each eye's final back buffer after the post chain and the upscaler and before the HUD panel, so the readouts keep their colours through exposure changes and do not smear under temporal reconstruction. It works with and without single-pass stereo, and it skips the far-field pass and the clean view at the everything scope. Everything is behind `controllers.overlay.enabled` and acts only under the controller scheme.

Producers run at different rates on different threads, so each source owns a retained slot that its submission replaces wholesale and stamps, and the renderer draws a slot until its time to live runs out. A producer that stops submitting (its toggle turned off, a mode change, a menu) disappears on its own. The store's lock is a leaf, so any thread may submit. A position is in world space, anchored to a hand, or an offset in world axes from the head; an anchored position is placed from the render-time hand pose or head position, so a hand-attached end follows the controller at frame rate even when its producer submits at the sim rate, and a point placed in the cockpit frame rides with a falling or driving player instead of trailing them by a tick's travel.

The overlay's own producers run once per rendered frame on the render thread:

- `grapple_highlight`: a ring on a valid, in-range hook point, in its own colour with a centre dot when the target can be tethered, and a thin outer ring when the grapple's aim assist turned the ray onto it;
- `grapple_beam`: a beam from the wrist to the grapple's target point, while that point is current;
- `gun_readout`: nothing by default (`None`), a dot at the hit point (`Dot`), or a line from the sight to the hit point (`Laser`);
- `ghost_hands`: a translucent ghost at the controller of each yielded hand, drawn at `xray_alpha` where the scene is in front of it;
- `debug_gizmos` (on by default): each hand's grip and aim axes;
- `body_rig` (on by default): the body rig's neck and waist pivots, the spine between them, and its rings on the floor, coloured by the step phase ([body/rig.md](../body/rig.md#settings-and-diagnostics));
- `body_mirror` (off by default), submitted from the pose-finalization pass rather than the render thread: Rico's solved skeleton reflected in a mirror ahead of him, with the targets it was posed toward ([body/procedural-states.md](../body/procedural-states.md#the-body-mirror));
- the sight tuning marker, while the tuning tool is on and its marker is placed: a ring and a dot at the marker, and a dot where the dominant hand's sight line passes closest to it, all over the scene.

The grapple and gun producers submit only when the aim has run since their last submission; the ghost hands and gizmos follow the render-time hand poses and submit every frame. The grip targets' markers are submitted by the grip model at the input tick's rate ([grip.md, "Markers"](grip.md#markers)). The debug panel's controller pointer submits its laser at the input tick's rate too, whatever the control scheme, with both ends anchored to the pointing hand's aim pose ([hud.md, "Pointing at the debug panel with the controllers"](../hud.md#pointing-at-the-debug-panel-with-the-controllers)).

The game's own HUD reticles (`overlay.hide_game_reticle`, on, independent of `overlay.enabled`). The HUD movie's weapon reticle sits at the centre of the screen and its grapple reticle at the gaze's grapple aim, pinned to the centre when the grapple aims at a direct position ([aim-pipeline.md, "The reticles"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#the-reticles)), so under hand aim both mark a point nothing aims at. `controllers/overlay/game_reticle.rs` decides each group by the detected mode. The grapple reticle group is hidden everywhere under the controller scheme: on foot, swimming, falling, in the wingsuit, under the parachute, in every seat, and with `aim.grapple` off too, since the overlay's grapple highlight and gauntlet beam mark the grapple's target in the world and a cursor on the panel adds nothing to them. The weapon reticle group is hidden wherever the guns' aim belongs to the hands. On foot, swimming, falling, in the wingsuit, and under the parachute, it is hidden whenever `aim.gun` is on, armed or not, so it does not show in the gaps between hand-aim results (drawing, holstering, switching weapons, an empty hand). In the driver's seat it is hidden while `vehicles.driver_weapons_by_hand` is on, since the vehicle's built-in weapons then aim at the dominant hand's point. At a mounted gun it is hidden while the gun's point comes from the hands (`vehicles.mounted.aim_by_hands`), which has its own freshness. In a passenger seat, and with a menu up, it is hidden while the aim's latest results aimed the wielded weapon by hand, a result counting only while the aim's update count has moved in the last quarter second. A gun the gaze still aims keeps its reticle: the driver's weapons with `driver_weapons_by_hand` off, a mounted gun with its hand aim off, and every gun with `aim.gun` off. The groups are held invisible at the HUD's capture seam through the same clip handles and intent tracking as the full-screen overlay suppression (`hud/scaleform.rs`), which absorb the movie rewriting the weapon group's visibility on every HUD update, and are restored when the rule stops holding. Hiding them loses the reticle's hostile and allied tint, its out-of-range dimming, the weapon's spread, the grapple's in-range cue, and the attached-wire marker. The overlay's `grapple_highlight` covers the in-range cue, drawn only on a valid hook point and tinted for a tetherable one, and `gun_readout` (`Dot` or `Laser`) can mark the gun's hit point; nothing replaces the target tint, the spread, or the attached-wire marker. The hit marker (`MCI_inflict_damage`) plays at the weapon reticle, so it is held hidden with the weapon reticle group; the remote-explosive indicators are other clips and stay. Where the reticles show, the HUD atlas draws them at the aim point ([ui-decoupling.md](../rendering/ui-decoupling.md#the-atlas-as-built)).

The frame's primitives are resolved against the head (the cyclopean eye) rather than per eye, so both eyes draw identical world geometry and the stereo pair fuses. They are made relative to the frame's origin and sorted far to near, because the overlay writes no depth. Each eye projects them through a translation-free view-projection, as the engine's own geometry does, because at the game's world coordinates an absolute `f32` world-to-clip transform jitters by about a millimetre. The renderer uploads the primitives once, on the first eye's draw, and issues one instanced draw per eye over a structured buffer (`payload/src/shaders/overlay_vs.hlsl` and `overlay_ps.hlsl`). No depth target is bound: the pixel shader reads the main depth texture and tests against it with a tolerance (`depth_tolerance_m`, or `depth_tolerance_rel` of the view distance where that is larger), so the engine's depth-stencil view is never touched. Lines thinner than `min_line_px` fade instead of thinning. The draw saves and restores the pipeline state it changes, except the viewport, which every mod draw at this seam sets itself.

## Posture and roomscale

This is milestone 2, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/roomscale/` and `payload/src/vr/stage.rs`, and the settings are under `controllers.roomscale`, except the head remap (`controllers.body.remap_head`) and the seated crouch flick (`controllers.bindings.crouch_flick`). The body following the head about the room is the rig's ([rig.md](../body/rig.md), `payload/src/controllers/body/rig/`, `controllers.body.rig`). The design is in the plan's ["Posture"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#posture) and ["Roomscale design"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#roomscale-design). These parts do not depend on the controller scheme, because a gamepad player standing in a room moves the head in the same way. They act only while the VR source owns the head pose, and each part that changes behaviour is on by default, as part of the full gameplay preset.

When the runtime offers a STAGE reference space, the session creates one beside the LOCAL space. Once per frame the mid-eye pose in LOCAL is located against STAGE, which gives the head's height above the floor, and the play area's bounds are read again whenever the runtime reports a reference-space change. The bounds are logged (`stage: play area bounds`, info) the first time and whenever they change; a burst of reference-space changes that leaves them as they were logs at debug level only.

The floor is not trusted blindly across a reference-space change (`vr/stage.rs`, `FloorWatch`). A runtime may move the STAGE floor when it fires one: in one session Monado fired a burst of them and the floor dropped by about 0.35 m, which put the view a quarter of a metre above Rico's eyes and every standing measure off by as much. For five seconds after each change, a step in the head's height above the floor between two frames larger than a head moves in one (eight centimetres) is taken to be the floor's, not the player's, and is taken back out of the height every consumer reads, so the view and the body keep their standing height. Each such jump logs `stage: the floor jumped across a reference space change; the jump is taken out of the head height` at info level with the jump and the total shift, which the Body/tracking > Posture live status shows beside the height. The shift lasts for the session. A floor redrawn on purpose mid-session is taken out the same way; the posture's thresholds and the calibration then measure against the floor as it was. The eye views, the controllers, and the compositor layer stay in LOCAL, so a runtime without a STAGE space loses only the floor height.

The work is split in two halves. The frame half runs once per rendered VR frame, from the pose composition on the game thread, after the simulation tick and before the render update. It resolves the posture, remaps the head height, hands the cockpit to the rig's frame half for the cockpit shift ([rig.md, "The bookkeeping"](../body/rig.md#the-bookkeeping)), tracks the seat phase, and advances the fade, and it publishes the posture, whether the character counts as crouched, and the seat phase for the simulation half. The simulation half is the rig's root hook ([rig.md, "Who moves the root"](../body/rig.md#who-moves-the-root)), which also steps the crouch capsule on every tick the on-foot task wrote the proxy's velocity; the head probe runs on every simulation tick in every state ([rig.md, "Walls"](../body/rig.md#walls)).

Posture (`posture`). `Seated` and `Standing` select the posture directly ([rig.md, "Posture"](../body/rig.md#posture)). OpenXR has no signal for a seated user, so `Auto` tells seated from standing by the head's height above the STAGE floor (`roomscale/posture.rs`, `PostureClassifier`). The thresholds sit between the seated eye height (the saved `seated_eye_height`, or 0.72 of the standing eye height without one) and the standing eye height: until a head height is located the player counts as seated, because a seated treatment never forces a crouch while a standing one turns a seated player into a crouching stander; the first located height and the height at a manual recenter classify at once against the midpoint; after that a seated player stands once the head stays above 60% of the way to the standing eye height for 1 s, which a seated player cannot reach without standing up, and a standing player sits once the head stays below 40% of the way for 20 s, which is long so that a crouch behind cover stays a physical crouch. The head must stay at a chair's height for that: steady, within eight centimetres of where the wait started (a head that moves further starts it again from where it is, so a crouch that rises to peek and ducks again never sits), and above the floor level, the seated eye height less 0.12 of the standing eye height. A standing player whose head goes lower than that has got down onto the floor of the play area, sitting or kneeling on it, and stays standing however long it lasts, so the head keeps its true height; getting up from the floor onto a chair is seated after the usual wait. The first located height and a recenter still classify against the midpoint, so a player who starts or recenters sitting on the floor is seated. A crouch or an upright kneel held steady longer than the wait is the classifier's known failure; the explicit postures avoid it. Each change of the detection, or of whether the head is at floor level, logs `roomscale: the automatic posture's detection changes` at info level with the head's height and the thresholds. The controllers' heights are not used, because a crouching player's hands sit as low as a seated one's. With a standing eye height of 1.70 m and a seated one of 1.22 m, the midpoint is 1.46 m, so a head at 1.05 m is seated from the first frame.

Head height (`controllers.body.remap_head`, on). In the standing posture with a floor, the true eye height is remapped onto the character by the ratio of the heights, and the view's vertical cockpit offset is the remapped height less the character's standing eye height, which is where the animated head anchor already puts the eyes. The player's own standing eye height in the remap is the one the body rig measures ([rig.md, "The pelvis solver"](../body/rig.md#the-pelvis-solver)), seeded at the calibration and held within fifteen centimetres of it, rather than the calibration itself: a player who stands a few centimetres below the calibrated height, as most do once they relax, would otherwise see from Rico's collar. In the 2 November 2026 standing session the calibration's 1.706 m put the camera a median 8.4 cm below Rico's eyes on foot; the rig measured about 1.65 m, which puts it about a centimetre below. In the seated posture with a floor, the head's travel from the seated eye reference (`roomscale/seated.rs`) scales by the height ratio, so the eyes sit at the character's standing eye height while the player sits at the reference. A single calibrated height is fragile, because it belongs to one chair, one way of sitting, and one moment, so the reference follows the player. It starts at the saved `seated_eye_height`, or at the head's height on the first seated frame without one, and a new calibration starts it again. A manual recenter while seated captures the head's height as the reference. The adaptive baseline (`roomscale::seated::SEATED_REFERENCE`) samples the head height over 120 s and, once a quarter of the window is filled, moves the reference toward the median at most 1 cm/s whenever the two are more than 2 cm apart; a lean, a duck, or a look around held for less than half the window does not move the median, so deliberate head movement stays one to one. Otherwise in the seated posture, and in a standing posture on a runtime without a STAGE space, the baseline is the eye height at the last recenter, and vertical travel from it scales by the height ratio. The standing postures stay measured from the floor with no captured reference: the standing eye height is the rig's measure, which samples only a level gaze in the standing posture and ignores a crouch held for less than a few minutes. With the remap off, the vertical offset is the raw cockpit height, which already puts the seated eye height at the last recenter at the character's standing eye height. The seated crouch toggle lowers the seated eyes by `posture::SEATED_CROUCH_DROP_M`, with or without the remap, but only on foot and out of a vehicle seat: in a seat it would drop the view into the footwell, and under a canopy, in the wingsuit, or swimming into Rico's torso. The toggle keeps its state for when Rico is back on foot. On foot, flicking the dominant stick down past `controllers.bindings.crouch_flick` toggles it, and the toggle acts only in the seated posture (including a seated `Auto`), because standing players crouch physically.

The body following the head (`controllers.body.rig.steps.enabled`, on; the pause menu's `BODY FOLLOWS YOU`). On foot out of a vehicle seat, in either posture, the body rig separates a lean from a step, and a committed step walks Rico under the player's neck through the game's own movement, on top of the stick, while the cockpit shift keeps the camera on the real head ([rig.md, "Steps"](../body/rig.md#steps)). A seated player's head moves only by the upper body, so seated steps are rare ([rig.md, "Posture"](../body/rig.md#posture)). Neither posture turns the body without a turn from the player.

Teleports. A post-call detour on [`GameCameraManager::ResetCamera`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::game_camera_manager::GameCameraManager##func-ResetCamera) counts a teleport, because the game resets the camera right after every discontinuity it makes to the player: an instant or deferred teleport, a respawn, a load's start position, and a playlist commit. A move that bypasses the game world, such as a vehicle teleport carrying its driver, is caught by a per-tick distance test: a body displacement further than `rig::root::drive::WARP_MIN_M`, and further than three times what the proxy's speed covers in the tick, counts as a teleport too. The test forgets the body's last position when the locomotion task stops running, because the body may move any distance in between. On a teleport the head probe forgets its last clear position, and the rig starts its bookkeeping over ([rig.md, "Teleports and seat exits"](../body/rig.md#teleports-and-seat-exits)).

Recenters. What a recenter re-bases is the rig's ([rig.md, "Recentering"](../body/rig.md#recentering)): out of a vehicle seat, seated or standing, the yaw and the height only, after which the rig snaps the pelvis under the neck. A manual recenter also classifies the automatic posture at once and, while seated, captures the seated eye reference. The seat transitions request their own re-bases (`vr::request_seat_recenter`), which do neither, because they happen on the game's schedule rather than the player's.

The head fade (`head_fade.enabled`, on). On every simulation tick in gameplay, in every state (on foot, the wingsuit, the parachute, a vehicle, a ragdoll, a get-up), the rig's head probe casts from the last position the head held in the open to where it is now, through the eye centre and to either side of it ([rig.md, "Walls"](../body/rig.md#walls)), and a hit means geometry lies between them. A second cast from the animated head, which the body's capsule keeps in the open, forgives a stale last position, so a head the body has carried around the obstruction counts as clear again. The casts use the player aim's filter (see "Aiming"), because a plain ray query can hit the player's own ragdoll and hit-proxy bodies, which a head moving down or forward past the torso crosses. The head's depth past the hit sets the fade: clear up to `probe::HEAD_FADE_START_DEPTH_M`, black from `probe::HEAD_FADE_FULL_DEPTH_M`, and smooth between. A terrain check adds the eyes' depth below the heightfield, for a clip that carries the animated head into the ground with them. When the probe stops running (out of gameplay, or with the headset not driving the view), its result expires and its fade clears.

The comfort fade (`roomscale::fade::FADE_TIMING`). The head fade, the push-back past its cap ([rig.md, "Walls"](../body/rig.md#walls)), and the seat transitions each set a target darkness. The level eases toward the darkest target, darkening and clearing at its fixed rates, and the VR blit scales each eye's image by one minus the level as it copies the image into the swapchain. The engine's own screen fade is not used, because the game drives it for its own transitions and a second writer would fight them. Darkening in the blit also leaves the flat mirror unchanged. A gap in the rendered frames resets the fade.

Crouching. The eyes' drop below the character's standing eye height, after the head remap, marks the character crouched once it passes `crouch.enter_drop_m`, and standing once it is back above `crouch::CROUCH_EXIT_DROP_M`. Standing on the ground, the drop is the crouch the body rig measures, the head's drop that no bend at the waist explains, carried onto Rico's proportions, so a look at the floor or a lean over a table does not count ([rig.md, "Posture"](../body/rig.md#posture)). The crouch capsule below follows it.

The crouch capsule (`crouch.capsule`, on). While the character counts as crouched, the simulation half requests the game's crouching proxy state ([`ProxyState::CROUCHING`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::ProxyState##variant-CROUCHING)) through the character's pending proxy state ([`Character::m_PendingProxyState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_PendingProxyState)), which the engine applies on the simulation thread after the state tasks run. The request is made only while the pending state is the character's default ([`Character::m_DefaultProxyState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_DefaultProxyState)) or already the crouch, so the game's own proxy states (swimming, the parachute, vehicles, and ragdoll) always win. When the head rises, the request is released only once a ray up from above the feet to the top of a standing character finds the headroom to stand. The frame half also releases it when the capsule is switched off or the locomotion task stops running, and the simulation half releases it on any tick the headset is not driving the view (the headpose source is not VR, the roomscale frame is stale because the headset went idle, or the game is out of gameplay).

Seat transitions (`seat_transition`). The game's vehicle predicates do not separate an entry, a locked seat, and an exit, so the seat phase is classified from the body rule system's current state by name: entering, locked (including a manned mounted gun and the motorcycle), shuffling between seats, and exiting. The names are hashed at startup with the engine's state-name hash, so no hash is embedded. A state in none of the tables counts as out of the vehicle, which applies no fade and no re-base, and a state in none of them seen while the character still has a vehicle is logged. A locked state while the character has no vehicle keeps the previous phase. At each edge the mode acts:

- `Ride` (the default) lets the view ride the animations and, when the seat locks, re-bases only the cockpit's yaw, by the head's heading against the body at the latest follow bank ([rig.md, "The body turning under the view"](../body/rig.md#the-body-turning-under-the-view)). Without it the head's offset from the body when the body stopped following, inside the yaw's turn angle, carries into the seat as a look off the vehicle's axis: at 23:12 of the 2 November 2026 session the player looked 18 degrees off the car's axis for six seconds after a hijack, with Rico centred on the wheel. The re-base happens during the entry, a movement action the player took, and turns the view by at most the turn angle; a bank older than `recenter::SEAT_YAW_BANK_WINDOW` (15 s), or one a recenter has since spent, is not used.
- `Instant` darkens the view during the entry, clears it and re-bases fully when the seat locks, shows the exit, and re-bases yaw and height when the character is out.
- `Fade` does the same, darkens the view during the exit as well, and holds the view dark for `seat::SEAT_FADE_HOLD_S` after the seat locks and after the exit ends, so that the re-based view settles before it clears.

A shuffle between seats stays locked and does not re-base. The re-base at seat lock is needed under a head-anchored camera: the seated head bone sits lower than the standing one and elsewhere, so the standing cockpit offset is wrong once seated.

The debug UI's Body/tracking > Posture live status shows the posture, the STAGE space and its bounds, the head's height above the floor, the head height remap and its source, the eye drop and the crouch states, the teleports, the head probe, the fade's level and targets, and the seat phase with its last edge. It also has a button that toggles the seated crouch; the feature's controls are the crouch capsule and the crouch-below height. The steps, the lean, and the shift are in Body/tracking > Body fit ([rig.md, "Settings and diagnostics"](../body/rig.md#settings-and-diagnostics)).

The section opens with the camera height readout (`payload/src/hooks/camera/height.rs`), which diagnoses a view that sits too low or too high. Each gameplay frame the headpose places the camera, the camera hook records the camera's eye point and Rico's animated eye midpoint, rebuilt from the published anchors the way the camera hangs its eye point from them (the head anchor, the neck delta, and the eye arm), both measured up from the character's root, the translation of its world matrix. Beside the two heights and their difference the readout shows the vertical offset the cockpit composes with (the head remap's offset times the world scale), whether the remap is on, the head's height above the STAGE floor, the player's standing eye height from the body dimensions (the remap uses the rig's measure instead) and the character's eye height the remap assumes, the posture setting and the posture, the automatic posture's decision with its reason, thresholds, and dwell, the seated eye reference with its source and the adaptive baseline's median and sample count, where Rico is (on foot or in a vehicle seat), and the crouch, seated crouch, and capsule states, with the capsule's lack of headroom. With a zero vertical offset and no configured camera offsets the two heights agree, so a low view with a small offset points at Rico's animated eyes sitting below the assumed eye height, and a large negative offset points at the remap or at a seated eye reference above where the player sits. While Rico is on foot or in a vehicle seat the same readout is logged at info level (target `vr`) at most every five seconds. It only reads: nothing it records feeds back into the camera.

## Sockets, pouches, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier

This is the first half of milestone 3, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/` (`mod.rs`, `volumes.rs`, `docks.rs`, `foregrip.rs`, and `engine.rs`), and the settings are `weapons.holsters` and `weapons.foregrip`. What a grip takes, and when, is the grip model's ([grip.md](grip.md)). The design is in the plan's ["Grip semantics"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#grip-semantics), ["Holsters"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#holsters), ["Two-handed weapons"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#two-handed-weapons), and ["Hand tiers"](controllers-and-roomscale.md#hand-tiers). Everything here acts only while the bindings inject, which is only under the controller scheme with a controller active.

The weapon handling runs on the input tick, in two halves around the bindings' tables. The first half reads the wielded weapon and provides the weapon handling's grip targets: the body's sockets and pouches, a held two-handed weapon's foregrip, a gun's slide, charging handle, or bolt while its chamber waits (see [reload.md](reload.md)), and the rest listed in [grip.md, "Targets"](grip.md#targets). The grip model picks what each hand takes among every subsystem's targets. The second half acts on the grabs the grip model hands it and holds the fire inputs back during a weapon action. A gun stays in the hand while its grip is held; nothing else draws or docks a weapon.

Each pouch is offered with a reach margin of `POUCHES.reach_margin_m` (0.05 m) around it, a fallback taken only when nothing else is in reach: a hand feeling for a pouch on the body lands a little off it more often than not.

The body volumes ride Rico's skeleton, the body the player sees, and are reached by the hand as it is drawn, so what the player sees on the body is where they reach ([rig.md, "The body's volumes on Rico's skeleton"](../body/rig.md#the-bodys-volumes-on-ricos-skeleton)). The sockets are reached on their weapons ([physical-weapons.md, "The sockets"](physical-weapons.md#the-sockets)), with the radii of `SOCKETS` in `controllers::weapons::volumes`. Each pouch sits at its fixed offset (`offset_m`, mirrored for the left side) and radius (`radius_m`), from `POUCHES`, from a landmark of the skeleton: the chest's front on each side, 0.22 of the torso below the shoulder joints, about half the shoulder width out from the midline, and in front of the shoulder joints' line, for the grenade and C4 pouches; and the belt's front on the side of the hand without a gun, 0.17 of the torso above the hip joints, an eighth of the shoulder width out from the midline  (toward the middle of the belt), and in front of the hip joints' line, where the belly is, for the magazine pouch. The torso is the distance from the middle of the hip joints to the middle of the shoulder joints. The magazine pouch's offset carries it out from the belly's surface, and the chest pouches' brings them back to the chest's surface and a little below the landmark, where a hand laid on the chest with the gaze level closes. Looking down bends Rico's chest forward (the procedural torso pitches the upper back by a share of the look, and the body rig reads the neck pivot coming forward as a lean), and the player's own chest barely moves, so the chest pouches ride the chest turned back toward upright about the middle of the hip joints by the look's share: none of it for a look of up to about 10 degrees down, all of it from about 40, and only while the spine is within about 30 degrees of upright, fading to none at 50, so a body lying in a skydive or folded further than a look explains keeps its pouches on its chest (`BodyFrame::looking`). A lean with the head level keeps them on the bent chest.

The sockets' radii are 0.12 m at the hips, 0.17 m at the shoulders and the outer back, and 0.09 m at the chest. The magazine pouch has a radius of 0.075 m, 0.04 m in front of its landmark; the grenade pouch 0.09 m and the C4 pouch 0.1 m, each 0.04 m behind its landmark and 0.02 m below it. In the 2 November 2026 standing session, of the 48 grip presses at the chest with the gaze within 30 degrees of level that took a chest pouch or nothing, the old 0.075 m pouches 0.04 m in front of the landmark take 18 (the log has 17 takes), and these take 22; pouches moved the whole 0.13 m back to where the hand lands flat on the chest take only 17, because the player had learnt to reach out in front of the chest for them. The hit rates come from the press positions measured from the controllers in the head's frame against the pouches' centres fitted from the logged distances, since the recording does not hold Rico's skeleton. The grenade pouch is on the dominant hand's side of the chest and the C4 pouch on the other, whichever hand holds a gun: with a pistol in each hand there is no single gun hand, and chest pouches that changed sides with each handover would have to be found again each time. The magazine pouch is laid out around the gun hand rather than the dominant hand: the hand holding a gun, or with none drawn, the hand that last held one, or before any, the dominant hand. A single gun stays on the hand attach bone the game gives it, so a left-handed player can hold it in the right hand, and a layout keyed off the dominant hand would put the magazine pouch on the gun's side; remembering the last gun hand keeps it from changing sides at each dock. It sits at the front of the free hand's hip, or with both hands holding pistols, at the hip away from the gun hand; a layout for a left gun hand mirrors one for a right. With these, every volume keeps at least 0.01 m from every other, and every pouch at least 0.04 m from every socket, on skeletons of the character's proportions, the generic adult's, and a 1.8 m body's, facing ahead or turned, with either hand dominant (a unit test holds this).

Every socket and pouch is marked on the body, faintly until a hand comes near ([grip.md, "Markers"](grip.md#markers)), so their places can be learned. A pouch is placed only while its feature is: the grenade pouch with the grenade throw, the C4 pouch with the C4 throw, and the magazine pouch while the true reload reloads the held weapon. A grip press that takes nothing is logged with the nearest target it was out of reach of ([grip.md, "Readouts and logs"](grip.md#readouts-and-logs)).

Sockets (always on; the reaches `SOCKETS.hip_radius_m`, `SOCKETS.shoulder_radius_m`, and `SOCKETS.chest_radius_m`). Every grab at the body is the loadout table's decision ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The grabs against the table"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-grabs-against-the-table)), and the sockets' volumes are seated from the table ([physical-weapons.md, "The sockets"](physical-weapons.md#the-sockets)). Either hand may grab any socket: an empty hand draws the weapon on it, and a hand holding a weapon docks it on a free socket that fits it, by a grab or by opening its grip there. Nothing selects a slot or calls the game's holster, so the reload button's hold, which the game decides in the animation graph and which drops a heavy weapon, is never used for it.

`holsters.stick_click_cycles` (off) makes the off-hand stick click cycle to the next weapon, as a fallback for a player who cannot reach the sockets. It ships off because the click is half of the debug panel's controller chord, so cycling on it switched weapons whenever the panel was opened; the pause menu's `STICK CLICK CYCLES WEAPONS` turns it on. While a gun is in the off-hand the click retracts the tethers instead and does not cycle, so the two never share a press. Nothing else is bound to the off-hand stick click on foot.

The foregrip (`foregrip.enabled`). A two-handed weapon is a weapon in the two-handed, special, or heavy slot that is wielded rather than mounted, or armed in a hand beside the wielded weapon. It rides its holding hand and aims by that hand alone. The foregrip acts on the long gun held in one hand while the other hand is free, whichever of the two the game wields ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)); a brace takes the other hand, so there is one at most. While one is held, the off-hand has a grab volume of `FOREGRIP.radius_m` around the weapon's own grip point for the off-hand ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition) for that hand's [`WeaponGripHand`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponGripHand)), or, for a weapon without one, around a point `FOREGRIP.fallback_distance_m` along the barrel from the holding wrist. The weapon's geometry in the holding wrist's frame (the foregrip point, and the barrel's direction along the gun's sight line) is measured on the input tick from the gun's last placement relative to the wrist target it was placed from. It is measured only when that target was reached at full weight, and it is frozen for the length of a hold.

While the off-hand holds the foregrip, the holding hand's poses turn about its grip so that the foregrip lies on the line from the holding hand to the off-hand, and the off-hand's poses move so that its grip lands on the foregrip, keeping the controller's rotation. The turn is the shortest rotation that carries the direction from the holding grip to the foregrip onto that line, so a foregrip below or beside the barrel leaves the barrel where the weapon's geometry puts it rather than on the line itself, and the roll stays the holding hand's. The turn stops at `FOREGRIP.max_turn_deg` from where the holding hand points the weapon; past it the off-hand falls away from the foregrip and the hold lets go at the release distance, so an off-hand that crosses behind the holding hand cannot spin the weapon round toward the player. With the hands closer than `FOREGRIP.min_span_m`, the line between them is too short to aim along and the hands stay as they are, and between that and twice it the pose fades out smoothly rather than snapping back. The pose is applied to the cockpit-frame hands that every hand subsystem starts from, so the IK targets, the late update, the gun's sight line, and the overlay all see the same two-handed pose. The engine's own off-hand grip IK stays suppressed (see "Hands"): the mod places the off-hand on the `MAIN` pass with the rest of the arm, so the late update corrects it like any other controller-driven hand. Engaging and releasing blend over `FOREGRIP.blend_s`. The foregrip is held as a real one is (`FOREGRIP.hold`), while the off-hand's grip is held, and the hold ends when the grip opens. A braced foregrip is offered to its hand as held, so the hand keeps it over anything as near. The hold also ends when the off-hand moves further than `FOREGRIP.release_distance_m` from the foregrip (measured with the holding hand in its full two-handed pose, so what counts is what the turn and a slide along the gun cannot take up), when a physical gun's brace breaks because the tracked off-hand has left the gun body ([physical-guns.md, "The seat and the joints"](physical-guns.md#the-seat-and-the-joints)), when the weapon leaves the hands or another weapon is drawn, and when a hand loses tracking, the controllers go inert, or the foregrip is switched off. The recoil spring is stiffer under the grip (see "Recoil, haptics, and the grapple hand").

The action-runs tier. The hand tiers classify a reload, a weapon draw, or a holster ([`StateBitFlag::SB_IS_RELOADING`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_RELOADING), [`SB_IS_DRAWING_WEAPON`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_DRAWING_WEAPON), and [`SB_IS_HOLSTERING_WEAPON`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/state::StateBitFlag##variant-SB_IS_HOLSTERING_WEAPON)) as the weapon-action state, which the plan's table assigns the action-runs tier. The grapple states are classified before it. In the action-runs tier the game plays the action (the reload's timer and the weapon model's own magazine and bolt animation, the draw, or the holster) while the hand stays on its controller at full weight. The fire inputs are dropped from the tables' output while the state holds and the effective tier of the hand holding the weapon (or, during a draw, the hand the pouches are laid out around) is action-runs, so a trigger pull does not fire during the action.

Fire with empty hands. The game draws the selected weapon when the fire input arrives with nothing wielded or during a holster, and when the precision-aim input arrives with nothing wielded ([`Inventory::UpdateWeaponHandling`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-UpdateWeaponHandling)). Under the controller scheme a weapon comes out of a holster, the weapon wheel, or a slot select instead: for the length of that call the local player's fire and precision-aim entries of [`Character::m_WeaponInputs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WeaponInputs) read zero whenever they would draw (nothing wielded by [`Character::GetWieldingWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-GetWieldingWeapon), or a holster playing for the fire input), and they are put back afterwards for everything that reads them later in the tick. The hold-back lives in the weapon handling's existing detour (`controllers::aim_state`), with the logic in `controllers::weapons::unarmed_fire`.

The Weapons > Hold, transfer, and grab regions live status opens with its status rows ([physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md#settings-and-readout)), then shows the wielded weapon, its hands, and whether it is two-handed; each hand's volume and tier; the foregrip's measured geometry and whether it is the weapon's own grip or the fallback; the hold and its weight, the turn the last two-handed pose wanted and whether the limit held it, and the grabs and releases; the draws the empty-hands fire input was kept from; a watch on each held gun's rotation relative to its wrist target from one tick to the next (the `FLIP WATCH` row counts and the log records a change of more than a right angle as the gun turning over in the hand; a diagnostic for a gun seen inverted: a flip there is the gun's attachment changing, while a flip in the headset without one is the hand's pose); whether fire is held back; and the recent grabs and what each did.

## Recoil, haptics, and the grapple hand

These are the second half of milestone 3, and have not been run in the headset.

Recoil (`controllers.weapons.recoil`). A detour on [`WeaponBase::Fire_ProjectileBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-Fire_ProjectileBase) reads the change in the weapon's per-tick recoil ([`m_RecoilPitch`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_RecoilPitch) and [`m_RecoilYaw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_RecoilYaw)) across each shot of the local player's gun, and kicks a per-hand damped spring by it: about the controller's grip, a pitch of the barrel (the wrist frame's fingers axis, along which the hand holds the gun) about the axis across it, a yaw about the axis up the handle square to the barrel, and a shift back along the barrel. The spring's offset is applied to the active hands after the foregrip pose, so the IK, the late update, the sight line, and the overlay all carry it. Under the foregrip the kick is smaller, the spring stiffer, and the off-hand moves with the gun. Held in one hand, a long gun's kick is multiplied by its class's one-handed kick scale, in whichever hand it is, which gives way to the two-hand scale as the foregrip engages (see the one-handed weight below). While the guns aim by hand, a replacing detour on [`WeaponCameraModifier::ProcessCameraContext`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/camera::weapon_camera_modifier::WeaponCameraModifier##func-ProcessCameraContext) writes the camera transform without turning the boom by the recoil (or running the snap-aim assist), because the boom's turn persists into the gameplay camera's yaw and pitch.

The one-handed weight (`controllers.weapons.heft`, on). Held in one hand, a long gun is awkward, so that the player braces it on the foregrip. The class comes from the weapon's [`m_Class`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Class): pistols, revolvers, and SMGs are sidearms, which carry no one-handed weight by default (the SMGs are told apart for the two-handed weight below); assault rifles, shotguns, and sniper rifles are rifles, which droop and trail visibly; launchers, miniguns, mortars, the mounted-calibre guns, and the special weapons are heavy, which hang well down at arm's length and trail a quick turn far behind. By default, at full reach a rifle sags about 18 degrees and a heavy weapon about 30, and a third of a turn a second leaves a rifle about 14 degrees behind and a heavy weapon about 27; both springs are a little underdamped, so the gun swings past the aim and settles. A braced gun trails the same turn by under half of that. An unset class counts as a rifle in a two-handed slot and a sidearm otherwise. Each class has its own tuning (`HEFT.sidearm`, `HEFT.rifle`, and `HEFT.heavy`, in `controllers::weapons::heft`). The input tick publishes each hand's gun, its hand, and its class, the wielded weapon and a weapon armed beside it alike, and each hand's gun has its own weight, spring, and freeze ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)). The weight is applied to the active hands after the foregrip pose and before the recoil, so the IK, the late update, the sight line, and the overlay all carry it, and a shot goes where the drooping gun points. The holding hand's poses turn about the controller's grip. The barrel is the wrist frame's fingers axis, along which the hand holds the gun, not the grip's -Z, which runs up the handle. The barrel pitches down about the horizontal axis across it by `sag_deg` plus `reach_sag_deg` as the hand moves from `reach_near_m` to `reach_far_m` from the head, scaled by how level the barrel is, so a gun aimed up or down still droops toward the ground; it sways by `sway_deg` on two incommensurate waves at `sway_hz` (half of it close in, all of it at full reach), in pitch about that horizontal axis and in yaw about the vertical, so the sway never rolls the gun; and the result is followed on a damped rotational spring (`lag_stiffness`, `lag_damping_ratio`), so the gun trails a fast turn of the wrist and settles after it. The whole turn is limited to `max_angle_deg`. A one-handed shot's kick is multiplied by `kick_scale`, and a detour on [`WeaponBase::GetCurrentScatterValue`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetCurrentScatterValue) multiplies the scatter the game gives the local player's shot by `spread_scale`. The scatter starts from the weapon's accumulated scatter, so the first shot of a burst from a settled weapon may have little to widen. The foregrip's weight scales all of it down, to nothing once the grip has engaged. The code is `controllers::weapons::heft`, and the Weapons > Support, weight, and recoil live status shows, for each hand, its gun's class, the unbraced share, the reach, the sag, the sway, the spring's lag, the whole turn, and the count of widened shots. The volumes on a held gun (the foregrip, the well, and the action's handle) are placed on the gun as it is shown, in its two-handed pose and with its weight (not its recoil), so they are where the player sees them. While a round for the reload is in hand, the gun in the other hand freezes, and while the off-hand is in the foregrip's volume before it grabs, the gun it closes on freezes: the gun keeps the turn it had on the hand, without lagging or swaying, so the well and the foregrip move rigidly with the hand, and the spring follows on from there once the freeze ends.

The two-handed weight (`controllers.weapons.heft.braced`, on). Braced on the foregrip, a long gun keeps a weight of its own, so that it handles as a heavy object held in two hands rather than a pointer: it follows where the hands turn it on a critically damped rotational spring (`stiffness` per class, the damping ratio fixed at one), so a quick swing leaves it a little behind and it settles on the aim without overshooting, and its barrel sags a little (`sag_deg` per class). The classes are `smg` (the lightest), `rifle`, and `heavy` (the launchers and the other heavy weapons, the heaviest); pistols and revolvers carry none in either hold. A swing at a steady speed trails by about twice the speed over the square root of the stiffness: at a sixth of a turn a second, by under two degrees for an SMG, about three for a rifle, and about five for a heavy weapon by default. `HEFT.braced.strength` (1) scales it all, the sag by the strength and the lag time by the strength (the stiffness by its inverse square), and zero carries no weight. The whole turn is limited to `max_angle_deg` (8 degrees). While the foregrip is held, the off-hand is turned with the gun about the holding hand's grip, so the gun drives both hands and they stay on it; the drawn off-hand strays from its controller by at most the capped angle across the span between the hands. As the grip engages, the one-handed and the two-handed weights blend by the grip's weight: the sags and the limits linearly, and the springs by their natural frequencies, a rigid side counting as a very stiff spring. The kick and the spread keep their one-handed scaling. The one-handed weight's switch (`controllers.weapons.heft.enabled`) and the two-handed weight's switch (`heft.braced.enabled`) are in the debug UI's Weapons > Support, weight, and recoil.

Haptics (`controllers.haptics`, [haptics.md](haptics.md)). Two detours cover every source of the game's rumble, one-off and continuous alike, so a vehicle's engine and road rumble reaches the controllers as well as weapon fire, damage, impacts, and the rest. A pre-call detour on [`RumbleManager::SpawnRumble`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/rumble_manager::RumbleManager##func-SpawnRumble), which every one-off rumble goes through, picks out the weapon fire rumble inside a shot and plays it on the hand whose gun fired it (`Event::Shot`), raised by the shot's kick, and always lets the game's own call through so the rumble it starts still feeds the game's per-frame sum. A second pre-call detour on [`Gamepad::SetMotorValue`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::gamepad::Gamepad##func-SetMotorValue) sees that sum once a frame: the manager's left and right motor strength, already decayed and distance-attenuated, covering every active rumble at once, including a vehicle's own continuous engine and road rumble, which never calls `SpawnRumble` at all ([`RumbleManager::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/rumble_manager::RumbleManager##func-Update)). It forwards the low (left) motor to the left hand and the high (right) motor, which also carries the trigger motors, to the right hand as a sustained level (`Sustain::GameRumble`) refreshed every frame, which the mixer holds off a hand while one of the mod's one-off cues plays there (the shot among them, so the two paths do not double up on the firing hand), and, unless `gamepad_rumble` says otherwise, replaces the value it passes through with zero so the physical gamepad stays quiet while the controllers take over. The grapple hand plays its own cues when a wire appears (the hook leaves the gauntlet), when the active wire attaches, and on a yank. A hand that enters a socket or a pouch, or moves from one into another, gets a tick (`Event::VolumeEnter`), and a draw, a dock (by a grab or by opening the grip over the socket), and a foregrip grab or release (whatever ended the hold) a click (`Event::VolumeGrab`), on the hand that did it; the pouches play their own outcomes.

Each hand's gun. A gun in each hand fires from its own trigger and aims along its own sight line ([physical-weapons.md, "Firing"](physical-weapons.md#firing)). While the off-hand holds a gun, in a mode whose triggers fire the hand weapons (on foot, swimming, falling, under the parachute, and in a passenger seat), its trigger, where it would retract the tethers, fires that gun, and the off-hand's stick click retracts instead; in the wingsuit and the driver's seat neither trigger fires a hand weapon. Two pistols are a pistol in each hand, each its own weapon ([physical-weapons.md, "Separate pistols"](physical-weapons.md#separate-pistols)). The game's pair, kept whole while the pistols are not made separate, is one weapon with a gun in each hand: the game updates one gun per tick, the one at [`m_NextWeaponToFire`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_NextWeaponToFire), and points that index at the other gun after every shot, so the game's own fire input, which either hand's trigger presses, fires both pistols in turn ([aim-pipeline.md, "Which gun fires"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#which-gun-fires)). Recoil and the fire rumble follow the gun that fired, read from that gun's attach bone at the per-shot seam.

The anchored tier (`controllers.hands.anchor`). In the grapple reel and hang states the grapple hand keeps its controller position and turns so that its grip's pointing axis runs to the active wire's attach point ([`GrapplingHook::GetActiveWireAttachmentGraphicsWorldPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHook##func-GetActiveWireAttachmentGraphicsWorldPosition), read at the input tick). The mounted guns anchor the hands too (see "Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight"). A grapple hand that holds anything stays free instead of taking the line, so that what it holds stays where the controller puts it: a gun, wielded or armed in that hand, a prop, a grenade, or a charge, as the loadout table has it ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The model"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-model)). A free grapple hand neither turns to the line nor swings the hang. While hanging, a post-call detour on [`NStateTask_GrapplingHangTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::NStateTask_GrapplingHangTask##func-Update) takes the grapple hand's horizontal displacement from where it was when the hang began (relative to the head) and applies an impulse against it at the hips to the hang proxy's body, scaled from the game's own disabled continuous swing ([`GrapplingHangTask_SmallImpulseSwingMagnitude`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::grappling_hook::GrapplingHangTask_SmallImpulseSwingMagnitude)). The hand's motion pumps the swing on top, capped together with the hold ([physical-grapple.md](physical-grapple.md#the-hang-swing)).

The yank (`controllers.bindings.yank`). At the input tick, the grapple hand's distance from a body point below the head is tracked over a short window; a fast enough drop of at least the length fixed in `GRAPPLE_YANK`, while a grapple wire is live and the line is not already carrying the character (the reel-in, the wind-up to a hang, or the hang, where the grapple hand's pull swings the body), presses the grapple for one tick, which the game reads as the tap that zips. The pull also drives the reel's speed and the tether retract, and a yank during the reel boosts it ([physical-grapple.md](physical-grapple.md)).

## Vehicles, mounted guns, and flight

This is milestone 4, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/`, and the settings are under `controllers.vehicles`.

Driver weapons (`driver_weapons_by_hand`, on). The player's post-camera update hands the driven vehicle the gaze point through [`Vehicle::SetAimTargetPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-SetAimTargetPosition), and every built-in weapon aims from the stored point. With the option on, a pre-call detour substitutes the dominant hand's aim point from the same tick's aim update, for the vehicle whose local-player seat is the driver's. Turrets are left to the game: a turret turns toward its weapon's aim point at its own limited rate ([`TurretWeaponComponent::UpdateTurretAiming`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::TurretWeaponComponent##func-UpdateTurretAiming)), so a hand-aimed turret trails the hand as it trails the gaze. The motorcycle's and jetski's personal weapon is a wielded weapon on the hand's attach bone, so the per-hand gun aim already aims it by the dominant hand. Out of the driver's seat the dominant trigger fires the weapon in the hands or on the mount, a gun in a hand also fires from that hand's upper face button, and in a passenger seat the seated body turns toward the gun hand's aim rather than the gaze ([vehicle-weapons.md](vehicle-weapons.md)).

Mounted guns and gunner seats (`mounted.aim_by_hands`, on, which also anchors the hands on the handles). A mounted gun is the heavy slot's weapon in its mounted state. The hand tiers classify the mounted-gun state before the vehicle states, because a gunner seat is a vehicle attach state outside the riding set and would read as an entry, and the anchored tier is built for it. On the input tick the gun's origin, its barrel, and the handles the weapon's grip table places for the mounted state ([`WeaponBase::GetGripPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-GetGripPosition)) are read. When the hands take the gun, a reference is taken: the hands' midpoint, the lever from the handles to the gun's origin in the body frame, and the turn that makes the lever lie along the barrel. The barrel then points from the hands' midpoint through the pivot, so pushing the hands left swings the muzzle right. The resulting point is handed to the gun through the per-weapon seam and becomes the character-level aim point, which the game draws the gun toward. The rendered hands sit on the handles with the controllers' rotation. Each hand takes its handle with the grip and only the holding hands aim, and each shot kicks the handles and pulses the hands on them ([vehicle-weapons.md, "Mounted guns"](vehicle-weapons.md#mounted-guns)).

Physical controls (each on by default). The driver's seat offers a wheel (car and boat), handlebars (motorcycle, and the ATV, jetski, and duck, told apart by their steering fork), a cyclic and a collective (helicopter), or a flight stick and a throttle (plane), each behind its own toggle under `physical`. The wheel and the handlebars are placed where the vehicle's own are, read from its parts each tick, with the fixed specs in `PhysicalControlsTuning` as the fallback, and the vehicle's rendered wheel turns with the steering ([steering-wheel.md](steering-wheel.md)); they are centred on the vehicle and never mirrored. The sticks and levers are placed in the cockpit frame relative to the seated character's eyes, which the head pose pins to the character's animated head as it rides the seat, with their sides mirrored for a left-handed layout. A control is a grip target held only while the grip is: a grip press near it takes it, and only releasing the grip lets go, however far the holding hand drifts from the control: it keeps working the control from its position and rotation. Each take and let-go logs `vehicle control: took the control` or `vehicle control: let go of the control`, with the hand, the control, and the reason (`grip released`, `taken by the other hand`, or `gone`), at info level. A hand that passes through a control, or rests on it with the grip open, takes nothing, and a grip that holds a control does not fire the vehicle's weapons. With both hands on controls (the wheel, or the cyclic and the collective) no grip is left to fire them; moving vehicle fire to another input while a control is held is not built. While held, the wheel turns by the hands' twist about its axis (or, as an option, their travel around its hub), accumulated so that it turns past half a turn and carries on across hand-overs, and steers by its angle over its lock ([steering-wheel.md, "Turning the wheel"](steering-wheel.md#turning-the-wheel)), the handlebars steer by their turn and lean by their roll, a stick's top follows the hand across the horizontal plane and springs back to centre when let go (the flight stick's twist works the rudder), and a lever follows the hand along its axis with a detent around neutral (the throttle stays where it is let go, and has an afterburner gate at the top of its travel); the sticks and levers give haptic stops and detents, and the horn and the nitrous are worked by hand too, by the wheel's hub, a button beside the wheel, and the twist grips ([cockpit-controls.md](cockpit-controls.md)). Each held control replaces the tables' analog writes for the actions it drives, and the stick keeps every other axis. The holding hand is drawn on the control, recomputed from the latest controller pose at every rate. A green marker on each control's grab point near each hand marks where the hand would take it ([grip.md](grip.md#markers)).

Wingsuit gestures (`wingsuit_gestures.enabled`, on). The recognizer is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/wingsuit_gestures/`, and the input integration is in `bindings/flight.rs`. While falling, a stable flight pose held for `flight_dwell_s` deploys the suit: a fall's start is a fresh start, so arms spread before the step off or under a cut canopy count, and only a fall out of the suit needs a bilateral lowered or withdrawn pose first. The recognizer watches from the moment Rico leaves the ground on foot (the view anchor's `airborne`, from the jump's grace on), so the deploy goes in during the game's jump, which takes it once Rico is descending and high enough; a refused request is asked again after `DEPLOY_RETRY_S` while the pose is held ([wingsuit-gestures.md](wingsuit-gestures.md)). In the suit, a stable flight pose (both arms spread past `flight_lateral_span`, within `flight_angle_deg` of level, and both palms within `flight_roll_deg` of flat) held for `flight_dwell_s` arms closing and braking. The suit's entry is the fresh start: a player who opened the suit with the button and already has their arms spread arms it without first drawing them in, and one who entered with the arms drawn in does not close it before spreading them. Deliberately lowering or withdrawing both arms closes the suit after a dwell. One lowered arm, crossed arms, and a bank do not close it. Wide arms with both hands turned to face the wind hold the air brake. A hand faces the wind when its wrist is rolled about the fingers from palm-flat toward palm-forward by `brake_roll_deg` to `brake_roll_max_deg` (60° to 150°), the roll the arm steering's wrist tilt reads, so the brake reads the same with the arms raised or swept and with one wrist bent back further than the other. A palm turned on to face up does not brake, which keeps the band clear of the roll's wrap at palm-up. An armed brake entry, hold, and wrist-release interval suppress only arm-derived pitch, until both palms are back within `pitch_release_roll_deg` of flat; bilateral bank and stick steering remain available. The arms are measured against the way the body faces, the horizontal normal to the line between the wrists, rather than the head's heading, since a flier looks around, down, and along the turn while the arms stay spread. The head's heading stands in while the wrists are within 0.15 m of each other or their normal is more than 75° from the head's heading (`MIN_ARMS_SEPARATION_M` and `MAX_ARMS_FROM_HEAD_DEG` in `wingsuit_gestures`). Each change of the recognizer's phase (unavailable, with the gate that blocks it; waiting for a neutral; waiting for a flight pose; armed; braking) and each deploy or close is logged at info level as `wingsuit gestures: ...` with the arm measures it saw, while the player is off the ground on foot, falling, or in the suit, and once when they leave all three.

The fixed flight buttons are physical left trigger for the machine gun, physical right trigger for rockets, physical right B for boost, and physical right A for the contextual canopy action. The held air brake gesture is also the landing: the game closes the suit into a slide onto the feet once a braking suit is slow and low enough ([wingsuit-gestures.md, "Landing"](wingsuit-gestures.md#landing)). Rockets have no default grip binding, boost has no default stick-click binding, and B does not close the suit. A handheld gun reserves only its own physical trigger, so the other trigger can still fire its suit weapon. The left-stick-click and right-B debug chord retains priority over boost and consumes its buttons until neutral. A canopy request cancels a pending suit gesture both before recognition and after the weapon and hand-interaction actions run.

What a hand holds does not suspend the gestures: a gun, a grenade, a charge, or a prop rides along in the hand while the arm pose drives the suit, and a held gun still fires on its own trigger. Gestures suspend while a grip works something other than a held item (a grab that tick, a support hold on the other hand's gun, a hold on the world or a vehicle), either hand squeezes its grip within reach of a socket or pouch on the body (a draw or a holster starting; a hand resting there with its grip open claims nothing, since the gestures' lowered arms put the hands at the hip sockets), a mechanical interaction is active, a reload holds fire, the inventory is stashed, or a grapple is held, attached, or traversing. Blocked weapon actions do not suspend them: the game blocks them around a draw or a holster and through some of a fall's start, and the grip and slot claims cover the draw and the holster. Menus, recovery masks, stale or missing tracking, and action-set waits also reset them. Resuming requires a new flight pose; a fall keeps its start through a suspension, so it needs no new neutral. Geometry uses one live packet containing the current located head and raw controller grips from the same baseline, predicted display time, and controller publication, with one snapshot of body dimensions and the grip-to-wrist calibration. The packet is captured before replay replaces the head. Recenter, follow-bank changes, runtime reset, replay start or end, and source-clock changes invalidate its epoch; replay does not supply live gesture geometry. The thresholds and dwell intervals are under `controllers.vehicles.wingsuit_gestures`. In a seated session on Index controllers, the earlier measures never armed or braked across three flights: the player entered the suit with the arms already spread, so the neutral the suit then required never came; the head turned up to 65° from the arms, which narrowed their measured spread; and the palm normal's alignment with forward missed a right hand turned to the wind with its wrist bent back. The present measures are checked against poses sampled from that session (`wingsuit_gestures/tests/seated.rs`) and have not been flown since.

Wingsuit arm steering (`arm_steering.wingsuit`, on). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/arm_steering.rs`. With the hands further apart than half the arm span (`arm_steering::MIN_SPREAD`), the difference in their heights banks, past `deadzone_m` and fully at `full_bank_m`, and `arm_steering.pitch_by` chooses what pitches. Everything is measured in the cockpit frame, whose vertical is the player's, with forward the horizontal normal to the line from the left hand to the right, so a player who turned since the last recenter still pitches by their own forward and back. The shoulders are placed below the tracked head by the body dimensions' eye-to-shoulder drop, which is the same seated and standing, and a little behind it.

- `WristTilt` (the default): both wrists' roll about the fingers' axis, from the pose with the back of the hand as near straight up as that axis allows. Tilting the leading edges (the thumb side of a palm held flat) down dives and up climbs, like a wing's angle of attack, past 5° and fully at 30° (`TILT_DEADZONE_DEG` and `FULL_TILT_DEG` in `arm_steering`), measured from `tilt_neutral_deg` (0°, the palms flat). Because the roll is taken about the fingers, raising, lowering, or sweeping an arm turns the reference with it and reads nothing, so the pitch is independent of the bank. The headset test of the height pitch found it laggy against the stick: moving both arms up or down takes longer than turning the wrists, and it moves the hands' height, which the bank also reads.
- `Height`: both hands above or below the shoulders, lowered diving, fully at `FULL_PITCH_M`.
- `Sweep`: the hands' midpoint ahead of or behind the shoulders' midpoint, swept back diving, fully at `FULL_SWEEP_M`.

The arms steer whatever the hands hold: a gun or a prop rides along in the hand, and the arm is still a wing ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)). A hand holding something takes no parachute handle. The bank stays the height difference in every mode. Each wrist's tilt is the same gesture for both wings, so a bank read from a difference in tilt would turn the wingsuit whenever one wrist rolls more than the other while the player means only to pitch. `invert_pitch` reverses the pitch. Once steering, the arms keep steering until they come in past a share of the arm span narrower by a tenth, so a wing held near the threshold does not flicker. While the grapple's button is held, the arms keep the stick vector they had when it was pressed (`arm_steering::hold_while_aiming`), and steer again once it is up: the grapple aims along the off-hand, and pointing that hand at a target narrows the spread or lowers it against the other hand, which would stop the steering or bank the suit mid-aim. Each hold and release is logged at info (`wingsuit arm steering: the grapple is aimed`), and the readout's `held_for_grapple` says which. Nothing is filtered: each input tick's pose writes that tick's stick. The result is added to the move stick and written to the move half-axes the wingsuit task reads. With the overlay on, the overlay rings each hand in the wingsuit, faint while the arms are in and lit with a line between the hands while they steer, so that spreading the arms shows when they take over. The debug UI's Air feature (under Movement/vehicles) has the deadzone and the full bank, and its live status reads out the spread against the share needed and whether the arms steer, the bank and the pitch input with the tilt, height, and sweep together, the arms' stick vector, the player's stick, and the sum written. The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has the arm steering's switch, the pitch measure, and the inversion. The wingsuit's rendered arms stay yielded to the animation.

Parachute handles (`parachute_handles.enabled`, on). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/parachute.rs`. The hands are not on the handles by default. One handle hangs a little above, ahead of, and outside each shoulder, where a hand raised to a canopy's toggle comes to rest standing or seated: a free handle's volume is centred where the game's rig rests that handle's prop (`vehicles::parachute_rig`), with a radius of 0.20 m (`RADIUS_M` in `controllers::vehicles::parachute`), and only that side's hand takes it, so a hand reaching across the body takes nothing from the other side's handle; while the rest is not observed, the free handle is not offered. The handles sit below eye level because a handle above it is out of sight, and a player does not find it. A grip press inside the volume takes the handle, held only while the grip is: only releasing the grip lets go, however far the hand goes from the handle, so a natural pull, which runs down and back toward the body, keeps it. Each take and let-go is logged at info level (`parachute: took a handle`, and `parachute: let go of a handle` with the reason: `grip released` or `gone`). A held handle is offered to its hand as held, so the hand keeps it over a shoulder socket as near. Each handle has a reach margin of 0.43 m (`REACH_M`) around it, a fallback target, so a press that misses a free handle by a little still takes it when nothing else is in reach ([grip.md](grip.md#resolving-a-hand)). Both are sized from the player's misses, about three times the volume of the first handles, and shrink only to keep the two handles' volumes from overlapping: with both rests known, each radius is at most half the spacing between them, and never less than 0.10 m (`HandleRadii`, `MIN_RADIUS_M`). Within a handle's volume the nearer target takes the press, as everywhere ([grip.md](grip.md#resolving-a-hand)), so a press right at a shoulder socket still draws the long gun there. Each handle is marked while the handles act, as every target is ([grip.md](grip.md#markers)): a faint dot out of reach, ringed in reach, and solid while held, drawn where the hand holds it, anchored to the head at render time. A held handle's pull is how far its hand has come down from where it took the handle, past `DEADZONE_M`, reaching a full pull at `full_pull_m`; raising it is slack. The right pull less the left banks toward the side pulled further, and the share both are pulled past `brake_threshold` (of a full pull) holds the stick back, which the parachute reads as its slow-down ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The stick's vertical axis"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-sticks-vertical-axis)). A handle let go or never taken is neutral. The handles' vector is added to the move stick and written to the same move half-axes the stick writes, so the game's own parachute physics flies the canopy, and the stick still steers and dives alongside; while neither handle is held, the tables' stick writes stand alone. The hands do not dive: a parachute's toggles only slow it, so diving stays on the stick. The holding hand is drawn on its handle, straight below where it took it and no further down than a full pull. The parachute's own handle props ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The handle props"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-handle-props)) are drawn in Rico's hands by the game wherever the hands are, so a hand that has not taken its handle would be drawn gripping the game's while the mod's mark reads it free. A post-call detour on the rig's sync (`vehicles::parachute_rig`) puts each handle a hand does not hold back on its cord, as the game does for a hand it does not place, and leaves a held handle in the hand that holds it; while the handles do not act, the rig is the game's. The debug UI's vehicle readout counts the handles put back. The pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has the switch; the full pull and the brake threshold are in the debug UI's Air feature, under Movement/vehicles. The controls summary lists the handles on the grips under the parachute, with a mod string, since the game has none. The wingsuit and the parachute both have the head look-steer removed, so the head steers neither.

Ripcord (`ripcord.enabled`, on). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/vehicles/ripcord.rs`, with the input tick's half in `ripcord_tick.rs`. While falling or in the wingsuit, a ripcord at the back of the hip on the dominant side opens the parachute. It sits on the player's tracked body, not Rico's: in a fall and the suit Rico flies prone under the view, so his drawn hip is nowhere the hand can reach. The hip joint is estimated below the shoulder joints by the body dimensions (the shoulder height less a generic share of the standing height, `HIP_HEIGHT_FRACTION`), which holds seated as well as standing, and the ripcord's volume is centred 0.06 m out, 0.06 m up, and 0.12 m back from it along the torso's heading (`FROM_HIP_M`, mirrored for a left-handed player), behind the holster, with a radius of 0.12 m (`RADIUS_M`) and a reach margin of 0.22 m (`REACH_M`) that takes a press nothing else claims, as the parachute handles' does. Only the dominant hand takes it. It is taken like a handle, with a grip press inside its volume, and held only while the grip is. A hold that carries the hand `pull_m` (0.15 m) from where it took the ripcord, in any direction, pulls it once: the input tick clicks the canopy action (`OPEN_PARACHUTE`), the one the canopy's button drives, and pulses the hand. The click goes in before the wingsuit gestures commit, so it suppresses a suit request on the same tick. A hold pulls once; letting go and taking it again pulls again. The game decides whether the canopy opens from the state Rico is in. The ripcord is marked as a handle is, always faintly and ringed in reach. Each take, pull, and let-go is logged at info level (`ripcord: took it`, `ripcord: pulled it, opening the parachute`, and `ripcord: let go of it` with the reason). The switch and the pull distance are in the debug UI's Air feature, under Movement/vehicles, which also reads out who holds it. The controls summary lists it on the dominant grip while falling and in the suit, with a mod string.

The wingsuit's head look-steer. The air movement core adds a camera look-steer computed by its own side-effect-free function, [`NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/input::parachute##func-NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering), for the local player. A post-call detour zeroes its output while the mode detector reads the wingsuit or the parachute, under the controller scheme, for a head-decoupled local player. The core also serves the falls, which the gate leaves alone, and the wingsuit task's own update is never hooked.

The wingsuit's stall flip. Slowing with the nose raised makes the wingsuit's blend state play a stall clip, and a full loop that recovers from it, over a physics body that never loops ([wingsuit-stall.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-stall.md)), so the body turns a circle the player never gestured while the arms they fly with stay put. While the head pose comes from the headset (the debug UI's Air feature shows whether the patch is in), the blend state's one write of its stalling flag ([`WingsuitBlendState_StallFlagSet`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::WingsuitBlendState_StallFlagSet)) is patched so it never stalls (`hooks::input::wingsuit`): neither clip plays, the air brake and the wingsuit's close act stay available, and a player who pitches up loses lift and drops. The patch is a byte of the game's code, applied and reverted through the hooks' patcher as the head pose's source changes, so it covers every character in a wingsuit, of which only the player flies one. Whether the slowdown before a stall still feels wrong without the loop is for the headset to tell.

The wingsuit's barrel roll. The wingsuit's evade is a barrel roll whose clip rolls the body a full turn and carries it sideways ([wingsuit-evade.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-evade.md)), a roll the player's body never makes. The air physics queues its act straight on the animated model, past the `Character::QueueAct` filters, so while the head pose comes from the headset (the debug UI's Air feature counts the rolls dropped), the pre-call on [`AnimatedModel::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-QueueAct) drops [`ACT_BOOST_WINGSUIT_BARREL_ROLL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable) for the local player's model (`hooks::input::wingsuit`), matched by the address the player's pose-finalization pass publishes, as the hit reactions are ([procedural-body.md](../body/procedural-body.md#the-hand-over-table)). The roll's directions are segments of its one clip, so every evade goes. Each drop is logged at info level (`wingsuit: dropped the barrel roll`). Whether the evade's charge is still spent without the roll is for the headset to tell.

## Grenades, aim assist, and reloads

This is most of milestone 5, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/` (`grenade/`), `payload/src/controllers/aim/` (`assist.rs`, `targets.rs`), and `payload/src/controllers/motion.rs`. The engine side is in [aim-pipeline.md, "Grenades"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#grenades) and ["Aim assist"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#aim-assist-updatealltargets). The scopes are the next section, and the sight lines are under "Aiming"; the hand-tier promotions are not built.

The hands' recent motion is kept for the throws. Every published controller frame adds each tracked grip's pose and velocities to a half-second history; an untracked frame adds nothing, so an inferred pose never feeds a throw. A velocity is the runtime's own where it reports one, and a difference of positions otherwise.

The grenade throw (`weapons.grenade_throw`, on; the tuning is `GRENADE` in `controllers::weapons::grenade`). The grenade pouch on the chest (see "Sockets, pouches, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier") exists only while the throw is on. It draws from the game's own grenade stock, is offered and drawn on the chest only while the stock has a grenade (or the game's unlimited ammunition applies), and takes back a grenade pressed onto it with its pin in; while the throw is on, the game's own grenade pickups credit nothing ([grenades.md, "Stock"](grenades.md#stock)). Either hand may grab it with no gun drawn; while one hand holds a gun only the other may, so a grenade is never primed in the gun's hand, and while both hands hold pistols it is not placed. A grip press there asks the game's grenade weapon to prime a grenade in whichever hand grabbed it through its own prime request ([`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::m_PrimeGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##field-m_PrimeGrenade)), which the throw animation normally raises. The request is set before the weapon's update and refused when the game's grenade permission ([`Inventory::m_DoesAnimationAllowGrenades`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##field-m_DoesAnimationAllowGrenades)) is off, no grenades are left, or a throw of the game's own is under way; a refusal buzzes the hand that grabbed (`Event::PouchRefused`) and is logged at info level with its reason. The grenade comes into the hand with its pin in and its fuse still; a trigger press on that hand pulls the pin, and from then a grenade held too long explodes in the hand ([grenades.md, "The pin"](grenades.md#the-pin); [physical-weapons.md, "Grenades as objects"](physical-weapons.md#grenades-as-objects) also covers catching a live grenade). While it is held, it sits upright in the hand as a hand holds a grenade (`GRENADE.hold`; [grenades.md, "In the hand"](grenades.md#in-the-hand)), and the fuse warns in its last `GRENADE.warning_s` seconds: a ring at the hand turns from green to red, and the hand pulses at a rate that rises toward the end.

Opening the fingers throws, not the grip's press ending: a throwing hand relaxes its squeeze well before the fingers leave the handle, and on a force-sensing grip the press ends before the throw's peak, which would drop the grenade at the feet. The fingers are read through the grip's value (`payload/src/controllers/weapons/opening.rs`): on the Index controller the capacitive sense of the fingers round the handle, on a Touch controller the grip's pull. A hand counts as closed once the value passes 0.6 or the grip is pressed, and opens when it falls under 0.35; a controller whose value is not read opens with its grip's press. The release is logged at info level (`grenade: the fingers opened, and the grenade leaves the hand`, with `speed_mps`). The release's motion, linear and angular, is the one a gun's let-go takes, from the same estimate and under the same cap ([grenades.md, "The throw's motion"](grenades.md#the-throws-motion)), and how the grenade leaves the hand is in [grenades.md, "The throw's motion"](grenades.md#the-throws-motion). While a grenade or a charge is in a hand, the fire input is held back unless the other hand holds a gun: with nothing wielded, the fire input draws a gun into the hand that holds the explosive. When the throw stops acting (its toggle, a mode without free hands, the gamepad scheme), a held grenade goes back to the inventory ([`ThrownGrenadeWeapon::CancelPrimedGrenade`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::ThrownGrenadeWeapon##func-CancelPrimedGrenade)). No button throws a grenade: the dominant stick click, where the gamepad RB's grenade throw would sit, carries only the detonation (see below). While a throw is under way, any `THROW_GRENADE` is still held back so that a wind-up of the game's own cannot take the grenade, and the dominant hand's aim point is still written into the aim control's grenade slot after the aim update, for the game's own throw.

The C4 throw (`weapons.c4_throw`, on; the tuning is `C4` in `controllers::weapons::c4`). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/c4/` (`mod.rs` and `stage.rs`), and the engine side is in [planted-explosives.md](../../engine/gameplay/planted-explosives.md). The C4 pouch sits on the front of the chest on the side the grenade pouch leaves (`POUCHES.c4`; see "Sockets, pouches, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier"), exists only while the throw is on, and is taken from as the grenade pouch is: by either hand with no gun drawn, by the free hand while the other holds a gun, and not at all while both hold pistols. A grip press there primes a charge in whichever hand grabbed it, with a click (`Event::C4Prime`). The game's own planting goes through its planting mode: the explosives slot is selected, a ghost is aimed with the camera, and the charge is placed on the aimed surface by an animation. The mod bypasses all of it and never touches the selected slot. The controller layout leaves the planting mode's plant input unbound, and the trigger in it would draw a pistol, so a charge chosen from the weapon wheel comes into the hand instead: when the planting mode starts ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::m_IsInPlantingMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##field-m_IsInPlantingMode)) with a hand free (the dominant one first), the mod leaves it with the game's own holster ([`Character::HolsterWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HolsterWeapon)) and, once it has ended, primes a charge in that hand as the pouch does (`c4: the weapon wheel's explosives leave the planting mode for a charge in the hand`). A planting mode that has not ended within a second, or a hand that took something else meanwhile, gives no charge, and the log says so. From then a charge from the wheel and one from the pouch are the same. Before the C4 weapon's serial update ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::UpdateFromUserPostSim_Serial`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-UpdateFromUserPostSim_Serial)), which the inventory runs every frame whatever is wielded, it calls the weapon's own prime ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::PrimeExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-PrimeExplosive)), which spawns the charge as the planting mode's track message does. Priming moves the weapon to its wielding state, so the state it was in is put back at once. A grab is refused while as many charges are placed as may be ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::CanPlantExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-CanPlantExplosive)), while the game does not allow planting ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsPlantingExplosives`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsPlantingExplosives)), and while the game's own planting holds the weapon; a refused grab buzzes the grabbing hand (`Event::PouchRefused`) and is logged at info level with its reason. Placing a charge consumes nothing, so the cap is the C4 count. A held charge pressed back onto the C4 pouch is taken back without being placed, so it never counts against the cap or the detonator ([grenades.md, "Putting it back"](grenades.md#putting-it-back)). While a charge is held, the weapon's attachment update puts its in-hand model and its transform in the hand instead of at the right-hand bone, flat on the palm with its sticking face out (`C4.hold`; [grenades.md, "In the hand"](grenades.md#in-the-hand)). The other hand takes a held charge from it as it takes a held grenade or a gun, by closing on it ([grip.md](grip.md)): the throw then belongs to the hand that took it, and the loadout table logs the move as a handover. A charge already placed comes back the same way (`controllers::weapons::c4::placed`): each placed charge within a few metres is a grab volume for each free hand (`GrabVolume::PlacedCharge`, radius 0.1 m, also reached from the controller's grip when the drawn hand cannot crouch to it), offered only while no charge is held or asked for, and a grip press there takes the charge nearest the hand. Before the weapon's next serial update primes the hand's charge, the placed one comes off the weapon's live list ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::RemoveFromActiveList`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-RemoveFromActiveList), which drops its HUD hint and light) and is removed ([`Explosive::RemoveSelf`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-RemoveSelf)), in that order ([planted-explosives.md](../../engine/gameplay/planted-explosives.md#the-weapon-and-its-charges)), so a take works at the cap. The hand then holds a primed charge as a pouch grab leaves it: let go, it is thrown; pressed onto the pouch, it goes back; pressed onto a surface, it sticks, but only once the stick probes have found it clear of every surface, so the surface it came off does not take it straight back (`c4: took a placed charge back into the hand`). A take is refused, the charge left placed, when the charge went off or was removed since the offer, while the game's own planting holds the weapon, and while the game does not allow planting, with the refusal buzz and an info-level log line. The grapple's snatch reaches the same take through `c4::placed::take`, choosing among `c4::placed::charges`.

A charge pressed onto a surface sticks there instead of being thrown ([physical-weapons.md, "C4 pressed onto a surface"](physical-weapons.md#c4-pressed-onto-a-surface)). Opening the fingers throws, as for a grenade (`c4: the fingers opened, and the charge leaves the hand`), with the release motion a gun's let-go takes ([grenades.md, "The throw's motion"](grenades.md#the-throws-motion)). Once the charge has its physics body, the weapon makes it live ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::Fire_Projectile`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-Fire_Projectile)), which adds it to the placed charges the detonation reads, and the mod then drops it from the hand the way the game drops a charge under the parachute: it moves the charge to the grip ([`Explosive::SetPhysicsTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::Explosive##func-SetPhysicsTransform)), drops it into the simulation ([`PlantedExplosive::DropExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::PlantedExplosive##func-DropExplosive)), clicks the throwing hand (`Event::C4Throw`), gives its body that motion, linear and angular, through the owned-body setter a let-go gun's body takes ([`PlantedExplosive::SetVelocity`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::explosive::PlantedExplosive##func-SetVelocity), which sets only the linear part, is the fallback when the body refuses it), and plays its trail. Under the parachute the weapon has already dropped it from the right-hand bone, so the mod only moves it to the hand and replaces its motion. The explosive's own collision handler sticks the charge to what it lands on. A release once the cap is reached takes the charge back rather than throwing one that nothing would detonate. When the throw stops acting, a held charge is taken back ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::DespawnPrimedExplosive`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##func-DespawnPrimedExplosive)).

The detonation goes through the game's detonate input ([`WeaponInput::E_INPUT_DETONATE_EXPLOSIVES`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::WeaponInput##variant-E_INPUT_DETONATE_EXPLOSIVES)), so it detonates the placed charges exactly as the game's own detonation does, under the game's permission ([`Inventory::GetAnimationAllowsTriggeringExplosives`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-GetAnimationAllowsTriggeringExplosives)). The dominant stick click, on foot, falling, and under the parachute, carries `DETONATE_EXPLOSIVE` alone, which the gamepad rules act on once it has been held past the game's hold threshold, after the stick click chord's window; a tap does nothing. The wrist detonator requests a detonation instead: the C4 weapon's serial update then raises the detonate input for its own update only, putting the game's value back afterwards, until the weapon starts the detonation ([`PlantedExplosiveWeapon::m_AlreadyDetonatedOnThisInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::PlantedExplosiveWeapon##field-m_AlreadyDetonatedOnThisInput) rises) or a short timeout passes. A detonation the weapon starts, from either source, plays a long thud on both hands (`Event::C4Detonate`) and flashes the wrist button.

The wrist detonator (`weapons.wrist_detonator`, on; the tuning is `C4.detonator`; the code is `c4/wrist.rs` and `c4/press.rs`). A button sits on the inside of the wrist of the hand that is not the dominant one, as a watch is and beside the wrist readout, at `offset_m` in that hand's grip frame with its face along `normal`, both given for the left hand and mirrored for the right, and the dominant hand's index fingertip presses it. The button stays on that wrist whatever the hands hold, so the player always finds it in the same place; it does not follow the guns or the pouches. A dominant hand that holds nothing and is closing its grip (reaching to take a charge or a grenade from the other hand, say) is not read, so it cannot press the button on the way; a fingertip first seen on the button once the grip opens is blocked, as one that regains tracking is. The press is tested in the off hand's grip frame as that hand is rendered (the hands the palette edit draws, which fall short of the controllers where an arm cannot reach or a hand blends with the animation): the button sits at a fixed place in that frame, as the overlay draws it, and the fingertip is the tip of Rico's drawn index finger, published by the finger curls in the posed wrist's frame and placed on the rendered wrist (see [attachments.md](attachments.md#wrist-fingertip)), brought into the off hand's frame. The detector and the drawn button therefore share one frame, the finger the player sees on the button is the one that presses it, and the approach speed is the finger's against the wrist. Until the render frame has drawn both hands, the hands' tick targets stand in for them. While the finger curls do not write that hand, the fingertip falls back to the controller frame, carried with the hand to where it is rendered: the tracked hand skeleton's index tip when the runtime reports one, and otherwise a point in the dominant controller's aim frame: `fingertip_pointing_m` while the finger is off the trigger and `fingertip_resting_m` while it touches it. A press is a touch from any direction in front of the wrist: the fingertip within `touch_radius_m` (0.05 m, wider than the drawn button because the drawn fingertip and wrist land a few centimetres from the player's own) of the face's centre and no more than `max_behind_m` behind its plane presses at once when it was closing on the centre at `min_entry_speed_mps` or more as it came in, and otherwise once it has kept touching for `dwell_s`. A fingertip further behind the plane has come through the wrist, which a real finger cannot, and is blocked; so is one first seen touching, so that regaining tracking never presses. A blocked fingertip is free again once it leaves the touch sphere. After a press the button presses again only once the fingertip has moved away past `release_radius_m` (0.07 m). A press with charges placed requests the detonation and clicks both hands (`Event::DetonatorPress`); a press with none detonates nothing, buzzes the pressing hand (`Event::DetonatorEmpty`), and flashes the button grey; both are logged at info level. A detonation's charges stay placed for a while after it starts, because the detonate act schedules them partway through its animation and each then runs down its own timer, so from the start until the count of placed charges falls to zero, rises (a charge placed since is not part of it), or `DETONATION_IN_FLIGHT_S` (3 s) passes, the charges are in flight (`c4::detonation_in_flight`): the button is dim, and a press counts as one with nothing to detonate rather than queueing a second request that could set off a charge placed just after. The button is drawn on the wrist through the overlay (`weapons.c4.detonator.overlay`, the visualization drawer's "Wrist detonator button"), lying on the wrist facing out of it: a dim disc with a rim while no charges are placed, a red one while there are, with a pip beside it for each placed charge in a row along the forearm (up to eight), turning yellow as a slow touch dwells, flashing white on a press, and flashing orange and larger when a detonation starts. A faint ring around it marks the touch sphere. Within `approach_radius_m` (0.1 m) of the button, a white dot marks the fingertip point that is tested, a second ring closes from the approach radius onto the touch sphere as the fingertip nears, the touch sphere's ring brightens, and the pressing hand ticks once on the way in (`Event::DetonatorNear`) and then hums, more strongly as the fingertip closes in, rising with the square of how far it has come from the approach radius to the touch sphere (`Sustain::DetonatorApproach`), so the last centimetres are felt most. The fingertip is not drawn toward the button. A blocked touch turns the rims and the dot red until the fingertip leaves the touch sphere. It acts in every mode outside the menus.

Melee has no controller binding. The game's melee input (`PUSH_GRAPPLE` under the hammer ability) is on no button and no gesture starts it, because the game's melee attack lunges the body toward its target, taking the body from the player. An empty hand's palm still meets the world ([physical-hands.md](physical-hands.md)), but passes through characters.

Per-hand aim assist (`aim.assist_strength`, 0 by default; the reach and the occlusion tolerance are fixed in `controllers::aim::assist`). For each gun hand, the target sets that the aim control's last pass included are scored with the game's own weapon fitness ([`PlayerAimControl::GetFitnessScore`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##func-GetFitnessScore)), with the camera getters returning the hand's sight line so that the fitness cone is the hand's. Only live hostile targets ahead of the hand and within reach count. The best-scored one whose line from the hand is clear to within the tolerance of its body position is the candidate, and the hand's aim point turns toward it by the strength, its distance moving by the same fraction.

The true reload (`weapons.reload`, on) has its own page, [reload.md](reload.md): the game's own reloads of the hand weapons are stopped, and the player ejects with the reload button or a flick, loads from a universal belt pouch into the gun's real magazine well or by pushing the gun onto the pouch, and works the slide, charging handle, bolt, or pump after running dry (the bolt and the pump after every shot).

The empty weapon (`weapons.auto_switch_when_empty`, off; the cue is `Event::DryFire`). The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/empty.rs`, and the engine side is in [weapon-selection.md, "The switch away from an empty weapon"](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-selection.md#the-switch-away-from-an-empty-weapon). The game switches to another weapon when the trigger of an empty weapon that cannot reload is pulled, which would take a gun out of the hand that drew it. A detour on [`WeaponBase::CheckIfReloadShouldBeTriggeredBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-CheckIfReloadShouldBeTriggeredBase) marks the check of the local player's weapon on its thread, and a detour on [`Inventory::TryToSwitchToValidWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::inventory::Inventory##func-TryToSwitchToValidWeapon) reports, inside a marked check and with the setting off, that no weapon qualified, without switching. The game then handles the pull as it does with nothing to switch to: the out-of-ammunition notice and the gun's out-of-ammunition effect at the press edge, and no shot. The switch's other caller, the weapon-equip state task, runs outside a check and is unaffected, as are manual selection, scripted switches, and dropped weapons, which do not use it. Both act only under the controller scheme. The empty gun stays in the hand and can be holstered; the reload button does nothing until ammunition is picked up, and dual-wielded pistols, which share a magazine, both stay out. A press-edge pull of an empty gun that cannot reload also pulses the hand holding the pulled gun, whether or not the switch is suppressed. The weapon section shows the dry fires and the switches suppressed and allowed.

The Weapons > Grenades, C4, and wrist detonator live status shows the throw's stage, the fuse, the last release velocity, and the throw's steps; the C4 throw's stage, the charges placed and the cap, the charges put back into the pouch and taken back from where they were placed, the planting and detonating permissions, the throw's steps, whether a detonation's charges are in flight, and the detonations with the source of the last one (the wrist button or the detonate button) and whether the game started it; the wrist button's phase, the fingertip's distance from the button and height in front of its face, where the fingertip was read from, and the presses; the true reload's readout (see [reload.md, "The readout"](reload.md#the-readout)); and each hand's aim-assist correction.

## Scopes

This is the rest of milestone 5, and has not been run in the headset. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/weapons/scope/`, the shaders are `payload/src/shaders/scope_lens_vs.hlsl` and `scope_lens_ps.hlsl`, the setting is `weapons.scopes`, and the lens's tuning is `LENS` in `controllers::weapons::scope`. The engine side is [aim-pipeline.md, "Precision aim and sniper zoom"](../../engine/gameplay/aim-pipeline.md#precision-aim-and-sniper-zoom).

Precision aim is suppressed. It adds a main-camera zoom hint, and on a sniper rifle it shows the 2D sniper overlay and hides the character, which hides the hands. Every entry into it goes through [`Player::SetAimMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-SetAimMode): the precision-aim input, the sniper rifle's return after a bolt-action cycle, and the guided rocket launcher's shot. A detour there refuses precision aim under the controller scheme, so the mode, the hint, the aim control's aim type, and the weapon's own mode all stay on normal aim. When the scheme takes over while the player is already in precision aim, a post-call detour on the game's own per-tick exit check ([`Player::UpdateAimMode`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::player::Player##func-UpdateAimMode)) leaves it through the same setter, which undoes the overlay and the hidden body as the game does.

The lens (`scopes`, on). A scoped weapon is one of the sniper class ([`WeaponBase::m_Class`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_Class)), identified on the input tick in either hand, the wielded weapon or one armed beside it. There is one lens: with a scoped gun in each hand, it is the scope raised to the eye, the one an eye is at, or, with both or neither, the one whose eyepiece is nearest an eye ([two-handed-inventory.md, "The gun in either hand"](two-handed-inventory.md#the-gun-in-either-hand)). Each scoped gun's sight line comes from the gun aim, kept in the holding wrist's frame between aim updates and placed from the render-time wrist each frame, so the lens follows the controller at frame rate. The eyepiece is on the sight line `LENS.eyepiece_back_m` behind its origin, which with the weapon's sight correction is above the muzzle. The embedded sight table's scoped entries give every scoped weapon one; a weapon without one has its sight line along the barrel, below the scope, and the readout says so. While an eye is within `LENS.max_eye_distance_m` of the eyepiece and behind it, that eye sights, and the lens is drawn on it as a disc of `LENS.lens_radius_m` facing back along the sight line; the other eye sees the game's scope model.

The lens resamples a rendered eye image rather than rendering the scene again. At the end of [`RenderEngine::PostDraw`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/graphics_engine::render_engine::RenderEngine##func-PostDraw), each dispatch copies its eye's back buffer (both halves under the collapse) before the world-space overlay draws, while a scoped weapon is held, with the camera that rendered it. After the overlay, the sighting eye draws the lens: a point of the lens the eye sees at a tangent `t` off the sight line shows the scene at `t / zoom` off it, taken at the aim point's distance and projected into the source image, so the image is right at the target. The rim darkens from `LENS.vignette_start` of the radius, and a crosshair marks the sight line. Outside the source image the lens is black.

The source image is the other eye's whenever that eye has an image. The sighting eye's own image has the scope's model in front of exactly the region the lens magnifies, because the scope sits on the line from that eye to the target; the other eye, a few centimetres to the side, sees past it. In two-pass stereo, when the first eye sights, the second eye's image is the previous frame's, reprojected by its own camera, so the lens lags the scene by a frame there. Without one, the lens reads the sighting eye's own image, which shows the scope's model.

The narrow-field scene render is not built. It would be a third dispatch in the frame loop in `hooks/game.rs`, beside the far-field share's extra dispatch, and would need:

- a dispatch kind of its own, with the between-dispatch restores (effect info, frame counters, the constant-buffer ring, the SSAO and GI histories, the add-list parity) run before it as before the second eye, and prepass sharing to skip shadows and reflections;
- a narrow-field camera in the `SetupRenderCamera` hook: the sighting eye's position, the sight line's orientation, and a projection of the lens's true field, with no upscaler jitter;
- a smaller render: the scene targets' size is a re-initialisation, so the only cheap lever is a viewport in a corner of the full-size targets, with every full-screen pass (the post chain, the upscaler, the far-field composite, and foveation) either skipped or confined to it;
- the temporal effects kept out of it: the upscaler's history, auto-exposure, and motion blur would take the narrow view as the next frame of an eye;
- the `PostDraw` tail told apart: no HUD, overlay, capture copy, recorder sidecar line, or screenshot for it, its image copied into the lens's texture instead, and its flip blocked;
- the gate on the scope being at the eye, so the cost of a nearly full scene render is paid only while sighting.

The Weapons > Aim, sights, and scopes live status shows whether precision aim is suppressed and the refusals and exits, the scoped weapon with the lens and its hand, the eyepiece's distance from the nearest eye, and the lens's state: why it is not drawn, or which eye sights and which eye's image it reads, and the path in use.

### Arm reach

A fully extended player arm must ask for a fully extended character arm about the joint HumanIK actually reaches from. Four parts do this, and a fifth keeps the rendered hand with the controller when the solve still falls short. All five are fixed on in `hands::reach::REACH` and act only where the hand IK or the late update acts, except the reach limit, which acts wherever the controller hands compose.

At the pre-solve seam the hands measure the character's arms (`controllers/hands/rig.rs`) from the bones HumanIK drives for the shoulder, elbow, and wrist effectors, found through the solver's node-and-bone pairs rather than by name. HumanIK's shoulder node is the upper-arm joint, not the collar ([humanik.md, "Effector-id model"](../../engine/character/humanik.md#effector-id-model)), so the measured chain is exactly the one the wrist target is solved along. Each tick gives the upper-arm and forearm lengths and the shoulder joint's offset from the eye-bone midpoint, turned into the body frame's axes. The lengths settle over half a second and then hold, since the rig carries no scale; the offset follows the animation over `REACH.shoulder_smoothing_s`. The same offset is also kept in the clip's chest frame, square to the line across the clip's shoulder joints, followed over `REACH.chest_shoulder_smoothing_s` (0.5 s): a run swings the animated shoulders about 10 cm forward and back each stride, which a remap from the raw joint turned into a rifle pumping in and out of the hands while moving (277.6 to 279.6 s and 380 to 382 s of the 6 November 2026 session). A sample with a segment shorter than 5 cm or longer than 60 cm is dropped and counted.

The measured remap places the wrist at the character's shoulder, the head's cockpit position plus the measured offset (on foot, while the body rig estimates the torso, the offset in the clip's chest turned by the torso's heading, which the procedural chest faces; elsewhere the offset in the body frame), and scales its offset from the player's estimated shoulder by the character's measured shoulder-to-wrist length over the player's. The player's length is half the arm span less the shoulder width, less the hand, the figure the calibration splits into the segments; the saved segment lengths are not read. Both are divided by the world scale, since the character's arm is in world metres. The measured remap applies whether or not `controllers.body.remap_hands` is on; that setting governs only the estimated fallback, used for an arm not yet measured. The offset is from the eye-bone midpoint, so it places the shoulder correctly only because the cockpit head composes to the eye midpoint the camera renders from (`CockpitTransform::current`, [render-time-anchoring.md](../rendering/render-time-anchoring.md#the-rule)), the same frame the reach limit carries the solved shoulder into. Composed from the published head, which follows the head joint, the remap's shoulder sat the eye arm (about 0.125 m) behind and below the limit's, and the limit clamped a reaching hand by up to about 0.15 m. With both in the same frame, what is left between them is the player's head standing off Rico's (the remap's shoulder follows the player's eyes, the limit's stays on Rico's body), which the body's follow closes; a reach past it is the intended clamp.

The reach limit (`hands::reach::ReachLimit`, `REACH.reach_limit`) then holds each remapped wrist within the character's measured shoulder-to-wrist length of his shoulder joint, as the last solve left it: the skeleton read after the pose-finalization pass ([rig.md, "The body's volumes"](../body/rig.md#the-bodys-volumes-on-ricos-skeleton)), carried into the cockpit frame. Before a fresh read, and whenever the read fell back to the last plausible skeleton, the shoulder is the measured animated one relative to the eyes, as the remap places it. A wrist past the reach goes to where the line from the shoulder to it meets the sphere, and the grip and aim poses move with it. The limit sits in the composition every controller hand starts from (`hands::active_hands_raw`), so the hand body's goal, the wrist target, the grab volumes' test, the carried and jointed holds, and the gun hands all start from a hand the arm can reach, and the drawn arm stays on the drawn hand without stretching. A controller held past the arm's reach, into a wall or across the body, leaves the hand at full extension along the line to it, so the hand keeps following the controller's direction rather than stopping or vanishing, as the hand body already does at a wall. The reach pull still lets the shoulder follow: a wrist at the limit pulls the girdle at full ramp, and the next tick's limit is about the moved shoulder. Only the crouched floor reach reads the controller before the limit (`hands::controller_grips`), since it measures where the player's own hand is.

Each wrist's position target carries a pull that ramps, by a smoothstep, from zero at `pull_start` of the character's arm length (the target's distance from his pre-solve shoulder joint) to `pull_max` at `pull_full` and beyond, so the shoulder and chest follow a reach the arm alone cannot make and the solve stays local otherwise. Beneath the ramp is a flat floor of zero (`WRIST_PULL` in `controllers::hands::ik`). Pull rides the position target ([humanik.md, "The four control values"](../../engine/character/humanik.md#the-four-control-values-and-which-list-drives-which)). When the body IK pinned that side's shoulder this tick, the pin's reach yields by the same ramp, so it does not hold the girdle back. On the procedural torso the pull is zero: the torso is the player's, and the arm reaches alone.

With the late update on, the late update corrects against the solved wrist rather than the IK target. After the solve, each driven hand's shortfall, the tick's target less the solved wrist in the world, is published with the solved joints. With the reach limited, a shortfall comes only from a body that moved against the hands within a tick, or a solve that did not converge. At render time the reference the correction is taken from is the target moved back along the shortfall by the part the detach covers: the shortfall beyond `detach_threshold_m`, up to `detach_cap_m`. The rendered hand and the gun therefore leave the end of an arm that cannot reach by up to the cap, toward the controller. The forearm entries also scale along the corrected forearm's axis about the elbow, bounded to half again or two thirds of its length, so the forearm stays attached to the detached hand. Past the cap the hand stays at the cap. The detach rides the correction's tier weight, so while a hand blends toward the animation part of the intended offset from the target is detached too. The shot origin, the muzzle flash, and the sight line stay on the sim-rate transform, so with the hand detached they can be up to the cap away from the rendered gun.

The Body/tracking > Hand presentation live status shows the player's arm length, and per hand the character's measured upper arm, forearm, total and ratio, the eye-to-shoulder offset, the sample counts, the target's extension as a fraction of his arm, the pull queued, how far the shoulder pin was released, the solve shortfall, and the detach applied.

## Unverified

The C4 throw and the wrist detonator rest on these, unchecked in the game and the headset: that priming outside the planting mode and putting the weapon's state back leaves the weapon, the inventory, and the game's own planting undisturbed; that the inventory's attachment pass runs the C4 weapon's placement every frame, so the held charge follows the hand; that the primed charge's body follows its transform while it is held and is where the hand is when it drops, rather than where it spawned; that a dropped charge sticks to the ground, walls, characters, and vehicles as the parachute drop does, and that a charge thrown at the player's own feet sticks without hitting the thrower; that the base weapon fire run by making a charge live (its rumble, heat, and the out-of-the-hands fire effects) is acceptable with another weapon in the hands; that no animation's track message despawns a held charge (the readout reports one gone); that a detonate input raised for the weapon's update alone starts the detonate act as the held stick click does; that charges inside the game's safety range around the player do not detonate, which is the game's own rule; that removing a placed charge from inside the C4 weapon's serial update, and priming in its place in the same update, disturbs nothing else that holds the charge (an object it is attached to, the HUD); that the drawn index chain extended by the distal share lands on the rendered fingertip closely enough to press what the player sees; and the pouch's placement, the wrist button's placement and normal on both the Index and Touch controllers, the fingertip estimates, and the touch radius, entry speed, and dwell, all untuned.

The arm reach rests on these, unchecked in the headset: that the eye-bone midpoint is close enough to the eye point the camera hangs off the published head (the head joint plus the neck and the rest-pose eye arm, plus the HMD's travel), so the measured offset places the shoulder correctly relative to the head; that the pre-solve shoulder joint, read eye-relative while the player's head moves relative to the character's animated eyes, is close enough to where the solve reaches from; that the character's arm is about the player's, so the ratio stays near one (the readout shows it); the pull ramp's start, full, and maximum, and that a wrist pull at the `UPPER_BODY` step moves the shoulder and chest without disturbing the head target; that releasing the body IK's shoulder pin by the ramp does not let the torso lean collapse; that the solved shoulder read a tick late is close enough to where the next solve reaches from that the limit does not hold a hand visibly short of a reachable controller, and that a hand held at the limit while the controller goes on reads as reaching rather than as lag; the detach threshold and cap; and that a forearm stretched by up to half reads acceptably, given that the stretch also scales the forearm's normals.

The grapple's aim assist rests on these, unchecked in the game and the headset: that calling the game's grapple scoring before the aim update leaves nothing behind once the game's own target pass has run; that aiming the ray at a candidate's body position makes the game's direct aim or its own scoring take that candidate; and the cone and the strength, untuned.

The Index grip and trackpad presses rest on these, unchecked in the headset: that Monado reports the grip's squeeze force and the trackpad's force on the Index profile; that the force press and release defaults suit a deliberate squeeze without tiring a held grab, a grenade release, or a held vehicle control; that the trackpad force defaults suit a click-like press; and that the menus take a held tab action from the trackpad as they did from the grip.

The semantic bindings rest on these, unchecked in the headset: that the runtime's digital edges of a float action (thresholded by the mod) arrive in step with the raw grip's and trigger's, so a grab latched from the raw grip and the grip action masked by it agree on every press; that the bound sources the runtime reports name the components the masks match (through wineopenxr as well as natively), or at least name the hand, or else are all opaque, where the defaults stand in (Monado's are); that a press of the off-hand trackpad's force for a weapon slot, which also touches the pad, showing the controls summary at the same time is acceptable; that holding a slot's select action from the press to the release opens the game's selection cross and commits on the release, as the weapon wheel relies on; and the trackpad quadrant margin.

The menu navigation and the chords rest on these, unchecked in the headset: that every menu the controllers reach takes its stick and tab input through the base menu input, rather than an override that reads other actions (the map and the commlink have their own handling); that the menu push threshold, the chord window, and the recenter hold time suit the Index sticks; and that the delay the chord window adds to a lone stick click goes unnoticed for a weapon step or a horn, and adds acceptably to the detonate hold.

Posture and roomscale rest on these assumptions, none checked in the headset (the rig's are in [rig.md, "Unverified"](../body/rig.md#unverified)): that the crouching proxy state's capsule, which has the non-player standing radius, suits the player; that the seat-state name tables cover every entry, locked, shuffle, and exit state (a state in none of them counts as out of the vehicle and is logged); and the fade timings.
