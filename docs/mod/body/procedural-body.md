# The procedural body

Without it, Rico's body is the game's animation with corrections on top: HumanIK targets for the head, the torso, and the hands ([head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md), [rig.md](rig.md#the-torso)). The pelvis, the legs, and the shape of the spine stayed the clip's, so the player's waist and Rico's disagreed by whatever the clip did, and a hand on the player's hip landed beside Rico's. The procedural body builds the pose from the player instead. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/procedural/`, its settings are `controllers.body.procedural`, and the pause menu's `VR: BODY` page has the switch (`BODY FROM YOUR HEAD`).

It is on by default (`FULL`). The write seam has not been seen working in the headset yet; see the last section, and set `BODY FROM YOUR HEAD` to `OFF` on the `VR: BODY` page if it misbehaves.

## Where the pose is written

The pose is written at the HumanIK solve gate: a pre-call detour on [`HumanIK::HasTargets`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##func-HasTargets) (`hooks::character::gate`), acting for the local player's solver and the `MAIN` pass. That call has one caller, inside the pose-finalization pass, after every game writer of IK targets and the first weapon attachment refresh, and before the solve, the second attachment refresh, the model-space sync, the hit shapes, the AI's view of the character, and the attached effects ([skeleton.md, "Who reads the pose before `UpdatePropEffects`"](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#who-reads-the-pose-before-updatepropeffects)). A pose written there is the one bullets hit and NPCs see, which a write at `UpdatePropEffects`, where the head override is, would not be. The solver is found from the character, which embeds it, and the gate runs inside the HumanIK gate the game checks first (the global switch and the reduced level of detail).

The gate has no step of its own, so the pass's entry records the pass's step on the thread running it, and every follower, spring, and phase of the body advances by it rather than by the wall clock between gates, which a hitch or a frame that runs several ticks would stretch or squeeze.

The writes go through [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) into the current pose, parents first, so each write carries the bones under it. The pose producer's pose pair ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#bone-store-hkapose-model-space)) makes the pose written this tick next tick's `T0`, so the skinning interpolates it like any other.

## The rig

The body drives the hips, the pelvis, both legs down to the toes (`LeftToeBase`), the four spine bones, the neck, the head, and the two clavicles (`LeftShoulder` and `RightShoulder`, measured for the chest's turn). `rig` finds them by name in the skeleton the animation controller holds ([`AnimationController::m_Skeleton`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##field-m_Skeleton)), and checks that each bone's parent is the one the solver assumes: the legs hang from `Pelvis`, a sibling of the spine under `Hips`. It also measures each arm from the reference pose, for the clavicles: where the shoulder joint (`LeftArm`) sits on its clavicle, and the arm's length down `LeftForeArm` to `LeftHand`. A skeleton that differs is refused with a warning (`procedural rig: ...`), and the body stays the animation's. The rig is kept for the skeleton it was built from: the animation controller, its skeleton resource, the Havok skeleton under it, and the bone count, so a controller that comes to hold another skeleton is rigged afresh. Every bone index is checked against the skeleton's bone count before a joint is read or written, since [`AnimationController::SetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-SetJoint) does not check it. The skeleton is logged once per session (`body: skeleton bone`, one line per bone), which is how a live Rico's hierarchy can be checked against the shipped one.

## Modes

- `Off`: nothing happens at the gate. Switching to it from `FULL` first blends the body back to the animation at the hand-back's pace (`blend_out_s`), with the case `switched off`.
- `PassThrough` (`TEST` in the menu): the animated pose of the driven bones is read and written back unchanged, then read again. Every five seconds it logs `procedural body: pass-through wrote the animated pose back` with the largest read-back error in metres and degrees, which should be zero. Nothing on screen should change; if anything does (a twitch, a weapon off the hand, the head override fighting), the write path is wrong.
- `Full` (`FULL`): the body, below, standing and moving. Settings saved while this mode was named `Body` load as `Full`.

## The body

The body takes over while Rico is on foot on the ground with the headset driving the view, standing or moving; the hand-over table below gives the rest of the game's states back to the animation, or takes part of the body in them on the animation's hips ([procedural-states.md](procedural-states.md)). The pre-solve IK that would pose the same bones (the body IK's shoulder, chest, and hand-pin targets) stands down once the procedural torso has its full weight, and is queued as before below it. At the gate, every MAIN-pass target on the owned effectors, the game's and these alike, has its reach and pull faded by its region's weight (`neutralize_targets`): the hips, the waist, the chest end, and the shoulders by the torso's, and the hip joints, the knees, the ankles, and the feet by the legs'. The fade scales the value queued this tick, or the value before the last fade when nothing queued it again, so a half-weight region keeps half the target rather than less each tick, and the live reach the engine eases toward the target is held at most at the faded target. So the hand-over is as gradual as the blend, and at full weight the solve after the gate leaves the pose alone. The head's own target keeps its reach, so the body IK's head target closes what is left between the neck and the player's head, but its pull fades by the torso's weight, so it cannot drag the procedural spine and hips after the head.

The engine reaches the gate only with the global HumanIK switch on and the character's reduced level of detail off. The pass's entry marks the pass, and the gate marks it as gated; a pass that ends without its gate hands the body back (the pre-solve IK is no longer told to stand down, and the blend starts over) and queues the wrist targets kept for the gate. The engine places the weapons on the solved pose ([`Character::UpdatePropTransforms`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropTransforms), with [`PROP_TRANSFORM_AFTER_SOLVE`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character)) only when the gate reports a queued target, so when the gate writes a procedural pose and nothing is queued, it places them itself, and a gun never stays where the animated hand held it.

Each region's weight follows its case's with a critically damped spring: `blend_s` toward the procedural pose and the quicker `blend_out_s` back to the animation, whose authored motion is under way by then. A death or a ragdoll hands both regions over at once, since a ragdoll writes the pose itself. With the legs on the animation and the torso procedural (a jump with `air_legs` off), the animated legs hang from the procedural hips; the region blend carries each leg pose by the blended hips, so the legs never part from the hips mid-blend.

Handing back is continuous because the animation has been playing all along. Taking over restarts from where the animation left the body: when the legs come back, the feet are planted where the animated feet stand and step to their places from there, and when the torso comes back, the hips' reach drop starts at the animated hips' height below the procedural one, so a landing's crouch rises at the drop's pace instead of popping up.

Every change of case is logged at info level (`procedural body: the hand-over case changes`, with the case left and entered and both weights), and the readout shows the case and both weights.

The predicates the table calls at the gate walk the character's own rule system and interaction graph; the engine calls the same ones from the same pass for the character (the stunt position from the pose-finalization pass itself, and the grapple hang from the ground information), so the reads are made where the engine makes them. The vehicle seat, the traversal, and the airborne modes come from the mode detection the input tick publishes, so they lag by up to a frame; the state bits, the motion state, the health, and the proxy's support are read at the gate, so a jump or a step off a ledge takes the feet off the ground on the tick it happens rather than after the movement task's stamp has gone stale. The ledge climb keeps no state on the character, so a post-call on its motion task stamps each tick of it for the local player (`hooks::character::signals`, `procedural::signals`), and the first tick of each climb logs `procedural body: the grapple's ledge climb starts` at info level. A scripted sequence that takes Rico over while the game runs sets [`m_InSequence`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_InSequence) for as long as it drives him, so the sequence's transform tracks and its own state machines pose him rather than the procedural body. A hit reaction keeps no state on the character either: [`Character::HitReaction`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HitReaction) queues the stumble or the knock-back straight on Rico's animated model, so a pre-call on [`AnimatedModel::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-QueueAct) stamps the local player's (`procedural::signals`, matched by the model's address, which the entry of the player's pose-finalization pass publishes every tick and which stops matching a few ticks after its last publish, so a freed model's address cannot match another character's after a death or a respawn; every other model's act is turned away by that address before anything else is done), logging `procedural body: a hit reaction starts` with the act at info level. The reaction then holds the body on the animation for `hit_reaction_hold_s`, renewed by each new act; the stumble plays out as the game authored it, and the body blends back once the hold ends. The flinch, a partial over whatever the body does, is left to the procedural body. The player has no on-foot vault or obstacle climb to detect: no state machine the game ships uses those acts ([hands-locomotion-and-state.md, 5.8](../../engine/character/hands-locomotion-and-state.md#58-ledge-climbs-and-vaults)).

### The player's head

The player's head is measured against the root at the height the steady view hangs it from ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md#the-steady-view)): on a staircase the view's smoothed root trails the proxy's, and measuring the head against the raw root would read every step up as a crouch.

The body is built under the neck pivot, not the eye point (`procedural::head`). The headpose is the eyes: the head anchor plus the HMD's travel. Looking down turns the eyes about the neck, so the eye point drops and comes forward by five to twenty-five centimetres although nothing below the neck moved, and building the hips under it half-crouched a player who only looked at the ground. The solve takes the eye point less the HMD's rotation of the arm from the neck pivot to the eyes (the torso estimate's generic arm, seven and a half centimetres down and eight and a half back, at the world scale), which is where the head bone is.

How far the hips go down is measured against the height the player is seen to stand at, not against the calibrated eye height. The runtime's floor can sit a few centimetres off the one the eye height was measured against (three to thirteen in one session), the animated idle's head sits a few centimetres below the reference pose's, and either read as a constant shallow crouch. The standing height is the neck pivot's height over the head anchor that nine in ten of the recent samples lie below: a sample every eighth of a second while Rico stands on foot on the procedural legs and the gaze is within twenty degrees of level, half a minute of them, seeded at the calibration (the pivot at the anchor) until two seconds' worth are in, and kept within fifteen centimetres of the calibration, so that half a minute crouched behind cover does not become standing. The head's drop is how far the pivot is below that, less `crouch_deadband_m` (four centimetres), and the hips follow the reference pose's head lowered by the drop; so a standing player's bobs and nods leave them at their standing height, and the idle's own head height does not count. A floor jump across a reference-space change ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)) starts the measure afresh and logs `procedural body: the floor jumped, so the standing height is measured afresh`. The Controllers tab's readout shows the standing height, its sample count, and the drop, and the lower body's change log carries them.

### The solve

The solver (`solver`, pure and unit-tested) works in Rico's model space from the animated pose:

0. The clip's own shape is settled out first: the hips lose the pitch and roll the animation gives them (a run swings them with every step), keeping their turn, and the spine and the neck go most of the way to the reference pose's shape on the hips, so a run's bob and the idle's slouch are not the body's. The walk's sway comes from the hips' draw toward the planted foot instead.
1. The hips follow the head down: their height is the head's less the reference pose's head-over-hips height, at most the reference standing height (so legs are straight when the head is at standing height), and at least `min_hips_height` of it. Horizontally they sit where the rig's pelvis puts them ([rig.md, "The pelvis"](rig.md#the-pelvis)): over the middle of the neutral stance the root carries, not over the feet, which leave it with every step, so a step does not move them; back over the heels in a squat; hinged back by a share of the lean the chase leaves alone; drawn a little toward the one foot down while the other swings; and back along the body's motion by its lean into it, followed by a stiff spring. When the torso comes back from the animation they start where the animated hips were. They turn to the player's torso heading from the torso estimate ([rig.md](rig.md#the-torso)), at most `pelvis_turn_limit_deg`, measured on the line between the hip joints. When a foot is further from its hip joint than the leg reaches (a long stride, a foot on a lower stair, a slope), the hips drop until it reaches (`reach_drop`), never below `min_hips_height` counted from where the head has already taken them, so a stride in a crouch does not sink the hips past a squat's floor, followed down quickly and back up at a third of the pace (`reach_drop_s`), so the body does not bob on every stride.
2. The chest twists to the torso heading, at most `chest_turn_limit_deg`, shared out up the spine (more of it higher), measured on the line between the clavicles. When the point under the player's head is closer to the spine's base than the spine is long, which happens once the hips are at their lowest and the head goes on down, the spine curls forward about the chest's sideways axis until the neck reaches it, as far as a back bends: a deep squat folds the back after the legs, and a head lower still is left to the head override. Then the spine swings so the neck points at the point under the player's head (the head position plus the animated head-to-neck offset turned by the head's rotation), shared out along the four spine bones and repeated three times to converge, never past `lean_limit_deg` from upright. A look down pitches the chest forward by about a third of it, at most about twenty-five degrees, in the upper back, and the whole spine then turns about its base to put the neck back on its line, so looking at the belt brings the chest over it. The neck then takes about a third of the head's turn against the chest, so a nod or a look aside bends the neck as well as the head; the head override sets the head itself after the solve.
3. Each leg is solved with analytic two-bone IK so the ankle reaches its foot's place, and the foot takes its place's orientation. The knee bends toward a pole that is the foot's forward (its orientation against the reference pose's) blended evenly with the hips' forward, so a turned foot turns its knee and a clip's leg bent backward mid-stride is not copied; the plane the animated leg bends in only breaks the tie when the pole lies along the line to the foot, and then never bends the knee backward.

The rotations are composed onto the animated bones, so no bone's rest frame is assumed; the settle and the neck's share compare the bones with the reference pose's, and the knee's pole measures the foot's turn against it.

### Crouching and seated players

A crouch is the head going down: the hips follow it to `min_hips_height`, the knees bend, and the spine curls for the rest. The roomscale crouch's capsule request still runs, so the crouch also lowers the collision. A seated player's head is remapped to Rico's standing eye height (the posture's height remap, [head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md)), so the solver gives a seated player straight standing legs; the seated crouch toggle lowers the remapped head by its preset and the legs fold the same way.

### The lower body's poses

`solver::lower` picks the lower body's pose each tick: standing, crouching (the hips more than a fifth of the way down to `min_hips_height`, and back to standing under a twelfth, so a head bobbing about one threshold does not flip the pose), kneeling, or sitting on the ground. Standing and crouching are the solver's own; a kneel and a sit are poses of their own that the solver mixes over its hips, feet, and knees by the pose's weight, which eases in and out over `kneel_blend_s` and `sit_blend_s`. A sit is mixed over a kneel, so going from one to the other crosses over rather than passing through standing.

The body kneels on one knee (`kneel`, on by default; `KNEEL BELOW A SQUAT` on the `VR: BODY` page) when the head asks the hips to go a few centimetres below the squat's floor and stays there for a quarter of a second, on foot on the ground with the procedural legs and not told to move (the commanded velocity below the walk's stopping speed, and the stick less than a third pushed). The knee that goes down is the trailing foot's, the one further back along the hips' facing by more than a few centimetres, or the off hand's side (`controllers.acting_hand`'s other) when neither trails. The hips move a little toward the kneeling side and are held there, in the world, for the whole kneel, so the root moving under them (the chase) does not slide them; their height follows the head between an upright kneel, the thigh straight above the knee, and one sat back toward the heel, below which the spine curls as in a squat. The kneeling knee rests on the ground ahead of its hip joint by as far as the thigh reaches past straight down, the shin lies back from it, and the foot is pitched toes-down with the toes tucked under. The other foot stands ahead of its hip joint with its knee up, and both knees point along the hips' facing at the kneel's start. The head rising a few centimetres past the floor, the body being told to move, or leaving the ground ends the kneel; once its weight reaches one the walking feet are planted where the kneel holds them, so letting go steps the feet back under the body from there rather than sliding them.

In standing play (the standing posture, [rig.md](rig.md#posture)) the body sits on the ground (`sit`, on by default; `SIT ON THE GROUND` on the `VR: BODY` page) when the head asks the hips to go below halfway between a sit and the kneel's lowest, and stays there, slower than a quarter of a metre a second, for `sit_after_s`, on foot on the ground, not told to move, and with the stick at rest. The kneel's shorter wait comes first, so a player going down to the floor kneels on the way and sits from the kneel. The hips go to the ground under the player's head, where a neutral head over them would be, with the hip joints a little above the ground, and are held there in the world. The legs come out in front: crossed, the right ankle over the left in front of the other leg, the feet turned across and rolled onto their outer edges and the knees out to the sides, when the head came down well ahead of the stance the player stood in; otherwise with the feet flat in front, a little wider than the hips, and the knees up. Once sat, leaning ahead of a neutral head over the hips for a second crosses knees-up legs, and sitting back up for a second puts crossed ones up, blending across. The seated posture's head is remapped to a standing height, so it never sits; its crouch toggle can kneel.

The sit ends when the head rises a little above where it sat (standing up, which passes back through a kneel or a crouch as the head goes up), when the stick is pushed, when the body leaves the ground, or when the posture stops being standing. While the body sits or is still getting up, the stick does not walk it (`body::procedural::sat_on_ground`, which the stick locomotion reads, [locomotion.md](locomotion.md)): pushing it stands the body up through a crouch, and the walk starts once the sit has let go. After the stick stood the body up, it does not sit again until the head has risen, so a player who stays low and still afterwards kneels instead.

Each change of pose logs `procedural body: the lower body's pose changes` at info level with the poses, the hips' height the head asks for, the squat's floor, the crouch's depth, the standing height and the head's drop below it, the kneeling side, how the legs lie, why a sit ended, the head's speed, whether the body is told to move, the stick, and whether the play is standing. The `VR: STATUS` page's `LOWER BODY` and `POSE INPUTS` rows show the same ([pause-menu.md](../pause-menu.md)).

### The feet

The feet's places come from the rig's legs ([rig.md, "The legs"](rig.md#the-legs); with `plant_feet`, the default). Each foot holds its place and orientation in the world while the body moves and turns above it, so the chase moving the root ([rig.md](rig.md)), the body turning to follow the torso, and the idle's own foot shuffle never slide it.

The feet rest around a neutral stance: the animated feet once Rico has stood still, not aiming, on level ground (the feet within a few centimetres of each other's height) for half a second, measured once per rig and made symmetric about the root. The feet's turns are kept only as a toe-out mirrored about the root, measured against the reference pose's feet, so an idle's twist of one foot and a slope's tilt do not stay in the stance (`procedural body: measured the neutral stance (model space)`, info level). Until then, the animated feet stand in for it while standing, and the reference pose's while moving. A foot's rest place is the stance carried by the root. A player leaning over a table or reaching for the floor moves the head without the root, so nothing steps and the body does not walk into what the hands reach for; the hips hinge back for the lean ([rig.md, "The pelvis"](rig.md#the-pelvis)). The legs pace themselves by the root's measured velocity, from the previous tick's root to this tick's, so the feet follow whatever moves the body: the stick, the chase, a push, or a wall that stops it while the stick is held. The feet never move the body.

A crouch walks in short, quick, low steps (`crouch_gait`, on by default; `SHORT STEPS IN A CROUCH` on the `VR: BODY` page). The crouch's depth is how far the head has taken the hips toward `min_hips_height`, from zero standing to one at a squat's floor, and the walk is scaled by it, eased in so a small dip of the head barely changes it: at the floor the stride is `crouch_stride_share` of the upright walk's and the lift `crouch_lift_share`, and each foot stays on the ground for at least `crouch_duty` of the cycle, so both feet are down together for longer. The speed is the root's either way, so the shorter stride is a quicker cadence, and the feet keep pace under hips that cannot drop far for a long stride (the reach drop has little room left in a crouch) instead of lunging after them. A standing step in a crouch lifts by the same share. The readout shows the depth, whether the feet walk, and how far ahead of its hip the last walking foot landed, and the walking debug line logs them with the stride, the duty factor, and the lift.

### In the air

In a jump, in a short fall within the jump's grace, and while hanging from a hand that grips the world, the legs stay procedural and hang in the air under the hips (`air_legs`, on by default; `LEGS IN THE AIR` on the `VR: BODY` page). The feet leave the ground with the body, carried from where they stood, and ease into the air's pose over about a tenth of a second: each ankle under its place in the neutral stance, below the hip joint by a share of the standing leg's height, `air_tuck` while Rico rises and `air_hang` once he falls at `air_fall_speed_mps` or faster, with the toes dropping by up to `air_toe_drop_deg` as the legs let down. The two-bone IK bends the knees forward for the tuck. The hips follow the head as on the ground, so ducking the head in the air tucks the whole body. The vertical speed is the root's over the pass, from the previous tick's root ([`Character::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT0)) to this tick's (a teleport's jump is ignored).

Falling, the legs reach for the ground below (`landing_reach`, on by default; `LEGS REACH FOR LANDINGS` on the `VR: BODY` page). Each tick Rico falls in the air's pose, a scene ray goes straight down under each hanging foot, as far as the fall covers in twice `landing_reach_s` and half a metre more (the same ray and filter as the footholds', below), and each foot with ground under it goes down toward it by how soon the fall reaches it: not at all while it is more than twice `landing_reach_s` away, all the way once it is `landing_reach_s` or less away, eased between. A foot goes as far as its leg reaches straight from the hips, or to the ground when that is nearer, and its toes come level as it reaches, so the leg meets a step, a slope, or a ledge below already stretched toward it; ground above a hanging foot (a ledge the tuck clears) leaves it hanging. A foot with nothing found under it hangs as before. The first reach of a fall logs `procedural body: the legs reach for the landing` at debug level with the fall's speed and the ground's distance below the feet, and the readout shows how far the legs have reached.

A fall past the grace, the wingsuit, and the parachute blend the legs to the animation from the air's pose rather than from the ground's. On landing, the legs come back to the ground quicker than they left it, and the feet are planted afresh where the stance wants them on the ground under them, not where the animation's legs hang.

Landing on foot dips the hips (`landing_dip`, on by default): the fastest fall of the jump, past `landing_dip_min_speed_mps`, kicks a critically damped spring on the hips' drop, `landing_dip_per_mps` deep per metre per second and at most `landing_dip_max_m`, deepest `landing_dip_s` after the landing and recovered a few times that later. The landing counts if the body is back on foot within a moment of the jump ending, since the movement task can take a tick or two to resume. The dip adds to the reach drop, so the knees bend and the feet stay planted. It moves only the hips and what hangs from them: the view is anchored to the root ([locomotion.md](locomotion.md)), so the landing reaches the view only as the steady view's own landing does. A hard landing is the game's authored motion and hands over to the animation instead. Each landing logs `procedural body: landed` at debug level with the fall speed and the dip's depth.

### The ground

The ground under a foothold is found with a scene ray straight down through it, from `max_step_up_m` above the root to `max_step_down_m` below it (`ground`, with `ground_casts`, the default). A step asks for it every tick it is in the air, since its foothold moves on; a planted foot keeps the height and the slope it landed on, so at most two rays are cast a tick. A foot lands at the ground's height plus the stance's ankle height, and tilts to the ground's slope, at most `ankle_tilt_limit_deg`. A surface steeper than about fifty degrees, a hit on a game object rather than the world (a prop, a held object, a body, a vehicle), or no hit, leaves the foothold at the root's height, which is the ground the proxy stands on. The physical hands' own bodies are not filtered by this, so a hand held under a foot can still be found. The ray ignores the player, as the aim's does.

The rays are cast from the solve gate itself. The engine casts its own ground ray in the same pass, before the gate: [`Character::UpdateGroundInfo`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdateGroundInfo) reaches [`Character::CalculateHeightOverGround`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-CalculateHeightOverGround) whenever a character is off its support, which builds its hit record and filter on the stack and calls the same query ([`PhysicsSystem::CastRay`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/physics::PhysicsSystem##func-CastRay)) on the same layer as the mod's cast does ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md#who-reads-the-pose-before-updatepropeffects)). The pass is queued for the parallel update of every character after the physics step, so such queries are made from the engine's worker threads against a settled world by design. The design's fallback, casting a tick ahead from the locomotion post-hook, is therefore not needed. The first cast of a session logs its thread (`procedural body: the first ground cast from the solve gate`, info level), and a tally of casts, hits, surfaces too steep, hits on objects, errors, and the largest ground offset is logged every ten seconds while casts run (`procedural body: ground casts`).

### The arms

The arms are the controllers' wrist targets ([controllers.md](../input/controllers.md)), solved by HumanIK on the procedural torso after the gate. While the procedural body poses the torso, the hand IK does not queue them at the pass's entry; it keeps its plan for the gate (`hands::ik::queue_or_defer`), and the gate queues it right after writing the pose (`hands::ik::queue_deferred`). The rotation target is a delta from the wrist as it stands before the solve, and only after the write is that the wrist the procedural torso carries, so a target queued at the entry would turn the hand by however far the torso moved it. At the gate:

- The wrist target is kept out of the torso (`keep_out`, on by default; `HANDS OUT OF THE BODY` on the `VR: BODY` page). The torso is two capsules along the written spine: the chest from `Spine2` to the neck (`chest_radius_m`) and the abdomen from the hips to `Spine2` (`abdomen_radius_m`). A target inside either, widened by `hand_margin_m` for the hand's thickness, moves straight out of it, so a hand on the belly rests on it and an arm across the chest goes around it. The recorded target is the moved one, so the render-time late update corrects toward it.
- The pull is zero, and the body IK's shoulder pin does not yield: the torso is the player's, and the solve may not bend it toward a hand. A hand beyond the arm's reach stops at the arm's length.
- The clavicles reach with the hands (`clavicles`, on by default; `SHOULDERS FOLLOW THE HANDS` on the `VR: BODY` page). Before the pose is written, the gate reads the wrist targets the hand IK is about to queue (`hands::ik::deferred_wrist_targets`) and turns each clavicle about its own joint, carrying the arm: up for a hand above the shoulder, by how steep the reach is and how straight the arm, at most `clavicle_elevation_deg`; forward for a hand reaching far ahead or across the body, at most `clavicle_protraction_deg`; and down a little for a hand reaching far below, at most `clavicle_depression_deg`. The forward and downward turns start once the hand is `clavicle_reach_start` of the arm's length from the shoulder joint and are full at its length, so a hand held near the body leaves the shoulder alone and a hand on top of the head lifts it only a little. The turns are scaled by the hand's weight and followed with the time constant `clavicle_follow_s`, so the shoulder moves smoothly when a hand is raised quickly or the tracking jumps. The shoulder joint then sits closer to a far target, so the arm's IK is not asked to stretch past its length. The hand IK measures the arm after the write, so the elbow hint and the reach use the turned shoulder.
- Each driven arm gets an elbow hint (`elbow_hints`, on by default): a weak position target on the elbow (`elbow_hint_reach`, scaled by the procedural pose's weight) at the middle of the shoulder-to-wrist line, out along a pole that points down, out to the arm's side, and a little back, the side and the back being the written chest's so the elbows turn with it, as far as the arm's length bends it, and kept out of the torso the same way. It gives the solve a natural elbow without fixing it.

The first gate that queues the wrist targets logs `hand IK: queued the wrist targets at the solve gate` (info level, with whether a procedural torso was there), and a keep-out push is logged at most every two seconds (`hand IK: kept the wrist target out of the procedural torso`, debug level, with the distance). The hands readout on the Controllers tab shows, per hand, whether the target was queued on the procedural torso, how far it was kept out, and whether an elbow hint was queued. The physical hands' offsets are composed into the wrist before the keep-out, so a hand body pressed against the world and the torso's keep-out both apply.

The gate's torso keep-out (`body::procedural::torso_keep_out`) is also the capsule geometry the physical hands' held-object keep-out queries, carried from Rico's model space into world space by the character's own transform (`TorsoKeepOut::to_world`); see [physical-hands.md](../input/physical-hands.md#the-players-own-body). The two keep-outs share this one source rather than each describing the torso's shape on its own.

### The limbs' twist

A hand turned far about the forearm, or a foot about the shin, would wring the skin at the one joint that turns (candy-wrapping). The skeleton's twist bones carry part of the skin along the limb ([skeleton.md](../../engine/character/skeleton.md): `df_LeftArmRollA01` under `LeftArm` and `LeftForeArmRoll` under `LeftForeArm`, and the right side alike), so the twist is shared out over them (`twist_bones`, on by default; `SPREAD THE LIMB TWIST` on the `VR: BODY` page). `body::procedural::twist` runs at the end of the pose-finalization pass, in the post-call `UpdatePropEffects` detour after the head override and the finger curls, and writes the twist bones with `AnimationController::SetJoint` over the solved pose. The twist bones are found by name, once per skeleton: any child of a limb segment (the upper arm, the forearm, the thigh, the shin) whose name has `roll` in it, so if Rico's skeleton has thigh or shin twist bones they are used too; the log lists them (`procedural body: the twist bones the limbs' twist is shared over`, info level).

On the forearm and the shin, the twist of the hand or the foot against the segment, about its length and against the reference pose, is shared out by where each twist bone sits along the segment: a bone most of the way to the wrist takes most of it. On the upper arm and the thigh, the segment's own roll against the clavicle or the pelvis is held back near the shoulder or the hip: a bone a fifth of the way down turns back by four fifths of it. The arms' twist bones take this by the torso's weight and the legs' by the legs', so the animation's own twist bones come back as the body hands back. Whether the engine's HumanIK sets the twist bones itself during its solve is not established; this write comes after the solve either way, and replaces whatever it left. The render-time late update corrects the hand after this, so the twist bones lag a hand correction by up to a tick.

The `VR: STATUS` page's `TWIST BONES` row shows how many twist bones were found on how many segments and the largest twist they carried on the last pass; `NONE FOUND` fails it.

### Diagnostics

The Controllers tab's "Procedural body" section shows the mode, the weight, whether the body owns the pose, a rigging error, the pass-through's read-back error, how far the hips sit below the animation's and the neck from its target, whether a foot is stepping, the body's speed and the walk's phase, the crouch's depth, the lower body's pose with its inputs and weights, the reach drop, whether the stance is measured, the last tick's ground casts, each clavicle's turn up and forward, and the legs' weight in the air, the root's vertical speed, and the landing dip. While walking, `procedural body: walking` is logged every two seconds at debug level with the speed, the crouch's depth, the stride, the duty factor, the lift, the phase, the reach drop, and the neck's error.
