# Wingsuit and parachute

The arms and hands in flight: the wingsuit's arm steering and barrel roll, the stall flip and the head's look-steer removed, the parachute's handles, and the ripcord. Opening, braking, and closing the wingsuit by pose are [wingsuit-gestures.md](wingsuit-gestures.md), and the flight buttons are [bindings.md, "Wingsuit"](bindings.md#wingsuit). The code is `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/wingsuit/` and `parachute/`, tuned by `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/tuning/vehicles.toml`, with the detours in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/hooks/input/`. The settings are under `controllers.vehicles`, and the pause menu's `VR: CONTROLS` page has the arm steering's switch, its pitch measure and inversion, and the handles' switch. Everything acts only under the controller scheme, and the Air feature (under Movement/vehicles in the debug UI) reads it out.

## Wingsuit arm steering

`controllers.vehicles.arm_steering.wingsuit` (on), in `wingsuit/arm_steering.rs`. With the hands further apart than the tuning's `arm_steering.min_spread` of the arm span, the difference in their heights banks, past `arm_steering.deadzone_m` and fully at `arm_steering.full_bank_m`, and `arm_steering.pitch_by` chooses what pitches:

- `WristTilt` (the default): both wrists' roll about the fingers' axis, from the back of the hand straight up. Tilting the leading edges (the thumb side of a flat palm) down dives and up climbs, like a wing's angle of attack, past `arm_steering.tilt_deadzone_deg` and fully at `full_tilt_deg`, measured from the setting `tilt_neutral_deg`. The roll is taken about the fingers, so raising, lowering, or sweeping an arm reads nothing and the pitch is independent of the bank. Moving both arms up or down, the alternative, is slower than turning the wrists and moves the heights the bank also reads.
- `Height`: both hands above or below the shoulders, fully at `arm_steering.full_pitch_m`.
- `Sweep`: the hands' midpoint ahead of or behind the shoulders', fully at `arm_steering.full_sweep_m`.

Everything is measured in the cockpit frame, with forward the horizontal normal to the line from the left hand to the right, so a player who turned since the last recenter still pitches by their own forward. The shoulders are placed below the tracked head by the body dimensions. The bank stays the height difference in every measure: a bank read from a difference in tilt would turn the suit whenever one wrist rolls more than the other while the player means only to pitch. `invert_pitch` reverses the pitch. Once steering, the arms keep steering until they come in past `arm_steering.spread_hysteresis` inside the spread. While the grapple's button is held the arms keep the stick vector they had when it was pressed (`arm_steering::hold_while_aiming`), because the grapple aims along the off-hand and pointing it at a target narrows or banks the spread. Each tick's pose writes that tick's stick, added to the move stick and written to the move half-axes the wingsuit task reads. The arms steer whatever the hands hold ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)). With the overlay on, the hands are ringed in the wingsuit, lit with a line between them while they steer. The wingsuit's rendered arms stay yielded to the animation.

## The barrel roll

`controllers.vehicles.wingsuit_evade.enabled` (on), in `wingsuit/evade/`. The boost wingsuit's evade is a barrel roll whose clip rolls the body a full turn and carries it sideways ([wingsuit.md#the-evade](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md#the-evade)); it starts when `WINGSUIT_EVADE` is clicked with the move stick out of its rest, and the stick picks the way it rolls. The arms throw it: with the arms spread at least `wingsuit_evade.min_separation` of the span, the bank of the line between the hands reaching `throw_bank_deg` either way after swinging `throw_sweep_deg` toward that side within `throw_window_s`. The next throw needs the arms back within `rearm_bank_deg` and `cooldown_s` passed, so one throw is one roll. A throw clicks the action and, for `direction_hold_s`, writes the move stick toward the lower hand's side, diagonally while the arms pitch past `diagonal_pitch`, so the game picks the roll the throw meant. No throw is taken while the air brake is held, while the grapple's aim holds the arms, or without the boost wingsuit in flight ([`Character::HasBoostWingsuit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HasBoostWingsuit)). The game still decides whether the roll plays. The view rolls with the body, as it banks with it. Each throw, and each throw turned away with its reason, is logged as `wingsuit evade: ...`.

With the switch off, no throw is taken, and while the headset drives the head pose the local player's barrel roll act ([`ACT_BOOST_WINGSUIT_BARREL_ROLL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable)), which the air physics queues straight on the animated model, is dropped at [`AnimatedModel::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-QueueAct) through the act arbiter (`controllers::acts`).

## The stall flip

Slowing with the nose raised makes the wingsuit's blend state play a stall clip, and a full loop that recovers from it, over a physics body that never loops ([wingsuit.md#the-stall-and-flip](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md#the-stall-and-flip)), so the body turns a circle the player never gestured. While the head pose comes from the headset, the blend state's one write of its stalling flag ([`WingsuitBlendState_StallFlagSet`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::WingsuitBlendState_StallFlagSet)) is patched so it never stalls (`hooks::input::wingsuit`): neither clip plays, the air brake and the close stay available, and a player who pitches up loses lift and drops. The patch is a byte of the game's code, applied and reverted through the hooks' patcher as the head pose's source changes, so it covers every character in a wingsuit, of which only the player flies one.

## The head look-steer

The air movement core adds a camera look-steer to the stick's steering, computed by its own side-effect-free function, [`NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/input::parachute##func-NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering), so in the headset looking around would steer. A post-call detour zeroes its output (`wingsuit/look_steer.rs`) while the mode detector reads the wingsuit or the parachute, for a head-decoupled local player; the same core serves the falls, which the gate leaves alone. The parachute's own look-steer has its own detour (`hooks::input::parachute`). The head steers neither.

## Parachute handles

`controllers.vehicles.parachute_handles.enabled` (on), in `parachute/handles.rs`. The hands are not on the handles until the player takes them. One handle hangs a little above, ahead of, and outside each shoulder, where the game's parachute rig places it: the rig's sync reads each handle's link before placing the handle prop, and a scoped `GetJoint` detour copies those reads (`parachute::rig`), composed through the parachute's transform. A handle above eye level would be out of sight. Each handle is a grip target for its own side's hand alone (`parachute_handles.radius_m`), with a wider reach margin (`parachute_handles.reach_m`) taken when nothing else claims the press ([grip.md, "Resolving a hand"](grip.md#resolving-a-hand)); the two volumes overlap at the usual spacing, which is harmless, since a hand never takes the other side's handle. A press right at a shoulder socket still draws the weapon there, because the nearer target wins. A handle is held only while the grip is, however far the hand goes, so a natural pull down and back toward the body keeps it, and a held handle is offered to its hand as held. A hand holding something takes no handle. Each take and let-go is logged (`parachute: took a handle`, `parachute: let go of a handle`).

A held handle's pull is how far its hand has come down from where it took it, past `parachute_handles.deadzone_m`, full at `parachute_handles.full_pull_m`; raising or reaching the hand forward pushes it the same way. The difference of the pulls banks toward the side pulled further; the share both are pulled past `parachute_handles.brake_threshold` holds the stick back, which the parachute reads as its slow-down ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The stick's vertical axis"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-sticks-vertical-axis)); and the share both are pushed pushes the stick forward, its dive. So both toggles pulled brake a canopy, and both front risers pulled down steepen its glide. The vector is added to the move stick, so the game's own parachute physics flies the canopy and the stick still steers alongside. The holding hand is drawn on its handle.

The game draws its handle props ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The handle props"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-handle-props)) in Rico's hands wherever the hands are, so a hand that has not taken its handle would be drawn gripping one. The rig's sync puts each handle no hand holds back on its link before the callback ends and leaves a held one in the hand. The canopy's targets from the native rig are [canopy-target-contract.md](canopy-target-contract.md).

## The ripcord

`parachute/ripcord.rs`, with the input tick's half in `ripcord_tick.rs`. While falling or in the wingsuit, a ripcord at the back of the hip on the dominant side opens the parachute. It sits on the player's tracked body, not Rico's, because in a fall and the suit Rico flies prone under the view and his hip is nowhere the hand can reach. The hip is estimated below the shoulder joints from the body dimensions (`HIP_HEIGHT_SHARE` of body-rig), which holds seated as well as standing, and the ripcord is offset from it along the torso's heading by the tuning's `ripcord.from_hip_m` (mirrored for a left-handed player), behind the holster, with `ripcord.radius_m` and a reach margin `ripcord.reach_m`. Only the dominant hand takes it, with a grip press, held while the grip is. A hold that carries the hand `ripcord.pull_m` from where it took it, in any direction, pulls once: the input tick clicks `OPEN_PARACHUTE`, the canopy button's action, and pulses the hand. The click goes in before the wingsuit gestures commit, so it suppresses a suit request on the same tick. Letting go and taking it again pulls again; the game decides whether the canopy opens. Each take, pull, and let-go is logged (`ripcord: ...`), and the controls summary lists it on the dominant grip while falling and in the suit.

## Unverified

Unchecked in the headset: the arm steering's signs (a lowered right hand banks right, leading edges tilted down dive) and tuning, and that the grip offset gives a wrist frame whose back-of-hand axis is straight up with the palms flat and spread; whether the slowdown before a stall still feels wrong without the loop; the handles' place by the shoulders standing and seated, their sizes, the full pull, the deadzone, and the brake threshold; and how strongly the stick-back slow-down brakes the canopy.
