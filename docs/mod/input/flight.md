# Wingsuit and parachute

The arms fly the wingsuit and parachute: they steer the suit, throw its barrel roll, open, brake, and close it by pose, take the parachute's handles, and pull the ripcord. The flight buttons are in [bindings.md, "Wingsuit"](bindings.md#wingsuit), and the engine side is [wingsuit.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md). The code is `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/wingsuit/` and `parachute/`, with the detours in `crates/jc3vr-body/src/hooks/input/`, tuned by `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/tuning/vehicles.toml`. Everything acts only under the controller scheme, and the debug UI's Air feature reads it out.

## Wingsuit arm steering

`wingsuit/arm_steering.rs`. With the hands spread wide enough, the difference in their heights banks, and `arm_steering.pitch_by` chooses what pitches: `WristTilt` (the default) reads both wrists' roll about the fingers, leading edges down to dive and up to climb like a wing's angle of attack; `Height` and `Sweep` read the hands above or ahead of the shoulders. The bank is never read from differing wrist tilt, which would turn the suit whenever one wrist rolls more than the other. Forward is the normal to the line between the hands, so a player who turned pitches by their own forward. While the grapple's button is held the arms keep their stick (`arm_steering::hold_while_aiming`), because aiming along the off-hand disturbs the spread. The result is added to the move stick, whatever the hands hold ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)).

## The barrel roll

`controllers.vehicles.wingsuit_evade.enabled`, in `wingsuit/evade/`. The boost wingsuit's evade rolls the body a full turn sideways ([wingsuit.md, "The evade"](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md#the-evade)) when `WINGSUIT_EVADE` is clicked with the move stick out of rest, and the stick picks the side. The arms throw it: with them spread, the line between the hands swings and banks past a threshold within a short window. A throw clicks the action and holds the move stick toward the lower hand for a moment, and the arms must come back level before the next. No throw is taken while braking, while the grapple holds the arms, or without the boost wingsuit ([`Character::HasBoostWingsuit`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-HasBoostWingsuit)). With the switch off, the barrel roll act ([`ACT_BOOST_WINGSUIT_BARREL_ROLL`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::symbol_table::EventIdSymbolTable)), which the air physics queues straight on the model, is dropped at [`AnimatedModel::QueueAct`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimatedModel##func-QueueAct) through the act arbiter (`controllers::acts`).

## The stall flip

Slowing nose-up makes the wingsuit's blend state play a stall clip and a recovering loop over a physics body that never loops ([wingsuit.md, "The stall and flip"](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md#the-stall-and-flip)), turning the body through a circle the player never gestured. While the headset drives the head pose, the blend state's one write of its stalling flag ([`WingsuitBlendState_StallFlagSet`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::WingsuitBlendState_StallFlagSet)) is patched out (`hooks::input::wingsuit`): neither clip plays, the brake and close stay available, and a suit pitched up simply loses lift.

The air movement core also adds a camera look-steer through its side-effect-free [`NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/module/input::parachute##func-NAirMovement_UpdateStdLookSteering), so looking around would steer. A post-call detour zeroes it in the wingsuit or parachute (`wingsuit/look_steer.rs`), and the parachute's own look-steer has another (`hooks::input::parachute`).

## Opening and closing

The pose recognizer, `wingsuit/gestures/`, returns commands without writing actions; the bindings' flight state (`crates/jc3vr-gameplay/src/controllers/bindings/flight/`) feeds it and turns its commands into actions. Its thresholds are the `wingsuit_gestures` tuning, in shares of the calibrated arm span. It reads one head-and-controller packet (`crates/jc3vr-view/src/vr/input/flight_pose.rs`) from a single input sample; lost tracking, recentering, or a calibration change resets the dwells. The arms are measured against the facing from the line between the wrists, because a flier looks around while the arms stay spread.

A stable flight pose held for `flight_dwell_s` deploys the suit. In a fall or on a yielding grapple line that pose is the arms spread, however the palms turn; under a canopy it also needs flat palms, since arms out on the toggles are the canopy's own pose. A fall out of the suit, or a canopy the player opened, needs a neutral pose first, so the arms that just closed the suit or opened the canopy do not reopen it. In the suit, a flat-palmed flight pose arms the closure and a deliberate withdrawal of both arms closes it.

The recognizer watches from the moment Rico leaves the ground ([locomotion.md](../body/locomotion.md)), not only once the mode detection reports a fall, because off a ledge the game plays its jump for a while first and its jump states already accept the suit's opening once descending and high enough ([wingsuit.md, "Opening from the air"](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md#opening-from-the-air)). A refused request is retried after `deploy_retry_s`, longer than the suit's entry takes to register, so a repeat never lands in the suit, where the same action closes it.

A pending gesture is dropped if a hand claims something that tick (a grip, a slot draw, the grapple, or the ripcord). Lowered arms at the hip sockets claim nothing unless the grip squeezes, so the neutral and closure poses do not cancel themselves.

## Braking and landing

The game lands the suit through its close act, which a held air brake queues every tick ([wingsuit.md, "The landing"](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit.md#the-landing)): slow and low enough, it becomes a slide onto the feet; higher up, it closes a slowed suit into a fall. So the brake is the landing, held in the last few metres; both need the gear mods `upgrade_wingsuit_air_brake` and `upgrade_wingsuit_cancel`. The brake gesture is wide arms with both palms rolled toward the wind, held for `brake_dwell_s`, and it latches until an arm comes in or both palms return flat. It engages only after a releasing pose (arms in or palms flat) has been seen in the suit, because a suit opened with the palms already to the wind would otherwise brake as it opens and close straight back into a fall. Wrists rolled to the wind also read as a full climb, so the brake flares the suit down to landing speed.

## Take-off from the ground

The boost wingsuit's take-off from the ground launches along the character's facing, but in VR the body faces where the grounded rig puts it. A take-off is a movement action, so it turns the body (`wingsuit/take_off.rs`): once `OPEN_WINGSUIT` has been held on the ground for `take_off.turn_after_s`, the head's heading becomes the body's follow target ([rig.md](../body/rig.md)), and the game's own turn brings the body under the view before the wind-up stops it. A short tap turns nothing.

## Parachute handles

`controllers.vehicles.parachute_handles.enabled`, in `parachute/handles.rs`. One handle hangs above, ahead of, and outside each shoulder, where the game's rig places the handle props, as a grip target for its own side's hand with a wider reach when nothing else claims the press ([grip.md, "Resolving a hand"](grip.md#resolving-a-hand)). A handle is held while the grip is, however far the hand goes. A held handle's pull is how far its hand has come down: the difference of the pulls banks, both pulled past `parachute_handles.brake_threshold` holds the stick back, which the parachute reads as its slow-down ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The stick's vertical axis"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-sticks-vertical-axis)), and both pushed up dives. The vector is added to the move stick, so the game's physics flies the canopy.

The targets come from reads the engine already makes in [`NParachuteObjectControl_SyncBones`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::parachute::NParachuteObjectControl_SyncBones) (`parachute/rig/mod.rs`): a scoped detour on [`AnimationController::GetBoneMatrix`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-GetBoneMatrix) marks the rig's re-derivation, and one on [`AnimationController::GetJoint`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::AnimationController##func-GetJoint) copies the handle-placement reads that follow, composed through the parachute's [`GameObject::GetTransform`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/game::GameObject##vfunc-GetTransform). A sample holds copies, not engine borrows, and a missing or stale one supplies no target. The game draws both handle props in Rico's hands wherever they are ([parachute-locomotion.md, "The handle props"](../../engine/gameplay/parachute-locomotion.md#the-handle-props)), so for a side no hand holds, the sync detour's post-call puts the copied joints back and the handle on its link.

## The ripcord

`parachute/ripcord.rs`. While falling or in the suit, a ripcord at the back of the dominant hip opens the parachute. It sits on the player's tracked body, estimated below the shoulders, not on Rico, who flies prone under the view. The dominant hand takes it, and moving `ripcord.pull_m` from the grab clicks `OPEN_PARACHUTE` once, before the wingsuit gestures commit, so it suppresses a suit request on the same tick.
