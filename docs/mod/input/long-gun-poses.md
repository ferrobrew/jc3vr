# Long-gun poses

Two stages of the hand composition move a held long gun away from where the hands hold it: aiming down the sights lifts it to the eye, and the wall push keeps it out of walls. Both act on the two-handed and special slots' guns in either hand, measured on the input tick with the foregrip's geometry, and both move the gun as one rigid body that the holding hand, and the hand on the foregrip, ride. The rest of the weapon handling is in [physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md). The numbers are the handling tuning (`crates/jc3vr-weapons/tuning/handling.toml`, [tuning.md](../tuning.md)).

## Aiming down the sights

The gun aim shoots along each gun's own sight line ([hands-and-aiming.md, "Gun aim"](hands-and-aiming.md#gun-aim)), so a gun is aimed by its sights already. A long gun raised to the eye is lined up with it so the player can look down those sights (`weapons::ads`, tuned by the handling tuning's `ads` section). It acts on the two-handed and special slots' guns, whose sight line in the holding wrist's frame is measured on the input tick with the foregrip's geometry: the sight line (with the weapon's sight correction) relative to the wrist target its last placement followed. There is one aiming eye, so with a long gun in each hand the one lined up is the one raised to it: the gun whose sights (or scope's weld) take the most hold of the eye, then whose stock takes the most hold of the shoulder, then whose sight line passes nearest the eye. The other hand's gun stays where its hand holds it.

The pose is a stage of the hand composition on the cockpit-frame hands, after the two-handed pose and the weight and before the recoil, so the drawn gun, the IK targets, the late update, the sight line the gun aim casts, and the scope's lens all see the gun where the eye has it. The gun moves as one rigid body; the holding hand rides it, and the hand on the foregrip with it. Nothing turns the gun toward a target. Three steps each fade in as their distance closes from an engage distance to a lock distance:

1. The stock. The butt is `ads.butt_behind_grip_m` behind the holding grip along the sight line, and the shoulder pocket is `ads.pocket_offset_m` from the holding side's shoulder in the torso's frame. Within `ads.shoulder_engage_m` of the pocket the gun turns about its front hand (the hand on the foregrip, or the holding hand without one) so the butt lies on the line to the pocket: the front hand sets where the gun points and the shoulder holds its back.
2. The sights. The aiming eye is the eyes' midpoint moved `ads.half_ipd_m` to the holding side. With the eye behind the grip by up to `ads.max_eye_behind_m`, the head looking within `ads.max_view_angle_deg` of the sight line, and the eye within `ads.engage_m` of the line, the gun turns about a point `ads.pivot_ahead_m` ahead of the grip, about where the front sight is, so the line passes through the eye. The gates fade rather than step (`ads.eye_behind_fade_m`, `ads.full_view_angle_deg`), so a head that comes to look along a gun whose line already runs through the eye eases the sights in. The front sight stays where the hands hold it, so the shot lands where the player sees the sights point.
3. A scope's cheek weld, for a scoped weapon, in place of the second step: the gun moves across its axis, keeping its direction, so the eye sits on the axis and the lens's view centres (`ads.weld_engage_m`, `ads.weld_lock_m`).

The solved move is applied through an ease (`ads::solve::ease`, `ads.ease_s`), kept in the holding wrist's frame so it travels with the gun. Inputs that change in a step (the front hand coming onto or off the foregrip, the shoulder estimate falling back to the arms' own shoulders, the raised gun changing hands) therefore glide rather than turn the gun at once. A gun that is not raised eases back to where its hands hold it, and a gun just taken starts there. The Status page's `Down the sights` row shows the mode, the hand, and how far the eye is off the line.

## Guns against walls

A gun that is a body joined to the hand bodies stops at walls along its whole length ([physical-guns.md](physical-guns.md)). A gun with no body (its hand's body parked, or a heavy weapon whose own body is in the world) would pass through a wall, since the weapon's bodies are out of the world in the wielding state, so a held long gun is pushed out of walls by a ray probe (`weapons::collide`, tuned by the handling tuning's `collide` section). The push is a stage of the hand composition after the sights and the recoil; for a hand whose gun is a body, the composition restores the sighted pose afterwards (`stages::keep_physical`), so the push moves only a kinematic gun hand. It acts on the two-handed and special slots' guns in either hand, each with its own probe, wall, and push.

The probe runs from the holding grip along the barrel to `collide.tip_margin_m` past the muzzle (the first gun's current fire point), cast each aim update (the simulation phase outside the physics step) with the player aim's own filter, which passes through the player, the player's weapons, foliage, fences, and glass. It is cast along the gun's pose before any push, so the push never moves where the wall is looked for. The wall it meets is a plane (the hit point and its normal, turned to face the grip), kept for `collide.keep_s` past the last hit so a probe grazing an edge does not let the gun bounce in and out. Each hand pose then pushes the gun out of the plane:

1. it turns about the holding grip, away from the wall, by the least angle that puts the tip on the plane, up to `collide.max_turn_deg`, so a gun pushed into a wall at a slant slides along it;
2. what is left is pulled back along the gun into the hands, up to `collide.max_retract_m`.

The holding hand rides the gun, and the hand on its foregrip with it, so both hands stay on the stopped gun while the player's hands go on into the wall. A gun whose grip is itself through the wall is not pushed. Because the proxy is a ray, the gun is stopped by what it points into, not by what its sides brush, and it does not push loose objects. The Status page's `Gun against walls` row shows the contact and the push.

## Unverified

- Whether the measured sight line is right for every long gun (one without a trusted sight correction lines up its bore rather than its sights), and whether the stock's offsets put it on the shoulder the player feels.
- Whether one ray along the barrel keeps a rifle out of walls at the angles players hold one (a gun brushing a wall side-on is not stopped).
