# Wingsuit pose controls

`controllers/vehicles/wingsuit_gestures/` recognizes deployment, braking, and closure from one coherent head-and-controller observation. It does not infer a pose by combining independently updated globals. `vr/input/flight_pose.rs` publishes a complete packet only when the head and controllers belong to the same input sampling operation. Replay, unavailable tracking, origin changes, recentering, and runtime replacement invalidate live packet use.

The recognizer requires fresh observations. A fall's start is a fresh start: a stable flight pose held for the flight dwell requests deployment, whether the arms were spread before the player stepped off or are still spread under a canopy they cut. Only a fall out of the suit needs a neutral (both arms lowered or drawn in) before the flight pose, so arms spread as the suit closes do not open it again. In the suit, a stable flight pose arms it, the suit's entry being the fresh start. Wide arms with both wrists rolled to face the wind request braking. Deliberate bilateral withdrawal can request closure only after the recognizer arms. One withdrawn arm, tracking loss, menu capture, or an interaction claim does not count as a closure gesture. Button deployment with lowered arms does not immediately close the suit.

The arm measures are taken against the body's facing, read from the line between the wrists, because a flier's head turns away from the arms. The palm is each wrist's roll about the fingers, the measure the arm steering's wrist tilt reads, so raising or sweeping the arms does not change it. [controllers.md, "Wingsuit gestures"](controllers.md) has the thresholds, the fallbacks, and the logging.

## Interpretation and finalization

The binding-owned `FlightState` retains the grip offset and body dimensions used to interpret observations. Any changed component resets arming, dwell, braking, and pitch reservation without rewinding the observation watermark. Repeating an old packet after calibration cannot contribute new dwell or reactivate held output. A fresh flight pose can arm the recognizer again in the suit, or deploy it while falling; a fall keeps its start through a suspension, so a claim or a menu in the middle of a fall does not ask for a neutral afterwards.

The recognizer watches from the moment Rico leaves the ground on foot, as the view anchor reads it (from the jump's grace on; [locomotion.md](../body/locomotion.md) has the grace), not only once the mode detection reports a fall. Off a ledge the game plays its jump for about a second before it reports one, and its jump states take the suit's opening as its falls do, once Rico is descending and high enough over the ground ([engine: wingsuit-opening.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-opening.md)): so the flight pose's dwell is met during the jump and the deploy goes in there. A request the game refuses (still rising from a jump, or too near the ground) is asked again after `DEPLOY_RETRY_S` in `wingsuit_gestures` while the pose is held, which is longer than the suit's entry takes to reach the mode detection, so a repeat never lands in the suit, where the same action closes it.

Finalization checks packet currency, availability, and current per-hand interaction claims again after other handlers run. A contextual canopy request suppresses a pending suit contribution. Newly acquired grip, trigger, mechanical, or grapple ownership also cancels the contribution. Blocked weapon actions do not: the game blocks them around a draw or a holster and through some of a fall's start, and the grip and slot claims already cover a draw and a holster. A hand has the grapple while the grapple button is held, while its line is attached, or during a traversal; tethers left in the world claim neither hand, so a fall after tethering a car still opens the suit. A hand claims a slot on the body only while its grip is squeezed within the slot's reach, so the lowered arms of the neutral and the closure, which bring the hands to the hip sockets, do not stand the gestures down; the grip press that starts a draw does. Braking reserves arm-pitch steering only while the recognizer has an active eligible brake command. Stick steering remains available.

## Landing

The game lands the wingsuit through its close act, which a held air brake queues on every tick ([engine: wingsuit-landing.md](../../engine/gameplay/wingsuit-landing.md)). Once the suit is slow enough and low enough, the act turns into a slide onto the feet; faster, it does nothing, and the glide that meets the ground ends in a ragdoll. So the brake is the landing: wide arms with both palms to the wind, held as the suit comes in low over level ground, slow it until the game closes the suit into the slide. The `air_brake` action holds the same pair of actions from any analog input a custom layout binds it to; it ships unbound, since it is a float and the free buttons in the suit are clicks.

The brake weakens while the suit turns: the game's brake gives up part of its drag and lift for the steering's sideways input, and the arm steering's bank is that input. Level wings brake hardest. The stall flip's removal ([controllers.md, "The wingsuit's stall flip"](controllers.md)) keeps the brake and the close act open as the suit slows with its nose up, where the game's own stall would close both until it recovered. Held higher up, the same brake closes the suit into a fall once it is slow, so the landing's brake belongs in the last few metres. Both the brake and the landing need the game's gear mods for them (`upgrade_wingsuit_air_brake` and `upgrade_wingsuit_cancel`); without the second, the close act never fires and the suit stays open until it meets the ground.

The arm-pitch reservation while braking keeps the brake's rolled wrists from also reading as a full climb. A flare (pitching up while braking) stays on the stick.

In the seated session the brake gestures never armed, so none of the three flights braked. The arms show what the player tried: in the first flight both palms rolled to the wind for about a second, two seconds before the ground, then rolled back past flat; in the third they rolled on past palm-forward toward palm-up for two seconds before the ground, past the brake's band and into the wrap of the arm steering's wrist tilt. The recording does not hold the suit's speed or height, so how far short of the landing's gates the flights fell is not measured.

## The ripcord

While falling or in the suit, a ripcord at the back of the hip on the dominant side opens the parachute: grip it and pull it out, the way a parachute handle is taken ([controllers.md, "Ripcord"](controllers.md)). The pull clicks the canopy action, the one the right lower face (A) drives, before the gestures commit, so a pull cancels a pending suit request as the button does.

## Take-off from the ground

The boost wingsuit's take-off from the ground (the use button held on foot, the rule states `S_JC_WINGSUIT_BOOST_TRANSITION_FROM_GROUND_INTO` and then `..._FROM_GROUND`) launches the suit along the character's facing. In VR the body faces where the grounded rig puts it under the view, not where the head looks, so the suit could leave off to one side of the player's gaze. A take-off is a movement action, so it turns the body (`controllers/vehicles/take_off.rs`): the head's heading relative to the view, from the cockpit pose, stands in for the torso's as the body's follow target ([rig.md](../body/rig.md)), so the game's own turn brings the body around under the view, and the view does not move. The game turns the body only while its movement task runs, and the wind-up stops it, so the turn starts with the hold that leads to it: once the use button (read from its `OPEN_WINGSUIT` action, one of those the gamepad's button carries) has been held on the ground for `TURN_AFTER_S` (0.2 s), the target tracks the head's heading, and the body has the rest of the hold, about a second, to come around. On entering the take-off the heading is held where it was until the take-off ends; letting go of the button before the wind-up lets the target go, and a tap to pick something up turns nothing. A hold that enters a vehicle's gunner seat also turns the body toward the gaze until the entry starts. The log has `the use button held on the ground turns the body to the head's heading` when the turn starts and `the take-off from the ground holds the body on the head's heading` on entering the take-off, each with the heading and whether the body was following. The turn needs the body to be following, which it does on foot with the follow setting on. It is on by default (`vehicles.take_off_to_head`, in the debug UI's Vehicles feature). The launch's climb angle is left to the game.

## Native actions and validation

The converter emits the narrow suit deployment or closure action and the native held airbrake pair. Shared binding integration supplies these contributions alongside the fixed physical-button map; the recognizer does not fabricate raw trigger fire. Engine state and animation permissions still determine whether a native request succeeds.

Pure tests cover independent shoulder and palm geometry, scale, observation identity, dwell boundaries, calibration edits, interaction suspension, late canopy priority, and exact converter output, and poses sampled from a seated player's flights check the thresholds against arms that flew. These tests do not establish physical controller calibration, native acceptance during every transition, or headset comfort. Live flight validation remains required.

## Headset checklist

1. Tether two objects, walk off a ledge, and spread the arms: the suit opens, and the log does not say "the left hand has the grapple".
2. Hold the grapple button while falling: the gestures stand down until it is let go.
3. With the boost suit, stand at the foot of some stairs, look up them with the body turned 20° or more away, and hold the use button to take off: the body turns to the gaze during the hold, before the wind-up, without the view moving, and the suit launches where you look. The log has the hold's line and then the take-off's, both with `following=true`. Tap the use button to pick something up: the body does not turn.
4. Fall with the arms lowered by the sides, grips open, then spread them: the suit opens, and the log does not alternate between "waiting for both arms lowered" and "squeezes its grip at a slot on the body". Draw a pistol from the hip while falling and spread the arms with it: the suit opens without lowering the arms again, and the log has no "weapon actions are blocked".
5. Spread the arms on a cliff top and step or jump off: the suit opens during the game's jump, within about half a second of leaving the edge, not after it reports the fall.
6. Under a canopy, spread the arms and cut it: the suit opens about 0.2 s after the cut.
7. In the suit, keep the arms spread and close it with the button: it stays closed until both arms are lowered and spread again. Hop on flat ground with the arms spread: nothing opens, and the log shows the recognizer waking for the hop and going idle again.
