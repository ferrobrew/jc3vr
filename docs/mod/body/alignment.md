# Body alignment

The aim is the Boneworks topology from [head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md#boneworks--bonelab-alignment): the player's body is ground truth, inferred from the head and hands, and Rico's body is posed to match it as far as his skeleton and the game allow. Before this work Rico's head followed the headset and his hands followed the controllers, but his facing followed only the turn input, so a player who turned in the room left Rico's chest, hips, and feet pointing where they were, with the head twisted on top. This doc covers the torso estimate, the body turning under the view, the chest and pelvis turn, the holsters on Rico's skeleton, and the readout that measures what is left. The code is in `payload/src/controllers/body/torso.rs`, `payload/src/controllers/body/alignment/`, and `payload/src/headpose/follow.rs`, and the settings are `controllers.body.alignment` and `controllers.weapons.holsters.anchor`.

## The torso estimate

Nothing tracks the torso, so its heading and joints are inferred once per rendered VR frame from the cockpit-frame head pose and the raw controller grips (`controllers::body::torso`), in the player's own metres. The heading starts from the head's: the forward axis on the ground, blended with the up axis as the head pitches, so that looking straight down at the belt still has a heading. The torso holds its heading while the head turns inside a deadzone (`deadzone_deg`, 25° by default), follows by the excess past it, eased over `follow_s`, and is dragged along at once past the neck limit (`neck_limit_deg`, 65°). Looking around therefore does not spin the body, and turning the whole body brings it round.

Two other cues pull the heading toward theirs, each by a weight scaled by how far it can be trusted. With both hands tracked and at least `hands_min_spread_m` apart, the normal to the line between them, nudged toward their midpoint's direction from the neck, says which way the chest faces; a line more than about a right angle from the held heading (crossed hands, hands behind the back) is ignored, and hands behind the body count for half. While the head moves forward along its own heading at a walking pace, the walk's direction counts too; stepping back or sideways does not.

The joints hang from a neck pivot set below and behind the eyes in the head's frame, so nodding moves the eyes and not the shoulders. The shoulder joints sit below the pivot by the body dimensions, along the torso's heading, and match the estimate the hand remap has always used for an upright head ([controllers.md, "Body dimensions"](../input/controllers.md#body-dimensions)). The pelvis sits a spine's length below the shoulders: it follows the shoulders horizontally with a time constant (`pelvis_follow_s`), so a quick lean tilts the spine and a step carries the pelvis along, and the lean is bounded by `max_lean_deg`. The hip joints straddle the pelvis at a generic fraction of the shoulder width.

A recenter turns the estimate with the cockpit frame, so the torso stays where it is in the room.

## The body turning under the view

The view composes the head onto Rico's body frame, so turning Rico turns the view ([head-and-body.md, "HMD pose to camera"](head-and-body.md#hmd-pose-to-camera)). That is why the turn input was the only thing allowed to turn him, and why the game's turn toward the aim is suppressed in VR. With `root_follow` on, the view frame becomes the body frame turned back by the body's heading relative to the view, so the body can turn without the view following it (`headpose::follow`).

While the body follows (on foot, in gameplay, under the headset, with the estimate on), the view's heading is the turn accumulator's, which only the stick, the mouse, or a snap step moves, kept as a pair spaced one input tick apart. The offset between the body and the view is read on each side of the pair from the same tick-spaced body transforms ([`Character::m_WorldMatrixT0`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT0) and [`Character::m_WorldMatrixT1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_WorldMatrixT1)) that the view interpolates between, so the composed view heading is exactly the accumulator's whatever the body does: the game's own rotations, the orientation executor's lag, or the turn toward the torso. The body is steered, through the same face-dir target the turn input uses ([`NStateTask_LocoUtil::EvaluateCharacterOrientation`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::locomotion::NStateTask_LocoUtil##func-EvaluateCharacterOrientation)), to the view's heading plus the torso's heading in the cockpit frame, held within `follow_deadband_deg` of it. The body therefore turns only because the player's torso did, and the view turns only because the player pushed the turn input, which is the rule in [head-and-body.md](head-and-body.md#yaw-coupling-schemes) for scheme B and the rule that the player is never reoriented without a movement action.

Two side effects follow from the view no longer riding the executor. A snap turn snaps the view at once instead of sweeping it at the executor's rate, and a smooth turn turns the view at the turn input's rate rather than the slower of that and the executor's step. The lead clamp that kept the accumulator near a lagging body is skipped while following, because a lagging body no longer drags the view.

When the body stops following (a jump, a vehicle, the parachute, swimming, or the setting turned off), the offset freezes and the view rides the body again, turned by a constant, with the body steered to the accumulator plus the frozen offset so nothing moves. At the top of the next frame the frozen offset is folded into the cockpit baseline: the baseline's yaw turns by it, the offset goes to zero, and the cockpit shift and the torso estimate turn with the frame, all before the frame locates the views, so the composed view is unchanged. Off foot the cockpit frame's forward is then the body's again, which the vehicles' physical controls, the driving view, and the flight steering all assume. The fold waits while the grapple filter holds the view or the swim levelling drops the body's tilt, since both compose with the turned frame. A recenter clears the offset outright, because it re-bases the cockpit to the head and turns the view anyway.

Everything that composes the cockpit into the world already goes through the view frame, so the hands, the aim, and the head pose follow without change. The consumers that relate the cockpit to Rico's own body take the offset back out: the head's body-relative rotation (the head-bone override, the neck twist, and the body IK's head rotation), and the camera's tracked eye arm, which is held at rest in the view frame rather than the body frame, because the headset's translation already carries the player's eyes round with a turn of the torso.

## The chest and pelvis turn

With `drive_chest` on, the body IK's torso rotation ([head-and-body.md, "Body IK via HumanIK"](head-and-body.md#body-ik-via-humanik)) turns the chest toward the player's torso instead of by `yaw_share` of the head's turn: the turn is the torso's heading carried into Rico's model space, relative to his facing, scaled by `chest_weight` and bounded by `chest_limit_deg`. With the body following, it covers what the deadband leaves; without it, it is the whole of the alignment, up to the limit. The squared stance ([controllers.md, "Posture and roomscale"](../input/controllers.md#posture-and-roomscale)) adds `stance_turn_share` of the same turn to the pelvis and the feet while it squares a standing character, so the legs face the torso as well. The pelvis is not turned outside the squared stance, because without the ankles pinned a pelvis turn would swing the legs.

With `torso_shoulders` on, the hand remap estimates the player's shoulder joints from the torso's heading rather than the head's, so looking over a shoulder no longer swings the arms' pivots with the head.

## The holsters on Rico's skeleton

`holsters.anchor` picks the body the holsters and pouches ride ([controllers.md, "Holsters, grab volumes, the foregrip, and the action-runs tier"](../input/controllers.md#holsters-grab-volumes-the-foregrip-and-the-action-runs-tier)). `Body` places them from the player's body dimensions, turned to the torso's heading when the estimate is on, so they stay at the player's hips, belt, chest, and shoulders when the player turns in the room; the player's own hands, before the body-scale remap, reach them. `Rico` takes the landmarks from his skeleton instead. After the pose-finalization pass's solves ([`Character::UpdatePropEffects`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-UpdatePropEffects)), the bones HumanIK drives for [`Effector::HIPS`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-HIPS), the hip joints ([`Effector::LEFT_HIP`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-LEFT_HIP) and its right twin), and the shoulder joints ([`Effector::LEFT_SHOULDER`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::Effector##variant-LEFT_SHOULDER) and its right twin), found through [`HumanIK::m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/animation::ik::HumanIK##field-m_HIKNodeAndBonePairs), are read with the head bone and published with the character's world transform. The weapon handling carries them into the cockpit frame through the head pose and the view frame on its next input tick.

The landmarks keep their meaning. The hip volume sits beside the middle of the hip joints, as far out as half the distance between the shoulder joints; the belt's front sits above and in front of it; the shoulder volumes sit on the shoulder joints; the chest volume sits between them; and the grenade and C4 pouches sit on the chest's front below them. The hip and belt volumes ride the pelvis (the line through the hip joints) and the shoulder, chest, grenade, and C4 volumes ride the chest (the line through the shoulder joints), so their configured offsets turn with the girdle they belong to. The seated adjustments apply as on the player's body. On the skeleton the volumes are reached by the hand the player sees, after the remap, since the landmarks are the character's. For a skeleton built to the body dimensions the two anchors place every volume at the same point, which a unit test holds, so the tuned offsets and the no-overlap guarantee carry over.

Where the skeleton is older than a quarter of a second or does not describe an upright body (the shoulders a plausible height below the head and the hips below them, both girdles a plausible width, the chest near the head), the volumes fall back to the player's body. That covers vehicles, the ragdoll, and the skydive.

The skeleton anchor is meant to go with the body following the torso. Without it, Rico faces where the turn input left him, and a player who turns in the room reaches for hips that are no longer beside them.

The default stays `Body` until the skeleton anchor has been checked in the headset.

## The readout

The Controllers tab's "Body alignment" section shows the estimate (the torso's heading, the head's, the neck's twist, the hands' heading and trust, the walk's trust, the chest and pelvis in the cockpit frame, and the spine's lean), whether the body follows and its heading relative to the view and its target, the chest turn the body IK took, which body the holsters rode, and the divergence after the solves. The divergence is the player's landmarks carried into Rico's model space less his own: the torso's turn from his facing, from his chest's line, and from his pelvis's line, and the offsets of the chest, the pelvis, both shoulder joints, and both hip joints. Its checkbox turns the `body_alignment` tracing target up to `DEBUG`, which logs the same picture once a second. The settings are in the tab's settings under "Body alignment", and the holster anchor with the holster settings. The pause menu's `VR: BODY` page has `root_follow` and `drive_chest` as `BODY FACES YOUR TORSO` and `CHEST FACES YOUR TORSO` ([pause-menu.md](../pause-menu.md#body-calibration)).

The offsets mix two things that are expected to differ: the proportions (Rico is taller than most players, and the volumes and hands are remapped onto him), and the placement the head pose gives the player's body, which hangs from Rico's animated head. A steady vertical offset of a few centimetres is the first; a horizontal offset that grows with a lean or a turn is the second, and is the one to watch.

## Unverified

None of this has been seen in the headset. It rests on these:

- that turning Rico in place through the orientation executor, with the game's turn acts suppressed, reads acceptably: his feet pivot with him unless the squared stance holds them;
- that the camera does not visibly translate as Rico turns under it: his animated head sits a few centimetres off his root's vertical axis and swings with him, and the composed head position follows it;
- that the turn accumulator's input-tick pair and the character's world transforms swap on the same tick, which the exact cancellation of the body's turn in the view relies on;
- that folding the frozen offset into the baseline produces no visible jump in the hands or the grab volumes on the frame it happens;
- the estimate's defaults: the deadzone, the neck limit, the hands' and walk's weights, and the pelvis's follow time;
- that the HumanIK hip and shoulder nodes are Rico's anatomical left and right, and that his post-solve joints give the holsters a better place than the estimated body does.
