# Floating HUD in VR

The flat game draws its HUD — health, ammo, minimap, reticle, objective markers, distance labels — straight onto the final LDR image (rendering §10). In VR that would pin the whole HUD to the screen plane at infinity, which is unreadable and uncomfortable. Instead the mod renders the HUD into its own texture, then draws that texture as a quad floating a couple of metres in front of the camera, damped so it follows the head without feeling nailed to it. World-anchored markers are a separate problem, handled by reprojection rather than by the panel — see "World-to-screen" below.

## Presentation: in-engine quad now, OpenXR layer later

There are two ways to present the HUD texture in 3D. An **OpenXR quad layer** (`XrCompositionLayerQuad`) hands the runtime the texture plus a pose and a size; the compositor draws it once at display rate, reprojected every frame and sampled directly — sharpest text, robust to game-framerate drops. An **in-scene quad** is one we draw into each eye ourselves, inside the stereo render.

The mod uses the in-scene quad. It lets distance, size, and follow-lag be dialled in against the existing desktop stereo render — the side-by-side preview — with no headset in the loop. A quad layer can only be seen in a headset, so it can't be iterated on the desktop, and no OpenXR runtime is wired up yet; the in-scene quad shows up in the preview immediately. Its downsides (the compositor double-samples it, so text is slightly softer; it's coupled to game framerate) don't matter while tuning, and we need an in-scene quad for world markers regardless. The quad layer stays on the table as a later swap for final sharpness once we're in-headset.

## Redirecting the HUD into our texture

The `CUIManager` singleton (the Scaleform GFx UI manager) renders into the engine's back buffer by default. `CUIManager::InitPlatformRT` is where it binds its render target: it builds a Scaleform render buffer (`m_RenderBuffer`), pulls the RTV and DSV from the engine surface (`GetRTVFromSurface` / `GetDSVFromSurface`), and binds them via `RenderTargetData::UpdateData`. It runs once at startup and again on every device or resolution reset (`InitializeSystem` / `RestoreAfterReset`).

The mod redirects the HUD by calling `RenderTargetData::UpdateData` itself (`payload/src/hud/binding.rs`): it creates its own offscreen HUD texture with an `ID3D11RenderTargetView` and depth-stencil view over it, then calls `UpdateData(m_RenderBuffer's inner RenderTargetData, myRTV, nullptr, myDSV)` to swap the UI's bound RTV/DSV to our texture. `UpdateData` refcounts the views it swaps (releases the old, AddRefs the new), so it's safe to call standalone. Because the rebind isn't tied to startup, it works at any time — a late-injected mod (where `InitPlatformRT` has already run) just calls `UpdateData` once after creating its texture, with no dependency on `InitPlatformRT` firing again. The mod does **not** hook `InitPlatformRT`; the redirect path does call it to rebuild `m_RenderBuffer` and reflow the movie rectangle to the texture's aspect (patching `m_BufferHeight` / `m_ViewRectBottom` for a non-square texture), then calls `UpdateData` to point the views at our texture.

The redirect installs lazily on the render thread and re-applies on a resolution change (a render-size change means the engine re-ran `InitPlatformRT` and discarded our rebind). The redirect target's own size is independent of both: the HUD is a flat panel viewed at a fixed apparent size, so `hud.render_resolution` pins its longer axis outright, or `hud.render_scale` derives it from the geometric mean of the per-eye render resolution (see [`swapchain-ownership.md`](stereo/swapchain-ownership.md) §6). Disabling the redirect — or unloading the mod — restores the engine's own binding by re-running `InitPlatformRT` so the UI never renders into a freed texture.

Once the HUD renders into our offscreen RTV instead of the engine's working surface, it's **automatically excluded from the scene composite** — the final composite writes directly into `m_BackBufferLinear` (which aliases swapchain buffer 0), and the HUD being off the working surface means it is not composited (rendering §12). No separate composite-suppression patch is needed. The mod then samples our texture as a 3D quad. `CUIManager::RenderOffScreenTextures` is *not* the tool here — it's for registered in-world movie screens, not the HUD.

## Comfort: lazy follow

A HUD rigidly locked to the head is the worst case — the eyes can't settle on it and head tremor is amplified. World-locked is the other extreme — it slides out of view the moment you turn to fight. The comfortable middle is a delayed, lazy follow: the panel eases toward a head-relative target, with position decoupled from orientation.

The panel sits at a configurable distance (3 m default, inside the 1.5–3 m comfort band — far enough to avoid vergence strain, near enough to read), flat. The gameplay HUD panel eases toward the head's current orientation with critically-damped quaternion slerp, with no deadzone; position is de-jittered separately. Because the orientation follow is a single slerp toward the head quaternion, it tracks yaw, pitch, and roll together. Roll matters for JC3: parachute, wingsuit, and vehicle sections all bank heavily, and VR head-tracking adds more. Without a deadzone the panel always tracks the head; the halflife alone controls how snappy the follow is. A quick flick barely moves the panel because the halflife limits how far it can travel in a short interval, but the panel never gets stuck at a permanent offset from where the head is looking.

The damper is a critically-damped exponential, so it converges fast with no overshoot and is frame-rate independent: `alpha = 1 - 2^(-dt/halflife); current = slerp(current, target, alpha)` (Holden's damper). The follow has two halflives, both exposed as sliders: a rotation halflife (0.2 s default) and a position de-jitter halflife (0.1 s default). See `payload/src/hud/config.rs` and `payload/src/hud/state.rs`.

The panel has two modes (`HudMode`, chosen per frame by `current_mode`): the gameplay HUD, which head-follows as above, and full-screen UI — movies, loading screens, and menus — which is world-static, latched in place so the head can look away from it. Each mode carries its own aspect (`hud_aspect` / `movie_aspect`). The world-static latch is currently disabled (`WORLD_STATIC_MOVIE_PANEL = false`): outside gameplay the render camera has no valid pose, so the latch would capture a degenerate transform and strand the panel off-screen. Until the head can be driven from the player's pose (issue #5), the full-screen panel head-follows like the gameplay HUD and only the aspect differs.

## Stereo depth: one panel, projected per eye

The panel sits at a finite world depth with correct stereo disparity. Its world-space corners are computed once per frame on eye 0 (from the chosen pose, distance, and effective aspect) and cached; both eyes then project those same world-space corners through their own per-eye view-projection. Computing the corners per-eye instead would cancel the world transform against the per-eye view and collapse the panel to view space — head-locked, zero disparity, at infinity. The panel resizes with distance to keep a constant apparent (angular) size, so the distance slider (0.3–10 m) can be changed freely without the HUD growing or shrinking; the physical size derives from the size knob, the distance, and the aspect (`hud::panel_height`).

## World-to-screen: split the HUD

This is the crux. The game places objective markers, enemy pips, and distance labels by projecting a world point through the camera's view-projection to a screen coordinate. If we baked that into the lagging panel texture, a marker that should point 20° to the left would end up wherever the panel happened to have drifted. The HUD is split by what each element actually *means*:

- **Static HUD** — health, ammo, minimap, reticle frame — has no world anchor. It's drawn into the panel texture at its native flat positions and lags with the panel. That lag is correct and comfortable, and no world-to-screen is involved.
- **World-anchored markers** mean a *direction*, not a panel position. They must not be baked into a lagging screen-plane projection. The mod reprojects them onto the panel's surface: `CUIManager::Get2DInfo` is hooked (`payload/src/hooks/ui.rs`) and its VP and camera matrix are replaced with the floating panel's orientation (`hud::compute_panel_vp`), so a marker lands at the correct spot on the panel even after follow-damping lag. The panel VP uses the damped follow rotation for orientation and the active camera's position and projection, re-aspected to the panel.

The engine makes this directly doable because its world-to-screen takes the VP as a parameter. `CUIManager::Convert3DCoords` projects a world point through a supplied `vp`, divides by w, aspect-corrects, and maps NDC to pixels, returning false when the point is behind the camera. `Get2DInfo` forwards a caller-supplied `vp` to it and handles the on-screen test and edge-clamping (`ClampToScreen`). Because `Convert3DCoords`' aspect correction reads `m_CachedViewportRatio` (the window height/width, refreshed every frame), the hook temporarily sets it to the panel's `height / width` (= `1 / aspect`) for the call and restores it afterwards, so other world-to-screen consumers in the same frame are unaffected. When the redirect or quad is off, the original caller's VP and camera are passed through unchanged. The default VP is the render camera's `m_ViewProjectionF`.

One consumer bypasses `Get2DInfo` entirely: the grapple reticle projects through a default-VP wrapper (`CUIManager::Convert3DCoordsDefault`, whose only callers are in `CHUDUI::UpdateGrappleReticle`). That wrapper is hooked separately (`payload/src/hooks/ui.rs`) to route it through the same panel reprojection, and doubles as the source of the aim depth below.

## World markers in the world (prototype)

`hud.world_markers` (off by default, the HUD tab's "World markers (prototype)" section) takes the on-screen point-of-interest markers off the panel and draws each as a billboard at its world point, keeping the angular size it had on the panel. Whether a marker is on screen is decided with the headset's field of view rather than the panel's narrower subtense, so a marker visible in the world does not become an edge arrow; markers outside the headset's view stay arrows on the panel. Each marker is cropped out of the HUD texture and cut out of the panel, and placed on the nearest visible surface along its line of sight (the scene depth at its pixel, with a raycast fallback), so it never reads as farther than a wall it shows through. The design, the depth placement, its limits, and the atlas it leads to are in [ui-decoupling.md](rendering/ui-decoupling.md#world-markers-the-crop-prototype). The section's readout lists the drawn markers (id, world point, clip origin, crop texels, true and drawn distance, and what set the distance), the layout's snapshot match, and the atlas viewport probe's safe-area numbers.

With the HUD atlas on (`hud.atlas.enabled`, off by default), the markers move into cells of their own instead of being cropped, and two readouts leave the panel under the controller scheme: the weapon readout is drawn above the gun hand, a health bar the mod draws, with the game's heat readout below it, on the inside of the off-hand's forearm, and the weapon selection cross at the hand steering the weapon wheel. How they are moved, placed, and shown is in [ui-decoupling.md](rendering/ui-decoupling.md#the-atlas-as-built).

Every panel composite blends premultiplied alpha, because the HUD texture and the egui panels hold premultiplied colour ([ui-decoupling.md](rendering/ui-decoupling.md#the-alpha-of-the-hud-texture)).

## Depth layers: the render-root partition (parked)

**Parked, off by default**: gameplay works at full rate, but the first pause permanently stops the UI update pump — see the post-mortem in `docs/issues/08-14-hud-overlays-and-depth.md`. The code lives in `payload/src/hud/split/`, kept compiling behind the disabled `hud.split` flag. The shipped depth mechanism is the single-panel warp (previous section); dynamic panel distance from the depth distribution is the planned complement.

With one HUD texture, every element shares the panel's depth: a crosshair crossing a distant marker gets the marker's disparity, and Scaleform has already composited overlapping clips into the texture, so nothing texture-side can separate them. The split (`hud.split`, `payload/src/hud/split/`) partitions the movie's render tree across three render roots, each drawn into its own texture every frame and composited at its own depth:

- **Static** — the six corner/edge safe-area containers and the weapon-selection wheel — at the panel distance, as before.
- **Markers** — `MCI_poi_stage` (all POIs, including the auto-aim marker) and `MCI_hud` (the target-tracker stage) — at its own distance, or per-marker depths with the warp below.
- **Center** — `MCI_safe_area_center`: the weapon/grapple/mech reticles, pickups, and center indicators — at the aim depth.

`Render` draws the movie's latest *captured* display-tree snapshot, and capturing is only safe on the game update thread (`Context::Capture` mutates the same active snapshot every game-thread UI write touches, with no writer lock — see `docs/issues/08-14-hud-overlays-and-depth.md` for why a multi-pass split cannot work, and why refreshing one layer per frame undersamples world-to-screen'd content visibly). So the split reshapes the *render* tree instead, adding no captures and no masking: at the capture seam (a detour on `GFx::MovieImpl::Capture` — called at the end of `CUIManager::PreRender`, after `UpdatePOIs` and `Advance`, deferred render lock held), the layer containers' render nodes move from the movie's main root into two extra `TreeRoot`s created in the movie's own render context, each swapped with an empty **tombstone** container at its original index so the display side's cached child indices (`GFx::DisplayList::TreeIndex`) stay valid. Node-local updates — the game's per-frame POI matrix/visibility writes — reach the real nodes wherever they live; structural changes (POI pool spawns and despawns in the shared container) reconcile at the seam each frame via tombstone parent checks and adoption of unknown children. The `CUIManager::Render` detour then reimplements the original's body: one `BeginFrame`, one capture consumption, `HAL::Draw` of the main root (now static-only) into the display target, and each extra root into its own texture via the engine's own off-screen pattern (`CreateRenderTarget` from the layer's views, `PushRenderTarget`, draw, `PopRenderTarget`) — every texture at full rate, every frame, from the same snapshot.

Any missing precondition (handles not discovered, targets missing, full-screen UI) degrades to the single-texture render, with the partition torn down (every node restored to its tombstone's place) whenever the split leaves gameplay.

The composite draws the layers farthest-first (alpha-blended overlays without a depth test, so painter's order decides occlusion between layers), each with its own world-space corner set at the panel's constant apparent size — moving a layer changes only its stereo depth. The clip paths are the authored top-level names of `hud.gfx`; the runtime attachment prefix under the root movie is configurable (`hud.split_path_prefix`) until the in-game display-tree dump (the HUD tab's Scaleform section, `payload/src/hud/scaleform.rs`) pins it.

**Per-marker depth (the warp).** The `Get2DInfo` hook records each on-screen marker's panel UV and its world point's distance from the panel anchor. The marker layer then draws as a grid mesh whose vertex shader (`payload/src/shaders/hud_layer_vs.hlsl`) displaces every vertex along its ray from the panel anchor by a marker-blended depth field: each point stays on its own anchor ray, so the cyclopean image is identical regardless of the field — only stereo disparity varies, giving each marker's neighborhood the disparity of the thing it labels. Depths blend in disparity (1/d) space with a radial falloff per marker and an edge fade; edge-clamped markers (directional indicators, not world points) stay at the layer's base depth. Overlapping markers blend to a disparity-weighted compromise — the texture already composited them, so that is the best any single-texture approach can do.

**Aim depth.** The grapple-reticle hook records the aim point's distance; the center layer's distance eases toward it (critically-damped, 0.15 s halflife) and settles back to the configured distance when nothing has been targeted for half a second, so the reticle sits at the vergence of the surface it targets.

**Overlay suppression (issue #8).** `hud.suppress_overlays` holds the full-screen overlay clips — the drowning tint, the omni-damage flash and health paint, and both directional damage-indicator containers — invisible, forced ahead of every capture in the same game-thread hook (independent of the split).

## The interactive egui debug panel (issue #24)

The egui debug UI normally renders straight onto the game back buffer as a flat overlay. In VR that overlay is invisible — the desktop mouse cursor lands in neither eye, and `BLOCK_FLIP` suppresses the engine flip the overlay rides on — so the mod can render egui as a second floating panel, an interactive 2D surface in 3D space, reusing the same quad and virtual-cursor machinery as the gameplay HUD.

It is a wholly independent panel (`payload/src/hud/egui_panel.rs`): its own offscreen render target, its own lazy-follow damping, and its own eye-0-cached corners, so it never touches `HUD_STATE` and cannot perturb the gameplay HUD. egui is tessellated exactly once per frame (the single-use `RendererOutput`) on eye 0 into the panel texture via `EguiState::render_to`, which clears the target to transparent first (the egui renderer neither clears nor draws on an empty frame); both eyes then draw the same cached world-space corners through their own per-eye VP, and the desktop mirror composites the same panel texture as a flat, letterboxed, alpha-blended overlay for a legible desktop copy. There is never a second egui pass.

The pointer comes from the desktop mouse or the controllers, whichever was used last (`payload/src/hud/pointer/egui_route.rs`). The desktop mouse's window client-pixel position (tracked by the `WndProc` detour) is normalized against the window and fed to egui as if it were the pointer, with positions scaled to the panel texture; button and wheel state are re-sourced the same way, while keyboard, text, and paste keep flowing through the ordinary egui wndproc path. While the panel is up, `EguiState` sizes the egui layout to the panel texture (`set_panel_mode`) and suppresses the window-pixel mouse messages that would otherwise reach egui in the wrong coordinate space.

The egui panel is gated behind an OpenXR session and the opt-in `hud.egui_panel.enabled` flag, and defaults off, so with it off the debug-panel path remains byte-identical to the flat-overlay baseline. F8 summons and dismisses the panel in-headset, toggling the enabled flag and egui's input capture together so "panel visible" always means "egui focused". Distance, size, and follow lag are on the HUD debug tab.

Separately, `hud.flat_overlay_visible` (on by default) hides the flat egui overlay from the desktop window only — both the direct back-buffer path and the deferred mirror-overlay texture (`payload/src/hud/mirror_overlay.rs`) honour it — without touching the VR floating panel, the headset image, or the in-world overlays. F4 toggles it, releasing egui's input capture while hiding so the game keeps input; this is for recording the desktop side-by-side stereo preview without the debug UI in frame.

### Pointing at the debug panel with the controllers

With `hud.egui_panel.controller_pointer` on (the default), either controller points at the panel (`payload/src/hud/pointer/egui_controller.rs`). The panel publishes its corners each frame with the captured camera basis that placed them, as the gameplay HUD does for the game-UI pointer below, and the input tick intersects each hand's aim ray with them. The pointing hand's hit becomes egui's pointer position, its trigger the primary button, its upper face button the secondary button, and its thumbstick the wheel: each stick axis scrolls past a deadzone at a speed proportional to its deflection beyond it, in egui points, so a held stick scrolls continuously. The panel's cursor dot follows whichever source owns the pointer, and a line from the pointing hand's aim pose to the panel is drawn through the world-space overlay ([controllers.md, "The world-space overlay"](input/controllers.md#the-world-space-overlay)), so it needs `controllers.overlay.enabled`.

The pointing hand starts as the dominant hand. It passes to the other hand when that hand pulls its trigger with its ray on the panel, or when only the other hand's ray is on the panel, and never while the pointing hand holds a press. The pull that hands the pointer over does not click, because the player has not seen where that hand points until its cursor appears; the new hand's trigger must be released first. The press logic is the game-UI pointer's: the trigger press is the derived `TriggerPress`, whose release threshold sits below its press threshold so a partial pull does not chatter; a press clicks only after a released sample and only when it begins with the ray on the panel; a press held as the ray leaves the panel drags along its edge while the ray still meets the panel's plane; and a tap shorter than an input tick still clicks once.

A held trigger press stays a click until the hit leaves a slop around where the press began, `hud.egui_panel.click_slop` (a fraction of the panel's height, 3% by default, with horizontal travel measured by the panel's aspect). Until then the pointer, and the cursor dot with it, stays at the press point, and the release is reported there too, so egui sees no motion and the hand's tremor never turns a press into a drag. Once the hit leaves the slop, the pointer jumps to it and follows it for the rest of the press, which drags a slider or a scroll area. egui also stops counting a press as a click once it has been held longer than its click duration, which is under a second by default; while the panel is shown that duration is `hud.egui_panel.click_duration_s` (2 s by default), so a deliberate held press still clicks. Both are on the HUD tab under the panel's settings. When the ray leaves the panel, egui is told the pointer is gone, releasing a held button first.

The input tick steps the controller pointer on every tick, also while the bindings stand down for the panel, and queues one update per tick; the game thread drains the queue before running egui. Arbitration follows the game-UI router's rules. The desktop mouse is used when it moves, clicks, or scrolls. The controller is used when its ray lands on the panel, clicks, scrolls, or moves on the panel beyond a small distance from where it rested while the mouse was last used, so tracking jitter of a controller at rest never takes the pointer from the mouse. Ownership changes only while the owner holds no button, and a source taking over with a button held has that button withheld until it is released. The flat desktop overlay (F6) is untouched: the arbitration only runs while the floating panel is active, and resets while it is not.

The chord's presses never act twice. The debug panel chord (the left stick click held and the right B pressed) masks both presses from the frame the panel pointer reads, as it does for the bindings and the game-UI pointer ([controllers.md, "Bindings and injection"](input/controllers.md#bindings-and-injection)), so the B press that opens or closes the panel never right-clicks on it. While the panel is enabled, the input tick idles the bindings and clears the game-UI controller pointer, and while egui holds the game's input the `SendMouseEvents` route cancels every game-UI pointer source, so the trigger neither fires a weapon nor clicks a game menu behind the panel.

## Controller pointing in the game UI

The game's Scaleform UI takes the dominant controller's aim ray as its mouse while a menu action set is active (`payload/src/hud/pointer/`, `payload/src/hooks/ui/mouse.rs`). It follows `hud.redirect`, `hud.quad`, and `hud.cursor.enabled`, and does not depend on the egui panel.

Camera setup captures the un-offset center camera together with the frame's cockpit HMD pose and world scale, and loading-camera composition uses the same sample. The HUD places the panel from that capture and publishes the corners it drew with the basis that placed them; a frame that draws no panel pose publishes no panel, and a panel drawn from the render-camera fallback without a capture is logged once, since the pointer cannot reach it. The pointer recovers the tracking-to-world rotation from the rendered head and cockpit HMD rotations, composes the aim pose the way the hands are composed, and intersects the ray with those corners. No laser beam is rendered; a controller hover shows the panel cursor dot regardless of the desktop cursor visibility policy.

The trigger is armed only after a released sample, so a trigger held across a mode change, focus loss, tracking recovery, or a desktop drag never becomes a click, and a press that misses the panel cannot click by entering it while held. The pointer reads the trigger's latched edges as well as its level, so a tap shorter than an input tick still clicks once. The UI binding table uses the same trigger for page navigation; the pointer claims it for a whole press when the press began with the ray on the panel, and otherwise the page action is held until the physical release. The other hand's page navigation and the stick, face-button, confirm, cancel, and tab bindings stay available.

One router picks the source for each delivery through [`UIManager::SendMouseEvents`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-SendMouseEvents). Taking over emits one neutral frame, a desktop drag keeps ownership through its release, and a desktop button held across a transition counts only after it is released. When the ray leaves the panel the mouse parks off the movie at the engine's own gamepad park position and stays there until the desktop mouse moves or clicks, so the hover never jumps to wherever the desktop cursor rests. A tracked release outside the panel after a press on it is a real release and lands at the clamped hit.

Every cancellation goes through one path: the redirect or cursor turning off, egui taking input (F8), a missing or replaced movie, and shutdown (F5 ejects the payload), as well as tracking or focus loss through the router. A button still held on the live movie is released off the movie, which Scaleform treats as a release outside and not as a click, so a cancelled press on "Quit to main menu" does nothing. The tracker keeps only the movie's address for comparison and never dereferences a saved movie pointer; there is no destructor hook or movie generation token, so a replacement at the same address between two deliveries is not distinguishable. Shutdown cancels synchronously on the game thread before the hook-removal worker starts, but Flash still processes the release on a later advance.

[`UIManager::PreUpdate`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##func-PreUpdate) is the frame's input boundary. Its detour peeks the route and prepares the map's controller sample before the input dispatch, so the map and the Scaleform delivery see the same sample, and each is committed only when its consumer runs: the engine skips both the dispatch and `SendMouseEvents` on a frame with no input-priority UI, and such a frame consumes no transition.

[`UIManager::m_IsUsingGamepad`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::ui_manager::UIManager##field-m_IsUsingGamepad) stays set under the default `controllers.gamepad_semantics`, and the engine forwards it to every menu movie as the ActionScript `OnUseGamepad` call. The native delivery has no other gate on it, and the mod bypasses the gamepad park while the controller owns the UI, but whether each menu's ActionScript still honours mouse rollover and press in gamepad mode cannot be read from the binary; it needs checking menu by menu in the headset.

### The native map

The map ([`CommMapUI`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI)) is not a Scaleform widget for input purposes: [`CommMapUI::OnManageInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI##func-OnManageInput) reads the mouse only in its mouse branch, chosen by `UIManager::m_IsUsingGamepad`. While the controller drives the map, the mod clears that flag for the duration of the call and answers the map's effector lookups through [`InputActionMap::GetActionEffector`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::InputActionMap##func-GetActionEffector) with effectors it owns (`payload/src/controllers/map/`): [`MOUSE1`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::input_action_map::Action##variant-MOUSE1) and `MOUSE2` for the first mouse device, the delta axes held at rest, and the GUI zoom actions for the steering's own [`m_DeviceIndex`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/input::steering::Steering##field-m_DeviceIndex). No physical action-map slot is written. The map's cursor comes from the prepared controller UV through the `GetMovieSpaceMouseCursor` hook, and a drag follows that absolute cursor once it moves far enough from the press. [`CommMapUI::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI##func-Update) runs later, from `UIManager::PreRender`, and clears the flag the same way on a frame whose dispatch the controller drove, so the gamepad reticle hides, the selection-relative elements follow the map's mouse selection point, and the context menu opens there. The two scopes share one saved value and restore it once. The map's own behaviour in each branch is in [the engine input doc](../engine/gameplay/input.md#the-maps-cursor-and-selection).

The trigger is the map's primary button: a pull and release selects, as a left click does. The map turns a held press into a drag once its cursor leaves the press point by a few stage pixels, which the ray's tremor and the trigger pull itself exceed, so the map cursor is held at the press point while the ray stays within a small slop of it (`CLICK_SLOP` in `controller.rs`, in panel UV) and the release is delivered at that point, so the selection lands where the trigger was pulled. Once the ray leaves the slop, the map sees the live ray for the rest of the press and drags the map with it, and the release ends the drag. Only the map's cursor is held; the panel's own pointer keeps following the ray.

A (`GUI_OK` from the menu bindings) acts at the controller's cursor. In the mouse branch the map opens the context menu for a clicked `GUI_OK` at its last mouse selection point and does not select, so on its own A opened "Place waypoint" at the stage's top-left corner on a fresh map, or at the last trigger click, for whatever was last picked. The shim watches the engine's own lookup of `GUI_OK` on the steering's selector under the map's scope (the steering's readers resolve through the detoured lookup and skip it while the action is blocked, so a blocked confirm is never converted). The map reads the confirm before `MOUSE2`, so the conversion reaches it in the same dispatch; a confirm read only later in the dispatch, by the common UI handling while the map skips its own reads (during an auto-move), changes nothing the map reads but still counts in the readout. When the effector it returns is clicked, the shim reports `MOUSE2` released in the same dispatch, which the map reads afterwards: that is a right click, which selects at the cursor and opens the context menu there, the mouse's equivalent of the gamepad's A at its cursor. A dispatch that cancels the controller's ownership never does this.

The off-hand stick zooms: a push past a threshold steps one snapped zoom level, a held push repeats at a fixed rate after a delay, and the return threshold sits below the push threshold so a stick resting near it does not chatter; stick drift never zooms. A cancelled press never selects: the map treats a released `MOUSE1` outside a drag as a selection and a released `MOUSE2` as the context action, so a cancellation releases `MOUSE1` only when [`m_MouseDragging`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/ui::comm_map_ui::CommMapUI##field-m_MouseDragging) shows a drag in progress, and never releases `MOUSE2` (outside the confirm conversion above, which a cancelling dispatch never makes). The physical wheel reaches the map through its own device path and still zooms alongside the controller. The sticks do not pan the map while the controller drives it: the map pans from its stick actions only in its gamepad branch, so the controller pans by dragging with the trigger.

The Controllers tab's "Native map" section shows the pointer (hand, UV, trigger, and which trigger it claims from the menu bindings), counters for trigger selections, drags, and context actions from A, and the latest map dispatch: whether the controller drove it and why not otherwise (the route was not the controller, the map ran outside the prepared dispatch, no mouse device, no UI manager), the route, the delivered UV and whether it is held at the press point, the button and zoom edges, the map cursor in stage coordinates, whether A was converted, and the map's dragging, selection, and context-menu flags and selection point after the dispatch.
