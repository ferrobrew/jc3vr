# Weapons in vehicles

The driver's built-in weapons aim by the dominant hand, the hands fire the weapons they hold from a passenger or stunt seat, and a mounted gun is manned with both hands on its handles. The code is in `crates/jc3vr-vehicles/src/vehicles/`: `driving/aim.rs` for the driver's weapons, `passenger.rs` for the seated body's aim, and `mounted/` for the mounted guns (`runtime.rs` takes, fires, and tears them, `control.rs` holds the handle aim and the tear gesture, `grip.rs` draws the hands, `recoil.rs` kicks the handles, `turret.rs` turns a manned vehicle gun, `vehicle_hold.rs` frees a vehicle's gun, and `gunner_seat.rs` seats the player). Each shot reaches the recoil through the per-shot observer (`mounted::recoil::on_shot`) the vehicle handling registers with the weapons crate's fire seam. The mounted guns' tuning is the `mounted`, `mounted.recoil`, and `gunner_seat` sections of the vehicles' [tuning file](../tuning.md).

## The driver's weapons

Under the controller scheme the driver's built-in vehicle weapons aim by the dominant hand. The player's post-camera update hands the vehicle the aim control's weapon point through [`Vehicle::SetAimTargetPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-SetAimTargetPosition) only while the player drives, and every built-in weapon aims from the stored point ([`Vehicle::m_AimingTargetPosition`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_AimingTargetPosition)). A pre-call detour substitutes the dominant hand's aim point, cast by the aim update earlier in the same tick, for the vehicle whose local-player seat is the driver's, which also rules out every vehicle an AI drives. Turrets turn toward the point at their own limited rate, so a hand-aimed turret trails the hand as it trails the gaze. With no hand aim the gaze point goes through unchanged. The debug UI counts the substitutions.

The motorcycle's and the jetski's personal weapon is a wielded weapon rather than a vehicle weapon, so the per-hand gun aim already aims it.

## Passenger and stunt seats

In a passenger seat and on a stunt position, a gun in the hand aims along its hand's sight line and fires from its trigger or its hand's upper face button (the vehicle weapon action, which a gun in the hand takes over in every seat; [bindings.md, "Vehicles"](bindings.md#vehicles), [hands-and-aiming.md, "Gun aim"](hands-and-aiming.md#gun-aim)). In the driver's seat the triggers are the pedals, so a gun there fires from the face button alone.

A gun in a hand in a seat other than the motorcycle's is not the wielded weapon: the inventory's weapon update skips the wielded weapon there ([engine: weapon-equip-and-drop.md](../../engine/gameplay/weapon-equip-and-drop.md)), so the reconcile wields nothing in such a seat, and every gun in a hand is armed in its hand and fired by the mod's per-hand tick ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)). The same holds in the boost wingsuit. Leaving the seat wields the primary's again.

The seated upper body aims through the vehicle aim blend ([`VehicleAimBlendState::UpdateSamplers`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::VehicleAimBlendState##func-UpdateSamplers)), which turns the torso and gun arm toward the aim control's weapon point ([`PlayerAimControl::m_AimPos`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/aim::aim::PlayerAimControl##field-m_AimPos), its weapon slot), which the game sets from the gaze. A passenger looking ahead while pointing out of the side window would twist toward the windscreen, and the shot would leave the drawn gun's muzzle at an angle. So after the aim control's post-simulation update, in a seat other than the driver's and off a mounted gun (in a seat by `jc3vr_body::controllers::facts::seat::rides_in_seat`: Rico has a vehicle and [`Character::IsInRidingInVehicleState`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-IsInRidingInVehicleState) holds), while a gun hand aims, the weapon slot is set to the gun hand's aim point: the acting hand's, or the other's when only it holds a gun. The game copies the slot into the control parameters and the character's weapon point right after, in the post-camera update, so the body leans and points the gun where the hand does, and the drawn gun and the shot agree. With no gun in the hands the game's point stands. The aim update logs `the seated body follows the gun hand's aim` and `the seated body stops following the gun hand's aim`, and the Status page has a PASSENGER AIM row.

## Mounted guns

A mounted gun, standalone or in a vehicle's gunner seat, aims by its handles as a gun on a pivot does: when the hands take the gun, a reference is taken from their midpoint, the lever from the handles to the pivot, and the barrel, and from then on the barrel points from the hands' midpoint through the pivot. The barrel reaches the gun through its mount's motors, which the mounted aim drives toward that point, so the runtime keeps them running while the hands hold the handles and disables them only while a hand holds the gun's own body, which swings it on its joints.

A gun the hands grip by its own body, its holder, or a handle is driven without the use prompt: the runtime gives it a temporary shooter, fires it on the trigger, and tears it off on a sustained pull. Under the controllers a standalone mounted gun's use prompt is hidden from the moment the gun is seen (`mounted::runtime::hide_standalone_prompts`), so the approach never offers the native use, a state that is hard to leave; a vehicle's gun keeps its prompt, since the gunner seat needs the native use, and the gamepad scheme keeps every prompt. A native use the player already entered on a standalone gun is stopped and taken over, keeping the hands where the game placed them, and the aim is derived from the hands after that stop so the gun does not fire along a frozen barrel.

### Taking the handles

A hand holding a loadout item takes no handle ([two-handed-inventory.md, "Traversal"](two-handed-inventory.md#traversal)). Each hand takes its own handle like a physical control. A handle within reach ranks over every other grip target ([grip.md](grip.md)), so a hand closing on a handle takes it rather than the gun's body, its holder, or the vehicle part the gun turns on, whose loose hold no pull could turn. A grip press with the hand's grip within `mounted.grab_radius_m` of its handle takes it (a grab volume, `GrabVolume::MountedHandle`), and only releasing the grip lets go: a holding hand that drifts off keeps aiming from where it is.

Only the holding hands aim: two from their midpoint, one from itself, and a change of hands takes a new reference, so taking or letting go never jumps the gun. With both hands off, the gun keeps the direction they last gave it, held in the body frame, so a gunner can let go to throw a grenade and find the gun where they left it. A holding hand is drawn on its handle, blending on and off over the anchored tier's blend time with the controller's orientation kept (`vehicles::state::apply`). On a standalone gun held without the native mounted state the tiers leave the hands free and give no anchor weight, so the grip's own blend draws the hand (`mounted_hand_weight`). The handle pose declares matching finger grips at the same weight, and grapple-owned hands declare neither. A hand on a handle also leaves the physical hands' palms (`physical_hands::free_hands`, through the holds published in `jc3vr_body::controllers::facts::mounted`), so its hand body does not fight the gun. Native-use takeover is separate and can keep the hands with open grips until ownership is lost.

Taking a handle plays `Event::HandleGrab` and logs `the hand took the mounted gun's handle` with `manned`, whether the character mans the gun natively; letting go logs `the hand let go of the mounted gun's handle` with its reason. The Status page's MOUNTED GUN row shows which hands are on the handles and counts grabs, releases, and kicks.

### Recoil in the handles

Each shot of the gun the hands aim kicks a damped spring back along the barrel and down (`mounted.recoil.kick_back_m` and `kick_dip_m`), as the breech jumps back and the muzzle climbs about the pivot. The spring's offset moves the handles the hands are drawn on, and each shot plays `Event::MountedKick` ([haptics.md](haptics.md)) on the holding hands. The spring is stiff, and its kick is limited to `max_shots` shots' worth, so a minigun holds the handles back and shakes them rather than driving them off. The aim is left alone, since the game's gun takes its own recoil about its pivot. The shots are seen by the per-shot seam on [`WeaponBase::Fire_ProjectileBase`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-Fire_ProjectileBase); the game's own fire rumble still reaches the hands through the continuous rumble ([weapon-handling.md, "Recoil"](weapon-handling.md#recoil)).

### Tearing a gun off

A sustained pull on a held gun (`mounted.tear_lag_m` past the handle's reach, at `tear_load` of the hand's strength, for `tear_hold_s`; `control::Tear`) tears it off its mount (`runtime::tear_off`). The gun's own detach ([`WeaponBase::DetachMountedGun`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-DetachMountedGun)) stops its use and drops it, but removes only the constraint that attaches it and leaves the mount's horizontal and vertical hinges enabled (the game's own tear equips the gun in the same message, out of the physics world, so the hinges never act there), which would keep the gun on its tripod. So the runtime takes the attach constraint and the hinges before the detach, since the drop clears the weapon's references to them, and disables each afterwards ([`WeaponBase::m_ConstraintAttach`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_ConstraintAttach), `mounted::native::free_from_mount`); `mounted gun: the pull tore the gun off` counts them. The gun then goes into the hand through the loadout table, which holsters a heavy weapon ([physical-weapons.md](physical-weapons.md)), or, refused, stays where it fell with its pickup enabled ([`WeaponBase::PickupEnable`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-PickupEnable)), which the drop skips off a mount. The game's use-item action is no fallback: it picks up the nearest pickup rather than the torn gun, and its slot swap can eject the player's own weapons.

A gun on its own mount comes off only where the game lets it ([`WeaponBase::m_MountedCanBeDetached`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_MountedCanBeDetached)). The mounted weapons' entity files set it through `mounted_can_be_detached`, and only the standalone tripod minigun (`w201_minigun_mount`) has it; that the property fills the field was read from the data, not traced in the code.

A vehicle's gun (the armoured jeep's `w206`, the vehicle minigun `w201_minigun`, and the Uzhas cannon `w205`, none of which have the flag) comes off the way the vehicle's explosion lets it go (`mounted::vehicle_hold`). The vehicle keeps a reference to each gun it carries ([`Vehicle::m_MountedWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_MountedWeapon)) and places it on its mount part every update ([`Vehicle::UpdatePostParts_WeaponsAttachment_Parallel`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##func-UpdatePostParts_WeaponsAttachment_Parallel)), so the detach alone does nothing. When the vehicle explodes it drops each gun and clears its entry; the tear does the same for the one gun after the detach, while the vehicle is outside its parallel update phases ([`Vehicle::m_InParallelUpdatePhaseCounter`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::Vehicle##field-m_InParallelUpdatePhaseCounter)). The mount parts stay on the vehicle, empty.

From the gunner seat the same pull asks for the game's own exit from the seat (a click of the exit action, asked once more after `mounted.seat_exit_retry_ms`). Once the player has left the seat, the gun, which the exit stopped using, is torn off into the hand that pulled. The tear is given up if the player is still seated after `seat_exit_timeout_ms`.

## The gunner seat

A vehicle's empty gunner seat is entered by stepping into it (`mounted/gunner_seat.rs`): a player whose head stands behind an unmanned vehicle gun's handles (within `gunner_seat.spot_radius_m` of the point `spot_behind_m` behind their midpoint on the level, and no more than `spot_below_m` below them) for `dwell_ms`, while the game offers the gunner seat from where the player is ([`NStateTask_InputVehicleEnterTask::HasAvailableGunnerDestination`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::NStateTask_InputVehicleEnterTask##func-HasAvailableGunnerDestination)), is lowered into it: the vehicle-entry action is held, as the game takes a held entry past its hold time for the gunner seat ([`NStateTask_InputVehicleEnterTask::Update`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/vehicle::vehicle::NStateTask_InputVehicleEnterTask##func-Update)), until the player mans the gun or `hold_timeout_ms` passes. A gun is unmanned when it has no user, so a gunner pulled out ([character-grabs.md](character-grabs.md#holding-on-and-yanking)) or shot frees it. After a hold that seats nothing, the head must leave the spot before another starts. The log has `gunner seat: the player stands at an empty gunner seat; entering it`, then `gunner seat: the player mans the gun from its gunner seat` or `gunner seat: the entry did not seat the player`.

A gun's start of use in the mounted state fades a local player user out ([`Character::FadeCharacterInOut`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##func-FadeCharacterInOut)) and holds them transparent while they man it, for the game's turret camera. In the headset the view is at the head, so nothing of Rico would be drawn, not even his free hands. Under the controllers the mounted tick fades him back in as soon as he mans a gun while held faded out ([`m_FadingIn`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/character::character::Character##field-m_FadingIn) clear) and logs it (`mounted::input_tick::keep_gunner_visible`).

In the seat the gun is left to the native use, which holds the player in the seat until the exit action; the runtime neither stops it nor refuses its start. A vehicle gun turns on two powered hinges of its vehicle part, which the weapon's pre-simulation update drives toward a desired yaw and pitch: from the look input when the weapon turns from the stick for a player ([`WeaponBase::m_MountedInputIsControlledByWeapon`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##field-m_MountedInputIsControlledByWeapon)), and from the aim point otherwise ([`WeaponBase::UpdateMountedInput`](https://ferrobrew.github.io/pyxis/#/JustCause3_Steam_20206564/item/equipment::weapon::WeaponBase##func-UpdateMountedInput) decides). A gun that turns from the stick would ignore the hands, so while the hands hold the handles and their aim point is fresh, the look-input path is refused (`mounted::turret`) and the hinges turn toward the hands' point at the gun's own speeds and within its limits; a gamepad turns the turret as before. The log says once whether the gun turned from the stick, and the debug UI counts the ticks the hands aimed it. The vehicles' driving view also moves the gunner's view back from the handles ([steering-wheel.md, "The driving view"](steering-wheel.md#the-driving-view)).
